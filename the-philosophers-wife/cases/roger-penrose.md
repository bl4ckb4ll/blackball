# Roger Penrose: the people around *The Impossible Man*

**Case status (2026-10-17):** Informed first pass through the biography's publisher, an interview with its author, and several substantive reviews. **The complete book has not been inspected.** Specific quotations attributed to the book through a reviewer are secondhand here.

## The life alongside the scientific one

- **Joan Wedge (first wife):** Penrose married Joan and they had three sons. The marriage deteriorated. A detailed [Physics World review by Chanda Prescod-Weinstein](https://physicsworld.com/a/so-you-think-you-know-roger-penrose-be-prepared-to-be-shocked/) describes the biographer's account of Penrose's inattentiveness as husband and parent; it reports interviews with two sons who recalled seeing what they called **“physical aggression”** toward their mother. **Provenance:** sons' retrospective account, described by biographer, then by reviewer; not an independent finding here. It needs page-level review of the book and any responses.
- **Judith Daniels (not his wife):** During his marriage to Joan, Penrose sent Daniels numerous detailed letters, both about his work and about his wish for her romantic affection. Daniels' letters as characterized by the reviewer indicate that she did not reciprocate that romantic wish. The reviewing physicist explicitly calls her own theory of Daniels's reasons a *theory* rather than a fact. **Do not invent Daniels's interior thoughts**. The correspondence deserves careful reading in its own right.
- **Vanessa Thomas (student and second wife):** Thomas was a PhD student in Penrose's group at Oxford. Prescod-Weinstein reports that the relationship began while he supervised her and that she did not complete the doctorate; she later taught mathematics. Avoid inferring exactly who made which educational decision without Thomas's account. The review says the marriage eventually ended in separation.
- **Sons:** The same review recounts sons expressing wishes to improve their relationship with Penrose despite difficult childhood memories. It reports that when Barss asked Penrose about reconnecting, he replied that family involvement would distract him from work. This is a reviewer's rendering of an interview, **not a quote personally checked in the book**.

## How this differs from genius mythology

A spouse or doctoral student's abandoned work is not a mathematical theorem's price tag. Even if someone claims that total intellectual dedication requires sacrificing relationships, the claim needs evidence; greatness in mathematics is not a causal proof. Penrose's career benefited from his family, mentors, students, collaborators and institutions. Which contributions and supports had opportunity costs can be investigated separately.

His wife Joan, estranged wife Vanessa, Daniels, and children must not be treated as simply obstacles to a “greater” work. Equally, the fact of failed marriage does not settle who caused every disagreement.

## Competing interpretations

- Some reviewers regard the personal account as central to understanding how the science was made; others regard marital failures as ordinary for the period. Neither proves the specific conduct did or did not occur.
- A reviewer observes that Barss sometimes privileges Penrose's recollection despite obtaining interviews with the wives; check how much actual testimony they contributed.
- Barss himself says biographical recollections need not fit together into a single harmonious account. His stated practice was archival checking and offering Penrose opportunities to respond.
