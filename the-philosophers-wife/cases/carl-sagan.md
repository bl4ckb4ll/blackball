# Carl Sagan: three marriages, different witnesses

**Case status (2026-10-17):** First-person Margulis interview inspected; *The New Yorker*, *Guardian* and *Publishers Weekly* reviews inspected; selected quotations **reproduced by a reviewer of Keay Davidson's book**, but the complete original biographies are not yet read.

## Lynn Margulis (first wife, scientist)

Carl Sagan and Lynn Margulis married in 1957. Margulis obtained scientific training, raised two sons, and ultimately made major contributions to evolutionary cell biology, especially endosymbiosis. Their marriage ended in 1964. [Arizona State University's Embryo Project](https://embryo.asu.edu/pages/lynn-petra-alexander-sagan-margulis-1938-2011) provides a dated account of her education and career; [the Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/6101) confirms the dates.

In a 2004 [University of Chicago Magazine interview/profile](https://magazine.uchicago.edu/0402/features/speed-print.shtml), Margulis recalled being overwhelmed by the demands of children and an equally demanding husband. Her own verdict on having divorced twice: **“I quit my job as a wife twice.”** She argued that being a good wife, mother, and first-class scientist at the same time was impossible under the arrangement she experienced. This is her own account, though quoted within a journalist's profile.

Biographer Keay Davidson interviewed Margulis for *Carl Sagan: A Life*. A [2022 review reproduces a passage attributed to Davidson, p. 97](https://sheseeksnonfiction.blog/2022/06/05/carl-sagan-a-life-review/): Margulis described Sagan leaving diapers, laundry, and most housework to her as he traveled for scientific meetings. **This is a secondary reproduction of a biographical passage; the primary printed page has not been examined in this pass.** An independent [*New Yorker* review](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1999/11/22/billions-and-billions) likewise recounts Margulis's description of him as profoundly difficult in personal relationships.

**Serious testimony to verify:** The same 2022 review reproduces a statement attributed to Margulis in Davidson, p. 140, in which she alleges Sagan **“hit me a few times.”** Its provenance is *Margulis interviewed by Davidson → Davidson's published account → later blogger's reproduction*. Record it as an **allegation by a named participant**, not a fact independently verified here. Acquire the actual edition and locate its notes; check the exact passage, whether Sagan's side was documented, and whether other sources address it. Do not remove the testimony merely because it is uncomfortable.

## Linda Salzman (second wife, artist)

Sagan married the artist Linda Salzman in 1968; their marriage ended in divorce in 1981. Her artistic career and contributions must be recorded in their own right, rather than simply as a phase in his biography. The [*Publishers Weekly* review of William Poundstone's Sagan biography](https://www.publishersweekly.com/9780805057669) reports Salzman's difficult experience of the relationship's end when Sagan and Ann Druyan fell in love. Other secondary summaries of Davidson suggest disputes over his absences, domestic labor, and the expectations placed on Linda. **Her own testimony remains a priority**; do not fill the gap with Sagan's or a reviewer's psychology.

## Ann Druyan (third wife, writer and collaborator)

Sagan married Ann Druyan in 1981; they remained married until he died in 1996. Druyan participated materially in science communication and the Voyager Golden Record project. She and other friends described real companionship and love, a counterweight to any account that Sagan was always emotionally incapable. [Druyan speaking to *TIME*, 2017](https://time.com/4889677/carl-sagan-ann-druyan-solar-eclipse/) and [Carolyn Porco's first-person 1999 *Guardian* review](https://www.theguardian.com/books/1999/nov/20/biography) are distinct favorable perspectives. **Being generous to a colleague or a later partner does not negate a first wife's account; nor does a first wife's account describe every relationship.**

## New excerpts to verify in Davidson's printed biography

The [2022 reviewer of Davidson's biography](https://sheseeksnonfiction.blog/2022/06/05/carl-sagan-a-life-review/) reproduces several additional, specifically located passages. The source is **the later review of an interview in the book**, not firsthand access to Davidson's page or its recording:

- **p. 285:** Lynda Obst reportedly recalled Sagan expecting Linda Salzman to cook and clean for him so that he could concentrate on science, despite Linda's desire for more than domestic work. This is Obst's characterization of Carl's expectation, **not** Linda's own independently recorded view here.
- **p. 287:** Son **Nick Sagan** reportedly described Carl arguing with Linda over the ordering of toast and a breakfast shake to save approximately **twenty seconds per day**. The illuminating feature is the asymmetry: he used a calculation to optimize *her* service of him. Because the witness's statement reaches us through a biographer and a reviewer, check both the printed page and Nick's context before presenting dialogue in a story.
- **pp. 425–426:** Ann Druyan acknowledged that Carl seldom performed domestic chores (cooking, making beds or folding towels), **but described the marriage as profoundly loving**. It would be false to infer from continuing unequal housework that Druyan secretly shared Linda or Lynn's assessment. Her account is a distinct first-person testimony reproduced at two removes.

This sequence offers a sharper empirical comparison than saying that Sagan was sometimes “difficult”: different partners report different valuations of an apparently persistent division of domestic work. Distinguish household effort, subjective satisfaction, access to paid assistance, career loss and power in decision-making. **Critical next step** is to inspect Davidson pp. 97, 140, 285, 287 and 425–426 together; find Linda Salzman's *own* speech or archival materials before making claims about her unreported thoughts.
