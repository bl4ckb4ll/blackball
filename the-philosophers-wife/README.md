# The Philosopher's Wife

**Status: first-pass source investigation, 17 October 2026.** This is a branch-local research project and narrative essay within Blackball, not a verdict on intellectuals as a class.

**Question:** What was it actually like to live with celebrated philosophers, scientists, mathematicians, and public intellectuals? What unpaid work, affection, freedom, intellectual opportunity, professional advancement, safety, and recognition were required or sacrificed in the household? Was the celebrated person also miserable, or only the people around them?

The name is deliberate, but **“wife” is not a universal category**: include spouses, partners, children, students, assistants, and collaborators when there is evidence that they bore costs. Record each person as a person in their own right. Do not reduce an accomplished woman to a famous man's marital appendage.

## Questions and variables

For each household or relationship, investigate:

1. **What happened?** Date, location, duration, number and ages of children, division of domestic work, money, travel, teaching, research time.
2. **Who says so?** Wife's own contemporary words; other participant's account; children's recollections; documents; oral history; biographer's interpretation; reviewer quoting a biographer.
3. **What did each person want?** Professional work, affection, security, sex, children, solitude, reputation. Do not assume identical or stable wishes.
4. **What was the cost?** Unpaid care, interrupted education, lost income, isolation, coercion, fear, custody battles, professional erasure. Identify evidence for each cost, not just the outcome of divorce.
5. **Was the relationship always bad?** Record years of warmth, collaboration, later reconciliation, alternative accounts and changes over time; a divorce is not itself evidence of sustained misery.
6. **Who gained status?** Compare the famous person's reception in public with the private person's authorship, labor and achievements. Do not substitute success in physics for evidence of being a good spouse, or private misconduct for a refutation of physics.
7. **What would disconfirm the account?** Primary records, contradictory testimony, corrected chronology, omitted financial support, documented cooperative household decisions.

## Evidence rule

Blackball's ordering applies: original letters and contemporaneous records > first-person interviews and autobiography with perspective stated > biographies actually read > reviews of those biographies > bibliographic metadata and discovery snippets.

- **Never pretend a book has been read in full when only reviews or excerpts have been inspected.**
- Report accusations of violence as *attributed testimony* and say whether the quoted original was inspected; do not silently turn an allegation into an independently established finding.
- Do not write invented private scenes into a nonfiction dossier. A fictionalized version, if written later, needs a separate label.
- Leave spouses' scientific authorship unresolved unless specific work products or contemporaneous sources can establish it.
- No broad prevalence inference from selected famous cases: there is no sampled denominator.
- Keep short quotations and source links, not unauthorized reproductions of full copyrighted works.
