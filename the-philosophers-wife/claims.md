### Observations to test across cases

**Domestic labor is measurable even when happiness isn't:** hours of care, copying manuscripts, secretarial work, scientific inquiries, lost training time, staffing and wages. **Misery is reported, not inferred from divorce.** Emotional evaluations may vary across partners in the same person's life. Accounts of violence require strict attribution and direct confirmation of the witness and edition.

## Interpretive hypotheses — NOT findings

- **Asymmetric exemption:** Publicly praised intellectual work can supply a social rationale for escaping household care obligations. Requires comparative household evidence.
- **Double standards:** A thinker may preach equality and personal freedom but privately place unequal expectations on a partner. Test using actual expressed principles and documented demands in the same time period.
- **Biographical erasure:** A famous man's timeline may make the partner appear only when she serves, supports, leaves, or dies. Test by recoding biographies around the partner's independent life and authored work.
- **Reputation shield:** An admirer may downgrade first-person spouse testimony or praise unrelated scientific contributions instead of addressing a particular act. Test with paired accounts and response patterns.
- **Counterhypotheses:** Some partnerships support both careers; a particular spouse's recollection may be partial or mistaken; fame may neither cause nor excuse domestic harm; some intellectuals show exceptional practical care. **Seek actual countercases**, not abstract defenses.

## Claims not presently justified

- “Genius necessarily causes emotional cruelty.”
- “All these marriages were entirely miserable.”
- “Scientific achievements were necessarily built on abuse.”
- “An unhappy spouse must have secretly written the scientist's work.”
- “Every allegation in a review has been independently verified.”
- “These anecdotes measure the prevalence of suffering among intellectuals.”
