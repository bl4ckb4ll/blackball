# Hyde Park

Hyde Park is a Blackball case file for studying how elite institutional authority, ordinary work, poverty, neighborhood boundaries, policing, violence, and socially recognized "potential" coexist in one small geographic area.

The point is not to turn proximity into personal guilt. It is to make the local political economy visible enough that later claims about markets, opportunity, merit, poverty, public safety, and leadership can be checked against the material surroundings in which influential people lived and worked.

## Anchor observation: Venkatesh at the Hyde Park/Woodlawn boundary

Sudhir Venkatesh arrived at the University of Chicago in 1989. In the opening of *Gang Leader for a Day*, he describes Cottage Grove Avenue as a historic boundary between Hyde Park and Woodlawn. On the university side he describes a manicured Gothic campus and privileged students; on the other side, poor Black residents offering cheap labor and services, including changing oil and washing windows, as well as panhandling and selling drugs.

That passage is unusually valuable because it records an economic relationship rather than merely saying that a rich university sat beside a poor neighborhood.

See [Sudhir Venkatesh](../people/sudhir-venkatesh.md) and [University of Chicago — Hyde Park economic authority](../University%20of%20Chicago/hyde-park-economic-authority.md).

## People and institutional careers inside this geography

- **Milton Friedman:** joined the University of Chicago faculty in 1946 and remained until 1977. University accounts describe the Friedmans as Hyde Park residents. His Chicago years therefore predate Venkatesh's 1989 observation but belong to the same university-neighborhood political geography.
- **Eugene Fama:** entered the University of Chicago in 1960, joined the faculty in 1963, and spent essentially his entire academic career there apart from a two-year period in Belgium. He was on the faculty in 1989 when Venkatesh arrived and throughout Venkatesh's Chicago research years.
- **Barack Obama:** lived at 5429 S. Harper Avenue while working as a community organizer from 1985 to 1988; left for Harvard Law School; returned to Chicago and taught at the University of Chicago Law School from 1992 through 2004 while again living in Hyde Park/East Hyde Park for much of that period.
- **Sudhir Venkatesh:** arrived as a Chicago sociology graduate student in 1989, made the Hyde Park/Woodlawn boundary observation, and later became a Harvard Society of Fellows Junior Fellow from 1996 through 1999 while the Chicago fieldwork relationship was winding down.

Blackball should not manufacture a claim that any particular one of these people literally encountered a particular windshield washer on a particular commute. The stronger and better-supported fact is that prominent economists, lawyers, politicians, students, and scholars lived and worked inside the same compact social geography in which Venkatesh observed low-priced informal labor and a sharp boundary of wealth and institutional protection.

## 2021 killings and institutional response

Two different 2021 killings of University of Chicago affiliates belong in this file because both happened in Hyde Park and produced extensive institutional responses.

- [January 9, 2021 — Yiran Fan and the language of "potential"](2021-01-09-yiran-fan-and-the-language-of-potential.md). Fan was a 30-year-old Ph.D. student in the joint Chicago Booth / Economics financial-economics program. He was killed in a parking garage at Regents Park in East Hyde Park during a citywide shooting spree. University president Robert Zimmer later said scholars emphasized "just how much potential he had." The same shooting spree killed Aisha Johnson and Anthony Faulkner and wounded others, creating an unusually useful same-event comparison for how different victims' unrealized futures are described.
- [November 9, 2021 — Shaoxiong "Dennis" Zheng and the security response](2021-11-09-shaoxiong-zheng-and-security-response.md). Zheng, a 24-year-old recent UChicago statistics master's graduate, was shot and killed during a robbery near his apartment in Hyde Park. The killing led to a memorial, university-wide safety discussions, and new security measures.

## Blackball questions

1. Who is described as having "potential," a "promising career," exceptional ability, or a future of social consequence after a premature death?
2. How strongly do those descriptions correlate with elite educational affiliation, occupational pipeline, family resources, internships, professional endorsements, or institutional membership?
3. When several people are killed in the same incident, how much coverage, memorialization, institutional action, biographical detail, and future-oriented language does each receive?
4. Which forms of work were visibly available to poor residents around Hyde Park in different decades, and what did they pay?
5. Which forms of authority were available to university residents: professorships, finance, law, government, publishing, consulting, or control over institutions?
6. What did University of Chicago economists and social scientists write about labor, poverty, markets, human capital, policing, or inequality while living and working inside this local economy?
7. How did the University police and define the boundary between campus, Hyde Park, Woodlawn, Kenwood, and surrounding neighborhoods over time?

## Evidence discipline

Do not infer that Yiran Fan or any other victim came from a wealthy family without evidence. Elite educational placement can be measured separately from family wealth. Likewise, do not turn geographic coexistence into a claim that a named professor personally witnessed a named informal worker.

The testable structural claim is enough: **an institution whose members exercised enormous authority over how the world understood markets, opportunity, poverty, law, and public policy was physically embedded in a neighborhood where large economic differences were directly visible and where some residents sold very low-priced labor to the university population.**
