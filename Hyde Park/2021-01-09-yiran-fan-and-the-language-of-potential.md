# January 9, 2021 — Yiran Fan and the language of "potential"

## Event

Yiran Fan, age 30, was a fourth-year Ph.D. student in the Joint Program in Financial Economics run by Chicago Booth and the Kenneth C. Griffin Department of Economics. On January 9, 2021, he was shot and killed while sitting in his car in the parking garage of the Regents Park apartment complex in East Hyde Park.

Police identified Fan as the first victim in a shooting spree attributed to Jason Nightengale. The spree also killed **Aisha Johnson**, a 46-year-old doorwoman, and **Anthony Faulkner**, age 20, and wounded several other people.

This matters for Blackball because the same perpetrator and same day create a partly controlled comparison: multiple lives were destroyed by the same sequence of violence, while the victims occupied very different institutional and occupational positions.

## The University of Chicago's description of Fan

The University's memorial coverage described Fan as an exceptional student, collaborator, research assistant, teaching assistant, and promising scholar. Faculty noted that he had received the top grade in the first-year macroeconomics core examination and continued to excel.

At the January 14 vigil, University president Robert Zimmer said he had heard from accomplished scholars about Fan's achievements and **"just how much potential he had."**

This is the phrase Blackball should preserve.

The grief itself is not the object of criticism. Fan's killing was a catastrophe for him, his parents, his friends, colleagues, and community. The research question is narrower: what does an institution mean when it describes one dead young person's unrealized future as unusually large or socially consequential?

## "Potential" as institutional legibility

There are at least two meanings of "potential" that should not be collapsed.

1. **Unlived human future.** A person dies young and loses decades of possible relationships, work, experience, contribution, and change. This meaning does not require a prestigious résumé.
2. **Institutionally legible future.** A person has already accumulated markers that allow other powerful people to forecast a consequential career: elite enrollment, examinations, faculty sponsorship, research appointments, internships, fellowships, professional placements, publications, or other endorsements.

The second form can be observed and measured before anybody knows what the person would actually have accomplished. It is therefore partly a property of the **pipeline surrounding the person**, not simply an intrinsic quantity residing inside him.

A person without access to elite institutions may have equal or greater ability while leaving much less documentary evidence that journalists, professors, employers, or public officials recognize as "promise."

## Do not make an unsupported family-wealth claim

Fan's educational history demonstrates substantial institutional attainment: Peking University, graduate study in financial mathematics, research work, and the Chicago Booth / Economics Ph.D. program. That does **not** by itself establish that his parents were wealthy or that family money caused his trajectory.

Blackball should investigate family resources if reliable sources become available, but should not infer them from his university affiliations or nationality.

The broader hypothesis can be tested across many cases: **how much of publicly recognized "potential" is predicted by family resources and access to prestigious institutional pipelines?** Fan is evidence for the language and the institutional pathway, not presently evidence for parental wealth.

## A built-in comparison from the same shooting spree

The same January 9 spree killed at least three people whose biographies were presented differently in public coverage:

- **Yiran Fan, 30:** UChicago financial-economics Ph.D. student; extensive university obituary, vigil, memorial fund, posthumous dissertation defense and Ph.D.; repeated descriptions of exceptional scholarship and future promise.
- **Aisha Johnson, 46:** doorwoman at a condominium building one block north of Fan's building; killed while at work. Coverage prominently records family grief and her role at the building.
- **Anthony Faulkner, 20:** killed later in the spree at a convenience store on South Halsted Street.

This comparison should be developed carefully rather than rhetorically. Collect equivalent-source coverage for each victim and code:

- number and length of news stories;
- institutional memorials and official statements;
- words such as *promising*, *exceptional*, *brilliant*, *future*, *potential*, *leader*, *talented*;
- occupational and educational details;
- description of parents/family;
- named endorsements from powerful people;
- memorial funds, scholarships, awards, buildings, or named programs;
- policy or security responses explicitly tied to the death;
- whether coverage describes what the victim **was expected to become** rather than only who the victim already was.

The hypothesis is not that grief for an elite student is insincere. The hypothesis is that institutions may be much better equipped to **see and narrate the lost future** of someone already incorporated into a prestige pipeline.
