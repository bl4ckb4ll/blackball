# November 9, 2021 — Shaoxiong "Dennis" Zheng and the security response

## Event

Shaoxiong "Dennis" Zheng, age 24, had completed a University of Chicago master's degree in statistics in 2021. On November 9, 2021, he was shot and killed during a robbery near his apartment building on East 54th Place at Ingleside Avenue in Hyde Park.

The University held a memorial service at Rockefeller Memorial Chapel and organized public safety discussions involving University leaders and the Chicago Police Department. University communications describe Zheng's murder as an event that badly shook the campus community and helped precipitate immediate changes to security around campus, Hyde Park, and surrounding neighborhoods.

## Why this belongs in the Hyde Park file

The event is important first because a person was killed. Blackball's narrower research interest is what happens when violence crosses the socially meaningful boundary around an elite institution.

Sudhir Venkatesh's 1989 opening scene already records a university population living inside a protected Hyde Park enclave beside poorer Black neighborhoods. In 2021 the University itself again described Hyde Park and surrounding areas through the language of safety, policing, and protection after Zheng's murder.

That permits several empirical questions:

- Which killings in and around Hyde Park trigger university-wide messages, memorials, meetings with city leadership, increased police deployment, transportation changes, or other expenditures?
- How does the institutional response differ when the victim is a student/alumnus, university employee, unaffiliated neighborhood resident, or low-wage worker?
- What geographic perimeter does the University treat as its responsibility after different kinds of incidents?
- Does security policy protect the university population while shifting costs or police contact onto surrounding residents?
- How are violence statistics framed when recruiting students and faculty versus when demanding additional policing or city resources?

These are not answered merely by recording Zheng's death. The event is a retrieval anchor for measuring institutional response.

## Relation to the January 2021 Yiran Fan killing

See [Yiran Fan and the language of "potential"](2021-01-09-yiran-fan-and-the-language-of-potential.md).

Both Fan and Zheng were University of Chicago affiliates killed in Hyde Park in 2021. The incidents were different: Fan was killed during a random shooting spree; Zheng was killed during a robbery. Both generated substantial University mourning and public-safety activity. Keeping both events together makes it possible to distinguish repeated institutional response from the special circumstances of one crime.
