# Roger L. Geiger

Blackball starts here.

Roger L. Geiger has done something unusually valuable: he made the institutional history of American higher education legible without reducing it to a parade of presidents, slogans, or present-day policy categories. Across the research-university books and the two-volume history of American higher education, he keeps asking what colleges and universities were actually for at a given moment, who sustained them, what kinds of knowledge they rewarded, what careers they fed, and how money and institutions changed the answers.

That is exactly the kind of work Blackball should honor: patient historical reconstruction that lets later readers see the shape of an institution instead of merely inheriting its self-description.

And Geiger's work is visibly collective. His acknowledgments, edited volumes, students, colleagues, reviewers, and enormous citation apparatus make that clear. See [GRATITUDE.md](GRATITUDE.md). The goal is eventually to thank **every identifiable person whose scholarship Geiger cites**, not just Geiger himself.

## Start here

1. **The History of American Higher Education: Learning and Culture from the Founding to World War II** (Princeton University Press, 2014/2015). The broadest pre-1945 synthesis. Geiger organizes the history around recurring purposes of higher education—knowledge, careers, and culture—and follows the American college from the colonial era through the emergence of research universities and mass higher education.
2. **American Higher Education since World War II: A History** (Princeton University Press, 2019). The continuation: GI Bill expansion, mass higher education, desegregation and coeducation, the upheavals of the 1960s and 1970s, research revival, selectivity, stratification, costs, and changing academic standards.
3. **To Advance Knowledge** (Oxford University Press, 1986). The rise of American research universities, 1900–1940.
4. **Research and Relevant Knowledge** (Oxford University Press, 1993). The wartime and postwar research university and the federal research relationship.
5. **Knowledge and Money** (Stanford University Press, 2004). Research universities under increasingly strong market pressures.
6. **Private Sectors in Higher Education** (University of Michigan Press, 1986). Comparative higher education across eight countries.
7. **Tapping the Riches of Science** (with Creso M. Sá, Harvard University Press, 2008). Universities, science policy, and economic development.

Geiger also edited a long run of *History of Higher Education Annual* / *Perspectives on the History of Higher Education* volumes. Those edited books matter here because they expose the network of historians doing the work, not just the name on the spine.
