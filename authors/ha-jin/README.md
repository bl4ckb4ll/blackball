# Ha Jin (Xuefei Jin)

Ha Jin belongs in Blackball as a literary source for questions about education,
work, migration, family obligation, institutional status, survival, artistic
vocation, and the gap between cultivated ambition and the work actually
available to a person.

This dossier is an index into evidence, not a claim that fiction is labor-market
data. A novel or story can establish what a literary work represents. It cannot
establish how common the represented experience is without independent
empirical evidence.

BU's English profile records four major short-story collections in the selected
bibliography: *Ocean of Words* (1996), *Under the Red Flag* (1997), *The
Bridegroom* (2000), and *A Good Fall* (2009). AGNI's 2026 author note likewise
describes four short-story collections and updates the later book record through
*The Woman Back from Moscow* (2023) and *Waiting for Tank Man* (2025).

## A Free Life / Nan Wu

The existing Blackball issue #84 correctly identified *A Free Life* as especially
relevant to the project. The novel puts education and intellectual ambition
beside immigrant work, restaurant ownership, family expenses, health insurance,
and the struggle to protect time for poetry.

The dedicated source dossier is at
[../../sources/ha-jin/a-free-life/README.md](../../sources/ha-jin/a-free-life/README.md).

Nan Wu is fictional. Keep three layers distinct:

1. what happens to Nan inside the novel;
2. what Ha Jin says about the relation between himself and Nan;
3. empirical claims about immigrants, graduate students, restaurant workers, or
   occupational outcomes.

Only the first two can be supported by the novel and author interviews alone.
