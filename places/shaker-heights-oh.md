# Shaker Heights, Ohio

## Why this place is here

Shaker Heights is useful because it combines substantial household resources, a nationally visible public-school system, longstanding racial integration, and unusually elaborate public-school programming. It is not well described by the stereotype of a homogeneous wealthy suburb.

## Public economic anchor

U.S. Census QuickFacts reports for 2020-2024:
- median household income: **$104,073**;
- per-capita income: **$72,806**;
- poverty: **8.4%**.

Source:
- https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/shakerheightscityohio/INC110222

Census Reporter gives the same current ACS frame:
- https://censusreporter.org/profiles/06000US3903571682-shaker-heights-city-cuyahoga-county-oh/

## Public-school infrastructure

The city and district emphasize that Shaker Heights offers the International Baccalaureate program across the PreK-12 system, alongside AP, career-technical programs, foreign exchange, arts, athletics, and other enrichment.

Sources:
- https://shakerheightsoh.gov/389/Schools
- https://www.shaker.org/the-shaker-experience
- https://shhs.shaker.org/high-school-experience

The district also explicitly discusses racial differences in school outcomes and its equity programs. That material belongs in the dossier because it prevents "high-resource suburb" from being mistaken for uniform outcomes.

## Questions to investigate

- College admissions visits and matriculation patterns at Shaker Heights High School.
- AP/IB participation by race and income, where public data permit.
- Residential segregation / integration history and how school boundaries interact with it.
- Property-tax base and per-pupil spending compared with Cleveland and nearby suburbs.
- Tutoring, test prep, music, athletics, summer programs, student travel, and other paid enrichment.
- Domestic and care labor markets and commuting patterns.
- Alumni and family networks in medicine, law, academia, business, philanthropy, and government.
- Which parts of Shaker's reputation reflect public-school infrastructure rather than private-school selection.

## Evidence boundary

Shaker Heights is particularly important as a counterexample to a crude equation of "affluent suburb" with racial or institutional homogeneity. The dossier should preserve both the area's resources and its internal inequalities.
