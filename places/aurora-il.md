# Aurora, Illinois

## Why this place is here

Aurora is useful mainly because it forces the geographic problem into the open. The city is too large and heterogeneous to treat "Aurora" as one affluent social world.

The likely object of interest may be a narrower **east-Aurora / Naperville / Indian Prairie District 204** professional-suburban ecology. That has to be demonstrated rather than assumed.

## Public economic anchor

Census Reporter, using ACS 2024 1-year data, reports approximately:
- population: **180,791**;
- median household income: **$94,784**;
- per-capita income: **$41,085**.

Source:
- https://censusreporter.org/profiles/16000US1703012-aurora-il/

U.S. Census QuickFacts gives a similar 2020-2024 median household income of $93,633 and poverty around 9.5%.
- https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/auroracityillinois/HSD410224

These figures by themselves do not resemble Greenwich, Armonk, Newton, or Cupertino. A narrower unit may be required.

## School-boundary problem

Aurora crosses multiple counties and school districts. Some Aurora addresses feed into Indian Prairie School District 204, which also serves parts of Naperville.

Waubonsie Valley High School publishes a school profile and sits in this broader Aurora/Naperville district ecology:
- https://wvhs.ipsd.org/site-map

Future work should map:
- district boundaries;
- household income by tract;
- which Aurora neighborhoods feed which high schools;
- college-counseling and enrichment differences across those schools.

## Private lead — not evidence

A private account from people associated with Aurora described high-pressure professional parents using combinations of stimulants and sedatives to sustain demanding work and family schedules.

This is **not evidence of prevalence, causation, or even the relevant geographic subgroup**.

Do not convert county prescribing data into "Aurora parents are on uppers and downers." At most, public prescribing / PDMP data could establish rates for a geography. It would still not establish:
- parent status;
- income class;
- simultaneous use;
- medical indication;
- motive such as corporate work, parenting, or youth sports.

The anecdote is therefore only a research prompt about **achievement pressure, working hours, stress, sleep, medication, and family scheduling**.

## Questions to investigate

- Which Aurora tracts / subdivisions actually resemble affluent Naperville?
- Which school districts and high schools serve those tracts?
- College admissions visits and college-counseling infrastructure by school.
- Youth sports, club teams, lessons, summer camps, tutoring, and test preparation.
- Parent working hours and commuting patterns where data exist.
- Public adolescent / parent stress surveys.
- County-level prescription data, with strict scope limits.
- Childcare and domestic-labor markets.

## Evidence boundary

Until the geography is resolved, "Aurora" should remain a **research lead, not an affluent-enclave label**.
