# Richmond, Virginia

## Why this place is here

Richmond is useful only if the geographic and institutional unit is made explicit. **Richmond city as a whole is not an affluent enclave.** The likely comparison object is a narrower West End / independent-school / professional-family ecology inside the larger metropolitan area.

## City-wide anchor

Census Reporter, using ACS 2024 1-year data, reports approximately:
- population: **233,655**;
- median household income: **$63,390**;
- per-capita income: **$46,268**.

Source:
- https://censusreporter.org/profiles/16000US5167000-richmond-va/

A 5-year profile gives poverty around 18% city-wide.
- https://censusreporter.org/profiles/05000US51760-richmond-city-va/

These figures make it especially important not to describe Richmond itself as equivalent to Greenwich or Cupertino.

## Public-school infrastructure

Richmond Public Schools has an extensive College & Career Pathways structure including counseling, gifted education, Future Centers, dual enrollment, and college preparation.
- https://www.rvaschools.net/college-and-career-pathways

The district also operates AP 4 All and emphasizes access to rigorous coursework.
- https://rvaschools.net/ap-4-all

This public-school system belongs in the dossier rather than treating private schools as synonymous with Richmond education.

## Independent-school lead

St. Christopher's School, an independent boys' school in Richmond, lists 2026-27 upper-school tuition of **$36,300** plus additional fees. It says 23% of the student body receives need-based financial aid.
- https://www.stchristophers.com/admissions/affording-stc

Future passes should add:
- St. Catherine's School;
- Collegiate School;
- other major Richmond independent schools;
- current tuition, aid, college counseling, admissions visits, alumni networks, and matriculation data from their own publications.

## Geographic work still needed

Likely subunits to test rather than assume:
- Windsor Farms;
- Westhampton;
- Near West End / Far West End;
- Henrico County affluent tracts;
- private-school catchment areas.

The dossier should map those areas using tract-level income, home values, school locations, commuting, and institutional networks.

## Questions to investigate

- Which neighborhoods actually supply the independent schools?
- How much do private-school tuition and ancillary activities cost relative to local income?
- Which colleges regularly visit Richmond independent and selective public schools?
- What country clubs, charity events, foundations, debutante / social traditions, and civic boards remain institutionally important?
- How do elite-school and professional networks intersect with the city's much broader Black working- and middle-class history?
- Domestic work, childcare, household staffing, and service-worker commuting.
- Summer programs, travel, sports, arts, tutoring, and college consulting.
- Differences between Richmond city, Henrico, Chesterfield, and specific neighborhoods.

## Evidence boundary

The point of a Richmond dossier is not to relabel a diverse city as rich. It is to make visible the smaller high-resource social networks that coexist inside and around a city whose aggregate statistics look very different.
