# Greenwich, Connecticut

## Why this place is here

Greenwich is a useful case because unusually high household resources coexist with both a large public-school system and a dense independent-school / college-preparatory ecology close to New York City.

Do not flatten Greenwich into "rich people." Town-wide measures, the central Greenwich CDP, public-school families, financial-aid families, private-school families, service workers, and individual neighborhoods are different populations.

## Geographic unit

Preferred default: **Town of Greenwich**, with school and neighborhood subunits called out explicitly.

Census Reporter currently gives the town a population of about 63,856 using ACS 2024 5-year data:
- https://censusreporter.org/profiles/06000US0919033620-greenwich-town-western-connecticut-planning-region-ct/

The smaller central Greenwich CDP should not be silently substituted for the town:
- https://censusreporter.org/profiles/16000US0933690-greenwich-ct/

## Public-school infrastructure

Greenwich Public Schools reports more than 8,000 students and a long list of school programs and awards:
- https://www.greenwichschools.org/

Greenwich High School's school profile explicitly describes the town as a suburban community near New York City with many college-graduate residents in professional and business careers. The high school offers more than 350 courses and 42 varsity teams.
- https://ghs.greenwichschools.org/academics/guidance/school-profile

This is institutional self-presentation, but it is directly useful for documenting the environment schools present to colleges.

## Independent-school pipeline

Greenwich Academy is a private girls' college-preparatory day school. Its 2026-27 upper-school tuition is **$60,820**. The school says about 23% of students receive financial aid and that its average aid award is $43,015.
- https://www.greenwichacademy.org/admission/tuition-affordability

Its college-counseling page publishes 2022-2026 matriculation totals, including substantial numbers attending highly selective universities. It also describes individual counseling beginning before senior year and parent meetings throughout the upper-school years.
- https://www.greenwichacademy.org/academics/college-counseling

Brunswick School, the nearby boys' school that coordinates upper-school courses with Greenwich Academy, lists 2026-27 upper-school tuition of **$61,400**, with 21% of students receiving aid.
- https://admissions.brunswickschool.org/apply/tuition-financial-aid/

## Questions to investigate

- Which selective colleges visit Greenwich High, Greenwich Academy, Brunswick, and other local schools each year?
- How many college-counseling staff are available per senior at each school?
- What is the local market for tutors, test preparation, sports coaching, music lessons, summer programs, college consultants, and application editing?
- What are the actual household-service and childcare markets: nanny agencies, wages, schedules, live-in/live-out arrangements, domestic-worker commuting patterns?
- Which country clubs, charity benefits, parent associations, galas, and philanthropic boards create recurring adult networks?
- How does the public-school pipeline differ internally by income, race, neighborhood, and course track?
- Where do lower-paid workers who serve Greenwich households and institutions live, and what does their commute look like?

## Private leads — not evidence

A private secondhand account concerning interactions with affluent Greenwich wives helped motivate this dossier. It is not sufficiently public, specific, or verifiable to support any claim about Greenwich residents.

Preserve only the research question: **what household labor, childcare, consumption, and status practices are publicly observable in Greenwich, and how common are they?**

## Counterevidence / anti-caricature

- Greenwich contains a large public-school population, not only private-school families.
- Greenwich Academy and Brunswick both publish substantial financial-aid programs.
- Town-wide or school-wide averages do not describe every household.

The dossier should preserve these facts alongside evidence of extraordinary wealth and institutional access.
