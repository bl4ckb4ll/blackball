# Automotive technician — training, hiring, and pay

This Blackball section keeps three different outcomes separate:

1. **Instruction:** whether students learn the stated technical skills and earn a certificate.
2. **Employment conversion:** whether ordinary entrants obtain relevant, paid automotive-technician jobs, in what time, at what rate, and through what hiring mechanism.
3. **Terms of work:** pay distribution, credited flat-rate versus clock hours, tools, costs, physical demands, attrition, and prospects after hiring.

An employer's claim that skilled jobs are unfilled does not prove that a particular college graduate can get one. A college's claim to prepare students for employment does not establish a job guarantee or a measured placement probability.

Do not infer that *no* graduates ever obtain technician work from an account that no job is offered or guaranteed. Conversely, selected successful hires are not proof of the employment probability for an entering student. Recover the denominator and compare the work-first alternative.
