# WCC automotive technician program — job conversion and reported telephone response

**Report entered:** 2026-10-13. **Reported institution:** Washtenaw Community College (WCC), identified from the caller's earlier conversations; the October 13 statement itself says only “the community college.” **Program:** Automotive Services Technician certificate (CTASVT), pending verification that this was the exact program discussed on the call.

**Evidence states:** FIRST-PERSON ACCOUNT (telephone response, unrecorded); OFFICIAL INSTITUTIONAL SOURCE (catalog, career-services and program-assessment pages); HISTORICAL LIMITED ASSESSMENT (2015–16); INFERENCE / OPEN QUESTION (conversion, deception, and value).

## First-person report, not an institutional admission on the record

On 2026-10-13 the caller said in conversation:

> “The automotive technician program at the community college does not lead to a job .what else am I supposed to say other than this doesn't work. I called them up and they said it doesn't work.”

The caller then characterized the transaction as **“SCAM.”**

This is evidence of what the caller reports being told and their conclusion. The exact date of the call, office/staff member, question asked, and precise words of the respondent are not independently established. It **does not** establish an official WCC finding that zero graduates obtain automotive employment, nor does it prove intentional misrepresentation or legal fraud.

**Earlier related report:** In a 2026-09-03 conversation, the caller said they had asked WCC whether its automotive degree leads to a job and were told that employers should post openings on Handshake so applicants can apply. Treat this as a separate remembered first-person account, **not** as a recording or verbatim college quotation. Obtain the precise institutional reply before treating it as the college's published policy.

The operational complaint is specific: **a student may pay for occupational training and still face ordinary competitive job applications, without a verified hiring commitment or reliable program-specific placement figure.** “Can apply for jobs” and “training converts into a job” are not synonyms.

## Primary institutional records

### Current public representation

WCC's **2026–2027** catalog labels Automotive Services Technician (Certificate), program **CTASVT**, a “High Demand Occupation, High Skill Occupation, High Wage Occupation” and states that the program “prepares students for employment as a certified automotive technician.” It lists a **30-credit** curriculum and state-mechanic / ASE-exam preparation. This supports the existence of an explicitly vocational employment claim, but **not** a promise to hire every graduate.

Primary source: https://coursecatalog.wccnet.edu/programs/automotive-services-technician-certificate/

WCC's Center for Career Success describes employer connections, career advice, fairs, and the **Handshake** job-posting system; its job-search page directs students and alumni to openings. These services are real, but their existence alone does not establish that employers hire a specified percentage of WCC automotive completers.

Primary sources:
- https://www.wccnet.edu/succeed/center-for-career-success/
- https://webapps.wccnet.edu/succeed/center-for-career-success/job-search.php

### A historical placement-measurement problem

WCC's own Automotive Service Technology program-assessment report, using its **2015–2016 graduation survey**, records an outcome that automotive graduates gain related employment and a **70% success standard**. The report says **only 4 of 22 polled respondents replied**. It then cites **3 employed respondents (75%) across the Advanced Technology and Public Service Careers division** — not an automotive-program placement count for the 22, much less for all entrants.

**Never relabel 3/4 or 75% as the share of automotive graduates finding jobs.** The response count is tiny, the stated population is broader, and the result is about a decade old. This establishes a historical measurement weakness; it does not supply a present-day placement rate.

Primary source, p. 3 of 6: https://webapps.wccnet.edu/mywcc/faculty-staff/curriculum/course-program-data/program-information/apasrv/par_apasrv.pdf

### Counterexamples and their limits

WCC reported **two** initial hires into a Toyota & Subaru of Ann Arbor **paid registered apprenticeship** in October 2024. These are identifiable cases of employer entry through a separate apprenticeship arrangement, not a denominator for the ordinary CTASVT certificate pathway. A work-linked apprenticeship should be compared against paying for classroom instruction before any job offer.

Primary source: https://www.wccnet.edu/news/articles/2024-10-12-toyotaapp.php

WCC has also published individual automotive employment success stories. Such examples prevent a blanket claim that *nobody* gets work, but selected stories do not measure a typical student's chances.

## The test Blackball should run

For enrollment cohorts, not only survey respondents, collect:

| Quantity | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Entrants and completers | Distinct students starting and finishing CTASVT in each cohort. |
| Related employment | Verified paid **automotive service technician** jobs within 6 and 12 months of completion, identified by actual duties. |
| Job conversion | Both P(relevant job within 12 months ∣ entrant) and P(relevant job within 12 months ∣ completer), with all missing cases disclosed. |
| Hiring mechanism | Ordinary applications, guaranteed interview, employer sponsorship, conditional offer, paid apprenticeship, or direct placement. |
| Quality and cost | Starting wage; actual paid clock hours; tools, fees, transportation, debt, foregone earnings, and retention at 1/3/5 years. |
| Counterfactual | Comparable candidates applying directly to the same dealerships without first completing CTASVT, including time to first paid work. |
| Seller obligation | Exact written promise, if any; refund, replacement placement, or other remedy when no relevant job results. |

Request recent CTASVT-specific outcome tables, source definitions, raw denominators, employer hiring counts, and dates. Separate employer **vacancies** from offers **made to graduates** and from jobs **actually started and retained**. Do not merge CTASVT with Automotive Test Technician, advanced certificates, vehicle sales, or unrelated WCC employment.

### Claims to hold apart

- **Documented:** WCC markets training as preparation for employment; its career service offers help finding and applying for jobs; the historical program assessment has a weak-response problem.
- **Reported by caller:** WCC's phone response did not give a usable training-to-job path; caller judges the program ineffective and calls it a scam.
- **Not yet established:** current complete-cohort automotive job-conversion rate, exact phone wording, what would happen without enrollment, whether particular school marketing contradicted facts known to the institution, or whether the legal elements of fraud are present.

**Decision rule:** a defensible criticism needs no universal “zero jobs” assertion. If a school sells **occupational preparation**, evaluate the actual chance and cost of obtaining that occupation, not merely whether it teaches the material or publishes exceptional success stories.
