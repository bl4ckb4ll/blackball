# Groton School recruiting page, the Groton name, and Winthrop context

**Status:** source-review note / contextual juxtaposition, not a causal claim  
**Checked:** 2026-09-02

## Why this is here

Groton School's current employment page is useful as a small institutional case: it combines concrete labor terms with unusually explicit moral language about character, leadership, service, close community, and teaching as a vocation. The school's location also has a documented naming chain back to Groton, Suffolk, and the Winthrop family.

That historical chain is worth preserving, but the claim boundary matters. Nothing inspected here establishes that the school was founded to honor John Winthrop, that its founders were invoking Winthrop's colonial theology, or that current school rhetoric descends causally from him. The useful fact is a traceable institutional/geographic genealogy that can be put beside present-day rhetoric without turning juxtaposition into proof of inheritance.

## Current Groton School employment page

Groton School describes itself as a coeducational, primarily residential school of 383 students. Its employment page says the institution is concerned with academic achievement, intellectual growth, ethical awareness, sportsmanship, athletic endeavor, service, leadership, and character. It describes faculty participation in students' lives as essential and invokes the founding vision that teachers make teaching **“their life's work.”**

The same page currently advertises, among other positions:

- a one-year salaried Charles C. Alexander Teaching Fellow Program for recent college graduates; fellows teach two classes under supervision, observe other classes, coach athletics for at least two and preferably three seasons, assist with dormitory supervision, and participate in other parts of boarding-school life; housing, meals, utilities other than telephone, and medical insurance are provided under the stated terms;
- a full-time Campus Safety Officer position at a starting rate of **$23/hour**, with a Friday-through-Tuesday schedule and full-time benefits;
- an on-call medical-chaperone role in which the worker drives students to off-campus medical and behavioral-health appointments and must supply a clean, reliable passenger vehicle and evidence of adequate automobile insurance;
- seasonal coaching positions, including a listed $4,000–$4,500 term range for an assistant coaching role on the page as checked.

The page also advertises the school through a mission of **“character, scholarship, leadership, and service.”** The point for Blackball is not that these labor terms disprove the mission or vice versa; it is that the recruiting document contains both the material employment conditions and the institution's moral self-description in one primary source.

Source:
- Groton School, “Working at Groton”: https://www.groton.org/about/working-at-groton

## The school and Gilded Age elite formation

Groton School's own history says Endicott Peabody founded the school in 1884. The same institutional history describes Peabody's original project as a school intended to instill high-minded principles in the **“offspring of the most successful American entrepreneurs of the Gilded Age.”** That statement is more directly relevant to an elite-formation investigation than trying to infer one from the school's later alumni list.

Source:
- Groton School, “History”: https://www.groton.org/about/history

## Groton, Massachusetts → Groton, Suffolk → Winthrop

The Town of Groton, Massachusetts says the settlement became the Plantation of Groton in 1655 and was named in honor of original selectman Dean (Deane) Winthrop, who had been born in Groton, Suffolk, England. A Groton municipal archaeological report adds the useful relationship explicitly: Deane Winthrop was a son of Governor John Winthrop.

Sources:
- Town of Groton, Massachusetts, “A Brief History of Groton, MA”: https://www.grotonma.gov/community/about/groton-history/
- Groton Historical Commission / Archaeological Services, *Community-Wide Archaeological Survey, Groton, MA* (2011): https://portal.grotonma.gov/storage/Historical_Commission/Archaeology/Archaeology_Report_2011.pdf

John Winthrop himself had a direct connection to Groton, Suffolk. Historic England's listing for Groton Hall says Winthrop held his first court there as a Justice of the Peace in 1609 and became Lord of the Manor in 1618; it also identifies him as the first Governor of Massachusetts and a founder of Boston. The older *Dictionary of National Biography* traces the family possession further back, saying Winthrop's grandfather Adam obtained the manor of Groton in 1544 and that John's father Adam later owned it.

Sources:
- Historic England, “Groton Hall, Groton”: https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1285612
- *Dictionary of National Biography*, “Winthrop, John (1588–1649)”: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_National_Biography,_1885-1900/Winthrop,_John_(1588-1649)

## Winthrop's 1634 smallpox statement

The relevant document is a letter from John Winthrop to Nathaniel Rich, written in Boston on **22 May 1634**. The Gilder Lehrman Institute catalogs the surviving two-page letter as GLC01105. In the letter Winthrop wrote:

> “For the natives, they are near all dead of the smallpox, so the Lord hath cleared our title to what we possess.”

This supports a fairly strong but still specific statement: Winthrop interpreted catastrophic Indigenous deaths from smallpox providentially and connected those deaths to English title to the land.

A looser paraphrase such as “God loves us and God hates them” should **not** be stored as the quotation. The document does not literally say that. The disturbing proposition it does state is already explicit enough: Native deaths by smallpox are treated as the Lord's clearing of English title.

## Claim ledger

### Supported

- Groton School was founded by Endicott Peabody in Groton, Massachusetts in 1884.
- The school's own history explicitly places its founding mission among children of successful Gilded Age entrepreneurs.
- Groton, Massachusetts says its name came through Deane Winthrop and Groton, Suffolk.
- Deane Winthrop was John Winthrop's son.
- John Winthrop had substantial legal/manorial ties to Groton, Suffolk before emigrating.
- In 1634 John Winthrop explicitly interpreted Native smallpox mortality as the Lord having cleared English title.
- Groton School's 2026 employment page juxtaposes concrete labor conditions with explicit language of character, leadership, service, close community, and vocational dedication.

### Not established by these sources

- that Groton School was named specifically for John Winthrop;
- that Endicott Peabody consciously invoked Winthrop when founding the school;
- that the school's current moral rhetoric is causally inherited from Winthrop's theology;
- that sharing a place-name makes later Groton institutions responsible for Winthrop's beliefs.
