# Higher education history

Blackball uses higher-education history to test what colleges and universities were built to do, whom they served, how they were financed, and when later claims about teaching, research, careers, access, or public service entered the institutional record.

## Provisional Geiger acquisition map

[Roger L. Geiger](roger-l-geiger/README.md) is presently a bibliography,
lawful-access map, and inspection queue for the national history of American
higher education. No book-length Geiger text has been inspected for this
repository, so Blackball does not currently treat him as its default synthesis
or as an authority for substantive claims.

That means:

- use the listed works as acquisition leads rather than evidence;
- after inspection, cite the exact Geiger work, edition, and page when making a claim;
- follow inspected notes backward to the institutional records and earlier scholarship they cite;
- preserve disagreement with Geiger rather than treating his synthesis as self-authenticating;
- use primary records and focused institutional histories to decide narrow factual questions.

## Proposed inspection order

1. *The History of American Higher Education: Learning and Culture from the Founding to World War II*.
2. *American Higher Education Since World War II: A History*.

Publication metadata presents the pair as a continuous national account from
the colonial colleges through the contemporary period. That description is an
acquisition rationale, not a finding from inspected text. A proposed second
inspection sequence is:

1. *To Advance Knowledge: The Growth of American Research Universities, 1900–1940*.
2. *Research and Relevant Knowledge: American Research Universities Since World War II*.
3. *Knowledge and Money: Research Universities and the Paradox of the Marketplace*.

## Counter-checks

Geiger should be read against narrower studies and rival syntheses when the question requires it. Recurring checks include Laurence Veysey on the emergence of the American university, Hugh Hawkins on the founding of Johns Hopkins, John R. Thelin's national history, institutional archives, charters, presidents' reports, catalogs, enrollment records, student publications, and contemporary criticism.

The point is not to manufacture a false balance. It is to separate:

- Geiger's interpretive framework;
- the evidence he cites;
- competing interpretations;
- facts that remain uncertain;
- remembered wording that has not yet been located.

## Rights and repository policy

Do not mirror copyrighted books merely because a scan can be found. Store bibliographic records, lawful access routes, page-specific notes, short quotations within fair-use limits, and citation graphs. Store full text only when its license or public-domain status permits redistribution.
