# John Womack Jr. — citation graph, generation 1

Parent node: John Womack Jr., *Zapata and the Mexican Revolution*.

## Verification rule

This file distinguishes three things that must not be collapsed:

- **VERIFIED WOMACK CITATION EDGE** — the source is visibly cited in Womack's own text/notes.
- **BIBLIOGRAPHY-PAGE EDGE PENDING** — the source may also occur in the bibliographical references at pp. 413–435, but those exact pages have not yet been directly enumerated in this pass.
- **TOPICAL ONLY** — relevant to Zapata/Morelos but not yet shown to be cited by Womack. Topical works do not enter the Womack source graph.

The Vintage/ACLS catalog record places Womack's bibliographical references at pp. **413–435**. The Internet Archive item currently exposed for the book is access-restricted and offers no suitable downloadable files, so this pass does **not** pretend to have exhaustively transcribed those pages. Instead, it promotes a first set of edges that can be checked directly in the book's web-visible notes.

## High-value next-generation parents

These now have enough verified degree or evidentiary value to spider next:

1. **Jesús Sotelo Inclán, *Raíz y razón de Zapata*** — highest visible repeat-citation density in this sample and a gateway to the ASI documents.
2. **Archivo de Jesús Sotelo Inclán** — document-level provenance should be preserved as `archive → document → Womack`, not reduced to `archive → Womack`.
3. **Gildardo Magaña, *Emiliano Zapata y el agrarismo en México*** — participant history with its own documentary and recollection trail.
4. **Antonio Díaz Soto y Gama** — participant/political source whose retrospective claims must remain distinguishable from contemporary documents.
5. **Morelos official periodicals** — suitable for direct date/event verification and cross-checking local political records.
6. **Carreño's *Archivo del General Porfirio Díaz*** — edited primary-source collection that can be followed to individual documents.
7. **Büttner's UNAM thesis** — quantitative/demographic branch separate from biographical narrative.

## Explicit non-edge rule

Alan Knight, Samuel Brunk, Robert Millon, Arturo Warman, Frank McLynn, and other important Zapata/Mexican Revolution works remain **TOPICAL ONLY relative to Womack** unless Womack's text, notes, bibliography, acknowledgements, or another inspectable provenance record establishes the edge. A later scholar citing both Womack and one of those authors is not evidence that Womack cited that author.
