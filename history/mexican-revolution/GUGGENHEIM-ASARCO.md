# Guggenheim / ASARCO in Porfirian Mexico

## Why this node exists

The Guggenheim family is a concrete way to make the otherwise abstract phrase **foreign ownership and control of Mexican mining** inspectable. Their Mexican interests became intertwined with the American Smelting and Refining Company (ASARCO), a major mining-and-metallurgical firm operating under the investment regime encouraged during the Porfiriato.

This node should not be read as "the Guggenheims caused the Mexican Revolution." It records one important part of the political economy against which agrarian, labor, nationalist, and anti-Porfirian grievances developed.

## Minimal chronology

- **1899:** American Smelting and Refining Company was organized as a consolidation of smelting and related properties, including Mexican mining interests.
- **1901:** ASARCO merged with M. Guggenheim's Sons. ASARCO's own corporate history lists Guggenheim-linked facilities including smelters at Chihuahua, San Luis Potosí, and Monterrey, and says the company acquired five additional mines in Mexico.
- **Porfiriato:** Mexican policy actively sought foreign capital for railways, mining, metallurgy, and other sectors. Scholarship on the Guggenheim/ASARCO expansion treats concessions, infrastructure, United States capital, and the Díaz regime as part of the same institutional setting.
- **1910–1912:** Early revolutionary fighting did not instantly destroy ASARCO's Mexican operations, although particular plants and transport networks were affected. This is a useful warning against treating the Revolution as a single abrupt economic break.

## What this establishes

The safe claim is that Guggenheim/ASARCO was a **large, identifiable foreign-capital mining and smelting concern in Mexico** and therefore belongs in any source trail about foreign ownership and the Porfirian economic order.

The stronger claims still require quantitative work:

- What fraction of Mexican mines, mineral output, smelting capacity, or mining capital was foreign-owned in a given year?
- What fraction was controlled specifically by United States firms?
- What fraction belonged to Guggenheim/ASARCO rather than other firms such as Greene/Cananea or Batopilas interests?
- How did ownership differ by copper, silver, lead, zinc, and region?

Do not replace those questions with "the Guggenheims owned most Mexican mines" unless a source actually establishes it.

## Sources

### Corporate / primary-company chronology

- ASARCO, [History](https://www.asarco.com/history/). Useful for the 1899 organization, 1901 merger with M. Guggenheim's Sons, listed Mexican smelters, and acquisition of additional Mexican mines. Because this is company-authored history, use it for chronology and corporate facts, not as the sole interpretation of Porfirian political economy.

### Mexican scholarly and public-history material

- Instituto Nacional de Estudios Históricos de las Revoluciones de México (INEHRM), material on *La expansión de los negocios de los Guggenheim en México. La American Smelting and Refining Co., 1890–1923*. The description situates Guggenheim/ASARCO among the United States mining and metallurgical firms that expanded during the Díaz regime and emphasizes both modernization and dependence on foreign markets.
- María de los Ángeles Cortés Basurto, UNAM / FES Acatlán, ["Compañía ASARCO"](https://blogs.acatlan.unam.mx/clioeconomia/2025/07/04/compania-asarco/). A concise Mexican economic-history overview of the Guggenheims, smelting, concessions, and Porfirian policy toward foreign firms.
- [El Colegio de México repository — study of Guggenheim/ASARCO expansion in Mexico](https://repositorio.colmex.mx/downloads/n583xx26m?locale=en). Of particular interest is the section on expansion and consolidation, 1905–1912, and the firm's experience during the opening phase of the Revolution.
- UNAM thesis, [*La inversión minera norteamericana ...*](https://tesiunamdocumentos.dgb.unam.mx/pmig2019/0024478/0024478.pdf). The chapter structure explicitly treats United States mining investment during the Porfiriato, the Guggenheim group and ASARCO under Díaz, and ASARCO during the Revolution.
