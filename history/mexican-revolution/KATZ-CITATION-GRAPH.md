# Friedrich Katz — archival and citation graph, generation 1

Parent node: Friedrich Katz, *The Life and Times of Pancho Villa* (Stanford University Press, 1998).

## Edge vocabulary

Katz requires a stricter distinction than an ordinary reading list:

- **VERIFIED PRINTED-BOOK CITATION EDGE** — directly visible in Katz's published notes, “Archival Sources” section, or bibliography.
- **VERIFIED KATZ RESEARCH-PROVENANCE EDGE** — Katz's papers/finding aid establishes that the material was in the research archive or notes used for the Villa book, but the exact printed citation has not yet been checked.
- **TOPICAL ONLY** — relevant scholarship or primary material with no established Katz provenance edge.

Do not silently promote a research-provenance edge into a printed-book citation edge.

It also states that, **in some cases**, the appendix and bibliography of *The Life and Times of Pancho Villa* were used by the archivists to determine the repository from which Katz's document copies came. Because it does not identify which cases, this validates the research-provenance graph but does not by itself prove every repository below appears on p. 911.

The finding aid separately identifies four folders as **“Villa book references and footnote sources”** (Box 31, folders 1–4), a folder of **“Villa book — bibliographic citations and notes”** (Box 40, folder 15), and describes the Villa research-note subseries as mostly archival notes named for their sources.

# VERIFIED KATZ RESEARCH-PROVENANCE EDGES

The following are not topical guesses. They are named source repositories/collections in Katz's preserved research files. Their exact p. 911 printed status remains to be checked.

## Mexico — national, state, local, and institutional archives

- **Archivo General de la Nación (AGN)** — numerous series/fondos, including Comisión Nacional Agraria, Obregón–Calles, Revolución/asuntos internos, Emiliano Zapata, and Villa correspondence/telegrams.
- **Archivo Histórico del Estado de Durango** — extensive letters to governors and related Durango material.
- **Archivo de San Juan del Río** — grouped with Durango holdings.
- **Archivo Histórico de Petróleos Mexicanos (PEMEX)** — 1911–1922 material.
- **Archivo de Namiquipa** — 1906–1939 material supplied with notes from Daniel Nugent and Ana Alonso.
- **Archivo de la Secretaría de la Defensa Nacional** — 1912–1920 and later military material.
- **Archivo de la Secretaría de la Reforma Agraria** — extensive Chihuahua, Coahuila, Durango, Guadalajara/Hacienda de Guadalupe, and Tamaulipas files.
- **Archivo de la Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores** — diplomatic reports, letters, embassy documents, and compilations.
- **Archivo del Supremo Tribunal de Justicia del Estado de Chihuahua** — including material on Villa's heirs and widows.
- **Centro de Estudios de Historia de México (CONDUMEX)** — Archivo Carranza; Archivo Federico González Garza; Archivo Rubio Navarrete; railway and Villa-related material.
- **Fideicomiso Archivos Plutarco Elías Calles y Fernando Torreblanca** — material on Obregón, Villa, Joaquín Amaro, and confidential reports.
- **Instituto Nacional de Estudios Históricos de las Revoluciones de México (INEHRM)** — letters, documents, clippings, and retrospective material on revolutionary leaders.
- **Universidad Iberoamericana — Porfirio Díaz papers** — extensive correspondence and reports from the Porfirian and revolutionary transition.
- **UNAM Archivo Histórico** — Amado Aguirre, Manuel Barragán, Gildardo Magaña, Jacinto B. Treviño, and Francisco Urquizo papers plus hemerographic material.
- **Archivo Municipal de Chihuahua** and other Chihuahua municipal/local material — including documents for 1911–1917 and related Guerrero/Casas Grandes files preserved in the subject-organized research series.

## United States repositories and collections

- **Arizona Historical Society** — U.S. Marshal's Office and Cananea Consolidated Copper Company material.
- **Harvard University — John Reed papers** — circa-1913 material copied for Katz.
- **Harvard/Baker Library — Lamont papers**.
- **Huntington Library — Albert B. Fall papers and Fall Committee material**.
- **Library of Congress** — correspondence and telegrams on Villa, the border, U.S. intervention, and named diplomatic/political papers.
- **Pomona College Library — José María Maytorena papers** — correspondence with Felipe Ángeles, Villa, Madero, and others.
- **United States National Archives** — Department of Justice, Department of State, War Department, Military Intelligence, and other records; the preserved files include extensive Villa-specific correspondence, reports, microfilm guides, and records on the political/military situation in Mexico.
- **University of Arizona — Alberto R. Piña collection of Mexican documents**.
- **UC Berkeley, Bancroft Library** — Emil Holmdahl papers, Silvestre Terrazas papers, Carranza correspondence, and related Villa/Chihuahua material.
- **University of New Mexico — Albert B. Fall papers**.
- **University of Texas at Austin** — Buckley papers, Lázaro de la Garza papers, Mexican Northwestern Railway material, oral histories, and Corralitos Company material.
- **University of Texas at El Paso** — McNeely/Benton/Parral collections, oral histories, Villa manifesto, and Mexican Northwestern Railway records.
- **Yale University** — Polk papers and related correspondence.

## European and international repositories

- **British Foreign Office** — Mexico material with Katz's research notes.
- **British Science Museum — Pearson/Lord Cowdray papers** — including oil-company correspondence.
- **British Public Record Office** — dispatches and correspondence on Villa, British consular reporting, oil companies, German activity, Villa's surrender, and his assassination.
- **Austrian archival sources on Mexico**.
- **French archives** — including the French Foreign Ministry and Nantes archive.
- **German archives** — foreign-ministry and other German records.
- **Spanish archives** — embassy/foreign-office and other diplomatic material.

## Personal, participant, and private collections

- **Roque González Garza papers**.
- **Martín Luis Guzmán papers** — questionnaires, clippings, memoirs, reports, and Villa-related material; an oversize source entry also describes copies of Villa penciled memoirs.
- **Rubén Osorio papers** — Guerrero/Cuchillo Parado material, Villa correspondence, oral histories, and Chihuahua sources.
- **Pizaña, Aniceto papers** — remembrances and correspondence.
- **Private and family archives** — assorted Villa documents and correspondence retained as separately identified provenance rather than merged into anonymous “primary sources.”
- **Hugo Scherer correspondence** — the finding aid explicitly labels 1911 photocopies as **“for Villa book,”** although the original repository is recorded as unknown.

# Printed bibliography: no speculative promotion

The pages at 919–954 are the correct mechanical next target, but their individual rows were not directly exposed by the reliable public preview used in this pass. Therefore **no secondary work is being promoted merely because it is standard Villa scholarship or because Katz knew the author**.

In particular, works by Alan Knight, Mark Wasserman, John Reed, Martín Luis Guzmán, Francisco R. Almada, Ivar Thord-Gray, Michael C. Meyer, and others may be highly plausible bibliography entries. They remain **TOPICAL ONLY / PRINTED EDGE PENDING** until the published bibliography or notes are directly inspected. Research files bearing an author's name can establish a Katz research-provenance edge, but that is still not identical to a printed bibliography edge.
