# Reviel Netz

This directory is an initial source map for Reviel Netz's work on ancient mathematics, Archimedes, diagrams and deductive practice, literary culture, and the history of technologies of spatial control.

## Why Netz belongs here

Netz is a historian of ancient mathematics whose work treats mathematical reasoning as a concrete historical practice: diagrams, lettered figures, formulaic language, manuscript transmission, notation, and the social/literary setting in which mathematical texts were produced and read. His Archimedes work also connects philology, manuscript conservation, imaging, textual criticism, and the reconstruction of mathematical arguments.

Stanford's current profile describes his main field as the history of pre-modern mathematics and emphasizes cognitive practices including visual culture, the history of the book, literacy, and numeracy. It lists his major Cambridge books through *Why the Ancient Greeks Matter* (2025).

## Core reading map

### Greek mathematical practice and deduction

- Reviel Netz, *The Shaping of Deduction in Greek Mathematics: A Study in Cognitive History* (Cambridge University Press, 1999). Start here for the lettered diagram, the pragmatics of letters, mathematical lexicon and formulae, and the historical formation of deductive practice.
- Reviel Netz, *The Transformation of Mathematics in the Early Mediterranean World: From Problems to Equations* (Cambridge University Press, 2007). Follow-on study of changes in mathematical form and problem practice across the early Mediterranean.
- Reviel Netz, *Ludic Proof: Greek Mathematics and the Alexandrian Aesthetic* (Cambridge University Press, 2009). Connects Hellenistic mathematical proof with Alexandrian literary and aesthetic practices.
- Reviel Netz, *A New History of Greek Mathematics* (Cambridge University Press, 2022). Broad synthesis useful as the current general entry point to his account of Greek mathematics.
- Reviel Netz, *Why the Ancient Greeks Matter: The Problematic Miracle that was Greece* (Cambridge University Press, 2025). Later synthetic work placing Greek intellectual production in a wider historical argument.

### Archimedes

- Reviel Netz, *The Works of Archimedes, Volume 1: The Two Books On the Sphere and the Cylinder: Translation and Commentary* (Cambridge University Press, 2004).
- Reviel Netz, *The Works of Archimedes, Volume 2: On Spirals: Translation and Commentary* (Cambridge University Press, 2017).
- Reviel Netz and William Noel, *The Archimedes Codex* (Weidenfeld & Nicolson, 2007; later U.S. editions). Accessible account of the Palimpsest project and what its recovery changes about our evidence for Archimedes.
- Reviel Netz, William Noel, Nigel Wilson, and Natalie Tchernetska, eds., *The Archimedes Palimpsest*, 2 vols. (Cambridge University Press, 2011). The scholarly project: catalogue/commentary plus facsimile/transcription.
- Reviel Netz, Ken Saito, and Natalie Tchernetska, “A New Reading of Method Proposition 14: Preliminary Evidence from the Archimedes Palimpsest,” parts 1 and 2, *SCIAMVS* 2 (2001) and 3 (2002).
- Reviel Netz, Fabio Acerbi, and Nigel Wilson, “Towards a Reconstruction of Archimedes’ Stomachion,” *SCIAMVS* 5 (2004), 67–99.

### Mathematical diagrams, cognition, and the book

The central Blackball reason to keep Netz together rather than scatter these titles into a generic history-of-mathematics list is methodological. In *Shaping of Deduction*, the diagram is not decoration added after a proof. The interaction among a lettered figure, repeated linguistic formulae, and inferential structure is part of the historical technology by which Greek deduction works. This is directly relevant to any study of mathematical notation, diagrammatic reasoning, proof presentation, and the material form of technical texts.

### Literary culture

- Reviel Netz, *Scale, Space and Canon in Ancient Literary Culture* (Cambridge University Press, 2020). Extends the concern with textual scale, form, and material/intellectual organization beyond mathematical texts.

### Technology, animals, coercion, and space

- Reviel Netz, *Barbed Wire: An Ecology of Modernity* (Wesleyan University Press, 2004; later paperback editions). A history organized around the restriction of movement by barbed wire, moving from cattle and settler colonization through twentieth-century systems for controlling people. Keep this linked both to history of technology and to histories of coercion, enclosure, animals, colonial settlement, camps, and spatial control.
