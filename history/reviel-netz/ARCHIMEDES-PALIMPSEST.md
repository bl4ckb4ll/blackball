## Keep the evidence layers separate

The Archimedes Palimpsest is unusually easy to turn into a simplified “lost genius rediscovered” story. Netz himself warns against exaggerating what was unknown before Johan Ludvig Heiberg examined the manuscript in 1906 and what became newly readable after the modern project began in 1998.

For Blackball, treat the Palimpsest as a stack of distinct evidence layers:

1. **Archimedes' ancient works** — the mathematical arguments themselves.
2. **Byzantine manuscript transmission** — surviving copies, lost exemplars, copying, diagrams, and the tenth-century Archimedes codex later reused.
3. **The medieval palimpsest** — parchment erased/reused for a prayer book.
4. **Heiberg's 1906 examination** — an earlier modern recovery of material from the manuscript.
5. **The post-1998 conservation and imaging project** — physical conservation, multispectral and other imaging, and computational image processing.
6. **Transcription and critical editing** — determining what the damaged manuscript actually says.
7. **Historical/mathematical interpretation** — what the readings imply about Archimedes' methods, infinity, combinatorics, diagrams, and mathematical style.

A claim established at one layer should not automatically be promoted to another.

## Core Netz-related sources

### Scholarly edition

Reviel Netz, William Noel, Natalie Tchernetska, and Nigel Wilson, eds., *The Archimedes Palimpsest*, 2 vols. (Cambridge University Press, 2011).

Stanford describes Volume 1 as covering the techniques, discoveries, and codicological evidence, including conservation, imaging/post-processing, and the component manuscripts. Volume 2 provides texts including the Greek *Floating Bodies* and unique copies of *Method* and *Stomachion*, along with newly recovered Hyperides material and a commentary on Aristotle's *Categories*.

### *Method*, Proposition 14

- Reviel Netz, Ken Saito, Natalie Tchernetska, “A New Reading of Method Proposition 14: Preliminary Evidence from the Archimedes Palimpsest (Part 1),” *SCIAMVS* 2 (2001), 9–29.
- Reviel Netz, Ken Saito, Natalie Tchernetska, “A New Reading of Method Proposition 14: Preliminary Evidence from the Archimedes Palimpsest (Part 2),” *SCIAMVS* 3 (2002), 109–125.

Stanford summarizes these readings as evidence relevant to “actual infinity” in Archimedes. The papers themselves, not the profile summary, should govern any stronger formulation when they are inspected in full.

### *Stomachion*

Reviel Netz, Fabio Acerbi, Nigel Wilson, “Towards a Reconstruction of Archimedes’ Stomachion,” *SCIAMVS* 5 (2004), 67–99.

Stanford describes the Palimpsest evidence as possibly showing combinatorics in Archimedes. Preserve the “possibly” unless the source being summarized makes a stronger claim.

### Archimedes translations/commentaries

- *The Works of Archimedes*, Vol. 1: *The Two Books On the Sphere and the Cylinder* (2004).
- *The Works of Archimedes*, Vol. 2: *On Spirals* (2017), DOI https://doi.org/10.1017/9781139019279.

Cambridge states that Volume 2 uses reconsideration of the Greek text and diagrams made possible by discoveries from the Palimpsest and emphasizes Archimedes' scientific style as well as results.

### Accessible history

Reviel Netz and William Noel, *The Archimedes Codex* (2007).

Use this as an accessible narrative and guide into the project, but do not substitute it for the scholarly edition or papers when recording exact readings of damaged text.

## Why this belongs in Blackball beyond “history of mathematics”

The project makes several histories touch the same object:

- history of mathematics;
- history of the book;
- manuscript studies and codicology;
- textual criticism;
- conservation science;
- imaging and image processing;
- the history of diagrams;
- the sociology and financing of scholarly projects;
- museum/library custody and the modern antiquities market.

That makes the Palimpsest a good test case for source provenance. A digital image is evidence about a physical leaf; a transcription is an interpretation of that image; an edited Greek text is a further scholarly construction; a historical claim about Archimedes is another layer again.
