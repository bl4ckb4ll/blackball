# Apprenticeship retrieval queue

These are sources to retrieve and inspect directly. They are included because they bear on apprenticeship, artisan mobility, training, exclusion, or the transition from master/journeyman craft organization to wage employment.

Do **not** treat the fact that a later polemical or tertiary work cited one of these as evidence for the later work's interpretation. The underlying source must stand or fall on its own.

## Priority A — apprenticeship itself

### William J. Rorabaugh — *The Craft Apprentice: From Franklin to the Machine Age in America*

- Oxford University Press, 1986.
- Repeatedly cited in the modern literature on U.S. apprenticeship.
- Retrieve for: craft apprenticeship's social bargain, master-apprentice relations, decline/transformation, mobility toward journeyman/master status, industrialization.
- Compare directly with Jacoby 1991 and Hamilton 2000 rather than using any single decline narrative.

### Paul H. Douglas — *American Apprenticeship and Industrial Education*

- Columbia University, 1921; later reprints exist.
- Retrieve for: early-twentieth-century diagnosis of apprenticeship and industrial education.
- Historical source as well as analysis; record edition used.

### Massachusetts Bureau of Statistics of Labor — “The Apprenticeship System”

- *Thirty-Seventh Annual Report*, 1906–1907.
- Retrieve for: contemporary state investigation and evidence used by later apprenticeship historians.

### U.S. Commission on Industrial Relations — *Final Report and Testimony*

- 1910s.
- Retrieve apprenticeship-related testimony/sections rather than treating the enormous commission corpus as one source.

### Magnus Alexander — “Apprenticeship in the Metal Trades”

- Later reprinted in John R. Commons, ed., *Trade Unionism and Labor Problems*.
- Retrieve for: metal-trades apprenticeship, employer organization, industrial training.

### O. M. Becker — “Modern Adaptations of the Apprenticeship System”

- *Engineering Magazine* 32 (December 1906): 321–338.
- Retrieve for: employer/engineering-era attempts to redesign apprenticeship under industrial production.

## Priority B — artisan mobility and the promise of becoming independent

### Stuart M. Blumin — “Mobility and Change in Ante-Bellum Philadelphia”

- In Stephan Thernstrom and Richard Sennett, eds., *Nineteenth-Century Cities: Essays in the New Urban History*.
- Inspect especially the cited discussion around pp. 198–200 in the edition located.
- Verification target: master/journeyman ratios and whether movement from apprenticeship/journeyman status to independent master actually remained common in Philadelphia.
- Do not preserve the numerical claim until the exact table/source chain is inspected.

### Edward Pessen — *Jacksonian America: Society, Personality, and Politics*

- Multiple editions/reprints exist; record exact edition and pages.
- Retrieve for: artisans, wage employment, class structure, mobility, and the transition away from independent craft production.

### Roger W. Shugg — *Origins of Class Struggle in Louisiana*

- Locate exact original publication and the later Baton Rouge edition/reprint before citing.
- Retrieve for: movement from small property holding toward sharper class division in the lower South and urban/industrial context.

## Priority C — race, access to trades, and apprenticeship exclusion

### Leon F. Litwack — *North of Slavery: The Negro in the Free States, 1790–1860*

- University of Chicago Press, 1961.
- Retrieve for: exclusion of Black workers from skilled occupations and apprenticeship; Frederick Douglass's difficulty using his caulking skill in northern shipyards; racial barriers to training and trade entry.
- Follow Litwack's primary citations for each occupational claim.

### Philip S. Foner — *History of the Labor Movement in the United States*, vol. 1

- Retrieve the cited early-labor and Jacksonian-era sections, especially material around pp. 142–150 and 183–188 in the edition located.
- Use as an interpretive labor-history source and citation map, not as a substitute for newspapers, union records, laws, or court records.

### Frederick Douglass — autobiographies, speeches, and occupational testimony

- Retrieve the primary passages about caulking, shipyard exclusion, common labor, and skilled-trade barriers.
- Prefer Douglass's own dated texts over later paraphrases.

## Priority D — contract enforcement and apprentice complaints

### Court petitions by apprentices

Build a primary-source sample of petitions/complaints involving:

- failure to teach the promised trade;
- excessive household/menial/unrelated labor;
- violence or corporal punishment;
- inadequate food/clothing/lodging;
- withheld wages, tools, clothing, or freedom dues;
- contested absence/runaway allegations;
- extensions of term;
- discharge before completion;
- transfer to another master;
- requests for judicial release.

For each case preserve **what the apprentice alleged, what the master alleged, what evidence survives, and what the court actually ordered**. Complaint records are evidence of disputes, not automatically proof that every allegation was true.

## Priority E — engineering/trades comparison

Find historical sources that let Blackball distinguish:

- apprenticeship as actual skill formation;
- apprenticeship as helper/cheap-labor status;
- engineering-school training;
- shop-floor promotion and workplace learning;
- employers hiring already-trained workers instead of training entrants;
- union restrictions on apprentice numbers;
- employer restrictions or discrimination in access;
- technological/organizational changes that reduce, change, or relocate craft skill.

The goal is not to crown either engineering school or apprenticeship. It is to make both answer the same empirical questions.
