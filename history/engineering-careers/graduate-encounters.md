# How often would these encounters occur?

The passenger reported that his Uber driver had a master’s in electrical engineering. He also reported civil engineering graduates picking orders at Amazon. Each first-person observation supplies information about a person’s work that an employed/unemployed table would miss. The older accounts repeat the same driver. Count that driver once. A statement such as “96% of respondents were employed” could hold even if several worked outside the field. First establish whether the institution’s claim concerns any job, work closely related to the degree, professional-level work, or permanent placements and its date. A driver’s principal occupation, other jobs and reason for driving require their own evidence. A verified counterexample would refute a guarantee covering that person. Several encounters across workplaces invite an inquiry into prevalence.

## A numerical example

Suppose a hypothetical institution asserted that 96% of its graduate cohort had principal jobs in the field. Set the out-of-field fraction in that cohort to 4%. In a setting where such graduates are $w$ times as likely to be encountered as their in-field classmates, the selected fraction is $0.04w/(0.96+0.04w)$. If the observer learns a mismatch on a fraction $d$ of those encounters, the probability per distinct sampled graduate is $p=d(0.04w)/(0.96+0.04w)$. For $n$ independent exposures the chance of at least two observed mismatches is $1-(1-p)^n-np(1-p)^{n-1}$.

Ten rides with five returning drivers give five distinct drivers. A warehouse can put co-workers in the same sample through the same hiring channels. The table varies the number of distinct independent exposures and gives no confidence interval for the reported encounters. If contemporaneous logs become available, keep distinct people, which settings recur, which degrees were learned, and the time window. Under an explicit institutional claim, a sensitivity calculation can model unequal exposure. Cluster resampling can retain dependence among coworkers under a documented sampling design.
