# Electrical engineering careers

## Who gets to design a Wi-Fi chip?

Morse Micro brought Neil Weste onto its team in 2016. He had coauthored *CMOS VLSI Design* and had led the design of Radiata’s Wi-Fi modem chip. At Morse Micro he designed the core physical-layer engine for Wi-Fi HaLow. The founders’ account describes meeting an engineer who had already delivered closely related work. What reading his book did for a student’s hiring prospects remains unmeasured. [Weste’s career and recruitment sources](../../people/neil-weste.md#who-got-the-work)

A graduate at the same company described a different sequence of assignments. Alex Hiller joined the graduate program in March 2021, tested Wi-Fi silicon on the bench, and helped scale production testing. In 2023 an op-amp he designed was fabricated on one of the company’s chips. In 2024 he moved into full-time design. His account identifies who first gave him time on the hardware and when he began designing circuits. [Hiller’s interview in the Cambridge University Engineering Society magazine](https://magazine.cues.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Magazine_25-26.pdf)

Morse Micro’s first paid graduate intake had more than 50 applicants, 12 interviews and four selections. Those figures belong to the January 2020 intake. Hiller entered the following year. Measuring how many entrants eventually reached design work would require following all members of a given intake through later assignments. [Morse Micro’s first-intake report](https://medium.com/@MorseMicro/reach-farther-in-your-career-c2f0521482d8)
