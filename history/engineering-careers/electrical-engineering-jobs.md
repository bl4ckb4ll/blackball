# What openings could a new electrical engineering graduate actually get?

## U.S. awards and occupational openings

IPEDS recorded **14,446 bachelor’s and 8,330 master’s first-major awards** in Electrical and Electronics Engineering in 2023–24 in the 50 states and DC. Bachelor’s awards rose from 11,455 in 2009–10. The 2023–24 computer engineering general series adds 9,207 bachelor’s and 3,566 master’s awards under its own code. [Annual counts, school tables and release hashes](electrical-engineering-degree-counts/README.md)

BLS projects about **16,300 annual openings** in electrical and electronics engineering over 2025–35. That includes 11,400 electrical engineer openings (SOC 17-2071) and 4,900 electronics engineer openings excluding computer engineers (SOC 17-2072). It includes replacement openings from occupational exits and transfers, plus growth. The government identifies a bachelor’s as the typical entry education. It does not count how many openings employers open to recent graduates or how many people employers actually take. [BLS occupational projections and worker characteristics](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) · [Separation and openings method](https://www.bls.gov/emp/documentation/separations.htm)

The national figures answer the capacity question only at the scale of the occupation. 16,300 is a projected count across experience levels, against 22,776 first-major awards across two degree levels in one earlier year. If entrants want jobs with those occupational titles, the available places must be shared with experienced applicants and relevant graduates of other majors. Some EE graduates enter other paid work that uses their engineering training. The data on this page cannot say how many people will be competing for each opening, how many can reach it where they live, or whether all new graduates could find work they trained for.

## Michigan

Michigan’s 2023–24 cohort-size baseline was **759 bachelor’s and 119 master’s first-major awards** in CIP 14.1001. Michigan projects 605 annual openings in 2024–34 in the electrical engineer occupation. Michigan also reported 550 online electrical engineer advertisements in April 2025, including carryovers. It said 90.7% of the ads with a stated education minimum required a bachelor’s degree. The state data give a projected average and a one-month advertisement count. Neither provides the number of funded seats accepting new graduates in the award year. [IPEDS school file](electrical-engineering-degree-counts/2024.md) · [Michigan occupational projection](https://www.michigan.gov/mcda/-/media/Project/Websites/mcda/reports/2026/Michigan-Hot-50-Job-Outlook-through-2034.pdf) · [Michigan April 2025 advertisement report](https://www.michigan.gov/mcda/labor-market-information/michigans-labor-market-news/2025/06/05/hwol-june-2025)

### A same-period campus comparison

Michigan State’s engineering destination report lists 87 electrical engineering undergraduate graduates for fall 2023 through summer 2024. IPEDS lists 105 first-major EE bachelor’s awards at UNITID 171100 for July 2023 through June 2024. The source windows end in different periods. A student-to-award crosswalk would be needed to reconcile the figures.

Michigan State’s detailed table covers 1,048 engineering graduates across majors and gives 863 known outcomes, 670 working and 116 in continuing education. For the whole engineering cohort, 670/1,048 establishes a 63.9% floor for working, rising to 81.6% if all 185 unreported graduates were working. The school’s 790/863 headline includes more categories than work alone. Its poster shows 77.8% engaged in employment. The detailed table reports 77.6% in the working category and lists volunteering separately. Neither source allocates the working count to those 87 EE graduates. ## What would decide whether there are enough jobs?

For a defined year and region, count funded places that accepted a new graduate’s experience profile and record actual hires when available. Match the same period with each local program’s graduating and seeking cohorts, including where graduates sought work. Check adjacent job functions and competing degrees, commuters, people moving into or out of the region, people going directly to further study, employer training and conversion to permanent work. With partially observed destinations, divide known target hires by the total cohort for the floor and add the count of missing destinations for the ceiling. A college-wide headline cannot supply an EE denominator.
