# Empire podcast — source discipline

## Evidence levels

This pass uses three levels and should not blur them.

### 1. Catalogue-level episode coverage

For the broad run through the back catalogue, the evidence is the published episode title, date, guest metadata, and description in public podcast indexes and the show's own site/feed.

A catalogue-level summary says **what the episode presents itself as covering**. It is not a claim that every minute of the audio was listened to or that every historical assertion in the audio was independently checked.

### 2. Episode-level checking

For selected Blackball-relevant runs, the notes use more detailed published descriptions and cross-reference named guest books. These notes can identify arguments and mechanisms worth following up, but they still should not be treated as transcripts unless a transcript is explicitly cited.

### 3. Book/source dossiers

People dossiers list books that were named in the episode description or independently verified from an author/publisher/university page. A list headed "selected books" is deliberately not represented as a complete bibliography.

## Dynamic-feed warning

Podcast platforms report different episode totals because some count trailers, bonuses, club/member releases, revised entries, or subscriber feeds differently. Around mid-2026, public indexes also disagree by one on some episode numbers. Therefore:

- prefer **title + date** to number alone;
- preserve the number shown by the source used;
- do not infer a missing episode from arithmetic;
- do not convert a subscriber-early-access date into a public-release date.

## Contested subjects

Many series cover active or highly contested political and historiographical fields: Israel/Palestine, Iran, colonial violence, famine, slavery, nationalism, terrorism, racial rule, reparations, and the legacy of empire.

For these, Blackball should preserve:

1. the episode's framing;
2. the guest's actual published argument;
3. the underlying primary evidence where obtainable;
4. major competing interpretations;
5. empirical quantities that could discriminate among accounts.

Do not turn a podcast host's rhetorical confidence into evidentiary confidence.
