# Empire episodes 383–388 — Celts, Rome, Picts, Saint Patrick and Vikings

## Run outline

The six-part sequence uses archaeology, textual evidence and later myth-making to ask what can actually be said about pre-/early-medieval identities in Britain and Ireland.

- **383:** Ronald Hutton on "Celts," druids and later constructions of pagan Britain.
- **384:** Eleanor Barraclough on Celtic worlds and the limits of a single ethnic label.
- **385:** Dáibhí Ó Cróinín on Saint Patrick, including Patrick's enslavement and later Christian mission.
- **386:** Gordon Noble on the Picts, Roman frontiers and northern political organization.
- **387:** Eleanor Barraclough on Viking invasion, settlement, plunder and integration.
- **388:** Viking mythology and religious change, including the interaction between Norse religion and Christianity.

## Blackball use

Patrick's enslavement is a reminder that "slave" and "skilled/unskilled" are not occupational categories that can be read off identity: status can be imposed on people with very different abilities.

The series also provides a useful warning about administrative labels. "Celt," "Pict," "Briton," "Roman" and "Viking" can refer to different mixtures of language, political allegiance, geography, occupation or later scholarly convention. Do not turn a convenient label into a fixed biological population.
