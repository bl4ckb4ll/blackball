# Empire episodes 48–71 — slavery, abolition and aftermath

## What the run does

This is one of the most directly useful Empire series for Blackball because it refuses to make Atlantic plantation slavery the only historical slave system while still giving the Atlantic system sustained attention.

The sequence moves through ancient slave societies, Viking/Slavic enslavement, Mediterranean and Barbary slavery, the Royal African Company, Caribbean plantation revolt, Black lives in Britain, Black abolitionists, military service, Sierra Leone, Haiti, British abolition, emancipation and later arguments about compensation and reparations.

## Mechanisms worth extracting

### Corporate organization

The Royal African Company material should be coded as a company dossier, not merely a moral anecdote. Relevant variables include monopoly privileges, shareholders, state protection, forts, shipping, financing, mortality, sale prices, and the relation between private profit and royal/state power.

### Delegated coercion

Plantation owners, trading companies and imperial officials depended on a chain of agents: captains, factors, soldiers, overseers, drivers, local brokers, jailers and others. This fits Blackball's recurring **delegated coercion / intermediary enforcer** category.

### Labor and productivity

Where the episodes discuss plantation production, link them to the Edward Baptist material already in Blackball. The question is not only that labor was unfree; it is how daily output was measured, quotas were imposed, punishment was administered and productivity changed.

### Resistance and bargaining power

Tacky's Revolt, Haiti, desertion, military enlistment, escape and abolitionist organization provide different forms of outside option and collective action under conditions where ordinary labor-market bargaining was legally suppressed.

### Abolition is not the end variable

Keep separate dates and outcomes for:

- slave-trade prohibition;
- legal emancipation;
- compensation to owners;
- apprenticeship or transitional coerced labor;
- wage and land access after emancipation;
- political rights;
- imperial expansion after abolition.

## People/books to follow

- Alex Renton — *Blood Legacy: Reckoning With a Family's Story of Slavery*.
- William Dalrymple and Anita Anand's wider imperial bibliographies supply context but are not substitutes for slavery specialists.

## Source discipline

Episode descriptions are an index into subjects. For quantitative claims about numbers transported, mortality, profitability, productivity or compensation, record the underlying dataset or archival source separately.
