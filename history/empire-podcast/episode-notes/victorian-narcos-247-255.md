# Empire episodes 247–255 — opium, the East India Company and the Opium Wars

## Core story

The series links Britain's tea demand, the East India Company's territorial control in India, Indian opium production, illegal sales into Qing China, state/company revenue and British military force.

The useful point is institutional: a drug market was not simply a collection of private smugglers. Imperial revenue, a chartered company, licensed/organized production, merchant networks and gunboat diplomacy formed an interacting system.

## Blackball questions

### Follow the money

Build a measurable chain:

Indian cultivator -> Company procurement/monopoly -> auction/merchant -> smuggling network -> Chinese purchaser -> silver flows -> Company/British fiscal position.

For each link ask about price, margin, debt, coercion, legal status and bargaining power.

### Separate producer from beneficiary

Indian farmers producing poppy were not equivalent to the merchants and state actors capturing trade profits. "Britain sold opium" is too aggregated for labor analysis.

### Coercion has several layers

- coercive or constrained cultivation arrangements in India;
- criminalization/prohibition in China;
- bribery and smuggling;
- state military violence when Qing enforcement threatened the trade;
- treaty concessions after war.

### Technology is not neutral here either

Steamships, naval gunnery and logistics mattered, but the decisive question is what political/economic arrangement the technology enforced.

## Modern analogy warning

The series ends by connecting opium history to fentanyl and contemporary drug politics. Preserve the analogy as a question, not an identity claim. Modern synthetic-drug supply chains, prohibition regimes, ownership structures and state capacities differ sharply from nineteenth-century Company opium.
