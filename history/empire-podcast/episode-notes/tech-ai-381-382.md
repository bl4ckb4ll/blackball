# Empire episodes 381–382 — technology companies and *Empire of AI*

## Episode 381 — corporate-empire analogy

The hosts compare billionaire-controlled technology firms and infrastructure with historical chartered companies such as the East India Company. The useful Blackball move is to turn the analogy into metrics rather than accept or reject it rhetorically.

Possible comparison dimensions:

- power granted directly by a state;
- independent coercive force;
- territorial administration;
- monopoly or gatekeeping power;
- control of communications/logistics infrastructure;
- ability to impose terms on workers/suppliers/users;
- political lobbying and regulatory capture;
- cross-border reach;
- accountability and legal remedies.

On most of these dimensions a modern technology company is **not literally an East India Company**. The analogy can still identify concentrated private control over infrastructure without erasing institutional differences.

## Episode 382 — Karen Hao, *Empire of AI*

Karen Hao joins the show to discuss the political economy of large AI companies, especially OpenAI, using themes from *Empire of AI*.

Blackball should extract concrete variables from the "empire" framing:

- capital expenditure and financing;
- compute ownership/access;
- electricity and water demand;
- data acquisition and labeling labor;
- contractor wages and geography;
- model-development labor hierarchy;
- terms imposed on downstream users/developers;
- government contracts and regulatory influence;
- environmental externalities.

This is a good test case for Blackball's general method: **turn criticism into a measurement gradient**. "AI empire" is weak as a conclusion but potentially useful as a prompt for measurable concentration, extraction and bargaining-power questions.
