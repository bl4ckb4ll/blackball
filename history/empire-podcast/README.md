## Why Blackball is recording it

The podcast is useful as a **map into books, historians, journalists, and historical disputes**, not as a final authority. Its strongest Blackball value is that it repeatedly makes institutions and mechanisms visible: companies, armies, plantations, slave systems, bureaucracies, borders, finance, drug monopolies, racial rule, famine, rebellion, forced migration, propaganda, and decolonization.

Several recurring Blackball questions can be asked across the feed:

- Who captures the surplus produced by imperial trade or coerced labor?
- Which middlemen turn remote policy into everyday coercion?
- When is violence direct, and when is it delegated through companies, local rulers, police, overseers, landlords, armies, or collaborators?
- What happens to ordinary workers, soldiers, enslaved people, peasants, refugees, and colonized subjects after the dramatic political event is over?
- Which claims rest on quantitative evidence, which on archives, which on memoir, and which are narrative synthesis?
- When a guest has a book, does the episode accurately represent the book's evidence and its historiographical opponents?

The right use is therefore: **podcast -> episode claim -> guest/host book -> bibliography and primary sources -> competing scholarship**.
