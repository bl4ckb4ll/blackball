# Empire podcast — episode-series index

**Coverage:** public numbered catalogue from the 2022 launch through **#391, 30 August 2026**. This is a catalogue-level synthesis from titles/descriptions and verified guest metadata, not a claim of end-to-end listening to hundreds of hours. See [source discipline](source-discipline.md).

The catalogue is best understood as overlapping miniseries rather than 391 isolated notes.

## 1–20 — East India Company, Raj, independence and Partition

The opening run establishes the show's central territory: how a joint-stock company became a territorial power, how Company rule became Crown rule, and how anti-colonial politics culminated in independence and Partition. The sequence moves through the East India Company, 1857, the Koh-i-Noor, Sophia Duleep Singh, Udham Singh, Gandhi, Jinnah, Mountbatten, Partition, East African Indians, imperial collapse, indenture, federal alternatives, the constitution and railways.

Blackball use: distinguish private-company extraction from state rule; trace armies, tax collection and local intermediaries; separate infrastructure-output claims from who paid, who owned, and who benefited.

## 21–42 — Ottoman Empire

A long chronological run moves from Byzantium and early Ottoman expansion through Constantinople, Suleiman, the harem, Cyprus and Lepanto, Anglo-Ottoman relations, coffee, slavery and elite households, nineteenth-century decline, Gallipoli, the Armenian genocide, Sykes-Picot, the Arab Revolt, Palestine, Atatürk and the end of the empire.

Guests include Bettany Hughes. The series is useful for avoiding the common error of treating European overseas empires as the only imperial form.

## 43–47 — British/Raj transition and resistance

This bridge returns to British India and resistance to colonial rule, culminating in "Rebels Against the Raj." Treat episode numbering here as catalogue metadata rather than a perfectly stable sequence.

## 48–71 — slavery, slave economies, abolition and aftermath

A major run begins with the history of slavery and compares ancient, Viking, Mediterranean, Atlantic and Caribbean systems. It includes the Royal African Company, Tacky's Revolt, Francis Barber, Olaudah Equiano and other Black abolitionist voices, enslaved military service, Sierra Leone, Toussaint Louverture and Haiti, Wilberforce, abolition, emancipation, compensation/reparations and the long afterlife of slavery.

Guest Alex Renton appears in the reckoning/reparations material. See [deeper slavery notes](episode-notes/slavery-48-71.md).

## 72–95 — Russia, the Great Game, Afghanistan and revolution

The show traces Muscovite/Russian imperial expansion through Ivan, Peter and Catherine; Anglo-Russian competition in Central Asia; the British invasion and retreat from Afghanistan; Crimea; Mary Seacole and Florence Nightingale; Russian expansion in North America and Central Asia; Tolstoy; the Russo-Japanese War; the 1917 revolutions, Lenin, Rasputin and the Romanovs; then connects the imperial past to modern Russia and Ukraine.

Guests include Simon Sebag Montefiore.

## 96–125 — Persia/Iran, Greek-Persian wars and the Islamic Republic

The sequence starts with Cyrus and Darius, Marathon, Thermopylae, Salamis and Alexander, then moves through Persian religion, late antiquity, the rise of Islam, Ferdowsi, Timur, Safavid Shi'ism, Isfahan, Nader Shah, constitutional revolution, the Pahlavis, 1979, Saudi-Iranian rivalry, Hezbollah, the Iran-Iraq War and the Houthis.

Guests include Lloyd Llewellyn-Jones, Paul Cartledge, Barnaby Rogerson, Ali Ansari and Kim Ghattas.

## 126–143 — food, Buddhism and women who ruled

Episodes on Company foodways and the invention/standardization of "curry" lead into the Buddha, Ashoka, Greco-Roman/Indian exchange, Buddhism in China and Nalanda. A separate strand foregrounds Cleopatra, Helena, Theodora, elite women in steppe/Mongol history, Nur Jahan and Isabella of Castile.

## 144–162 — Victoria, the American Revolution and Indigenous dispossession

The series moves from Queen Victoria to the British Atlantic and the creation of the United States: Pocahontas, settler-Indigenous conflict, Washington, Jefferson, Hamilton, Adams and Franklin, the Revolution, Native nations under the new republic, the Trail of Tears, the Oregon Trail and the Gold Rush.

Guest Katie Hickman appears in the westward-migration material.

## 163–178 — U.S. expansion, overseas empire, famine, Korea, Cuba and Vietnam

The catalogue links continental expansion to Hawaii and the Philippines, with Daniel Immerwahr joining the show for "How To Hide An Empire," Theodore Roosevelt and the Philippine-American War. It then turns to the Bengal famine, North Korea and the Korean War, Castro/Cuba and CIA plots, and the Vietnam War.

This run is particularly useful for testing where the word **empire** adds explanatory value and where it risks flattening distinct systems.

## 179–200 — Indian Ocean networks, Scotland, piracy and corporate pursuit

Episodes cover the "Indosphere," Roman-Indian commerce, Chinese empresses, Southeast Asian states and Angkor, then the formation of Britain, Jacobitism and Culloden, Scots in India/slavery/abolition/North America. A mathematical-history detour covers Indian numerals and Fibonacci. The pirate sequence follows Blackbeard, pirate suppression, trials and East India Company manhunts.

## 201–222 — Raj at war and the Mughal dynasty

After "Raj at War" and literary episodes on *Treasure Island* and *Peter Pan*, a long Mughal sequence covers Babur, Humayun, Akbar, Jahangir, Thomas Roe and the East India Company, Mughal painting, Shah Jahan and the Taj Mahal, succession struggles and imperial decline.

Nandini Das appears for Thomas Roe and *Courting India*.

## 223–246 — plants, art, Chagos and Ireland

"Empire of Plants" and related art episodes examine botanical and visual systems of empire. The show then treats the Chagos Islands/Britain's remaining imperial geography before a large Ireland run: Tudor plantations, Cromwell, the Boyne, Irish participation in empire, the Great Famine, Easter Rising, IRA, civil war, the Troubles, Good Friday and modern Ireland.

Guests include Alastair Campbell, Diarmaid Ferriter and Fintan O'Toole.

## 247–255 — Victorian narco-state / Opium Wars

This run follows tea demand, the East India Company's Indian opium monopoly, smuggling and state revenue, Chinese prohibition, British coercion and the Opium Wars. The narrative connects a legal imperial monopoly and military power to a drug market rather than treating the war as an abstract diplomatic quarrel.

See [deeper notes](episode-notes/victorian-narcos-247-255.md).

## 256–277 — Yalta, Berlin, Greenland, Canada and Panama

The show moves from Stalin, Churchill and Roosevelt at Yalta through the race to Berlin and the Berlin Blockade; then Greenland's Norse and Danish colonial histories and U.S. strategic bases; French/British settlement in Canada, the War of 1812 and Indigenous residential-school legacies; and finally the Panama Canal.

Guests include Eleanor Barraclough, Daniel Immerwahr, Mark Horton and Matthew Parker.

## 278–290 — Partition beyond India, Suez and the Cholas

Sam Dalrymple's *Shattered Lands* frames the breakup of British Asian imperial space: Burma/Myanmar, Dubai and the Gulf, Pakistan, Kashmir and Bangladesh. The Suez sequence examines Nasser, Eden, invasion and the collapse of British/French pretensions. Anirudh Kanisetti then joins for the Chola Empire and Indian Ocean power.

## 291–301 — Gaza across millennia

An unusually long temporal sequence moves from Philistines and ancient empires through Roman/Byzantine Gaza, Islamic conquest, Crusaders, Ottoman rule, World War I, the British Mandate, Palestinian revolt, the 1948 war and the creation of the Gaza Strip.

Guests include Josephine Quinn, Eugene Rogan and Caroline Elkins. Modern political/legal conclusions require especially careful cross-checking. See [deeper notes](episode-notes/gaza-291-301.md).

## 302–321 — imperial literature, culture and photography

The show reads Orwell, Conrad, Austen/Brontë, colonial cartoons, Tintin, cricket, Kipling and V. S. Naipaul as archives of empire, then turns to photographers of Hitler and Churchill, relic-taking, photography in the Congo Free State and Anglo-Indian visual culture.

Guests include Maya Jasanoff, Tim Wigmore, Andrew Lycett and Benjamin Moser.

## 322–329 — the 1857 uprising in depth

An eight-part return to 1857 follows the Bengal Army mutiny/uprising, Delhi, Kanpur, Lucknow, Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi, British counterattack and retribution, and the formal birth of the Raj. This should be cross-linked to the show's opening episodes so that 1857 is not treated as a one-off event detached from Company military labor, recruitment and taxation.

## 330–337 — Iran in 1979/2026 and the Bronze Age collapse

Scott Anderson and Ramita Navai join contemporary-Iran episodes that compare the 1979 revolution with conditions in 2026. The show then pivots to a Bronze Age collapse series on palace states, warfare, trade and systemic breakdown, with guests including Stephen Fry, Eric H. Cline, Aidan Dodson, Campbell Price and other ancient-world specialists across the surrounding run.

## 338–345 — Mao and revolutionary China, with Middle East interruptions

The Mao sequence covers his rise, communist insurgency, Japanese invasion and Nanjing, Stalin, the Great Leap Forward and the Cultural Revolution. Current-events/history episodes on Iran and Lebanon interrupt the sequence. Jung Chang appears in a bonus interview; Ali Ansari appears on Iran.

## 346–353 — Arab-Israeli wars, occupation and Lebanon

The sequence runs from Nasser/PLO politics through the Six-Day War, post-1967 occupation, Yom Kippur and the oil shock, Camp David, the 1982 Lebanon war, Hezbollah and hostage-taking. Guests include Eugene Rogan, Kim Ghattas and Raja Shehadeh in related episodes/bonuses.

## 354–361 — Bolívar, spice empires and Louis Theroux

Four episodes on Simón Bolívar and Spanish American independence are followed by Dutch/English spice-company rivalry, including the colonial history behind New York. A long-form interview with Louis Theroux uses his reporting on settlers, extremist subcultures and the manosphere to discuss tribalism and ideological communities.

## 362–368 — Ancient Egypt and religious change

Episodes cover late Bronze Age Egypt, Akhenaten, Nefertiti, Tutankhamun, modern Tut-mania and arguments about Egyptian/Levantine religion and the origins of later monotheistic traditions. Guests across the run include Aidan Dodson, Campbell Price, Francesca Stavrakopoulou, Lloyd Llewellyn-Jones and Eric H. Cline.

## 369–375 — early British Indians, Mount Rushmore and Ken Burns

Episodes recover early Indian lives in Britain and imperial Britain, then discuss Mount Rushmore and U.S. memory. Anita interviews Ken Burns on the American Revolution, Native dispossession and the growth of U.S. power. Public directories disagree around this boundary by one episode number; title/date are safer than number alone.

## 375/376–380 — Algerian Revolution

A six-part sequence traces the French conquest of Algeria, settler colonial rule, repression and FLN insurgency, the Battle of Algiers, independence, the revolution's international influence, and its connection to the later rise of Jean-Marie Le Pen and the French far right.

## 381–382 — technology companies as empire; *Empire of AI*

The hosts compare modern technology corporations and billionaire-controlled infrastructure with historical chartered companies, then interview Karen Hao about OpenAI and the political economy of large-scale AI. This is analogy plus contemporary reporting, not a literal claim that a software company has the legal status of the East India Company.

See [deeper notes](episode-notes/tech-ai-381-382.md).

## 383–388 — Celts, Rome, Picts, Saint Patrick and Vikings

A six-part Britain/Ireland sequence asks what "Celt" meant, how Roman rule interacted with local populations, who the Picts were, how Saint Patrick's enslavement shaped his life, and what Viking settlement/warfare looked like beyond stereotype. Guests include Ronald Hutton, Eleanor Barraclough, Gordon Noble and Dáibhí Ó Cróinín.

See [deeper notes](episode-notes/celts-picts-vikings-383-388.md).

## 389–391 — Road to 9/11, public episodes at cutoff

Jason Burke joins episodes 389–390 on radical Islamist movements, Sayyid Qutb, Ayman al-Zawahiri, Abdullah Azzam, the Soviet-Afghan war and the institutional origins of al-Qaeda. Steve Coll joins episode 391 on the bin Laden family's Saudi construction fortune and Osama bin Laden's formation.

Apple lists #392, "Bin Laden vs Saudi Arabia," dated 2 September 2026 as **subscriber early access**; later #393–394 are also shown ahead of general release. They are not counted as public-feed coverage at this cutoff.

See [deeper notes](episode-notes/road-to-9-11-389-391.md).
