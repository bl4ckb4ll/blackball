# History

These notes are source-spidered reading guides rather than encyclopedia replacements.

## Default method

1. Start with a Wikipedia article as a navigational hub and record the exact revision used.
2. Write a short account of the event or subject, separating well-supported facts from disputed details.
3. Follow every numbered citation in that Wikipedia article and every item in its general bibliography.
4. Collapse repeated citations into an annotated source ledger, but preserve a citation-by-citation index so nothing disappears.
5. For each source, record what kind of source it is, what it is useful for, and obvious limitations or conflicts with other evidence.
6. Prefer stable links to public full text, libraries, journals, archives, or the cited Wikipedia footnote. Do not mirror copyrighted books merely because they are cited.
7. Treat contemporary newspapers, memoirs, corporate accounts, union accounts, government testimony, and later scholarship as different kinds of evidence rather than interchangeable authorities.
8. Preserve disagreements. If contemporary sources conflict about something such as who fired first or how many people died, say so.

The default spider is **one hop from the Wikipedia article**: every source cited by Wikipedia is accounted for. Bibliographies inside those sources can become a second-hop corpus when useful.
