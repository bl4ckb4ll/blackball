# Eric Foner — *Reconstruction: America's Unfinished Revolution, 1863–1877*

## Why Blackball is recording this

Eric Foner's *Reconstruction: America's Unfinished Revolution, 1863–1877* is a major scholarly synthesis of the post-Civil War Reconstruction era and belongs in Blackball's history corpus alongside material on slavery, emancipation, labor, citizenship, political power, and the remaking of the South after the Civil War.

This file is currently a **source node, not a chapter-by-chapter reconstruction of the book**. The bibliographic identity and chapter map below are directly checkable from institutional/catalog sources. Blackball should not attribute detailed arguments, quotations, page claims, or chapter conclusions to Foner until the relevant text has actually been inspected.

## Bibliographic identity

- **Author:** Eric Foner
- **Title:** *Reconstruction: America's Unfinished Revolution, 1863–1877*
- **First publication:** 1988
- **Original publisher:** Harper & Row
- **Original physical description:** xxvii + 690 pages
- **Updated edition:** Harper Perennial Modern Classics, 2014, with a new introduction
- **Author affiliation:** DeWitt Clinton Professor Emeritus of History, Columbia University

Columbia's history department identifies Foner as a specialist in the Civil War and Reconstruction, slavery, and nineteenth-century U.S. history and lists this book among his publications. Columbia's emeritus-professor page identifies the 1988 book and notes that it received the Bancroft Prize, Parkman Prize, and Los Angeles Times Book Award.

## Blackball follow-up queue

When a legitimately inspectable copy is available, useful first passes would be:

1. inspect the preface/introduction for Foner's stated intervention in Reconstruction historiography;
2. inspect **The Meaning of Freedom** and **Ambiguities of Free Labor** for the treatment of emancipation, labor relations, land, autonomy, and citizenship;
3. inspect **The Making of Radical Reconstruction**, **Blueprints for a Republican South**, and **The Challenge of Enforcement** for federal power, voting, constitutional change, and organized resistance;
4. inspect **Redemption and After** for the defeat or rollback of Reconstruction governments and rights;
5. transcribe the bibliography/index structure only from a copy that can actually be inspected, preserving page and edition information;
6. record disagreements with later Reconstruction scholarship rather than treating Foner as a terminal authority.
