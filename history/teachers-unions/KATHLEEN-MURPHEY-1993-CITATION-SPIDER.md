## What Murphey is doing

Murphey treats Boston teacher organization as a problem of a segmented labor force. Gender and grade level produced different pay, status, training, career paths, and organizational loyalties. Broad-jurisdiction AFT locals in 1919 and again in the late 1930s/1940s failed to attract most classroom teachers; a citywide union became durable only after divisions had narrowed and after teachers’ identities and organizational strategies had changed. The article uses labor and education history, contemporary newspapers, School Committee material, and AFT archival records.
