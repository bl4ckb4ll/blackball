# Harvard

This folder tracks Harvard as a recurring **credentialing, selection, sponsorship, and authority-conversion institution** in Blackball cases. It should not be treated as a generic folder for everything connected to Harvard.

For the current Hyde Park / University of Chicago case, two trajectories matter immediately: Sudhir Venkatesh and Barack Obama. The Bryan Stevenson material adds a separate Harvard Law accountability case.

See [Hyde Park](../Hyde%20Park/README.md), [University of Chicago](../University%20of%20Chicago/README.md), and [Bryan Stevenson — *Just Mercy*](bryan-stevenson-just-mercy.md).

## Bryan Stevenson: professed values versus conduct

In *Just Mercy*, Stevenson contrasts Steve Bright with professors he encountered at Harvard Law School:

> “He showed none of the disconnect between what he did and what he believed that I’d seen in so many of my law professors.”

This is not merely an anecdote about whether individual professors were pleasant or intelligent. It supplies a general accountability test for an institution that speaks in the language of justice, public service, rights, and professional ethics: compare those stated commitments with faculty conduct, ordinary career incentives, clients, student treatment, recruiting, and the actual work graduates perform.

See the [canonical source note](../sources/bryan-stevenson/just-mercy/README.md) and the broader [Erin Brockovich law-work folder](../Erin%20Brockovich/README.md).

## Sudhir Venkatesh: Chicago fieldwork → Harvard Junior Fellowship

Harvard’s Society of Fellows records Sudhir Venkatesh as a sociology Junior Fellow from **1996 through 1999**.

This date belongs in the same frame as the later “rogue sociologist” branding. Venkatesh’s Chicago research began in 1989. Near the end of *Gang Leader for a Day*, he recounts receiving the Harvard fellowship, telling J.T. that he would be leaving Chicago, and then building an academic reputation from the gang research as Harvard absorbed more of his time.

The phrase “rogue sociologist” became a published book subtitle later, but the **research trajectory retrospectively branded as rogue already included a Harvard Junior Fellowship**.

See [Sudhir Venkatesh Junior Fellow, 1996–1999](sudhir-venkatesh-junior-fellow-1996-1999.md) and [the full Venkatesh dossier](../people/sudhir-venkatesh.md).

## Barack Obama: Hyde Park → Harvard Law → Hyde Park/UChicago

Obama’s chronology creates a second Chicago–Harvard–Chicago loop:

- **1985–1988:** community organizer in Chicago; lived in Hyde Park while working farther south.
- **1988–1991:** Harvard Law School.
- **1990:** elected president of the Harvard Law Review.
- **1991/1992 onward:** returned to Chicago; later taught at the University of Chicago Law School from 1992 through 2004 and lived in the Hyde Park area for much of that period.

Harvard therefore sits between Obama’s first Hyde Park/community-organizing period and the later phase in which he held legal, academic, and political positions with much greater institutional leverage.

This is not evidence that Harvard alone caused his political rise. It is a clearly documented credential and network node whose contribution can be investigated rather than assumed.

## Research questions

1. What material support did the Society of Fellows provide Venkatesh from 1996–1999: stipend, housing, research expenses, travel, office, and freedom from teaching?
2. Who selected Venkatesh and what evidence was presented in support of his fellowship?
3. Which publications, talks, appointments, and relationships followed during and immediately after the fellowship?
4. How did Harvard describe the purpose and prestige of the Junior Fellowship in the 1990s?
5. For Obama, what did Harvard Law provide beyond the J.D.: Law Review leadership, faculty sponsorship, recruitment, networks, publishing opportunities, and visibility?
6. Which later positions are actually traceable to Harvard relationships, and which are merely chronologically subsequent?
7. In both cases, what resources were available to the people whose poverty, neighborhoods, or political problems supplied the subject matter of the later elite career?
8. For Stevenson’s law-school period, what values did Harvard Law profess, what occupational paths did it make easiest, and how did faculty conduct compare with those professed commitments?
9. What percentage of students entered expressing public-interest intentions, what work did they eventually perform, and what role did debt, rank, and recruiting play?
10. What did Harvard Law do for the paying student, separately from what Harvard Law did for professors, firms, courts, and its own prestige?

## Source discipline

Do not convert sequence into causation. “Chicago → Harvard → later authority” is documented chronology. Claims that Harvard caused a particular appointment, book contract, election result, or professional opportunity require a specific link.

Likewise, do not describe Venkatesh as institutionally outside academia during 1996–1999. The Harvard Society of Fellows is itself an elite academic appointment.

Stevenson’s sentence records his own observed judgment. Use it as a lead for comparing claims and conduct, not as automatic evidence that every Harvard law professor behaved identically.
