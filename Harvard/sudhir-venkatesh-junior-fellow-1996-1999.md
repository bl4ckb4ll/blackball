# Sudhir Venkatesh — Harvard Society of Fellows Junior Fellow, 1996–1999

## Verified institutional status

Harvard's Society of Fellows historical roster lists **Sudhir Venkatesh — Sociology — Years Served as a Junior Fellow: 1996–99**.

This is important because *Gang Leader for a Day* later carries the subtitle *A Rogue Sociologist Takes to the Streets*. The book's research narrative, however, includes Venkatesh's transition into a highly selective Harvard academic fellowship.

The precise claim is:

> The phrase "rogue sociologist" became published branding later, but the research trajectory being branded as rogue included Venkatesh's 1996–1999 tenure as a Harvard Junior Fellow.

Blackball should not weaken this into the less interesting observation that the subtitle appeared after Harvard, nor strengthen it into the unsupported claim that he publicly used the phrase "rogue sociologist" during 1996–1999.

## Relation to the Chicago fieldwork

Venkatesh began graduate study and fieldwork in Chicago in 1989. Near the end of *Gang Leader for a Day*, he recounts learning in spring 1996 that he had received the three-year Harvard fellowship and telling J.T. that he would be leaving Chicago. He describes his visits to Chicago becoming less frequent as Harvard consumed more of his time, while his academic reputation increasingly drew on what he had learned through the gang research.

The sequence therefore is:

**Chicago fieldwork among poor residents and gang members → Harvard Society of Fellows Junior Fellowship → Columbia professorship → mass-market "rogue sociologist" persona**

That sequence creates a material research question: how does access to people with very little institutional power become knowledge with high value inside elite institutions?

## Why this belongs in the Harvard folder

The Junior Fellowship is not merely another line on a biography. It is a clean example of institutional conversion: research access obtained in extremely poor Chicago communities becomes part of a record that supports residence inside one of the most prestigious scholarly institutions in the country.

That does not establish wrongdoing. It does establish an asymmetry worth measuring: **the subjects and the scholar occupy radically different positions in the market for the knowledge created from their shared encounters.**
