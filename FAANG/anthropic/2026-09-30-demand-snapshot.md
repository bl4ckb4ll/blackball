## Initial worker-facing interpretation

The useful fact is not simply that Anthropic pays well. Its current demand surface contains many different kinds of technical work: research engineering, ordinary software systems, infrastructure, product engineering, security, compute/data-center work, tooling, support, technical education, and applied deployment.

That breadth creates more possible routes than a model-research-only picture would suggest. It does **not** establish that any of those routes are accessible to a worker starting from a low-wage or low-autonomy job.

The next research step is therefore accessibility, not aspiration:

- how senior is the actual opening distribution?
- which routes have demonstrable entry points?
- which qualifications can be built independently while still employed?
- which routes require a geographic move only after an offer versus before one?
- where does Anthropic explicitly accept equivalent experience?
- what do actual employee backgrounds show?
- which adjacent employers buy the same evidence at lower entry barriers?
- what side work can produce evidence or income rather than merely consuming training time?

Do not recommend a path until those questions have enough evidence to justify the worker's time.
