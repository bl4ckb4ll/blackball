## Why 120 became 12

The broad retrieval screen found **120** postings containing terms such as `compiler`, `LLVM`, `MLIR`, `OpenXLA`, `StableHLO`, code generation, or toolchain language.

Most are not safe to call compiler jobs. Examples in the mention-only pool include:

- Neuron runtime/driver roles;
- ML systems and kernel-performance roles;
- Graviton systems work;
- generic centralized AWS SDE postings that enumerate compiler engineering among many possible specialties;
- Ring/mobile jobs that mention toolchains;
- unrelated jobs whose qualifications or surrounding stack contain compiler-related terms.

Those records remain useful as **adjacent-work evidence**, but counting them as compiler vacancies would answer a different question.

## Concentration

The audited core is visibly concentrated rather than spread evenly across Amazon:

- AWS Neuron / Annapurna Labs accounts for most of the set;
- two current roles are in Amazon Devices' machine-learning compiler work;
- Cupertino alone contains eight of the twelve requisitions.

That concentration matters for career-risk analysis. A specialty can be real and well-paid while still depending heavily on a small number of organizations, locations, products, or hardware programs.

This census does not yet tell us whether the Amazon concentration is unusual. The same method needs to be run against other employers with plausible compiler demand: Google, Apple, Microsoft, NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, Meta, compiler/toolchain vendors, EDA firms, and relevant automotive/embedded employers.

## Qualification signal

The machine screen places degree language in Basic Qualifications for only two of these twelve records (10427132 and 10432392); the other ten place degree language only in Preferred Qualifications. That observation is about where degree words appear, not yet whether Amazon accepts equivalent experience in each case.

The previously inspected 10528817 posting also illustrates why formal sections must remain separate from narrative prose: its narrative calls C++/Java experience a “must,” while its formal Basic Qualifications only require experience with at least one programming language and place compiler-specific experience under Preferred Qualifications.

## What this does and does not say

It now supports the statement:

> At one global Amazon Software Development snapshot on 2026-09-16, Blackball found 12 advertised requisitions whose title or inspected work description directly centered compiler work, among 2,613 distinct Amazon requisitions in the category.

It does **not** support:

- “0.46% of Amazon programmers are compiler engineers”;
- “Amazon hires only 12 compiler engineers”;
- “compiler specialization gives a 0.46% chance of employment”;
- “compiler coursework is or is not worthwhile”;
- extrapolation from Amazon to the whole software labor market.

Those require employee counts, hiring flow, applicant supply, cross-employer demand, adjacent-role substitution, and educational-cost evidence.
