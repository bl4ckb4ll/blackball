# Meta employment map

This is an employer case in Blackball's large-technology-employer area. The point is not to repeat Meta's recruiting copy or tell someone that a computer-science degree "should" lead here. The point is to map what work Meta is actually advertising, where it is done, what evidence the employer asks for, and whether there are observable entry routes for people who are not already established insiders.

## First organizational surface

Meta's public careers material exposes several broad technical areas rather than one generic "software" market. Current public pages include:

- software/product engineering;
- artificial intelligence and machine learning;
- infrastructure;
- production engineering;
- data centers;
- privacy/security;
- data and analytics;
- research;
- AR/VR and devices/hardware;
- enterprise engineering.

These are employer-facing categories and product/team descriptions, not yet a verified internal org chart.

## Why Meta matters for the broader career map

Meta is useful because its demand surface is not confined to a narrow "software engineer" stereotype. Its infrastructure material explicitly includes data centers, production engineering, systems security, large-scale compute, networking, operations support, and hardware-adjacent work. Its AI material spans research and product work. That creates possible overlap with computer science, electrical engineering, mechanical engineering, controls, facilities, networking, and operations.

Blackball should not infer that a degree in any of those fields creates access. The question is what requisitions actually exist and what backgrounds are accepted.

## Worker-facing questions

For someone who has studied computer science, electrical engineering, mechanical engineering, or adjacent material but does not have the desired job, record:

- which current requisitions are genuinely junior or early-career;
- which require prior employment doing substantially the same work;
- which accept equivalent experience or direct evidence instead of a particular degree pedigree;
- which are attached to university/new-graduate pipelines;
- whether there are technician, production, data-center, hardware, reliability, or operations roles that form real adjacent entry points;
- exact office/location requirements;
- interview gates;
- what independent artifacts could demonstrate the required ability;
- whether a smaller employer buys the same skill at a lower access barrier.

The employer's own statement that a field is exciting or that opportunities are broad is discovery metadata, not evidence that a worker can get hired.

## Measurement plan

1. Build a timestamped inventory of current Meta requisitions.
2. Preserve job ID, title, location, team/technology area, experience wording, education wording, and responsibilities.
3. Separate individual-contributor level from management level.
4. Classify the work from the posting body rather than the degree name.
5. Identify actual university/new-graduate and internship routes separately from experienced-hire requisitions.
6. Repeat the snapshot to distinguish durable demand from temporary bursts.
7. Build crosswalks from observed work to CS, EE, ME, technician, and other training backgrounds only after the employer demand is visible.

## Current evidence boundary

This first pass establishes only that Meta publicly recruits across software, AI, infrastructure, production engineering, data-center, security/privacy, research, data, and hardware/device-related work. It does not yet establish how many current openings exist in each category, how accessible they are, or whether a particular educational path is worth pursuing.
