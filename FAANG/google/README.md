## Why this case matters

Google exposes explicit early-career and internship gates alongside experienced engineering demand. That makes it possible to distinguish "Google hires software engineers" from the more useful question "what does an actual entry posting require?"

A current **Software Engineer, Early Career, Campus** posting lists:

- bachelor's degree in computer science or a similar technical field **or equivalent practical experience**;
- data-structures/algorithms experience from coursework, projects, research, internships, school or work, explicitly including open-source coding;
- software-development experience in at least one programming language.

The same posting is offered across multiple US locations rather than one campus.

Current search results also expose internships, student research, hardware architecture, infrastructure, AI/ML and PhD early-career roles.

## Important evidence boundary

"Equivalent practical experience" and "open-source coding" are unusually concrete posting language, but they do not establish the acceptance rate of applicants without degrees or internships.

## Next measurements

1. Freeze all US early-career and internship requisitions.
2. Separate BS, MS, PhD, internship, apprentice, and ordinary experienced-hire routes.
3. Record exact locations and whether remote work is actually offered.
4. Track how often equivalent-practical-experience language occurs.
5. Compare required experience with independently buildable evidence.
6. Search public employee histories only after the posting census is stable.
