# Microsoft employment map

Captured: 2026-09-30.

Primary sources:
- https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/professions.html
- https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/students
- https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/universityinternship
- https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/datacenters.html

## Public work surface

Microsoft's profession map is useful because it makes clear that the company buys much more than software development. Current categories include:

- software engineering;
- hardware engineering;
- security engineering;
- research, applied, and data sciences;
- data center;
- corporate technology support;
- technical support;
- supply chain;
- real estate, facilities, and construction;
- customer success;
- design;
- analytics;
- sales and technology sales.

The data-center surface explicitly includes technician work involving hardware startup/shutdown, troubleshooting, diagnostics, and quality checks.

## Entry gates

Microsoft has an **Early in Profession** surface that explicitly includes people joining from school, a bootcamp, or a career pivot.

Its university internship surface includes:

- Discovery Program for rising first-year college students;
- Explore Microsoft for first- and second-year college students;
- ordinary university internships across multiple fields.

This is evidence of advertised gates, not their probability of success.

## Why this case matters

Microsoft lets Blackball test several different ladders inside one employer:

- conventional software-engineering entry;
- hardware engineering;
- research/data science;
- data-center technician/operations work;
- technical support;
- supply chain and facilities.

That is useful for comparing degree-heavy and technician/operations paths against the same employer.
