# Uber employment map

This is an employer case in Blackball's large-technology-employer area. Uber is useful because its engineering surface includes ordinary software work, infrastructure, information security, applications engineering, machine learning, maps, marketplace systems, and autonomous-mobility work, while also exposing explicit university/new-graduate pathways.

Primary sources:
- https://jobs.uber.com/en/teams/engineering/
- https://jobs.uber.com/en/teams/emerging-talent/
- https://jobs.uber.com/en/teams/emerging-talent/careerprep/
- https://jobs.uber.com/en/jobs/

## First engineering surface

Uber's current engineering page names:

- Applications Engineering
- Information Security
- Infrastructure Engineering
- Software Engineering

It also describes current engineering work across real-time systems, AI/ML, safety, autonomous mobility, maps, marketplace platforms, backend systems, full-stack work, and infrastructure.

Do not turn these recruiting labels into a complete org chart. Attach requisitions to narrower teams when the postings support it.

## Entry routes are a separate evidence layer

Uber currently exposes internship and university-graduate routes through its Emerging Talent surface. It also runs Uber Career Prep, an **unpaid** fellowship for undergraduate engineering students with workshops, technical practice, mentorship, and mock interviews; the program describes a conversion interview for possible software-engineering internship consideration.

Sources:
- https://jobs.uber.com/en/teams/emerging-talent/
- https://jobs.uber.com/en/teams/emerging-talent/careerprep/

That is unusually relevant to Blackball because it is an employer directly exposing part of the gate rather than a college claiming to know what the employer wants. But it is not yet outcome evidence. We need cohort sizes, selection, conversion, internships, hires, and failure cases where available.

Uber also publishes individual career stories involving community-college and nontraditional routes. Those are useful case records but are selected success stories, not denominators.

Examples:
- https://jobs.uber.com/en/people-stories/career-growth/from-community-college-to-silicon-valley-my-uber-career-prep-story/
- https://jobs.uber.com/en/people-stories/career-growth/uber-engineering-was-first-to-say-yes-to-nouru/

## Why this case is useful

For a worker or graduate trying to enter software, Uber lets us compare three distinct things:

1. **experienced-hire requisitions** — what teams are buying now;
2. **formal early-career gates** — internships, university-graduate roles, Career Prep;
3. **employer-selected success stories** — examples of people who crossed the gate.

Those must not be collapsed. The first tells us demand, the second tells us an advertised path, and the third proves only that some individual paths occurred.

## Worker-facing questions

- How many genuinely early-career engineering openings appear over repeated snapshots?
- How often are ordinary "Software Engineer" openings actually senior/staff work?
- Which locations recur?
- Are internships and university-graduate roles available to community-college transfers, adult students, or people returning to school?
- Does Career Prep materially change internship or hire probability?
- Which technical interview skills are explicitly tested?
- What prior projects or work samples appear to substitute for prestigious institutional history?
- Are there operations, systems, security, mapping, or infrastructure roles that provide alternate entry routes?
- Which adjacent employers buy the same skills with a lower hiring barrier?

## Measurement plan

1. Freeze timestamped engineering and emerging-talent job inventories.
2. Separate internship, university-graduate, ordinary IC, senior, staff, and management roles.
3. Preserve location, team/subteam, job ID, requirements, responsibilities, and posting/removal dates.
4. Track requisition churn; current search results already show very fast posting/removal cycles, so a single snapshot is inadequate.
5. Build a Career Prep evidence ledger: eligibility, cohort size, cost to participant, conversion mechanism, observed conversions, and missing denominators.
6. Compare Uber's direct gate evidence with what colleges and training providers claim is necessary.

## Current evidence boundary

This first pass establishes the advertised engineering families and the existence of explicit early-career programs. It does not establish their selectivity, conversion rate, accessibility to a particular worker, or whether any training route is worth the worker's time.
