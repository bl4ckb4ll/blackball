# Amazon Software Development requisition census — 2026-09-16

This is the first countable Amazon employer-demand snapshot in Blackball.

## Population and retrieval boundary

The acquisition queried Amazon Jobs' public `/en/search.json` endpoint for the global `software-development` category from **2026-09-16 15:57:26 UTC through 15:57:53 UTC**.

The retrieval returned:

- **2,613** initial search hits;
- **2,613** raw records across 27 pages;
- **2,613** distinct JSON source-record IDs;
- **2,613** distinct numeric Amazon requisition IDs after parsing `/jobs/<number>/...`;
- **0** duplicate requisition records;
- **0** records without a recoverable numeric Amazon job ID.

Receipt: [`../inventory/2026-09-16T155726Z/receipt.tsv`](../inventory/2026-09-16T155726Z/receipt.tsv)

This gives a clean **stock of advertised requisitions at one timestamp**. It is not a count of hires, employees, headcount openings, applications, or jobs filled. A requisition can conceivably correspond to more than one eventual hire, and a posted requisition may close without a hire.

This is **not yet a degree-requirement count**. A Basic Qualifications section can mention a degree while also allowing an experience equivalent, and a simple language screen does not understand that relationship. The next degree pass must classify the actual rule: required degree, degree-or-equivalent-experience, preferred degree, or no stated degree condition.

The compiler case shows exactly why this distinction matters.

## Compiler case: mention count versus role count

The broad compiler screen found compiler/toolchain language in **120** of 2,613 requisitions. Inspection immediately showed that this was not a compiler-job count: centralized AWS postings, runtime roles, mobile roles, and other jobs can mention compiler engineering or toolchains without being compiler vacancies.

A stricter screen requiring either an explicit compiler title or explicit compiler-work language outside a detected centralized/fungible pipeline reduced the queue to **12 core candidates**:

- 11 have compiler language explicitly in the job title;
- 1 has a generic title (`Software Development Engineer III, Annapurna Labs`, Amazon job 10528830) but its inspected body places the role in Neuron Compiler Engineering and describes compiler-development infrastructure;
- 3 of the 12 are management postings;
- 9 are individual-contributor or not identified as management by the current title screen.

The curated compiler ledger is in [`../../specialties/compilers/amazon-2026-09-16.md`](../../specialties/compilers/amazon-2026-09-16.md).

As a share of this **requisition stock**, 12 / 2,613 is about **0.46%**. That number is useful only with its boundary attached: one employer, one global Amazon Software Development snapshot, requisitions rather than hires, and a deliberately conservative definition of core compiler work. It does not establish the percentage of Amazon programmers who are compiler engineers.

The contrast between 120 mentions and 12 core candidates is itself a methodological result: simple keyword counting would inflate this specialty by roughly an order of magnitude in this corpus.

## What the census now lets Blackball ask

The denominator is now real enough to support constrained questions such as:

- How many explicitly compiler-centered requisitions were visible at Amazon at timestamp T?
- How geographically concentrated were they?
- How many were management versus individual-contributor roles?
- How do their degree and experience rules differ from the rest of the category?
- How many disappear or appear between repeated snapshots?

It still does **not** support a clean distribution of backend versus distributed systems versus ML versus security. Those broad tags need the same mention-versus-role separation just applied to compilers.

## Next measurement work

1. Build role-level classifiers for the large categories, beginning with ordinary application/backend work and distributed/infrastructure work.
2. Audit random positives and negatives for each classifier before publishing category shares.
3. Replace the degree-language screen with a rule-aware degree/equivalent-experience classifier.
4. Repeat the exact census on later dates so stock, additions, removals, and persistence can be distinguished.
5. Preserve management separately from individual-contributor requisitions.
6. Repeat the same bounded method for another major employer before generalizing from Amazon.

The purpose is not to make Amazon's taxonomy authoritative. It is to measure what a large employer is publicly asking the market for, with enough provenance that a student can compare that demand with what a school is asking them to spend years learning.
