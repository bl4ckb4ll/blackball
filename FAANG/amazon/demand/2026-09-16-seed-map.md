# Amazon software-demand seed map — 2026-09-16

This note extracts only what the deliberately selected ten-posting seed corpus can support. It is a **shape-finding exercise, not a market-share estimate**.

Amazon's Software Development category displayed 2,613 open jobs when inspected on 2026-09-16. The ten records here are too small and deliberately varied to estimate the composition of that population.

## Finding 1: one SDE title covers materially different work

Current inspected `Software Development Engineer` postings include:

- general US early-career placement;
- fungible placement across Ads, LEO, Devices, and Entertainment;
- AWS network-backbone automation;
- OpenSearch/distributed search infrastructure;
- AWS identity/authentication/authorization;
- multimodal RAG/document ingestion;
- automated cloud-security detection;
- productionization of workforce economics/behavioral-science/ML models;
- AI/ML network infrastructure.

The title therefore cannot be used as a sufficient description of the work.

## Finding 2: generic qualification templates recur across unrelated teams

Several experienced-SDE postings repeat a near-identical formal minimum:

- 3+ years professional software-development experience;
- 2+ years design/architecture experience;
- experience with at least one programming language.

Some postings add a bachelor's degree and object-oriented/large-scale-system language; others put a CS degree only in preferred qualifications. Meanwhile the substantive work ranges from identity to search, security, networks, RAG, workforce-science systems, and compilers.

For later classification, keep two layers:

1. `corporate_template_requirements` — repeated SDE-family language;
2. `role_specific_requirements` — the technologies, domains, and knowledge that distinguish the actual vacancy.

Otherwise repeated boilerplate can swamp the signal we are trying to measure.

## Finding 3: operations is part of the advertised software job

The seed postings repeatedly describe more than feature coding. Examples include deployment, monitoring, operational ownership, on-call rotations, troubleshooting production problems, documentation, code review, testing, incident prevention, mentoring, and architecture decisions.

This matters when comparing curricula or interview-prep material with work. A training path that prepares someone only to solve isolated coding problems may cover a hiring gate without describing the whole advertised job.

The current evidence does **not** establish how much employee time those activities consume.

## Finding 4: broad entry pipelines and narrow specialties coexist

The 2026 US SDE posting is broad: it invites applicants to identify expertise in machine learning, distributed systems/data management, databases, quantum computing, networking, query optimization, automated reasoning, embedded systems, data engineering, mobile, and game development while placement remains subject to business need.

At the same time, Amazon advertises narrow jobs such as AWS Neuron compiler engineering and AI/ML network infrastructure with much more specific stacks and background expectations.

This suggests an important research split:

- **general/fungible hiring channels**, where placement may occur after or during hiring;
- **team-specific requisitions**, where the vacancy itself names a concrete technical problem.

Do not mix the two when estimating what a candidate must know before applying.

## Finding 5: formal sections and narrative requirements can disagree

Job 10528817, AWS Neuron compiler engineering, says in its narrative that C++/Java experience is a “must,” but its formal Basic Qualifications require only one programming language. Compiler experience is described as preferred rather than required and appears under Preferred Qualifications.

Therefore a parser should retain at least:

- narrative requirement language;
- formal Basic Qualifications;
- formal Preferred Qualifications;

rather than flattening the whole posting into one set of keywords.

## Compiler-specialty boundary

The inspected Neuron compiler posting proves that Amazon currently hires for compiler work. Additional current compiler postings establish a visible cluster around Neuron/Annapurna and another current Machine Learning Compilers role in Amazon Devices. That is enough to reject the claim that compiler engineering is merely hypothetical at Amazon.

It is **not** enough to say what percentage of Amazon programmers are compiler engineers, whether the specialty is large relative to general software work, or whether compiler coursework is a good employment bet for a particular student. Those require denominators and comparisons.

## Measurement plan for a defensible demand distribution

For a dated census or near-census:

1. Define the population: for example, every job ID visible in Amazon's global `Software Development` category at timestamp T.
2. Preserve every ID and URL before classification.
3. Deduplicate by job ID; preserve multi-location postings as one requisition unless the question specifically concerns location slots.
4. Separate individual-contributor and management roles.
5. Record level where the posting makes it inferable without guessing.
6. Classify work with multiple tags such as `application_backend`, `distributed_systems`, `cloud_infrastructure`, `networking`, `database_search`, `security`, `embedded_systems`, `compiler`, `machine_learning`, `frontend_mobile`, `graphics`, and `other`.
7. Publish the tag rule before looking at aggregate proportions.
8. Manually audit ambiguous and randomly selected records.
9. Report both requisition counts and, when separately available, location counts. Do not silently treat either as number of hires.
10. Repeat snapshots to distinguish stock from hiring flow and to observe vacancy persistence.

## Questions still open

- Does Amazon Jobs expose a stable enough endpoint to enumerate the complete category reproducibly?
- How much duplication exists among centralized/fungible requisitions?
- How often do role-specific skills appear in Basic versus Preferred versus narrative text?
- What fraction of current Amazon software postings require a bachelor's degree formally?
- How often is on-call explicitly stated?
- How different are SDE I, II, III, principal, and manager demand profiles?
- What can be learned about actual hires rather than advertised requirements?
