# Craig T. Bouchard

Business executive, industrial entrepreneur, investor, and co-author with economist James V. Koch of *The Caterpillar Way: Lessons in Leadership, Growth, and Shareholder Value* (McGraw-Hill, 2013/2014).

## Why he is here

Bouchard is the operating-company half of the Bouchard–Koch writing partnership. He is **not a business professor at Duke**. The Duke connection reported in his university and publisher biographies is service on the leadership/athletics board. His career before *The Caterpillar Way* was primarily banking, derivatives, software, steel, industrial distribution, acquisitions, and company building.

## Education

Illinois State University reports:

- bachelor's degree in social sciences education, 1975;
- master's degree in economics, 1977;
- MBA from the University of Chicago.

Illinois State elected him to its College of Arts and Sciences Hall of Fame in 2008 and later awarded him an honorary degree.

## Banking and software career

An Esmark SEC filing describes a long finance career before his steel-company work:

- First National Bank of Chicago (later part of JPMorgan Chase), 1976–1995;
- Senior Vice President;
- roles including Global Head of Derivatives Trading, Head of Institutional Research, and Head of Asia Pacific;
- President and CEO of NumeriX, a Wall Street derivatives/software company, 1998–January 2003.

The same filing describes him as having helped found NumeriX.

## Esmark

Bouchard and his brother James P. Bouchard built Esmark in the 2000s. SEC materials identify Craig as Esmark's co-founder and president (later vice chairman and president) and James as founder/chairman/CEO.

The company expanded rapidly through acquisitions and then took control of Wheeling-Pittsburgh through a proxy fight and merger. Contemporary SEC material and Esmark records document the brothers' central management roles.

In June 2008, Esmark agreed to be acquired by Russian steel company OAO Severstal for **$19.25 per share**, an overall transaction including debt valued at approximately **$1.25 billion**. The acquisition closed in August 2008.

This is the clearest basis for remembering Bouchard as someone who built and sold a large company.

## Shale-Inland

Bouchard founded Shale-Inland in 2010, an industrial distributor and fabricator serving energy and other industrial markets. The Stephens Group documented Shale-Inland acquisitions during its rapid expansion.

In March 2012, Shale-Inland agreed to be acquired by a holding company controlled by TowerBrook Capital Partners with The Stephens Group as co-investor, and to be combined with HD Supply's IPVF business. **The transaction terms were not disclosed**, so a specific sale price should not be inferred.

## Later work

Publisher and Illinois State biographies also place Bouchard in later executive roles including Signature Group Holdings and Braidy Industries. Illinois State described him in 2018 as chairman and CEO of Braidy Industries.

His institutional affiliations have included service as a Boston University trustee, service with the University of Montana Foundation, and a leadership-board role associated with Duke University athletics. These affiliations should not be confused with a faculty appointment.

## James V. Koch connection

Koch and Bouchard were both Illinois State alumni, and Koch was on the Illinois State economics faculty during Bouchard's student years. In a 2014 interview Bouchard described Koch as his longtime mentor and adviser. They co-authored at least two books:

- *America for Sale: How the Foreign Pack Circled and Devoured Esmark* (2009), about the Esmark sale;
- *The Caterpillar Way: Lessons in Leadership, Growth, and Shareholder Value* (2013/2014).

## Claim check

### "Both authors were business professors at Duke"

No. Koch was an economics professor and university president, most prominently at Old Dominion University. Bouchard was a business executive and entrepreneur. Bouchard's Duke connection was a leadership-board/athletics role, not a professorship.

### "Both authors started and sold successful companies"

This fits Bouchard much better than Koch. Bouchard co-founded Esmark, which was sold to Severstal, and founded Shale-Inland, which was later acquired in a private-equity transaction. Koch's documented career is principally economics, university administration, research, consulting, and board service.
