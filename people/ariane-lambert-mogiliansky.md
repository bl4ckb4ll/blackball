# Ariane Lambert-Mogiliansky

Economist at the Paris School of Economics whose work includes corruption, institutional economics, and a line of research using non-classical or quantum-like mathematical structures to model uncertainty, belief, preference, and context effects.

## Why Blackball is recording her

Lambert-Mogiliansky is relevant to Blackball's uncertainty/false-precision thread because she has worked on models in which the classical event structure assumed by Savage-style decision theory is deliberately relaxed, and on models where an agent's preferences need not be treated as a fully determined latent list waiting to be measured.

That is especially useful when thinking about survey or behavioral data. A response need not be interpreted as a noisy observation of one precise underlying scalar. Her work supplies formal examples in which context, order, or the act of elicitation is part of the model.

Blackball should keep the distinction sharp:

- **empirical observation:** order and context effects exist in many behavioral settings;
- **modeling proposal:** quantum-like/non-classical structures may represent some such effects;
- **stronger interpretation:** preferences literally have the ontology of quantum states is not established merely by the mathematical fit.

## Relevant work

### Expected utility under non-classical uncertainty

With V. I. Danilov, Lambert-Mogiliansky published *Expected Utility Theory Under Non-Classical Uncertainty* in *Theory and Decision* 68 (2010), 25–47.

The paper extends Savage-style decision theory by replacing the Boolean lattice of events with a more general orthocomplemented partially ordered structure, then develops qualitative probability and expected-utility representation results in that setting.

For Blackball, the important point is structural: "uncertainty" need not always be represented by one classical Boolean event algebra plus a conventional probability measure.

### Type indeterminacy

With Shmuel Zamir and Hervé Zwirn, Lambert-Mogiliansky proposed *Type Indeterminacy: A Model for the KT (Kahneman-Tversky) Man*. The model uses quantum-mechanical mathematical formalism to represent agents whose preferences or behavioral types may be indeterminate until interaction/measurement and to model noncommutative order effects.

Use this as a formal counterexample to the reflex that every observed answer must be a noisy readout of a pre-existing precise preference.

### Continuing non-classical / quantum-like work

Her publication and working-paper record includes later work on dynamic consistency under non-classical uncertainty, preparation of belief systems, persuasion, and transparency using related formal machinery.
