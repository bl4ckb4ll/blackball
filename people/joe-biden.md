# Joe Biden

Joseph R. Biden Jr. (born 1942) is a Delaware lawyer and politician who served on the New Castle County Council, represented Delaware in the U.S. Senate from 1973 to 2009, served as vice president from 2009 to 2017, and served as president from 2021 to 2025.

> Research status: evidence-led dossier, last checked 2026-08-31. The section on sexual conduct distinguishes allegations, contemporaneous or later corroboration, denials, negative evidence, and unresolved claims. Nothing in this note should silently convert an allegation into an established fact.

## Education

- **University of Delaware:** B.A., 1965, with a double major in history and political science.
- **Syracuse University College of Law:** J.D., 1968.

The National Archives, Syracuse Law, the University of Delaware, and the Biographical Directory of the U.S. Congress agree on the basic chronology.

## Early career after law school

Biden's early career moved unusually quickly from legal practice into elected office.

### 1968: Prickett, Ward, Burt & Sanders

After graduating from Syracuse Law in 1968, Biden returned to Delaware. His first professional legal job was a roughly six-month clerkship at **Prickett, Ward, Burt & Sanders**, a prominent Wilmington firm headed by William Prickett, a Republican.

In his 2023 interview with Special Counsel Robert Hur, Biden himself named the firm and described working second-chair with Prickett. He also described the position in the context of Delaware's then-existing post-law-school clerkship/bar-admission process.

The Miller Center describes this first job more generally as work at a corporate law firm defending large businesses. Biden has repeatedly said that he found corporate practice unfulfilling.

### 1969: public defender

After leaving the corporate firm, Biden worked as a **public defender in Wilmington**. The Miller Center says that his clients were largely African Americans from Wilmington's east side.

This was not yet a full-time political career. Contemporary and later biographical accounts place him in a mixture of public-defense and private-practice work during this period.

### 1969–1970: Sid Balick, private practice, and Democratic politics

Biden later practiced with a firm headed by **Sid Balick**, a Delaware lawyer and Democratic political figure. Balick's current firm history confirms that Biden worked with him as a newly admitted Delaware lawyer.

Secondary biographical accounts also describe Biden forming **Biden and Walsh**, doing criminal/private legal work, and supplementing his income by managing property. These details are useful context but are less important than the high-confidence sequence: corporate-firm clerkship -> public defender -> private practice -> local elected office.

### 1970–1972: New Castle County Council

Biden won election to the **New Castle County Council in 1970** and served there until his election to the U.S. Senate in 1972.

### 1972–1973: election to the Senate

Biden defeated incumbent Republican J. Caleb Boggs in November 1972 while still 29. His wife Neilia and daughter Naomi were killed in an automobile collision on December 18, 1972, and his sons Beau and Hunter were seriously injured. Biden entered the Senate in January 1973 and was sworn in while his sons were still hospitalized.

The timing matters for interpreting one of the much later allegations discussed below.

## Compact early-career path

**University of Delaware (1965) -> Syracuse Law (1968) -> Prickett, Ward, Burt & Sanders clerkship -> Wilmington public defender -> Sid Balick/private practice -> New Castle County Council -> U.S. Senate.**

## Allegations and reporting concerning sexual or unwanted contact

The source record contains several distinct categories that should not be collapsed into one claim:

1. an anonymous allegation published by Alexander Cockburn in *CounterPunch* in 2008 concerning a staffer employed by another senator around the beginning of Biden's Senate career;
2. Tara Reade's allegations concerning her work in Biden's own Senate office in 1993, first publicly described in a more limited form in 2019 and expanded to an allegation of sexual assault in 2020;
3. reports by several other women in 2019 that Biden's touching, hugging, kissing, or other physical familiarity made them uncomfortable; most of those women were **not** Biden Senate staffers and none of those 2019 accounts alleged sexual assault.

The existence of reporting is established. The truth of the disputed underlying allegations is a separate question.

## Alexander Cockburn / CounterPunch: anonymous early-Senate allegation

### Original publication

On **August 23, 2008**, Alexander Cockburn published an article in *CounterPunch* titled **"'Change,' 'Hope' ... Why They Must be Talking About Joe Biden!"**

Cockburn wrote that Senate culture often included sexual advances toward staffers and interns. He then claimed that *CounterPunch* editors had, on more than one occasion, heard a recipient give vivid accounts of Biden making such advances. He described the woman only as **a staffer for another senator** and said Biden had accosted her in the Senate shortly after the automobile accident that killed his first wife.

Important evidentiary limits:

- the woman was **not named**;
- Cockburn gave no independently checkable description of the alleged act beyond characterizing it as an advance and saying she was accosted;
- the article supplied no contemporaneous document, witness, date, or response from Biden;
- the phrase "on more than one occasion" grammatically describes *CounterPunch* editors hearing the **recipient's account more than once**; it should not automatically be rewritten as multiple unnamed accusers;
- because Cockburn places the episode in the weeks after Neilia Biden's December 18, 1972 death, this cannot be Tara Reade's alleged 1993 incident.

The claim is therefore historically notable but weakly verifiable from the published article alone.

### 2020 republication

On **April 28, 2020**, during renewed reporting on Tara Reade, *CounterPunch* republished the relevant Cockburn material under the title **"Biden the Lout."** Jeffrey St. Clair's editorial introduction said the reprint showed that allegations concerning misconduct toward Senate staffers had circulated for many years.

That introduction is useful evidence of how *CounterPunch* interpreted its own archive in 2020, but it should not be allowed to increase the evidentiary strength of Cockburn's 2008 source. The body of the 2008 article still identifies only one anonymous staffer of another senator.

### Other coverage of the Cockburn item

The 2008 passage resurfaced in 2020 and was repeated by partisan and commentary outlets, including Breitbart, after Reade's allegation became national news. That republication demonstrates that the old Cockburn claim entered the 2020 media argument. I did **not** find a later mainstream investigation that independently identified Cockburn's anonymous staffer or independently substantiated the alleged early-1970s encounter.

## Tara Reade: Biden Senate staffer, 1993

Tara Reade worked as a junior staff assistant in Biden's Senate office in 1992–1993.

### 2019 public account: unwanted touching and workplace treatment

In 2019, as several women were describing physical interactions with Biden that made them uncomfortable, Reade told reporters that Biden had touched her shoulders and neck and, in one account, played with her hair. She also alleged that she was asked to serve drinks because Biden liked her legs, and that she experienced retaliation or reduced responsibilities after raising concerns.

The Associated Press interviewed Reade in April 2019 but did not publish a story at that time because it said it could not corroborate her claims and found aspects of the account inconsistent with other reporting. The Washington Post also interviewed her in 2019.

At that stage, Reade did **not** tell those outlets that Biden had sexually assaulted her.

### 2020 account: allegation of sexual assault

In March 2020, Reade gave a substantially more serious account. She alleged that in 1993 Biden pinned her against a wall in the Capitol complex, reached under her clothing, and penetrated her with his fingers.

Biden categorically denied the allegation, saying it "never happened."

The Washington Post, Associated Press, New York Times, PBS NewsHour, CNN, and other outlets investigated the claim. The source record collected here does not establish an adjudicated finding that the assault occurred.

### Evidence reported as partially corroborating Reade

Reporting identified people who said Reade had told them about serious mistreatment before 2020:

- former neighbor **Lynda LaCasse** told Business Insider and later other reporters that Reade had described an assault by Biden to her in the mid-1990s;
- Reade's brother said she had described inappropriate conduct in 1993, although his account changed in later follow-up and became more specific;
- a friend told reporters that Reade had described sexual harassment or assault around the time it allegedly occurred;
- a 1993 *Larry King Live* call by a woman whom Reade identified as her mother referred to the caller's daughter having serious "problems" while working for a prominent senator and not wanting to go to the press. The caller did not name Biden or describe sexual conduct;
- a 1996 court document concerning Reade's marriage reportedly recorded her then-husband saying she had described a problem involving sexual harassment in Biden's office, without identifying Biden as the person who harassed her or mentioning assault.

These items are forms of prior-report evidence, not eyewitness evidence to the alleged assault.

### Negative evidence and contradictory reporting

The reporting also produced substantial counterevidence and unresolved inconsistencies:

- **PBS NewsHour interviewed 74 former Biden staffers, 62 of them women.** None said Biden had sexually harassed or assaulted them, and none said they had heard rumors or allegations of such misconduct while they worked for him. PBS explicitly noted that these accounts do not disprove Reade's allegation.
- More than 20 of the PBS interviewees worked for Biden during the period when Reade was in the office. Former supervisors said they did not remember receiving the complaint Reade described.
- Ben Savage, who worked next to Reade in the mail operation, disputed her retaliation account and said he understood that performance problems led to her departure.
- PBS reported that former staffers described Biden as physically demonstrative and sometimes poor at reading whether a person wanted a hug, shoulder touch, or similar contact, while distinguishing that behavior from sexual harassment or assault in their own experience.
- Reade gave different accounts over time about why she left Biden's office and about whom she told inside the office.
- Reade's brother and at least one friend expanded their recollections after her public allegation became more serious in 2020.
- Later reporting raised separate credibility questions about claims Reade had made concerning her education and expert-witness credentials. Those issues do not by themselves decide whether her allegation against Biden was true, but they belong in a source dossier concerned with credibility and disagreement.

### The personnel complaint

Reade said she filed a complaint with a congressional personnel office in 1993. In a 2020 Associated Press interview, however, she said the complaint itself did **not** explicitly accuse Biden of sexual assault or use the phrase "sexual harassment." She said it concerned discomfort and retaliation.

No copy of such a complaint had surfaced in the reporting collected here. Biden called for relevant complaint records to be searched while maintaining that no assault occurred.

## Broader 2019 reports of unwanted physical contact

In spring 2019, roughly half a dozen to eight women publicly described physical interactions with Biden that they regarded as unwanted or uncomfortable. The best-known early account came from former Nevada lieutenant-governor candidate **Lucy Flores**, who said Biden approached her from behind at a 2014 event, touched her shoulders, smelled her hair, and kissed the back of her head.

The Washington Post subsequently reported additional accounts from women including Sofie Karasek and others who described close physical contact that made them uncomfortable.

These reports matter to the background question of Biden's physical behavior, but the categories should remain distinct:

- they were mostly **not allegations by Senate staffers**;
- they generally concerned unwanted touching or affectionate physical familiarity rather than sexual assault;
- Reade's later 2020 allegation was substantially more serious than the other 2019 accounts.

Biden responded in 2019 that social norms had changed, that he understood the need to respect personal space, and that he would be more mindful of boundaries.

## What can and cannot be said from the collected evidence

### High confidence

- Cockburn published the anonymous allegation in 2008, years before Reade's public allegations.
- Cockburn's unnamed source was described as a staffer for **another senator**, and the alleged timing was around late 1972 or early 1973.
- Reade worked in Biden's Senate office in 1993.
- Reade publicly alleged unwanted touching in 2019 and sexual assault in 2020.
- Biden denied Reade's sexual-assault allegation.
- Multiple people told reporters that Reade had described serious workplace or sexual mistreatment before 2020, though the accounts differed in detail.
- Large-scale reporting, especially PBS's 74-staffer review, found no other former Biden staffer who said they had experienced or heard of sexual harassment or assault by him while working in his office.
- Several women outside that narrow Senate-staffer category publicly said Biden's physical contact made them uncomfortable.

### Not established by the collected evidence

- the identity of Cockburn's anonymous staffer;
- independent verification of Cockburn's alleged early-1970s encounter;
- that Cockburn was describing more than one accuser;
- that Cockburn's source and Reade were connected;
- an eyewitness to Reade's alleged 1993 assault;
- a recovered 1993 complaint document substantiating an assault allegation;
- an adjudicated official finding that Biden sexually assaulted Reade.

## Research leads

- Identify Cockburn's anonymous staffer, if any surviving primary or archival source makes that possible without guessing.
- Search archived *CounterPunch* newsletters, correspondence, or Jeffrey St. Clair interviews for any additional provenance behind the 2008 allegation.
- Search Delaware and Washington newspapers from late 1972 through 1974 for contemporaneous gossip, complaint reporting, or named accounts that could independently bear on Cockburn's much later claim.
- Locate any surviving records from the congressional personnel office where Reade says she filed a 1993 complaint; do not infer their contents from her later recollection.
- Preserve reporting that cuts both ways: prior-report corroboration, office-culture testimony, contradictory recollections, and credibility problems should remain visible together rather than being collapsed into a verdict.
