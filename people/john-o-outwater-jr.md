# John O. Outwater Jr. (1923–2009)

Mechanical-engineering professor at the University of Vermont and father of writer and engineer Alice Outwater.

## Academic and engineering career

- Professor of Mechanical Engineering at the University of Vermont from 1956 until his retirement in 1993.
- Cambridge University: BA (1943), MA (1948), PhD (1976).
- MIT: ScD in mechanical engineering (1950).
- Registered professional engineer in Vermont.
- UVM reports that he was named Vermont Engineer of the Year and an Outstanding Educator of America in 1970.
- His obituary says he published about 120 research papers.

## Research

UVM summarizes Outwater's research as including:

- ski safety and mechanisms of skiing injury;
- slope design;
- fracture-mechanics test methods;
- composite materials;
- pultrusion;
- elastic constants;
- tree mechanics and sap flow.

Selected titles mentioned by UVM include:

- *The Engineering Problem of Ski Safety*;
- *On the Skiability of Snow*.

Other traceable work includes fracture-energy and adhesion studies in glass/epoxy systems and later work on pultrusion. A later literature even refers to an "Outwater–Murphy" fiber/matrix debond specimen, after work by Outwater and Michael C. Murphy.

## Ski safety

Outwater was the research adviser of UVM mechanical-engineering student Carl Ettlinger and later a long-term collaborator. Ettlinger developed the "504" ski-binding test machine, which UVM describes as an industry-standard machine for rigorous testing of binding release characteristics. UVM's current Ski Safety Lab explicitly places Outwater in this lineage.

This is a useful example of an engineering professor whose research was tied to a concrete consumer-safety problem rather than only abstract mechanical engineering.

## Family and social background

The biographical material is also relevant to the class material collected in this repository.

His obituary says that Outwater:

- was born in London in 1923;
- grew up in London and New York;
- spent summers in Norway;
- attended Stowe School in England;
- studied at Trinity College, Cambridge;
- later belonged to the Holland Society and the St. Nicholas Society of New York.

Those are facts reported by the obituary. Any interpretation of them as indicators of class position is an inference, but they are worth retaining because they provide unusually concrete evidence of the social world surrounding this academic family.

During World War II he served in the Indian Army and spent several years in India maintaining military vehicles. His obituary also reports that he later led archaeological expeditions supported by the Wenner-Gren Foundation to study how ancient stoneworkers quarried and cut large blocks without metal tools.

He married Alice Hooker Davidson in 1952. The couple later gave their names to the John and Alice Outwater Mechanical Engineering Endowment Fund at UVM.

## Alice Outwater connection

John O. Outwater Jr. was the father of Alice Brookfield Outwater, author of *Water: A Natural History* and *Wild at Heart*. Alice Outwater herself earned a bachelor's degree in engineering from UVM and a master's degree from MIT before working on the Boston Harbor cleanup and moving into environmental writing.

The connection matters because Alice Outwater's own biography sits inside a multigenerational engineering/university milieu: her father was a long-serving mechanical-engineering professor at UVM, and she herself trained in engineering at UVM and MIT.
