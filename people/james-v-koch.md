# James V. Koch

Economist, university president, professor, consultant, and co-author with Craig T. Bouchard of *The Caterpillar Way: Lessons in Leadership, Growth, and Shareholder Value*.

## Why he is here

Koch is the academic/economist half of the Bouchard–Koch partnership. Unlike Bouchard, Koch's documented résumé is overwhelmingly in economics, higher-education administration, research, consulting, and board service rather than founding and selling operating companies.

## Education

- B.A., Illinois State University, 1964.
- Ph.D. in economics, Northwestern University, 1968.
- Later recipient of several honorary doctorates, including one from Illinois State University.

## Academic career

Illinois State and Old Dominion biographies describe teaching and research appointments at institutions including:

- Illinois State University;
- California State University, Los Angeles;
- University of Grenoble;
- Brown University;
- University of Hawaii;
- Rhode Island College;
- Ball State University;
- University of Montana;
- Old Dominion University.

At Illinois State he was on the economics faculty from 1967 to 1978 and chaired the department for the final six years.

He later moved into senior university administration:

- Dean of the Faculty of Arts and Sciences, Rhode Island College;
- Provost and Vice President for Academic Affairs, Ball State University;
- President, University of Montana, 1986–1990;
- President, Old Dominion University, 1990–2001.

After leaving the ODU presidency he remained associated with the university as Board of Visitors Professor of Economics and later professor/president emeritus.

## Research and consulting

ODU describes Koch's research as principally applied microeconomics. Topics have included:

- industrial organization;
- economics of education;
- economics of intercollegiate athletics;
- discrimination and affirmative action;
- e-commerce;
- managerial effects on employee/player performance;
- CEO risk-taking.

An ODU biography credits him with ten books and more than 100 refereed-journal articles at the time it was written. Illinois State reported that he had served as consultant to more than 75 corporations and universities. The University of Montana lists clients including the World Bank, State Farm Insurance, and the NFL Players Association.

## Corporate and board involvement

Koch's career was not isolated from business. ODU records that he formerly served on the boards of the MacArthur Foundation and Wheeling-Pittsburgh Steel Company.

He also appears in 2006 SEC proxy materials as one of the director nominees in Esmark's proxy campaign for control of Wheeling-Pittsburgh. The same slate included Craig T. Bouchard and James P. Bouchard. This is an unusually direct link between Koch's academic career and the steel-company episode later covered in *America for Sale*.

That corporate involvement, however, is not the same thing as having founded and sold a company.

## Craig T. Bouchard connection

Koch was an Illinois State economics professor during Bouchard's years there as a student. Bouchard later described Koch publicly as a mentor throughout his business career and an adviser over many years.

Their books together include:

- *America for Sale: How the Foreign Pack Circled and Devoured Esmark* (Praeger, 2009), which combines Bouchard's first-person operating-company experience with Koch's economic analysis;
- *The Caterpillar Way: Lessons in Leadership, Growth, and Shareholder Value* (McGraw-Hill, 2013/2014).

*The Caterpillar Way* was based in part on a year of access to Caterpillar executives, managers, dealers, customers, labor figures, and analysts.

## Other books

Koch's other books include work on entrepreneurship, higher-education management, and college costs. Examples include:

- *Born, Not Made*;
- *The Entrepreneurial College President* (with James L. Fisher);
- *The Impoverishment of the American College Student*;
- *Runaway College Costs* (with Richard J. Cebula).

## Claim check

### "Both authors were business professors at Duke"

No. Koch was an economics professor and university president, most prominently at Old Dominion University. The Duke affiliation belongs to Bouchard and was a leadership-board/athletics connection, not a faculty post.

### "Both authors started and sold successful companies"

The available biographical record does not support this for Koch. It does support a long academic, administrative, consulting, and board career. The company-founder/sale story belongs primarily to Craig T. Bouchard, especially Esmark and Shale-Inland.
