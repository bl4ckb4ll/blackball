# Yiran Fan

## Why Blackball is recording him

Yiran Fan is central to Blackball's Hyde Park case because his death produced an unusually explicit example of elite institutional language about a young person's unrealized future. University president Robert Zimmer said scholars emphasized "just how much potential he had."

The point is not to diminish Fan's death. It was a catastrophe for him, his family, friends, colleagues, and community. The research question is what institutions mean by "potential," how they recognize it, and how much that recognition depends on already having entered a prestigious educational and professional pipeline.

## Verified biography

- Born in China in 1990.
- Came to the United States to study at the University of Chicago.
- Earned an S.M. in Financial Mathematics from UChicago in 2015.
- Entered the Joint Program in Financial Economics run by Chicago Booth and the Department of Economics.
- Worked as a research assistant and teaching assistant and was repeatedly described by faculty as an exceptional student.
- Was a fourth-year Ph.D. student in January 2021.
- **January 9, 2021:** shot and killed in the parking garage at Regents Park in East Hyde Park during a citywide shooting spree. He was 30.
- **2021:** faculty developed and defended his unfinished dissertation work, and the University awarded him a posthumous Ph.D.

## Documented benefit from University of Chicago affiliation

Fan's affiliation yielded directly observable academic resources: graduate degrees, advanced training, research and teaching roles, faculty mentorship, participation in the Booth/Economics research community, and a path toward an academic or financial-economics career.

After his death, the affiliation also produced forms of institutional recognition that are themselves relevant evidence: a University vigil, extensive memorial coverage, faculty testimony about his abilities, a memorial fund, and a posthumous dissertation defense and Ph.D.

None of this demonstrates that Fan came from a wealthy family. Blackball should keep **institutional attainment** separate from **family socioeconomic background** unless the latter is independently sourced.

## "Potential" as a measurable category

The Fan case lets Blackball distinguish two meanings:

1. **Human potential:** the open-ended life lost when any person dies young.
2. **Institutionally legible potential:** visible evidence that powerful organizations interpret as predicting a consequential career—elite enrollment, faculty endorsements, research positions, examination performance, professional placement, and similar signals.

Fan had a large quantity of the second kind of evidence. The question is whether public institutions and media use this evidence to rank the social significance of lost futures, even when they do not intend to do so.

## Same-event comparison

The January 9 shooting spree also killed [Aisha Johnson](aisha-johnson.md), a 46-year-old doorwoman killed while at work, and [Anthony Faulkner Jr.](anthony-faulkner-jr.md), age 20.

Because the deaths occurred in the same spree, the event provides a useful comparison for coding differences in coverage, biography, memorialization, institutional response, and language about the future.

See [January 9, 2021 — Yiran Fan and the language of potential](../Hyde%20Park/2021-01-09-yiran-fan-and-the-language-of-potential.md).

## Questions for investigation

1. What was Fan's family socioeconomic background? Record only sourced evidence.
2. What scholarships, assistantships, stipends, or other financial support funded his Chicago education?
3. What career path did faculty, employers, and Fan himself expect after the Ph.D.?
4. How often do UChicago memorials use words such as *potential*, *promising*, *exceptional*, or *future leader* for affiliated versus unaffiliated victims?
5. How did press coverage of Fan differ from coverage of Johnson and Faulkner in the same event?
6. Which institutional resources became available after Fan's death that would not ordinarily be available to a non-affiliated Hyde Park resident?
