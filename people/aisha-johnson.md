# Aisha Johnson

## Why Blackball is recording her

Aisha Johnson belongs in the January 9, 2021 Hyde Park shooting-spree comparison because she was killed in the same sequence of violence as University of Chicago Ph.D. student Yiran Fan, but occupied a very different occupational and institutional position.

Johnson's dossier should remain intentionally modest where the public record is thin. The disparity in available biography is itself part of what Blackball is trying to measure.

## Verified public facts

- **Age:** 46.
- Worked as a doorwoman at The Barclay condominium complex at 4940 S. East End Avenue in the East Hyde Park/Kenwood area.
- **January 9, 2021:** shot and killed while working at the building during the same shooting spree in which Yiran Fan and Anthony Faulkner Jr. were also killed.
- Family members spoke publicly about their grief in contemporary news coverage.

## Institutional position

Johnson was not identified in the sources reviewed so far as a University of Chicago student, alumna, faculty member, or employee. Blackball should therefore **not** place her in the UChicago-affiliate category without new evidence.

Her position is analytically important precisely because she was doing service/security-adjacent labor inside the same small geographic area in which highly credentialed university affiliates lived and studied.

## Same-event comparison

See [Yiran Fan](yiran-fan.md) and [Anthony Faulkner Jr.](anthony-faulkner-jr.md).

Fan's University affiliation generated a detailed institutional biography, vigil, faculty testimony, a memorial fund, and a posthumous Ph.D. Johnson's public record, from the sources currently reviewed, is much thinner and is centered on her job, family grief, and the circumstances of the shooting.

Blackball should measure that difference rather than assume an explanation for it.

## Questions for investigation

1. Who employed Johnson, at what wage, with what benefits, hours, and employment classification?
2. How long had she worked at The Barclay?
3. What biographical information did her family choose to make public?
4. Did her employer or building association hold a memorial, create a fund, or issue a statement?
5. How much local and national coverage did Johnson receive compared with Fan in the first day, week, month, and year after the shootings?
6. Which future-oriented words—*potential*, *promising*, *talented*, *future*—were used for each victim?
7. Was Johnson's death included in University of Chicago safety discussions or institutional messaging about the same spree?

## Source discipline

Do not fill gaps in Johnson's biography from assumptions about occupation, race, class, family structure, or education. Sparse documentation is not evidence of a sparse life.
