# Brennan Lee Mulligan

Writer, comedian, improviser, game master, and co-creator/regular GM of *Dimension 20*. As of 2026 he is also the Game Master for *Critical Role* Campaign Four and a co-creator of the independent actual-play podcast *Worlds Beyond Number*.

> Research status: evidence-led dossier, last checked 2026-08-31. Public audience counts are snapshots and will drift upward. Private streaming-company financials are mostly undisclosed.

## Name correction

The subject is **Brennan Lee Mulligan**, not Brandon/Burnley Mulligan.

## Why he belongs in the Blackball people file

Mulligan is a useful case for several Blackball themes at once:

- a humanities/philosophy education that he explicitly says remains useful in his paid work;
- a teacher whom he repeatedly names as personally important long after leaving school;
- an entertainment career in which moral philosophy is not merely biography but part of the actual method of storytelling;
- a form of popular intellectual work that reaches audiences far larger than a normal academic lecture or journal article;
- a creator working inside unusually successful independent subscription-media businesses rather than a conventional broadcast network.

The strongest point is not an inference. SUNY Ulster quoted Mulligan in 2023 saying that he uses lessons from Professor Tom Davis's philosophy classes **“every single day.”**

## Education: be precise about the credential

SUNY Ulster's own alumni profile says Mulligan graduated in 2005 with an **Associate's degree in Liberal Arts: Humanities, with a concentration in Philosophy**. It says he then earned a bachelor's degree at the School of Visual Arts in film/screenwriting direction.

This is worth stating carefully because profiles and Mulligan himself sometimes use shorthand such as “philosophy major” or “background in philosophy.” In a 2025 interview he said that someday he would like to go back to school, get a philosophy degree, and teach philosophy. That makes it especially unhelpful to silently turn his SUNY Ulster credential into a bachelor's degree in philosophy.

He entered SUNY Ulster unusually young. In the 2025 *Pedestrian* interview he says he started there at fourteen, studied the humanities, and studied with an “amazing philosophy professor,” Tom Davis, whom he calls “really important in my life.”

## The unusually direct “I use it every day” evidence

SUNY Ulster's 2023 alumni profile contains the cleanest answer to the recurring question of whether Mulligan thinks his philosophy education was practically useful.

The school quotes him saying:

> “I use the lessons I learned at SUNY Ulster ... every single day!”

He then connects philosophy directly with comedy and calls philosophy unusually practical for his line of work.

This should be cited instead of loosely paraphrasing him as saying “I use my philosophy degree every day.” The latter captures the idea but is not the exact wording, and his formal credential was the Liberal Arts/Humanities associate degree with a philosophy concentration described above.

## Professor Tom Davis

The professor Mulligan repeatedly names is **Thomas J. “Tom” Davis**, a longtime philosophy professor at Ulster County Community College/SUNY Ulster.

### Davis's career

A 1999 SUNY New Paltz announcement for a Davis lecture gives unusually useful biographical detail:

- professor of philosophy and English at Ulster County Community College;
- master's degree in American Studies from Seton Hall University;
- master's degree in philosophy from SUNY Albany;
- earlier teaching at Rowan College and Rutgers University;
- appointed at UCCC in 1980;
- recipient of the 1995 SUNY Chancellor's Award for Excellence in Teaching.

Davis's 1999 lecture was titled *Science, Religion and the “New” Philosophy: Taking the Middle Path between the Age of Rocks and the Rock of Ages*. SUNY New Paltz described his approach as drawing on the **American pragmatic tradition** and treating philosophy as a way to negotiate creative tension between science and religion. That does not establish that Mulligan adopted Davis's entire philosophical outlook, but it is useful evidence about the intellectual environment of the teacher Mulligan remembers so strongly.

Davis died on October 2, 2008, aged 60. His obituary says he taught philosophy at SUNY Ulster for 28 years and asks memorial donors to contribute to the Tom Davis Scholarship Fund.

### Mulligan's testimony about Davis

The evidence is strikingly consistent over more than a decade:

- **2014, ComicsAlliance:** Mulligan says he has a background in philosophy, names the late Tom Davis as a wonderful professor, and says he spent much of his youth thinking about ethics, values, and what makes a good person.
- **2023, SUNY Ulster:** he says lessons from Davis's philosophy classes are used every day in his work.
- **2025, Wildmage Press:** he says that at fourteen he encountered formal philosophy and ethics through Davis and connects that education to the ethical questions recurring throughout his art.
- **2025, Pedestrian:** he again names Davis without prompting as an amazing philosophy professor who was “really important” in his life.

This is stronger evidence of influence than merely finding a professor who happened to be listed in a course catalogue.

### An unresolved Davis attribution

Mulligan has elsewhere repeated a saying from “an old professor” that, at the level of people and civilizations, **personality predates ideology**. A circulating clip does not name the professor. Given Mulligan's repeated public praise of Davis, it is plausible that Davis was the source, and fans frequently make that attribution, but the current evidence does **not** establish it. Leave it unresolved unless a primary source names Davis.

## Moral philosophy in Mulligan's writing and GM style

There is not yet a large academic subfield called “the moral philosophy of Brennan Lee Mulligan.” There is, however, an unusually rich body of interviews plus a growing scholarly literature on *Dimension 20* and actual play. Together they make a fairly coherent picture.

### 1. Ethical problems are part of the story engine

In the 2014 *ComicsAlliance* interview about *Strong Female Protagonist*, Mulligan explicitly connects his philosophy background and Tom Davis to questions of ethics, values, responsibility, privilege, systems of oppression, and the inadequacy of reducing morality to superheroes punching discrete villains.

The important structural point is that the moral problem often shifts from **“Who is the bad person?”** to **“What system has constrained the available choices?”** That same move later becomes conspicuous in his tabletop worlds.

The 2025 Wildmage Press interview, *Villains, Accomplices, and Bystanders: Portraying antagonists and power systems*, makes the connection even more explicit. Mulligan says his work repeatedly returns to ethical conundrums he is personally wrestling with. The interviewer focuses specifically on how his games portray bad actors alongside entrenched systems of power and exploitation.

### 2. He does not describe the GM as an authoritarian storyteller

A recurring Mulligan formulation is that GMing is an **act of service**. The GM has enormous fictional power, but that power exists for the players and their characters rather than for the GM to dominate them.

In *The Creative Independent* (2020), he extends this across improv, LARP, and D&D: the storyteller spends effort lifting other participants up. That is a useful lens for his practical GM style. It helps explain why his games can contain severe villains and consequences without presenting the person running the game as an adversary trying to defeat the players.

### 3. Tenderness, inclusion, and safety are stated design commitments

In a 2020 *Geekiary* interview Mulligan says the *Dimension 20* table prioritizes **“tenderness and a play philosophy.”** He says he does not want to make mean-spirited D&D and describes the desired result as kind, inclusive, and joyful.

In *The Creative Independent* he calls diverse casting a moral imperative, emphasizes that players are friends and human beings before they are storytellers, and recommends safety tools when games approach intense emotional material.

That makes inclusion more than a demographic observation about the cast. In his own account it is part of the ethics of production.

### 4. Moral agency is often represented as constrained rather than purely individual

His *Unsleeping City* interpretation of Robert Moses is a particularly clean example. In the *99% Invisible* *Power Broker* discussion, Mulligan approaches Moses explicitly “as someone who studied philosophy” and talks about the limits of an American picture of freedom as something residing solely inside an unconstrained individual.

His fictional Moses expresses the point in terms of roads: people may think they freely choose right or left, but somebody else already decided where the roads go. This is a recurring Mulligan move: moral choice matters, but the architecture of institutions determines which choices are cheap, expensive, visible, or even possible.

### 5. *Mentopolis*: conscience, impulse, and systems that make moral action feel irrational

Rowan Zeoli's 2023 *Polygon* interview about *Mentopolis* is one of the clearest examples of Mulligan talking moral psychology rather than only politics. Discussing impulse, he rejects the assumption that virtue is always cool, deliberate control. He argues that people can have genuinely moral impulses and that oppressive systems can make moral action feel irrational precisely because compliance has been normalized.

That is philosophy doing direct work in a season's worldbuilding: parts of the mind become characters, and the conflict asks how conscience, attention, impulse, ambition, and social pressure interact.

### 6. Storytelling as a technology for values

In a 2025 *Scientific American* interview Mulligan carefully calls himself a **“philosophy-flavored comedian,”** not an academic. He then describes storytelling as a compressed way of carrying hard-won lessons, values, ethics, and cultural information. His question is not only how a story persuades another person but how a person makes their own values coherent to themselves.

For tabletop role-playing specifically, he stresses that the participants are simultaneously audience and storytellers. That distributes the authority to decide which values become meaningful in the story.

### 7. Dice and contingency are not treated as interruptions to authored meaning

A useful scholarly discussion is Katrin Rauch's Paidia essay *Performing Meaning Against All Odds: Dice in the Actual Play Anthology “Dimension 20”*. It analyzes Mulligan's famous willingness to “honor” an unexpected natural-20 result even when it destabilizes prepared worldbuilding.

Rauch's reading is that *Dimension 20* turns randomness into collaborative meaning through performer trust. This is not a moral-philosophy article in the narrow sense, but it matters to the ethics of his GM practice: authority is divided among GM preparation, player action, rules, chance, and collaborative interpretation.

Calvo Martín and García García's 2022 *AVANCA | CINEMA* paper likewise treats *Dimension 20* as a flagship actual play and studies how natural 20s and GM rulings can restructure narrative rather than merely decorate it.

## Serious criticism and scholarship worth keeping

These are not all making the same claim and should not be collapsed into “academics agree with Mulligan.” They are useful because they examine distinct features of the medium or his work.

### Direct interviews / primary statements

- **Juliet Kahn, ComicsAlliance (2014), “Do Powers Make The Woman? ‘Strong Female Protagonist’ Creators On Superheroes And LARPing.”** Early direct statement linking Tom Davis and philosophy to ethics, values, responsibility, privilege, oppression, and storytelling.
- **Khai, The Geekiary (2019), “Brennan Lee Mulligan Thinks Playing D&D Can Make You A Better Person… And He's Got A Point.”** Long interview on GM virtues, story versus game goals, and role-playing as a possible exercise in heroism and empathy.
- **Lior Phillips, The Creative Independent (2020), “Actor and writer Brennan Lee Mulligan on balancing art and stamina.”** Particularly useful on storytelling as service, safety, representation, power, politics, morality, and ethics.
- **The Geekiary (2020), “The Unsleeping City, Responsible Storytelling...”** Source for tenderness, play philosophy, kindness, inclusion, and joy as explicit production aims.
- **Rowan Zeoli, Polygon (2023), “Dimension 20's Mentopolis is a dark, dystopian take on Inside Out.”** Moral psychology of impulse, conscience, compliance, and unethical institutions.
- **99% Invisible (2024), *The Power Broker #11: Brennan Lee Mulligan*.** Extended applied political-philosophy discussion of Robert Moses, infrastructure, constrained choice, and individual freedom.
- **Hannah Rose / Wildmage Press (2025), “Villains, Accomplices, and Bystanders: Portraying antagonists and power systems.”** Probably the single most on-point interview for a dossier about Mulligan's moral philosophy as a GM.
- **Scientific American (2025), interview/podcast on Mulligan and D&D.** Values and ethics as information stored in stories; tabletop as shared authority over meaning.
- **WIRED (2025), “Dungeon Master Brennan Lee Mulligan Answers D&D Questions.”** Includes his criticism of D&D alignment as a thin taxonomy of moral philosophy alongside practical GM discussion.

### Scholarship / criticism

- **Jorge Calvo Martín and Francisco García García (2022), “Critical Success: The narrative weight of chance in TTRPG based shows (actual play),” AVANCA | CINEMA.** Chance, GM discretion, and narrative restructuring in actual play, including *Dimension 20*.
- **Emma French (2024), PhD thesis, University of Glasgow, *How Do You Want to Do This?: Dungeons & Dragons as transformative fantasy*.** Treats actual play including *Dimension 20* and Mulligan's *Escape from the Bloodkeep* and *Exandria Unlimited: Calamity* as transformative fantasy and collaborative counternarrative.
- **Katrin Rauch, Paidia, “Performing Meaning Against All Odds: Dice in the Actual Play Anthology ‘Dimension 20’.”** Close analysis of dice, contingency, improvisation, and Mulligan's “honor a Nat 20” practice.
- **“Role-ing The Dice: Identity, Intimacy & Consent in Table-Top Role Playing,” Journal of Consent-Based Performance (2024).** Uses Mulligan's comments about TTRPGs as community-building in a broader discussion of collective storytelling, identity, intimacy, and consent.
- **Kelsey June Martin (2025), “A New Kind of Evil: The Symbolism of ‘Escape from the Bloodkeep’.”** Independent criticism rather than peer-reviewed scholarship; reads the supposedly “evil” campaign as moving toward friendship, self-acceptance, creation, and renewal.
- **Clinical/narrative review (2026), “Tabletop role playing games as a way forward with structurally marginalized youth.”** Not a study of Mulligan's work, but cites his Wayfinder reflections while discussing autonomy, mastery, belonging, community, and self-discovery through TTRPGs.

## Audience: how many people watch Brennan-hosted episodes?

There is no public number for unique people watching a given *Dimension 20*, Beacon, or Dropout episode across all platforms. Dropout and Beacon do not publish episode-level unique-viewer figures. Public YouTube counts are therefore the cleanest reproducible lower-resolution evidence, with two major warnings:

1. **views are not unique viewers** — one person can generate more than one view;
2. they omit much of the audience — especially paywalled Dropout/Beacon viewing, Twitch streams, podcasts, clips, and later re-watches on other platforms.

### Public examples, checked 2026-08-31

- *Fantasy High*, episode 1, “The Beginning Begins”: roughly **8.4 million** YouTube views.
- *The Unsleeping City*, episode 1, “Start Spreading the News”: roughly **2.93 million** YouTube views.
- *Escape from the Bloodkeep*, episode 1, “Welcome to the Dark Side”: roughly **3.10 million** YouTube views.
- *Exandria Unlimited: Calamity*, episode 1, “Excelsior”: roughly **6.29 million** YouTube views, with Mulligan as GM.
- *Critical Role* Campaign Four, episode 1, “The Fall of Thjazi Fang”: roughly **6.5 million** YouTube views in the current YouTube index, with some current third-party trackers reporting somewhat more.

This is already a very large audience for multi-hour improvised tabletop sessions. It also leaves out the people who saw the episodes first or exclusively on paid services.

### Live audience

The scale is no longer only online. Reporting around the 2025 *Dimension 20* tour describes sold-out arena dates including Madison Square Garden and the Hollywood Bowl; Mulligan discussed playing tabletop role-playing in front of about **20,000 people**. Critical Role's Campaign Four announcement likewise described the Dimension 20 Madison Square Garden event as selling more than 20,000 tickets very quickly.

## Dropout: the financially relevant company for most of Mulligan's career

If the question is “what business made *Dimension 20* and Mulligan's regular hosted work economically viable?”, **Dropout** is the more relevant company than Beacon.

### Publicly reported business facts

*Fast Company* reported in 2024 that:

- Dropout had subscribers in the **mid-to-high six figures** after roughly doubling in 2023;
- about **85% of gross revenue** came from subscriptions;
- the service then cost $5.99/month or about $60/year;
- the business became profitable in 2023;
- it adopted employee profit sharing;
- it employed 27 full-time staff, with much production and talent hired project-by-project.

By May 2026, *TheWrap* reported that Dropout had passed **1 million subscribers in October 2025**, up 31% year over year. Its regular monthly price was then **$6.99**, with a separate optional $129/year Superfan tier. Sam Reich declined to give a newer exact subscriber count beyond saying the company continued to do well.

That gives us meaningful scale without pretending to know a revenue statement that the private company has not published. A purely illustrative calculation is that **1,000,000 subscribers paying $6.99 for twelve months would correspond to about $83.9 million in annual subscription billings**. That is **not** a Dropout revenue estimate: real subscribers include annual plans, different billing channels and historical prices; churn, taxes, app-store/payment fees, discounts, and Superfan payments complicate the arithmetic; and the company has not disclosed the distribution.

The more defensible claims are the ones actually reported: more than one million subscribers by late 2025, a subscription-dominant revenue mix, and profitability beginning in 2023.

## Beacon: relevant because Mulligan now GMs Critical Role Campaign Four

**Beacon is Critical Role's membership platform, not Mulligan's personal company and not the home platform that created Dimension 20.** It launched in May 2024. Mulligan's direct relevance to Beacon increased sharply when he became Game Master for *Critical Role* Campaign Four, which premiered October 2, 2025 and continues there in 2026.

### What is public

As of 2026-08-31 Beacon advertises:

- $5.99 monthly membership;
- $59.99 annual membership;
- same-day Critical Role VOD and podcast access;
- ad-free content;
- exclusive *Critical Role Cooldown* and Fireside Chats;
- early access to selected shows;
- event presales, merchandise discounts, and a private Discord.

Beacon's 2026 update also describes continued product investment in offline downloads and smart-TV apps.

### What is not public

I did **not** find a reliable public Beacon subscriber count, Beacon revenue figure, operating profit, or audited financial statement. Critical Role is a private company, and coverage of Beacon's launch notes that the company declined to discuss its finances.

The often-repeated **$9.6 million** Critical Role number is historical and should not be mislabeled as Beacon revenue. It comes from leaked Twitch payout data reported in 2021: Critical Role allegedly received about $9.63 million from Twitch between August 2019 and September 2021. That was gross platform payout before production costs, years before Beacon launched, and only one piece of Critical Role's business.

So the financially honest Beacon entry is: **known list price and product investment; unknown subscriber count and revenue.**

## A compact interpretation of Mulligan's GM philosophy

The evidence supports a fairly specific description without turning him into a systematic philosopher he does not claim to be.

1. **Ethics is a source of dramatic problems.** Stories are used to investigate questions he has not necessarily settled for himself.
2. **Individuals act inside systems.** Power is often represented through institutions, incentives, infrastructure, and constrained choice rather than only through villainous personalities.
3. **The GM's fictional power is fiduciary rather than sovereign.** Mulligan repeatedly describes running games as service to players and their stories.
4. **Kindness does not require low stakes.** His games can be violent, frightening, tragic, or politically angry while the social contract at the table is deliberately tender rather than mean-spirited.
5. **Chance has legitimate authority.** Dice can overthrow preparation; the GM's job includes making the surprising result meaningful rather than protecting a predetermined plot.
6. **The table is a moral collaboration.** Values are not encoded by one author alone because players continuously alter the story.
7. **Philosophy is practical craft for him.** This is the least inferential claim of all: he has repeatedly linked philosophy education to comedy and storytelling, and SUNY Ulster preserves his statement that the lessons are used every day.
