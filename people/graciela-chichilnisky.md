# Graciela Chichilnisky

Economist and mathematical statistician whose work on decision under uncertainty, catastrophic risk, rare events, and nonparametric econometrics is directly relevant to Blackball's treatment of false precision and model limits.

## Why Blackball is recording her

Chichilnisky is useful here because several of her papers explicitly challenge standard treatments of uncertainty at the point where low-probability, high-consequence events or very weak modeling assumptions matter.

That does **not** make her proposed alternatives settled probability theory. Blackball should preserve a narrower claim: she provides mathematically explicit attempts to state where standard expected-utility, countably additive probability, or unrestricted nonparametric estimation can become insensitive or fail under the conditions she studies.

For the current uncertainty/precision thread, three distinctions matter:

1. A recorded numerical probability is not automatically a faithful representation of uncertainty.
2. Rare-event sensitivity can be an axiom-level modeling choice, not merely a matter of adding more decimal precision.
3. Weakening assumptions on data-generating structure can expose existence or convergence limits rather than simply produce wider standard errors.

## Relevant work

### Choice under catastrophic risk

In *An Axiomatic Approach to Choice under Uncertainty with Catastrophic Risks* (published in *Resource and Energy Economics*, 2000), Chichilnisky argues that standard von Neumann-Morgenstern-style treatment can be insensitive to low-probability catastrophic outcomes. She proposes alternative axioms requiring sensitivity to both frequent and rare events and derives a representation combining expected utility with a catastrophic-risk component.

Record this as **Chichilnisky's proposed axiomatization**, not as a universal replacement for expected utility.

### Probability with "black swans"

In *The Foundations of Probability with Black Swans* (2010), she proposes a probability framework intended to give rare catastrophic events more weight than standard countably additive distributions. The paper characterizes measures containing countably additive and finitely additive components.

Again, the useful Blackball fact is that this is an explicit mathematical attempt to represent rare-event sensitivity. The stronger claim that it supplies the correct foundation of probability remains a claim of the paper.

### Limits of econometrics

In *The Limits of Econometrics: Nonparametric Estimation in Hilbert Spaces* (*Econometric Theory*, 2009), Chichilnisky studies nonparametric estimation on unbounded sample spaces and identifies conditions under which the estimators considered converge or fail to converge. The paper connects the boundary to assumptions about rare events and heavy tails.

This is directly relevant to Blackball's rule against treating "less parametric" as automatically assumption-free or universally safer.
