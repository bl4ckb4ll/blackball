# Jim Simons

## Why Blackball is recording him

Jim Simons (1938–2024) is useful for Blackball because the familiar compressed biography — mathematician, hedge-fund founder, billionaire philanthropist — can obscure an important early stage: from 1964 to 1968 he did classified cryptanalytic work at the Communications Research Division (CRD) of the Institute for Defense Analyses (IDA), in support of the National Security Agency.

That stage should remain visible when evaluating the institutions, technical training, state connections, colleagues, computing resources, and prior employment that preceded his extraordinary financial success. It should not, however, be expanded into a claim about his personal participation in surveillance of Americans without evidence connecting his own assignment to that activity.

## Career outline

- **1958:** mathematics degree, MIT.
- **1961:** Ph.D. in mathematics, University of California, Berkeley.
- **Early 1960s:** academic positions including MIT and Harvard.
- **1964:** joined the research staff of IDA's Communications Research Division in Princeton.
- **1964–1968:** worked on classified codebreaking and cryptographic problems while continuing his own mathematics research.
- **1968:** left or was dismissed from IDA after publicly opposing the Vietnam War and refusing to continue defense work while the war continued.
- **1968–1978:** chaired the mathematics department at Stony Brook University.
- **Later career:** moved into systematic trading, founded the firm that became Renaissance Technologies, accumulated enormous personal wealth, and later became a major philanthropist and funder of mathematics and science.

## Intelligence work: established record

The basic institutional and occupational facts are well supported:

1. A [2012 Simons Foundation account][sf-2012] says that Simons joined IDA/CRD's research staff in 1964, that the division focused on codebreaking, and that he devised algorithms for particular cryptographic problems.
2. A [2024 Simons Foundation account][sf-2024] describes him as a codebreaker for NSA while placing him institutionally at IDA.
3. In a [March 2019 MIT conversation][mit-event-2019], Simons described IDA as a highly classified workplace under NSA auspices that specialized in **"cracking Russian codes and protecting our own."** He said his own work included developing candidate codebreaking algorithms that programmers implemented and tested on computers. The [event transcript][mit-transcript-2019] is a third-party transcription; the official MIT page establishes the event and participants.
4. In a [2022 autobiographical talk][simons-2022-video], Simons again said that the assigned work was to **"break Russian codes."** A [searchable transcript][simons-2022-transcript] is available for inspection.
5. In the 2019 conversation, Simons said that an algorithm he developed at IDA solved a classified problem and led NSA to build a special-purpose machine that he later heard remained in use for decades. This supports the fact that **Simons made that retrospective claim**. It does not independently identify the algorithm, its target, its input data, or its operational use.

The most precise employment wording is therefore:

> Simons worked on classified cryptanalytic problems at IDA's Communications Research Division in support of NSA.

Calling him a **"codebreaker for NSA"** is supported biographical shorthand. Calling him a direct NSA employee is less precise than the documented IDA/CRD affiliation.

## SHAMROCK, the watch list, and MINARET

### SHAMROCK

The [Church Committee's NSA hearings, volume 5][church-vol-5], described SHAMROCK as a message-collection arrangement under which three international telegraph companies supplied international telegram traffic to NSA or its predecessors. It was not a collection of domestic-only telegrams: the hearings state that the participating companies had not operated domestic telegram services since at least 1963.

That does not make the program irrelevant to Americans. The same record says the supplied international traffic included U.S. citizens' messages. After magnetic-tape transfer began in the early 1960s, messages to, from, or mentioning U.S. citizens on watch lists could be selected for NSA analysts and then disseminated to other agencies.

SHAMROCK therefore **did overlap Simons's entire 1964–1968 IDA tenure**, and it did produce surveillance of Americans' international communications. But overlap is not assignment evidence. The hearings say that operational responsibility for the corporate acquisition arrangement was confined at any one time to the NSA director, deputy director, and one lower-level manager, while selected messages were sent onward to analysts. Nothing inspected here identifies Simons or IDA/CRD as part of either the acquisition arrangement or the downstream handling of selected SHAMROCK traffic.

### Watch-list activity before MINARET

The NSA watch list predated Simons's tenure. The [National Security Archive's document set][nsa-tracking] reports that it began in the early 1960s with U.S. citizens traveling to Cuba and later expanded. On **October 20, 1967**, the list began including anti-Vietnam-war and civil-rights activists after an Army intelligence request for information about possible foreign influence over domestic civil disturbances.

That expansion overlapped the final portion of Simons's IDA tenure. The overlap makes the 1967–1968 predecessor activity a legitimate research question. It does not establish his participation.

### Formal Project MINARET

The public chronology excludes formal MINARET from Simons's tenure. In the Church Committee hearing, NSA Director Lew Allen testified that the **MINARET term began in 1969** and formalized a message-handling process that had begun around 1967. The published charter is dated **July 1, 1969**. Simons had left IDA in 1968.

The correct open question is therefore not, "Did Simons work on formal Project MINARET?" It is narrower:

> Did Simons or IDA/CRD contribute in any way to the predecessor watch-list processing that operated during the final part of his tenure?

No inspectable source found in this review answers yes.

## Current supported formulation

> From 1964 to 1968, Jim Simons worked on classified cryptanalytic research at IDA's Communications Research Division in support of NSA. In later talks, he described the unit as specializing in "cracking Russian codes and protecting our own" and described his own role as developing candidate codebreaking algorithms. During his tenure, NSA also operated SHAMROCK and, beginning October 20, 1967, expanded watch-list processing involving antiwar and civil-rights activists. The inspected public sources do not connect Simons or CRD to either activity. Formal Project MINARET began in 1969, after Simons had left IDA.

The stronger sentence — **"Jim Simons spied on the American people"** — is not established by the current record. A supportable institutional statement is that he performed classified cryptanalytic work for an NSA-supporting research division during a period in which NSA operated programs that surveilled Americans.

## Bounded negative-source check

On **September 3, 2026**, this review checked the searchable OCR of the Church Committee's [NSA hearings, volume 5][church-vol-5], and [Book III of its final report][church-book-3], along with targeted searches for Simons, IDA, CRD, SHAMROCK, MINARET, and the watch list.

- No relevant occurrence tying **Jim Simons** to SHAMROCK, the watch list, or MINARET was found.
- No relevant occurrence tying **IDA's Communications Research Division** to those programs was found.
- The absence of a searchable hit is not exculpatory evidence. OCR can fail, the inspected public corpus is incomplete, and the search cannot rule out records outside that corpus or work described under unidentified project names.

This is a record of the present public evidence boundary, not a claim that no connection could have existed.

## Evidence that would justify an upgrade

A stronger personal-attribution claim would require at least one inspectable source such as:

1. an IDA or NSA assignment record naming Simons and the relevant program, target set, data source, or project number;
2. a technical report, memorandum, roster, or correspondence connecting Simons or CRD to SHAMROCK-derived traffic or U.S.-person watch-list processing;
3. a contemporaneous or credible first-person account identifying his own work with those activities;
4. documentation identifying the classified algorithm or special-purpose machine and showing that its operational use involved U.S.-person surveillance; or
5. a declassified audit, history, or investigation that places him in the relevant collection, selection, analysis, or dissemination chain.

Until then, keep **NSA/IDA cryptanalysis**, **agency-level surveillance of Americans**, and **Simons's personal tasking** as three distinct propositions.

## Blackball use

Do not use Simons as a generic earnings example for "a math degree." His outcome is not an ordinary occupational destination for mathematics graduates. Treat it as an extreme-case trajectory requiring decomposition of each enabling stage: elite education, classified technical employment, access to advanced computing and valuable problems, specialized colleagues, firm ownership, capital, survivorship, and an exceptionally rare outcome.

A useful comparison is to ask what a typical mathematics graduate receives at each corresponding stage: degree, first job, access to advanced computing and data, senior collaborators, ownership opportunity, capital, and exposure to unusually valuable technical problems.

## Remaining questions

1. Do declassified IDA/CRD task records from 1964–1968 identify Simons's projects or project numbers?
2. Can the algorithm and special-purpose NSA machine in his 2019 account be identified without speculation?
3. Did his work use raw intercepted traffic, derived cryptanalytic material, synthetic test data, or abstract problem statements?
4. Which collaborators or programmers worked on his classified algorithms, and did any leave inspectable oral histories or papers?
5. Did IDA/CRD receive any watch-list-selected material during 1967–1968, even if researchers were not told its collection source?
6. Which technical and organizational habits from CRD demonstrably carried into Renaissance, and which claimed continuities are retrospective storytelling?
