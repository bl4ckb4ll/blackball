# Alexandre Grothendieck — mathematical discovery, fear, and vanity

French mathematician and author of *Récoltes et Semailles* (*Harvests and Sowings*), a first-person reflection on mathematical creation, his past, and the mathematical community.

**Dossier scope:** This is a narrow source-grounded entry concerning Grothendieck's own account of the motivations of mathematical work. It is not yet a comprehensive mathematical biography or an adjudication of his conflicts with colleagues.

## First-person statement, 30 January 1986

In the preface “En guise d'avant-propos” he contrasts the experience of discovering something with the professional and personal motives that can surround scientific work.

- Scientists are not exempt from ambition, vanity, and rivalry; he specifically includes himself.
- Ambition does not, by itself, make a person discover a theorem or apprehend beauty.
- The original **“morsure de l'angoisse”** and **“pommade vaniteuse”** describe anguish and vanity used as an ointment to forget it. Roy Lisker translates them with **“sting of anguish”** and **“poultice of vanity”**.
- His hypothetical discoverer of fire is ordinary and fearful, not a demigod, although Grothendieck portrays the moment of discovery as free of fear and vanity.
- He warns that a heroic myth of science can conceal the human vulnerability that makes the myth attractive.
- He says outright that he has *not* reached the bottom of the problem of fear, vanity, and blocked creativity.

The passage introduces the images of the **Child / Worker** and the **Boss**, to be followed through the broader work.

## Blackball question

Grothendieck's passage helps distinguish **the intellectual worth of mathematics**, **the subjective experience of doing mathematics**, and **the status or material rewards of being recognized as a mathematician**. These can coexist, but evidence for one is not evidence for all three.

The aphorism “wanting to know” versus “wanting to be known as someone who knows” is a **later interpretive paraphrase**, not an exact Grothendieck quotation. Likewise, the proposition that prestige-seeking produces further anguish is an **open hypothesis**, not something demonstrated in this preface.

When using this dossier, avoid treating introspection as general survey evidence, and avoid romanticizing mathematical creativity as socially or economically detached.
