# Avital Ronell (1952–)

**Field:** comparative literature, German literature, literary theory, philosophy, deconstruction, feminist and queer theory, psychoanalysis, media and technology.

**University / institutional affiliation:** **New York University (NYU)**. NYU's current faculty directory lists Ronell as a professor in the German Department; recent 2026 publication and institutional records continue to identify her with NYU. She has also held the title University Professor of the Humanities and taught in German, English, and Comparative Literature. Earlier in her career she taught at the University of California, Berkeley.

**Status checked:** 2026-08-31.

## Why this dossier is here

Ronell's case is one of the clearest modern examples of the graduate-adviser power problem because the complainant, Nimrod Reitman, alleged that an internationally prominent dissertation adviser used an unusually intimate adviser relationship, constant demands on his time, sexualized conduct, and the threat of withholding academic cooperation to control him.

Unlike many public academic-misconduct controversies, this case includes a formal institutional finding: after an eleven-month Title IX investigation, **NYU found Ronell responsible for sexual harassment and suspended her for the 2018–19 academic year**. Ronell denied the allegations and disputed the interpretation of the relationship. Reitman then sued both Ronell and NYU.

The case also became a separate controversy about academic prestige because prominent scholars wrote to NYU in Ronell's defense before the investigation became public, explicitly emphasizing her intellectual standing and international reputation.

## Academic position

NYU records identify Ronell as a professor in German, with longstanding appointments in comparative literature and the humanities. Her work spans German and comparative literature, philosophy, psychoanalysis, deconstruction, feminist and queer theory, and media/technology studies.

## Nimrod Reitman's complaint to NYU

Reitman entered NYU's doctoral program in 2012 and worked with Ronell as his dissertation adviser. In 2017, after completing his doctorate, he filed a Title IX complaint with NYU's Office of Equal Opportunity.

Reitman alleged a multi-year pattern of unwanted sexualized and intimate conduct, including unwanted touching and kissing, demands for extensive personal contact and availability, and an adviser relationship in which he believed refusal could jeopardize Ronell's willingness to work with him and therefore his academic progress.

Ronell denied that she sexually harassed Reitman. Her public response characterized the highly affectionate language in their messages as exaggerated, playful, and “campy” communication between two adults with shared cultural and academic sensibilities. Her lawyers also emphasized portions of Reitman's communications that they argued were inconsistent with his later account.

The parties' descriptions of the relationship therefore diverged sharply.

## NYU's institutional finding

NYU investigated for eleven months. Reporting on the university's finding states that NYU found Ronell responsible for **sexual harassment, both verbal and physical**, concluding that the conduct was sufficiently pervasive to alter the terms and conditions of Reitman's learning environment. NYU suspended Ronell for the 2018–19 academic year.

Contemporary reporting also says NYU did **not** substantiate all of Reitman's accusations; accounts of the decision report that Ronell was cleared of other allegations including stalking, sexual assault, and retaliation. Downstream summaries should therefore preserve the narrower institutional finding rather than treating every allegation in the later lawsuit as having been proven by NYU.

## Reitman's lawsuit

On **16 August 2018**, Reitman sued Ronell and NYU in New York state court. His amended complaint asserted claims including gender discrimination, **quid pro quo sexual harassment**, hostile educational environment, and retaliation under the New York City Human Rights Law.

The quid-pro-quo allegation is important for retrieval because it is close to the pattern often remembered informally as “do this or I will not help your career.” The complaint alleged that Ronell used her control over doctoral supervision and advancement as leverage in the relationship.

A 2019 published court order establishes the procedural history and the nature of the claims. That order did **not** adjudicate Reitman's harassment claims on the merits; it granted Reitman's motion to dismiss a counterclaim Ronell had brought concerning a pre-suit tolling agreement.

Primary / court sources:

- Reitman's 2018 complaint: https://blog.simplejustice.us/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/FINAL-Complaint-Reitman-v.-Ronell-and-NYU.pdf
- **Reitman v. Ronell**, 2019 NY Slip Op 31578(U), New York Supreme Court, 31 May 2019, official court PDF: https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/pdfs/2019/2019_31578.pdf

The 2019 opinion is especially useful because it independently confirms the parties' academic relationship, the eleven-month NYU investigation, the filing date of the lawsuit, and the causes of action then pleaded. It should not be mistaken for a final judgment on whether the alleged harassment occurred.

## The faculty-support letter controversy

Before NYU's decision became public, a group of major scholars wrote in Ronell's defense. The signatories included prominent figures in feminist theory, philosophy, literature, history, and critical theory. The letter became controversial because it invoked Ronell's scholarly reputation and international standing while arguing against severe punishment.

That episode matters independently of whether one accepts Reitman's or Ronell's account. It is direct evidence of how academic reputation and professional networks can enter a disciplinary process. Several later commentators — including scholars sympathetic to Ronell's intellectual work — argued that the letter illustrated precisely the danger of allowing prestige to affect judgment about adviser-student conduct.

## Return to NYU

Ronell returned to NYU after the one-year suspension. Current NYU faculty information still lists her as a professor, and a 2026 journal publication gives New York University as her affiliation.

That fact is worth recording because the institutional sanction was substantial but temporary: the case did not end Ronell's NYU faculty affiliation.

## Blackball relevance

The case separates several propositions that are often blurred together:

1. Reitman made broad allegations of sexual harassment, sexual assault, stalking, control, and retaliation;
2. NYU conducted an eleven-month investigation and **did substantiate sexual harassment**;
3. NYU did not substantiate every allegation attributed to Reitman in public reporting;
4. Ronell denied harassment and offered a sharply different interpretation of their communications;
5. Reitman's civil suit included a quid-pro-quo theory tied directly to doctoral advising power;
6. the publicly available 2019 court decision is procedural and does not decide the merits of the harassment claims;
7. Ronell later returned to NYU and remains listed there in 2026.

For retrieval, the case is therefore useful not merely as a #MeToo controversy but as a concrete record of **how much power a dissertation adviser can exercise over time, attention, work, recommendations, and perceived career survival**.
