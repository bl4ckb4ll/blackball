# Shaoxiong "Dennis" Zheng

## Why Blackball is recording him

Shaoxiong "Dennis" Zheng belongs in the Hyde Park / University of Chicago case because his 2021 killing triggered a large university memorial and a visible public-safety response. His case helps separate personal grief from the institutional question of which deaths generate organized mourning, security expenditures, and policy attention.

## Verified biography

- Born in Sichuan Province, China, in 1997.
- Completed undergraduate study at the University of Hong Kong.
- Entered the University of Chicago master's program in statistics in fall 2019.
- Conducted research using machine learning to study gene regulatory networks.
- Received an S.M. in Statistics in June 2021.
- Wanted to work as a data scientist.
- **November 9, 2021:** shot and killed during a robbery in Hyde Park near his apartment building at East 54th Place and Ingleside Avenue. He was 24.

## Documented benefit from University of Chicago affiliation

Zheng's affiliation provided graduate training, faculty supervision, a research thesis, access to a statistics research community, and a credential aimed at entry into data-science work. Faculty and classmates described the department as an important intellectual and social community for him.

After his death, the affiliation also generated substantial institutional response: a memorial service at Rockefeller Memorial Chapel, University-wide communications, public-safety webinars, coordination with city officials and police, and announced security measures in Hyde Park and surrounding neighborhoods.

That response is not itself objectionable. It is a measurable institutional benefit of membership: the University had the capacity and incentive to mourn Zheng publicly and mobilize resources around the circumstances of his death.

## Research significance

The Hyde Park case asks whether affiliation affects what happens after violence. For each comparable homicide, Blackball should code:

- whether the victim was a student, alumnus, employee, contractor, resident, or unaffiliated neighbor;
- number and prominence of official statements;
- memorial events;
- named senior officials participating;
- police, shuttle, surveillance, or patrol changes;
- money committed to security or community programs;
- press coverage and biographical detail;
- language about future promise, scholarship, or social contribution.

## Questions for investigation

1. Which specific security measures followed Zheng's killing, and what did each cost?
2. Which measures were temporary versus permanent?
3. How far outside campus did the University's patrol and security footprint expand?
4. How did the response compare with UChicago's response to killings of non-affiliated residents in the same geography?
5. What employment outcomes did the UChicago statistics master's program advertise around 2019–2021?
6. What scholarships, assistantships, or family resources funded Zheng's education? Do not infer this from nationality or university attendance.
