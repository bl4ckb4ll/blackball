# University of Chicago

This folder treats the University of Chicago as an institution embedded in a specific labor market and neighborhood, not as an abstract producer of ideas.

The working question is: **what can be learned by putting the University's claims about markets, labor, poverty, human capital, public policy, merit, and safety next to the economic relations visible immediately outside its offices and classrooms?**

See also [Hyde Park](../Hyde%20Park/README.md) and [Harvard](../Harvard/README.md).

## Hyde Park / Woodlawn boundary

Sudhir Venkatesh arrived as a sociology graduate student in 1989. In the opening of *Gang Leader for a Day*, he describes Cottage Grove as a boundary between a manicured, privileged university environment and poor Black residents selling inexpensive labor and services, including changing oil and washing windows.

That observation should be linked to the people occupying authoritative positions inside the University rather than treated merely as colorful scenery in a sociology memoir.

See [Hyde Park economic authority](hyde-park-economic-authority.md) and [Sudhir Venkatesh](../people/sudhir-venkatesh.md).

## Relevant people and dates

### Milton Friedman

- M.A. in economics at Chicago, 1933.
- Joined the University faculty in **1946**.
- Remained a Chicago professor until **1977**.
- Produced major work on monetary economics, consumption, markets, regulation, public policy, and individual choice during these years.
- University accounts place the Friedman family in Hyde Park during the Chicago period.

Friedman does **not** overlap Venkatesh's 1989 observation in time. He is a prior generation of the same university-neighborhood relationship.

### Eugene Fama

- Entered the University of Chicago in **1960**.
- Joined the business-school faculty in **1963**.
- Apart from two years teaching in Belgium, spent his career at Chicago.
- Was therefore on the faculty when Venkatesh arrived in **1989** and during Venkatesh's subsequent Chicago research.

Fama is a clean chronological example of a globally influential theorist of markets working inside the university whose local economic boundary Venkatesh described.

### Barack Obama

- Moved to Chicago in **1985** as a community organizer and lived at 5429 S. Harper Avenue in Hyde Park while organizing farther south.
- Left for Harvard Law School in 1988.
- Returned to Chicago and taught constitutional law at the University of Chicago Law School from **1992 through 2004**.
- Lived in Hyde Park/East Hyde Park for much of this later period.

Obama connects three Blackball folders: [Hyde Park](../Hyde%20Park/README.md), the University of Chicago, and [Harvard](../Harvard/README.md).

### Sudhir Venkatesh

- Entered the Chicago sociology graduate program in **1989**.
- Conducted fieldwork in and around public housing and poor South Side neighborhoods.
- Became a [Harvard Society of Fellows Junior Fellow, 1996–1999](../Harvard/sudhir-venkatesh-junior-fellow-1996-1999.md).
- His later mass-market book branded the research career as that of a "rogue sociologist."

The institutional conversion path matters: poor residents and gang members supplied social access and information; the resulting research generated academic publications, appointments, prestige, and books.

## Violence, mourning, and socially legible futures

The 2021 killings of University affiliates in Hyde Park create another research surface.

- [Yiran Fan, January 9, 2021](../Hyde%20Park/2021-01-09-yiran-fan-and-the-language-of-potential.md) — Chicago Booth / Economics Ph.D. student killed in East Hyde Park. President Robert Zimmer explicitly relayed scholars' assessment of "just how much potential he had." The same spree killed Aisha Johnson, a doorwoman, and Anthony Faulkner, age 20, giving Blackball a same-event comparison for the language used to describe different lost futures.
- [Shaoxiong "Dennis" Zheng, November 9, 2021](../Hyde%20Park/2021-11-09-shaoxiong-zheng-and-security-response.md) — recent statistics master's graduate killed during a robbery in Hyde Park; followed by memorials, safety webinars, police coordination, and security measures.

The research question is not whether grief for these students was excessive. It is whether **institutional attachment makes a person's unrealized future more legible to organizations with the power to memorialize, publicize, protect, and act**.

## Research program: put theory beside the street

For prominent Chicago economists and social scientists, build parallel records containing:

1. dates actually living or working in Hyde Park;
2. campus office and residential geography where available;
3. major claims about labor, markets, poverty, human capital, incentives, regulation, crime, or public policy;
4. contemporaneous neighborhood wages, rents, unemployment, household income, informal work, segregation, and violent-crime data;
5. University employment of nearby residents, including occupation and wage;
6. University policing, patrol boundaries, security spending, and relations with surrounding neighborhoods;
7. explicit statements by scholars about Hyde Park, Woodlawn, the South Side, or the University's local economic role.

This is not an argument that local observation automatically refutes abstract theory. It is an empirical test of whether theories that claim wide applicability describe the economic world visible around the institution that produced them, and whether scholars acknowledged discrepancies when they did not.
