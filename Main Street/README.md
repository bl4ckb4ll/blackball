# Main Street

Main Street records ordinary, repeatable employment routes that exist in many towns and compares them directly with college, graduate school, professional school, trades, and self-employment.

The baseline is not “dream job versus failure.” It is the actual work a person can plausibly obtain, keep, and use to support a household.

For each employer or occupational route, collect:

- local pay and pay progression;
- full-time availability;
- schedule control and predictability;
- benefits and eligibility delays;
- physical demands and injury risk;
- hiring requirements;
- training actually supplied;
- promotion ladders and time to promotion;
- turnover;
- commute and geographic prevalence;
- compatibility with children and family life;
- whether a degree changes entry, pay, promotion, or ordinary task performance;
- whether entering four years earlier creates an experience advantage over a graduate who later enters the same workplace.

## Current case lead

- [Retail experience versus degree status](retail-experience-versus-degree-status.md) — a southeast Michigan manager reportedly supervised University of Michigan psychology graduates in retail and advanced through work experience into a corporate role while still regarding the graduates’ credentials as a form of superiority.

Main Street is a comparison baseline, not an attempt to romanticize low-wage or physically demanding work. The same scrutiny applies to pay, injury, arbitrary management, weak benefits, and limited advancement. The point is that an expensive education should have to beat a real alternative rather than an imaginary life with no work and no earnings.
