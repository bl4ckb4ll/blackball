## Reproducible first-pass counts

### Strict target

- Strict career/training mea culpa: **0 / 25**

### Control / near-miss categories

- Literal apology to a former graduate student for professor-caused harm, but **not** career-prospect harm: **1** (#16 Cohn).
- Especially strong first-person near-mea-culpas on training/career responsibility: **3** (#3 Harris, #9 Gray, #21 Sinno).
- Proposes, recommends, or enacts fewer doctoral entrants / program closure / recruitment hiatus because opportunities do not justify current scale: **8** (#3, #7, #8, #10, #15, #21, #24, #25).
- Clear responsibility resistance, deflection, or outcome-data opacity: **5** (#4, #12, #14, #19, #22).
- Clean “parents are to blame” example in the inspected corpus: **0**.

The categories above are intentionally narrower than “criticizes academia.”

## Additional quoted denial not counted as its own core item

Harris’s 2010 essay reports a colleague responding to the oversupply concern with: **“Sure, the market is bad, but our students always find jobs.”** Because the colleague is not identified in the article, this is preserved as a denial/example but is **not** counted as a separate person/source in the 25-item denominator.
