# Search log

Search date: **2026-09-03**

Purpose: locate retrospective first-person apologies by professors for recruiting, advising, training, or encouraging students into academic/scientific paths whose career prospects later proved materially worse than represented or reasonably implied.

## What counts as a hit

The strict target is not merely criticism of academia. A hit must combine:

- first-person responsibility;
- acknowledgement of wrongness or harm;
- student-side consequences; and
- an actual apology or unmistakable equivalent.

Near-misses are preserved rather than discarded.

## Chronicle of Higher Education

### Coverage

This was the most intensively searched publication in the first pass.

**53 domain-targeted Chronicle query strings** were issued, including:

- exact or near-exact apology language;
- culpability/responsibility language;
- overproduction/admissions language;
- warnings against graduate school;
- blame/deflection language;
- placement-data/transparency language;
- government/funding comparisons; and
- a separate **year-targeted query for every year from 2008 through 2026 inclusive**.

This is a domain-restricted indexed-web search, **not a complete database export of every Chronicle page**. Paywalls, indexing gaps, deleted pages, OCR problems, and unindexed newsletters can still hide material.

### Year-targeted sweep

For each year **2008, 2009, 2010, 2011, 2012, 2013, 2014, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026**, a query of the following form was run:

`site:chronicle.com YEAR professor graduate students PhD jobs "job market" responsibility`

The year sweep was supplemented with phrase searches because a single generic string is not enough to expose older column titles or paywalled pages.

## Real-apology control search

The broad sweep deliberately retained genuine professor/teacher apologies outside the target topic. This checks whether the search language can actually recover apology documents.

Examples recovered:

- Martin J. Cohn: says he wrote a former Ph.D. student a literal apology letter after realizing his mentoring drove her out of science.
- Patrick Hicks: **“Apology to my former student”** (2012).
- Dana Huff: “my ignorance caused harm ... I apologize to my former students” (2020).
- Teaching in Higher Ed: **“How to apologize [as a professor]”** (2015), explicitly prescribing “I apologize” / “I’m sorry,” naming the wrong, and changing behavior.

This reduces the likelihood that the zero strict result is merely an artifact of searching for language academics never use.

## Government/funding comparison search

A separate set of **15 targeted queries** looked for academic/scientific career harm attributed to Trump-administration science policies and funding cuts, across *The Chronicle*, *Nature*, *Science*, and *Inside Higher Ed*.

The search immediately surfaced numerous directly relevant pieces, especially in 2017 and 2025–2026. Representative examples:

- *Nature*, **“‘Shattered’: US scientists speak out about how Trump policies disrupted their careers”** (2026).
- *Nature*, **“Trump team’s science cuts threaten tenure hopes for early-career academics”** (2025).
- *Nature*, **“US universities curtail PhD admissions amid Trump science funding cuts”** (2025).
- *Nature*, **“#OpenToWork: how laid-off US scientists are coping with shattered careers”** (2025).
- *The Chronicle*, **“How Trump’s Cuts to Science Funding Are Derailing Young Scholars’ Careers”** (2025).

These are kept as a comparison set rather than coded as apologies.

## Parent-blame search

A smaller targeted search tested the user-suggested “parents are the problem” category across *The Chronicle*, *Inside Higher Ed*, Medium, and the open web.

The first pass **did not produce a clean professor-authored example blaming parents for the Ph.D./academic-employment mismatch**. Most hits instead concerned the difficulty of being a parent while in graduate school or academia. Therefore the current corpus count for this specific category is **0**, pending better evidence.

## Current limitations / retrieval queue

1. Search-engine indexing is not a complete archive.
2. Chronicle paywalls can expose enough text for classification in some cases but not every article in full.
3. Private letters and departmental emails may never have been published.
4. “Sorry” may appear in video/audio interviews that are poorly indexed.
5. Older faculty blogs may have disappeared or moved.
6. Discipline-specific newsletters and professional-society listserv archives remain undersearched.
7. Books/memoirs by senior professors remain undersearched.
8. The 1970s–2007 period has only been sampled, not year-swept. It may be especially valuable because the academic hiring contraction predates 2008 by decades.
9. A future pass should search professor memoirs, retirement interviews, oral histories, AAAS/APS/ACS/APA/MLA/AHA publications, and archived faculty web pages.

## Update rule

A found apology is not an inconvenience to the thesis. It is the object being sought. If one satisfies the four-part test, add it, quote it accurately, and change the zero count immediately.
