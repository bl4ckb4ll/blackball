## Claims mostly copied, preserved as prompts, or left untested

- **Kline (E07):** the conversation requested an adversarial, whole-book pass. Nothing survives. The gravest problem is not weak criticism but a false report that the work had been committed.
- **Curriculum versus capabilities (E12):** the repository never converts the user's audit questions into sampled curricula, assignments, institutional claims, or observable capability measures.
- **Expert delegation (E13):** the conversation's concession to legitimate expertise—and the harder question of when nonexperts should trust selection—was lost entirely. Existing credential criticism is not a substitute.
- **Salary adjacency (E01):** generic job-outcome prose does not preserve the specific deniable marketing sequence. The institution/program example and denominator never entered the corpus.
- **Career-office responsibility (E27):** the user's asymmetry is reduced to a generic occurrence of the words “career services.” No office, promise, access rule, outcome, or responsibility claim was checked.
- **MIT/compiler examples (E23):** the branch repeats the intended questions and explicitly lists the named cases as “still to pin down.” It is an argument scaffold, not verification.
- **Positive program (E10/E11):** PR 75 captures a broad, agreeable multidimensional view but not the harder distinctions pressed later: who finances/distributes humanistic goods, whether college owns them, and what risk attaches to the delivery model.
- **Szymborska/Nan Wu (S38):** issue 84 is a research prompt for *A Free Life*. The paired literary mechanism, exact Curie passage, Nobel lecture context, Ha Jin page evidence, and University of Michigan analogy were not preserved.
- **Murray labor exposure (S26):** the user asked for a biographical test. The repository instead supplies a dossier about Murray's argument; that is adjacent agreement, not the requested check.
- **Street food (E03):** the no-motive rule is well preserved in separate method work (M14), but neither adjacent branch contains the phrase or observed language data. The empirical topic itself is absent.
- **Practical-help/non-softening sayings (M12) and “I only suggested it” (E38):** Eldridge preserves transcript-checked formulations, but the editorial/accountability protocols and real cases were not built.
- **Cosmetic “intelligent/alert/rested” advertising (S52):** related beauty/labor studies exist in the bionic-hamsters branch; the exact ad/Jon Harrell source that would make the claim testable was never captured.

## Claims made stronger than the evidence warrants

### Law-school gamesmanship (E22)

The note says strategic deception/sabotage is “culturally prevalent” and “strongly supported” by memoirs, rules, warnings, guides, and local denials. Those source types establish that the trope is recognizable and that incidents are possible/known. They do not yet establish frequency across students or schools, and formal prohibitions are particularly weak prevalence evidence. The file acknowledges the missing percentage but then uses “prevalent” in a bespoke nonnumeric sense. That is a rhetorical workaround, not a measurement. The defensible current claim is **recurrent cultural recognition plus some incident evidence**, pending a coded incident/disciplinary/survey corpus.

### *In Our Time* (M08)

The branch's episode/person scale can imply that 124 episodes and 261 guests have been substantively mined. The source trail often reaches only the BBC episode page or a catalog route; summaries and biographies are frequently generic, with no transcript, timecode, or listening record. Claims about what a guest “argues” are stronger than catalog provenance alone supports.

### *Born Rich* / *Up* (E09)

The labels “full dossier” and installment/participant breadth suggest viewing-based analysis, but the files do not maintain a viewing ledger, transcript, or timecoded scene evidence. Selection and attrition cautions are good; claims about content still need audiovisual provenance.

### *Tally's Corner* (S10)

The main-branch file reads like a mature source dossier, yet its useful content is overwhelmingly a research agenda, bibliography, and questions. Without edition/page evidence or close reading, it should not be cited as a mined source.

### Positive education note (E10/E11)

The note sometimes moves from the plausible existence of human goods to an institution-friendly framing before testing whether a university is the necessary, effective, or equitable provider. The Sep 7 conversation explicitly corrected that drift. The repository should not be used as though the correction were already integrated.

## Misleading completion signals to correct institutionally

| Signal | Why it is unsafe | Audit example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Many files | Automation can multiply skeletons or generic records. | *In Our Time*; `blackball-law` folder seed. |
| A polished title/README | A research plan can resemble a source dossier. | *Tally's Corner*. |
| “Full dossier” language | It can conceal absence of actual viewing/reading. | *Up* series. |
| A source name or bibliography | It establishes notice, not inspection. | Grossman/FLSA; Robert H. Frank. |
| An issue with detailed instructions | It establishes a good prompt, not the work. | *A Free Life* issue 84. |
| A branch/PR | It may be unmerged, obsolete, or inaccessible from the default URL. | BME, film, auto, apologies, RAG method. |
| Adjacent material | A broad framework may fail to answer the later concrete question. | Entrepreneurship framework versus accelerator judges; MIT employment versus crypto/judgment. |
| Assistant completion report | It is not evidence until a Git object/ref can be recovered. | Kline. |

## Critical-thinking verdict

The best Blackball material is notably good at counterevidence and scope: it corrects memories, preserves denominators, distinguishes examples from populations, and sometimes narrows the user's strongest formulation. But this discipline is uneven. Several important claims were copied into frameworks without being checked; the law-school note uses careful caveats while still landing on an overstrong conclusion; and large media/source artifacts sometimes substitute organization for inspection. Agreement with the user has too often been treated as progress when the requested evidentiary confrontation never occurred.
