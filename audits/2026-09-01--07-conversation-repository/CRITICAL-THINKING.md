# Critical-thinking review

## Bottom line

Repository agreement with the user is not treated as validation. The strongest work in the period is strong precisely where it **narrows, complicates, or creates a way to falsify** the originating claim. The weakest work either copies the user's position, preserves only metadata, or turns a recognizable story into a prevalence-like conclusion.

## Claims that became stronger than the evidence warrants

### 1. Law-school sabotage: recognizability became “cultural prevalence”

The problem file is [`Erin Brockovich/law-school-competition-and-gamesmanship.md`](https://github.com/bl4ckb4ll/blackball/blob/a22babb8c9246d40130d373debfadab3bebb985a/Erin%20Brockovich/law-school-competition-and-gamesmanship.md) on open PR 82.

It says the evidence supports a “persistent, recognizable feature” and that “recurrence and cultural prevalence are already evidenced.” The cited classes include:

- a school's rule prohibiting hidden/damaged library material;
- schools/students saying their environment is **not** the hide-books type;
- admissions/profile material invoking the stereotype;
- commercial guidance and memoir leads, some not directly inspected;
- Granfield's broader gamesmanship interpretation.

This supports **recognizability** and a need to investigate. It may support recurrence after direct incidents are inspected. It does not establish prevalence in a defined population. A rule can respond to one incident, anticipation, or a generic professional norm. A denial can show that a story is culturally available without showing how often the behavior occurs. The “cultural prevalence” sentence should be narrowed until primary incidents and a denominator-bearing sample exist.

### 2. _In Our Time_: metadata verification became content narration

The open draft [PR 59](https://github.com/bl4ckb4ll/blackball/pull/59) carefully distinguishes `bbc-verified` and `cross-checked` rows, but those labels verify titles, dates, programme IDs, and guests. Topic files then assert what “the programme asks,” what “the guests distinguish,” or what a named guest “explains.” No per-episode transcript, timecode, listening receipt, or equivalent content provenance is attached.

The problem is not that every summary is necessarily false. The problem is that the repository currently makes a stronger claim about inspection than its evidence trail supports. Before adding later years, it should either:

- attach content-level inspection evidence; or
- relabel prose as programme-description-derived discovery metadata.

The 124-topic/261-person scale makes this especially important: a large tree can multiply an unsupported inference more efficiently than a small file.

### 3. Long files that look read but are only reading agendas

The merged [`economics/elliot-liebow-tallys-corner.md`](https://github.com/bl4ckb4ll/blackball/blob/3b193df9c10c5880e7a7616f6305dc230679881b/economics/elliot-liebow-tallys-corner.md) identifies the book, access routes, a chapter title, Blackball relevance, coding fields, and excellent questions. It contains no page-level evidence, quotation, or extracted job episode from the book and repeatedly defers close reading. It should not be represented as a mined source merely because it is long and merged.

The apprenticeship source ledger presents a milder version of the same risk. It is a valuable source map and comparison framework, but many entries explicitly say the article/book still must be inspected. That is **partial lane construction**, not completion of the source investigations.
