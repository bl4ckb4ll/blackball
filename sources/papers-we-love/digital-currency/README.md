## Evidentiary scope

These papers are primary or near-primary technical statements of proposed cryptocurrency systems. They are useful evidence for questions such as:

- What mechanism did a project claim to use?
- What problem did its authors say it solved?
- What assumptions did the design make about consensus, incentives, computation, or monetary issuance?

They are **not**, by themselves, evidence that a later exchange was solvent, that an investment was economically sound, that promoters behaved lawfully, or that a particular criminal defendant committed an offense.

## Later legal record

For the separate criminal record concerning FTX and Samuel Bankman-Fried, see:

- [United States v. Samuel Bankman-Fried](../../united-states-v-bankman-fried/README.md)

That corpus contains indictments, court orders, the appellate opinion, transcript links, and source provenance. The juxtaposition is useful: this directory preserves technical and intellectual claims associated with digital currency, while the court corpus records later adjudicated conduct in one major cryptocurrency business. Neither should be substituted for the other.
