# Professional inheritance — two sources, one unfinished lifetime question

**Originating question:** why should established professional families be able to move their children into security and wealth so much earlier than people without that support, and how long does everyone else wait, if they ever arrive?

This dossier connects the two studies without treating their different percentages as interchangeable evidence. It extends the [professional-class inheritance question](../../llm/questions/professional-class-career-inheritance.md).

## Do not average unlike statistics

The law report's **69% on printed/PDF page 20** concerns fathers working as managers or professionals. The medical study's headline concerns **parental household income**, not whether a parent is a doctor. Neither number is a measured percentage of careers obtained through nepotism. Neither estimates the share of all professionals who become wealthy.

Occupation, income, net worth, support and institutional access are related but distinct. A parent can have professional knowledge without owning much property. A student can receive housing from someone with modest current income. A wealthy household can supply no support to a particular child. A meaningful study records the transfer and the relationship rather than assuming them from one label.

The dates and selection stages also differ. A medical entrant survey begins before completion and practice; the law survey begins after professional admission. A comparison must not mistake differences in observation stage for differences between professions.

## The clock starts before qualification

A comparison restricted to years since first professional employment can erase the years spent trying to obtain it. Compare age, calendar conditions and years since entry separately.

Define a security condition before looking at outcomes. It might require adequate ongoing income, no unmanageable educational debt and a specified liquid reserve. Define a wealth threshold separately. A high annual salary, positive net worth and the ability to survive unemployment are not the same event.

Let **T** be the age at first reaching the chosen condition. The distribution

**F_group(age) = P(T ≤ age | family-background group)**

includes people who have not yet reached it. An average age among successful cases does not. For a bounded horizon τ, the integral

**∫₀^τ [1 − F_group(age)] d age**

is expected time before first reaching the condition within that horizon, with the clock's origin explicitly specified. A difference between groups measures years of delay in this defined sense, rather than merely contrasting the ages of winners.

First attainment does not measure lasting security. Someone may cross the threshold and later lose it. A second outcome should record the amount of time actually spent below the condition, or require sustained attainment for a prespecified period. Do not let one good salary year stand for permanent escape from insecurity.

Finite follow-up cannot distinguish every person who will never arrive from someone who arrives later. Record censoring and competing outcomes explicitly. “Never observed to reach” is honest; removing that person from the denominator is not.

## Waiting can cost more than elapsed time

A useful ledger records what happened during the wait: earnings forgone, tuition and fees, interest, ordinary paid work, dependents supported, housing costs, relocation, repeated applications, and assistance received. The same eventual salary can follow very different accumulated losses.

A family can also supply insurance without making a current payment. Distinguish a documented commitment to cover rent or an emergency from an actual transfer and from an observer's assumption that help would be forthcoming. Availability, amount, conditions and duration all matter. An unused guarantee can change a person's feasible choices, but its causal effect still requires evidence.

A person with no reserve may have to reject an unpaid opportunity that someone else can accept. That is a possible mechanism, not a finding measured by these two tables. An actual case needs the opportunity's terms, available alternatives and funding evidence. A narrative that says only that the second person was more committed would not establish the cause.

## A deliberately simple accounting example

Consider two hypothetical people with the same salary, expenses and ability to save $10,000 per year. At age 25, one receives $100,000 of assets. The other receives nothing and must first repay $50,000 of debt. Ignore interest, returns, taxes, inflation and all differences in later behavior.

After five years, the first has $150,000 and the second has just reached zero net worth. The salary comparison is equal; the balance-sheet comparison is not. Under these assumptions the $150,000 gap persists when both subsequently save at the same rate. The second does not automatically catch up just because a professional salary eventually arrives.

This is arithmetic under stated assumptions, not a claim about average professional families or a forecast. Its purpose is to show exactly what an earnings-only comparison omits. Add observed asset returns, transfers, household changes and liabilities only when data support them.

## Four distinct mechanisms to investigate

**Financial support:** paying costs or absorbing risk can change which pathways are feasible. Measure payments and commitments at their actual dates.

**Institutional preference:** an admission, hiring or assignment rule can favor an inherited relationship. Identify the rule, the decision and the affected alternatives. Do not infer an improper decision solely from an entrant's family background.

**Preparation shaped by resources:** support can affect earlier preparation and credentials. Controlling for credentials estimates a different question from the total association with family background. Do not automatically control away a candidate transmission mechanism and call the remainder the whole class effect.

**Ownership and succession:** clients, property, a practice or equity can be transferred independently of professional wages. A study of salaried entry may miss this channel completely.

These mechanisms can overlap. Their mere plausibility does not identify their size. Equally, a finding that one mechanism explains little would not establish that all inherited advantages are negligible.

## The normative question is not answered by parental affection

A parent's desire to protect a child does not establish a right to preferential treatment from institutions allocating opportunities to other people. Neither useful professional training nor hard work during that training proves that access was distributed justly.

Likewise, demonstrating that a particular professional is competent would not answer why someone else with adequate ability and fewer resources was excluded. Competence and the distribution of opportunities to develop and exercise it are separate questions.

The empirical task is to identify rules, resources, decisions and effects. The normative task is to state what distribution is being defended and why. Neither task should be replaced by a general reassurance about families wanting the best for their children.

## What would count as progress

The next evidence should connect family resources to actual transitions and elapsed years, include people left outside the profession, and record both earnings and balance sheets at common ages. A result can identify a barrier, quantify a delay, distinguish mechanisms or demonstrate that an expected explanation fails.

These two dossiers do not supply a universal number of years for everyone else. They establish bounded evidence about selected professional pathways and preserve the measurements needed to ask the larger question without making unsuccessful people disappear.
