## Retrieval rule

Keep four evidentiary categories distinct:

1. **contemporaneous praise or criticism** — evidence about what people publicly believed or promoted at the time;
2. **party allegations** — evidence about what HP, Lynch, prosecutors, or defense lawyers claimed, not automatically about what happened;
3. **judicial findings and jury verdicts** — evidence with the scope and standard of the particular proceeding attached;
4. **later retrospective narrative** — useful for studying reputation and institutional memory, but not a substitute for contemporary records.

The central research question is not “was Mike Lynch good or bad?” It is how a Cambridge technical researcher became a heavily validated public model of British high-technology success; what institutional and media machinery reinforced that model; how the HP dispute changed it; and how later courts produced a legally split record in which English civil findings favored HP-related claimants while an American criminal jury acquitted Lynch on every count submitted to it.

## Mirroring policy

This collection prefers official court and government copies. Where a public document is currently available only through a third-party docket mirror, the record says so explicitly.

Binary PDFs have **not** been copied into this repository in this pass. The GitHub connector used for this change is suitable for text files but not a sensible path for bulk binary mirroring. The court-document inventory identifies candidates for later local mirroring after provenance, redistribution rights, and file integrity are checked.

Wikipedia text has likewise not been silently copied. Wikipedia material is reusable under its Creative Commons license, but a compliant mirror needs attribution, revision provenance, and share-alike handling. The Wikipedia note records dated snapshots and known revision markers instead.
