# Estreicher and Radice — *Beyond Elite Law*

Samuel Estreicher and Joy Radice’s edited volume *Beyond Elite Law: Access to Civil Justice in America* is a direct source for the distance between elite legal institutions and ordinary people who need routine representation or help navigating bureaucracy.

## Core problem

The book’s introductory material describes the United States as operating an elite legal system largely for elites. Law schools produce graduates for major firms serving corporate clients and for a thin layer of prestigious nonprofit or impact work, while many ordinary people cannot afford counsel and do not qualify for the limited supply of publicly supported legal aid.

This belongs directly beside the Blackball question:

**What is a legal right worth to a person who cannot afford the lawyer and procedure required to invoke it?**

## Why it matters for legal education

The legal profession can simultaneously contain:

- highly refined constitutional and jurisprudential scholarship;
- expensive firms serving corporations and wealthy clients;
- prestigious public-interest organizations able to hire only a small number of graduates;
- overburdened public defenders and legal-aid offices;
- solo and small-firm lawyers who may receive little preparation for ordinary clients;
- large numbers of people handling eviction, debt, family, benefits, probate, disability, and employment matters without counsel.

Counting the first two worlds as evidence of a successful legal system can conceal the population that cannot use the system at all.

## Blackball uses

Use this source to investigate:

- the “justice gap” for working and middle-income people;
- the population too wealthy for legal aid but unable to pay ordinary lawyer rates;
- mismatch between legal-education prestige and preparation for routine practice;
- whether bar referral and modest-means programs produce actual representation;
- technology and nonlawyer assistance;
- public funding and legal-aid capacity;
- the supply of lawyers relative to the distribution of paying clients;
- why graduating more lawyers does not automatically produce affordable legal service.

Do not infer that every proposed reform in the volume works. The Washtenaw County case should test referral and reduced-fee mechanisms at the point of use.
