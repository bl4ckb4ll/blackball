# Journal of Applied Measurement — recent publisher abstracts, 2025–2026

Status: **title/abstract survey only**, accessed 2026-10-16. Source: [publisher's issues and accepted-abstract index](https://jamntnu.net/issues.html). The journal mixes ordinary psychometric applications, algorithmic methods, validity evaluations, and published empirical comparisons. "Newly accepted" is not equivalent to "published in a numbered issue". No full manuscripts were examined here.

## Purpose of selecting these papers

This is a **purposive sample of competing cases**, not a random journal audit. Include apparent weaknesses, genuine attempts at independent checking, and work that directly complicates the presumption that the entire community requires a single general factor. The absence of full papers is a strict limit.

### Rense Lange, Haniza Yon, Labiib Marzuki — "Semantic Factor Analysis using LLM Transformers: No Respondents Needed"

Accepted **17 April 2025**. Compares MiniLM and distilBERT similarity scores on the *text of 50 Big Five personality questionnaire items*, without giving the questions to participants. The publisher abstract says MiniLM recovered the five grouped factors under a five-factor extraction and rotation; at ten factors only two of five factor groupings matched the traditional structure. The authors themselves conjecture that factor patterns may reflect **shared language practices rather than psychological reality**. [Publisher abstract](https://jamntnu.net/issues.html).

**Significance:** This is not empirical measurement of latent personality from human responses; it is a demonstration that item semantics may mimic psychological factor structure. The paper explicitly raises a version of Blackball's ontological question. To verify: replicate on holdout surveys and semantic counterfactuals; compare predicted clustering with genuinely behavior-predictive attributes. Do not infer correctness of factors from the title or the abstract.

### Fatima T. Zahra and Sabiha Sultan — "Measuring Rohingya Children's Happiness Through Fathers' Eyes: Revisiting Validity With a Culturally Responsive Lens"

Accepted **22 September 2026**. Publisher abstract: 392 fathers of children aged 2–6; 14 translated binary-response items, 11 initially met prespecified mean-square fit limits, final selected short form had 9 items; checks dimensionality, education/employment-based differential item functioning, and reports person reliability 0.71 for short form. [Publisher abstract](https://jamntnu.net/issues.html).

**Significance:** Direct validity question: the observed variable is *fathers' questionnaire responses*. How much independent observation connects those responses to **the young children's happiness**, rather than fathers' assessments of it? Item deletion may improve statistical fit while changing interpretation. Study mentions cognitive interviews; obtain protocol and validation evidence before judging whether those address the gap.

### Bunyong Dejanipont and Mark Wilson — "Graphical Framework Using Item Response Modeling to Detect Nonuniform Differential Item Functioning"

Accepted **7 January 2026**. According to the abstract, a graphical method flags more nonuniform differential item functioning than one index/test-statistic comparison. [Publisher abstract](https://jamntnu.net/issues.html).

**Significance:** Counterexample to the claim JAM never tests weaknesses of measurement procedures; independent simulation and empirical verification still needed.

### Putcharee Junpeng and Mark Wilson — "The Efficiency of Integrating Multidimensional Rasch Analysis with Machine Learning Algorithms to Predict Mathematical Proficiency Waypoints in Probability and Statistics"

Accepted **4 December 2025**. Publisher abstract describes a **two-dimensional** mathematical assessment in 495 Thai seventh-graders and predictions made with decision trees. [Publisher abstract](https://jamntnu.net/issues.html).

**Significance:** This directly refutes the blanket assertion that all work under the name Rasch insists every competence reduces to one latent axis; it does not prove that either of the two dimensions is a real independent quantity.

### George Engelhard — "Using Explanatory Rasch Models to Measure Attitudes toward Censorship"

Accepted **13 June 2025**. Reuses a historical attitude scale and models item valence, respondent gender, and education in 950 participants. Abstract says education predicts responses; item valence and gender did not. [Publisher abstract](https://jamntnu.net/issues.html).

**Significance:** A good case for deciding whether the model discovers explanatory, independently meaningful new facts, or relabels well-known response patterns as an interval-scale "attitude" without further warrant. Requires full paper before judgment.

### Mark Wilson et al. — "Relating Selected Response to Constructed Response Items: Systematic Effects of Item Format"

Accepted **12 May 2025**. Publisher abstract says 741 secondary-school students randomly assigned to selected-response or constructed-response conditions; finds **correlated but different** psychometric dimensions, with the written-response condition harder. [Publisher abstract](https://jamntnu.net/issues.html).

**Significance:** Item *format* itself can change measured outcomes substantially. The experiment creates external variation, which is substantially stronger than an isolated fit score; implications for construct interpretation still require the full article.

## Audit flags

- A named human construct (children's happiness, attitudes, competence) does not become directly observed by measuring agreement with a set of statements.
- A latent factor that can be reconstructed from question wording without respondents raises the distinction between **semantic composition** and **behavioral variation**.
- The presence of independent data collection, simulations, and randomly assigned response conditions counts against an indiscriminate claim that every published result is empty. What those designs actually establish remains to be evaluated.
- Most abstracts do not supply enough details to test item deletions, model-selection chronology, holdout checks, or out-of-domain stability.
