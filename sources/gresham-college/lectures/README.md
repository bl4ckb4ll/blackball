# Gresham College lecture corpus

This directory is a Blackball source index, not a syllabus and not a claim that Gresham College is a degree-granting college.

Gresham states the institutional boundary unusually clearly: it sets no exams, awards no qualifications, and has no students. Its central activity is providing free public lectures. Blackball should therefore treat it as a public lecture publisher, venue, archive, and speaker network—not as evidence about enrolled students, a completed curriculum, degrees, admissions, tuition, or graduate outcomes.

## Canonical institutional sources

- [About Gresham College](https://www.gresham.ac.uk/about-us) — mission, history, and institutional description.
- [What is Gresham College and What Makes it Important?](https://www.gresham.ac.uk/watch-now/what-gresham-college-and-what-makes-it-important) — explicit statement that Gresham has no students, exams, or qualifications.

## Lecture-record schema

When an individual lecture is promoted from this discovery index into Blackball evidence, preserve at least:

- exact title and stable Gresham URL;
- speaker name and speaker-page URL;
- lecture date;
- podcast or video publication date, separately;
- venue when stated;
- Gresham professorship, visiting role, or other affiliation at the time;
- lecture series and Gresham topic tags;
- abstract or page description;
- transcript/text URL and format;
- audio and video URLs;
- downloads and extra-reading links;
- upstream books, papers, archives, datasets, cases, statutes, or other sources named in the lecture;
- claims selected for retrieval, with timestamps or transcript locations;
- limitations, disputes, and later corrections.

Suggested local path for a promoted record:

`subjects/<subject>/<speaker>--<lecture-slug>--<lecture-date>/README.md`

Do not use the podcast release date as though it were the lecture date. Gresham sometimes publishes audio weeks or months after the event.

## Attribution and evidence rules

- A lecture is primarily evidence for what its named speaker argued or presented, not automatically for what Gresham College believes.
- Preserve the difference between the speaker's own analysis, a quoted primary source, a cited researcher, an institutional statistic, and an anecdote.
- A Gresham topic tag is discovery metadata; it does not settle the discipline, quality, or scope of a claim.
- A transcript is useful for retrieval, but exact quotations should be checked against the lecture page and, where consequential, the recording.
- A speaker's title or institutional affiliation does not replace inspection of the evidence offered in the lecture.
- A public lecture can be an excellent explanation while still being downstream of the paper, book, legal decision, dataset, or archival source that should carry the claim.

## Blackball retrieval priorities

Gresham is especially useful for finding public-facing treatments of:

- labor, automation, unemployment, education, wealth, inequality, and professional institutions;
- London, British political and legal history, medicine, mathematics, religion, music, and the history of science;
- how scholars translate specialist work for a general audience;
- the institutional contrast between openly serving an audience and claiming to serve enrolled students.

Promote individual lectures only after inspection. A title, archive tag, search result, or podcast description is discovery evidence, not a substitute for the lecture and its cited sources.

## Completeness and rights boundary

This index points to a changing external corpus. On 2026-09-03 Gresham described its website as containing more than 3,500 videos, while the podcast page reported 3,020 episodes. The website, video collection, transcripts, and podcast are overlapping but non-identical publication surfaces.

Do not claim that this repository locally contains every lecture. Preserve links and metadata, and store snapshots only where licensing and repository policy permit. Do not mirror full copyrighted audio, video, or transcripts merely because they are free to access.
