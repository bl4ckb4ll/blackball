# Medical-school entrants and parental income — critical annotations

[Source identity and archive status](README.md) · [Published aggregate counts](published-counts.tsv) · [Law/medicine comparison](../../professional-inheritance/README.md)

**Primary source throughout:** Shahriar et al., [publisher full text](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2790107), DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2022.2621, published March 15, 2022, CC BY. Section names below identify locations in that article. The rendered figure and table were inspected alongside the HTML. The supplementary methods have not been inspected; that gap is recorded rather than filled from conventional practice. Calculations and proposed analyses below are Blackball's, not additional findings claimed by the authors.

The central question is **who receives a viable route into a well-paid profession, who must finance an uncertain attempt, and how many years of security are transferred with family support**. Neither a medical-school place nor a physician's eventual salary is itself a measure of wealth.

<a id="identity"></a>
## 1. Title, authorship and the object of measurement

**Source location:** title, citation and author affiliations.

This is a study of the socioeconomic composition of entrants, not a study of the causal return to medical training. Its unit on the student side is a matriculant reporting parental household income. That distinction should survive every later quotation of the headline result.

Three possible questions must not be merged: whether wealthy households supply a disproportionate share of entrants; whether having a physician parent changes admission chances; and whether medical education makes its purchaser wealthy. The paper directly measures the first through income distributions. Answering the second requires parental occupation and a population at risk of admission. Answering the third requires costs and later outcomes, including unsuccessful educational purchasers.

The title's cohort dates are substantive. A 2022 publication about 2017–2019 entry does not measure 2022 entry, much less current admission rules. Its date of discovery in Blackball is not a new observation. Likewise, a university affiliation identifies an author's institution; it adds no independent datapoint to the survey.

**Editorial test:** any answer saying “doctors come from…” must disclose that this source actually sampled entering medical students. Completion, residency, practice, ownership, and retirement assets remain downstream.

<a id="introduction"></a>
## 2. Introduction — class is not supplied by another diversity measure

**Source location:** Introduction.

The authors distinguish socioeconomic representation from more frequently discussed racial, ethnic and gender representation. For Blackball the important analytical move is to measure family resources directly rather than assume that another demographic label already captures them.

A profession could recruit a demographically varied group whose members mostly had financial support. It could also increase lower-income participation without resolving a particular racial exclusion. These are logically different distributions. Neither possibility makes the other dimension irrelevant. Cross-classification is needed precisely because one aggregate can conceal another.

The paper opens with a claim about workforce diversity and health-care quality supported by a reference. That is not an outcome tested by this study. Preserve the difference between the reason offered for conducting research and the measurements the research supplies. An argument about fair access need not depend exclusively on a demonstrated benefit to patients: who is permitted to obtain training and economic security is also a distributive question in its own right.

**Mechanisms to distinguish:** financial capacity to prepare; ability to apply widely; information about admission; affordability of attendance; and ability to survive a failed application. A list of mechanisms is not evidence about their respective sizes. The introduction does not identify which one accounts for a particular income gradient.

<a id="populations"></a>
## 3. Methods — the two populations and the missing earlier stages

**Source location:** Methods, first two paragraphs.

Students were included from the AAMC Matriculating Student Questionnaire for 2017–2019 if they reported parental income. The study concerns allopathic entrants. Its comparison households came from CPS-ASEC responses in 2016–2018. These are different sampling systems and different units of observation.

All US households are not identical to the households containing children of the relevant age. Household age, size, number of children, and educational eligibility can affect a comparison even before admissions occurs. Thus the national household baseline gives a meaningful descriptive representation measure, but it is not automatically the denominator for an individual child's admission probability.

A stronger access study would follow a defined birth or school cohort through interest, preparation, application, acceptance, enrollment and completion. The current entrant survey begins after several of those transitions. It cannot reveal, by itself, how many low-income people were deterred before applying or how many admitted applicants could not afford to enroll.

Do not respond to this limitation by saying the observed entrants are therefore uninformative. The source answers a bounded question about who arrived. The correct next step is to keep that finding and obtain the missing transition denominators, not to pretend either that everything has been established or that nothing has been learned.

**Comparison required:** entrants from a background divided by all relevant people from that background; applicants from a background divided by those who had considered the profession; and enrollment conditional on receiving an offer. Those fractions identify different points at which opportunity may be lost.

<a id="representation"></a>
## 4. Methods — representation index is not admission odds

**Source location:** Methods, representation-index definition; Table, RI rows.

Write the index as:

RI(income bin, group) = share of that group's students in the income bin ÷ share of that group's comparison households in the same bin.

Both numerator and denominator must retain the same group definition and national income boundaries. An RI above one indicates overrepresentation in the observed composition. It does not directly state the probability of admission after application.

The general reversal is:

P(background | entrant) ≠ P(entrant | background).

Bayes' rule can connect them only with the appropriate background population and overall entry probability. Substituting all households for age-eligible applicants cannot be repaired merely by writing the formula.

The top 5% and bottom 20% bins also have unequal national sizes. In the displayed student counts, 7,302 ÷ 1,844 is about 3.96. That is the number of observed top-five-percent students per observed bottom-quintile student. Adjusting for the nominal widths gives about 15.84 as a relative representation contrast. Neither is a demonstrated applicant-admission odds ratio.

**Why it matters:** a striking inequality does not need a stronger label than its data justify. Overstating it creates an avoidable error while adding nothing to the actual finding of highly unequal social origins.

<a id="testing"></a>
## 5. Methods — significance, effect size and uninspected details

**Source location:** final Methods paragraph; Supplement pointer.

The authors report chi-square testing and a .05 threshold. They describe P values as one-sided. A chi-square goodness-of-fit or independence statistic conventionally uses an upper-tail rejection region; that wording alone does not establish that an unjustified directional hypothesis was imposed. The precise test construction belongs in the uninspected supplemental methods.

A small P value does not measure the size of inequality, the probability that a causal explanation is true, or the moral importance of the difference. For this question, the actual distributions and denominators are more useful than a binary significant/not-significant label.

Reproducing tests requires the weighting scheme, population estimates, handling of pooled survey years, effective sample sizes and dependence assumptions. Rounded published counts are not a substitute for those details. Do not manufacture narrow confidence intervals by treating every reported household or student as an independent unweighted draw.

AAPOR reporting and the institutional determination concerning human-participants review describe reporting and ethical processes. Neither guarantees complete response, accurate income reports or a valid causal design. Conversely, the absence of an experiment is not a defect in a study whose stated objective is descriptive composition.

**Acceptance boundary:** Blackball has checked arithmetic in a published table. It has not replicated the authors' survey analysis or inferential tests.

<a id="response"></a>
## 6. Results — questionnaire response and income completion are separate

**Source location:** first two Results paragraphs.

Of 44,903 questionnaire respondents, 30,373 supplied parental income. The latter group is 67.6% of respondents and, according to the authors, 46.4% of all matriculants. The questionnaire's reported response rates of 65–71% concern another stage. Do not interchange these three coverage measures.

There are 14,530 questionnaire respondents outside the income analysis. Beginning in 2018, the survey permitted an unknown-income response. Pooling years can therefore combine a changed reporting interface with any actual change in social composition. A year difference in the included sample is not automatically an admissions trend.

Similarity of included and excluded respondents on observed characteristics cannot establish similarity on an unavailable income measure. It is evidence to report, not a universal missing-at-random certificate. The authors also lacked characteristics of people who did not answer the questionnaire at all.

A serious sensitivity analysis distinguishes item nonresponse from whole-survey nonresponse, and distinguishes those from people who never matriculated. Weighting a survey cannot recover never-matriculants without external information about them.

**Preserve both sides of the evidence:** uncertainty about exact population percentages is real; it does not follow that every imaginable population composition is compatible with the known counts. Section 13 calculates conservative bounds rather than using missingness as a rhetorical eraser.

<a id="overall"></a>
## 7. Results — the national distribution and a rounding discrepancy

**Source location:** first Results paragraph; Table, all-student row.

The article reports 50.5% of included students from the highest-income quintile and 24.0% from the top 5%. The table contains 7,302 students in the top 5% and 8,061 in the next 15%, out of 30,373. Direct addition and division give:

(7,302 + 8,061) ÷ 30,373 × 100 = 50.5811%,

which rounds to 50.6% at one decimal place. The separately rounded component percentages, 24.0 and 26.5, sum to the published 50.5. This is a plausible rounding explanation, not an author-confirmed correction. Store the prose value and the calculation separately.

The bottom-quintile count is 1,844, or about 6.0712%. The substantive contrast is not affected by a tenth-of-a-point rounding difference at the top. Precision auditing should make the evidence easier to use, not inflate a small discrepancy into a rejection of the study.

The three lowest income bins contain 1,844 + 2,578 + 3,263 = 7,685 students. Their combined share is about 25.30%, compared with 60% of the national household distribution by construction. This derived description shows that the issue is broader than the extreme bottom bin. It still does not provide a causal decomposition into applications, selection or ability to pay.

**Class interpretation:** the source documents concentration among entrants. It cannot tell whether a particular entrant received money, guarantees, information or a job introduction. Those mechanisms require additional measurements; their absence from the table must not be filled by biography-by-stereotype.

<a id="figure"></a>
## 8. Figure — compare each bar with the correct baseline

**Source location:** Figure, inspected in its rendered form.

The figure places household and medical-student income distributions beside one another for the overall population and each major racial/ethnic group. Every stacked bar totals 100%. It displays composition, not the absolute number of students or households.

The same national income cutoffs apply across groups. Accordingly, 20% of every racial group's households do not necessarily fall in the national top quintile. The within-group household bar is essential. Comparing a student bar to an imagined set of five equal slices would erase the different baseline income distributions.

The legend orders quintiles from high income to low income, whereas many readers expect the first quintile to mean the poorest fifth. Retain numeric percentile labels when redrawing; do not rely on an ordinal name such as first quintile. The split between the top 5% and the next 15% must remain explicit.

Stacked bars can make it harder to compare interior segments that lack a common baseline. The table is needed for exact values. A later Blackball visualization should keep the same denominators and offer either aligned differences or the RI alongside percentages. Any new chart must be labeled as a redraw of this historical cohort rather than an updated measurement.

**Reading result:** enrichment of high-income origins occurs within demographic groups as well as in the overall composition. That is not an argument to ignore demographic exclusion; it establishes why class should not disappear inside an aggregate diversity label.

<a id="table"></a>
## 9. Table — same question within four groups

**Source location:** Table, broad racial/ethnic rows and their household comparators.

The published top-five-percent RI values are 2.30 for non-Hispanic Asian students, 5.29 for non-Hispanic Black students, 6.56 for Hispanic students, and 4.85 for non-Hispanic White students. These are comparisons with the corresponding group's households, not a ranking of individual resources or a causal ranking of discrimination.

For example, a group with fewer high-income entrants in absolute terms can nevertheless have a larger representation ratio if high-income households are especially uncommon in its baseline population. “Smaller affluent share” and “larger relative enrichment” can both be true. Report both the fraction and its comparator rather than choosing whichever produces the preferred impression.

The four student groups sum to 28,142, not 30,373. Their displayed distributions are not an exhaustive decomposition of all respondents. Do not assign the remaining 2,231 people to a category inferred from arithmetic alone; the questionnaire definitions and detailed methods determine their classification.

The companion TSV records the displayed HTML counts without silently reconciling them. For example, the Asian household cells sum to one fewer than the displayed total, while the White household cells sum to one more. These tiny discrepancies could reflect rounding or another table-production step, but the mechanism has not been verified. They are provenance flags, not an excuse to alter source cells.

The strongest substantive use is to ask, within each group, which income origins are absent or uncommon among entrants. An account that substitutes a successful professional from a group for all people sharing that group will omit this within-group economic selection.

<a id="subgroups"></a>
## 10. Table — sex rows and Asian disaggregation

**Source location:** sex rows and lower, disaggregated portion of the Table.

The all-student men and women rows sum to 30,363, ten short of the overall count. The displayed table does not establish how those ten responses were classified. Do not invent a missing, nonbinary or other category and present it as the authors' coding. Keep the residual explicit.

The disaggregated Asian rows show why a broad label cannot substitute for a resource measure. For example, the reported highest-quintile shares differ substantially between Indian- and Vietnamese-identifying students. This is a statement about selected entrants in this study, not an inherent property of an ethnicity or a prediction about someone encountered in ordinary life.

Several subgroups are small. In a row with 33 students, moving one observation changes the percentage by about 3.03 points. Displaying a decimal place does not make that subgroup estimate comparably stable to a row with thousands. Sampling uncertainty, selection and classification must be distinguished from formatting precision.

The row printed “Other East Asian” appears beneath the Southeast Asian heading; its footnote names Indonesian, Cambodian and Laotian identifications. Preserve that exact display label in transcription and flag the inconsistency. Do not silently repair it to a guessed intended label, and do not reproduce its heading as a geographical assertion without the footnote.

**Useful next analysis:** compare resource distributions within categories and ask how aggregation changes the overall picture. More detailed labels are useful only when the observations and uncertainty support the distinctions; disaggregation should not create spurious certainty about very small groups.

<a id="footnotes"></a>
## 11. Table footnotes — classification rules are part of the data

**Source location:** notes a–e beneath the rendered Table.

The income thresholds are year-specific dollar amounts. The footnote says prior-year income limits were used to estimate prior-year parental income, and gives the example that the 2019 questionnaire represents 2018 parental income. A later analyst must preserve this timing rather than align series by the questionnaire year alone.

The national 80th-percentile boundaries shown for 2017, 2018 and 2019 are $121,018, $126,253 and $130,000. A fixed-dollar cutoff pooled across years would answer a different question from a year-specific percentile classification. Neither is interchangeable with net worth.

Income is combined gross parental income, not necessarily spendable support delivered to the student. Taxes, dependents, debt, regional costs and the willingness or ability to transfer money remain separate. Conversely, a wealthy household with low current income could be mischaracterized by income alone. This is a reason to add balance-sheet variables, not a reason to discard the income measure.

Race and ethnicity derive from self-identification in two different instruments. The footnotes describe mutually exclusive broad categories and explain the treatment of Hispanic identification. Exact mapping choices should be carried with any reuse, especially when subgroup labels differ between survey systems.

The remaining footnotes distinguish specific identifications, multiple East Asian identifications and respondents choosing an other category without a more specific ethnicity. A category is an operational definition used for analysis; it is not a complete account of the person.

**Data rule:** a clean rectangular TSV cannot contain the entire measurement model. Its source, coding notes, timing and unresolved display discrepancies must remain linked beside it.

<a id="discussion"></a>
## 12. Discussion — explanation and proposed remedies are not tested effects

**Source location:** Discussion before the limitations paragraph.

The authors interpret the distributions as evidence that low-income access deserves explicit attention. They discuss historical and contemporary forces, including structural racism, and propose socioeconomic context in admissions, upstream investment and financial monitoring of matriculants.

This dataset does not experimentally compare those interventions. A contextualized score, financial subsidy or pipeline program might affect different transitions and different populations. To evaluate one, specify who receives it, which decision changes, what outcome is measured, how much it costs and who remains excluded.

The proposed application measures also raise a distinct institutional question: does a form merely record disadvantage, or does recording it actually alter access? A field on an application is not evidence that applicants receive money, offers or protection against loss. Public commitment, implementation and measured effect require separate records.

The authors cite earlier work for the proposition that low socioeconomic status affects whether an interested student applies or gains admission. That is a lead to a different study, not a transition probability measured here. Similarly, claims about financing plans draw on another paper. Preserve the citation chain instead of attributing every statement in a discussion to the present dataset.

**Blackball's normative question is additional:** even a reliable route for already-supported families would not establish a social entitlement to preferential opportunity. Explaining how a benefit is transmitted does not justify the rule that makes it transmissible. Conversely, the desirability of a reform does not demonstrate its effectiveness.

<a id="limitations"></a>
## 13. Limitations — quantify what nonresponse could change

**Source location:** final Discussion paragraph. The following bounds are original calculations, not published estimates.

Self-reported income may be inaccurate, and errors could differ across groups. The paper does not validate income against tax records. The analysis below therefore assumes that reported income categories are correct; it addresses missing observations, not misclassification, inappropriate comparison populations or every other possible bias.

Among the 44,903 questionnaire respondents, 15,363 known-income students are in the top quintile and 14,530 lack income information. Assigning every missing-income respondent outside the top quintile yields 15,363 ÷ 44,903 = 34.21%. Assigning every one inside it yields 29,893 ÷ 44,903 = 66.57%. These are worst-case bounds for questionnaire respondents, not for all matriculants.

For all matriculants, use the reported coverage c ≈ 0.464 and the observed top-quintile share p = 15,363 ÷ 30,373. Under unrestricted missingness, the full-population share must lie between c × p and c × p + 1 − c. Numerically this is approximately 23.47% to 77.07%. The coverage is rounded, so these endpoints are approximate.

The lower endpoint already exceeds the national 20% benchmark. For the top 5%, the corresponding lower endpoint is approximately 0.464 × 7,302 ÷ 30,373 = 11.16%, above 5%. Thus, **missingness alone cannot eliminate overall high-income overrepresentation relative to this national household benchmark, if the known income classifications and coverage are correct**. It can substantially affect the magnitude. This calculation does not validate the household comparator as a child-level opportunity denominator.

The same argument does not establish every subgroup result. Subgroup-specific coverage is needed for subgroup bounds. Nor does it establish bottom-quintile underrepresentation under arbitrary missingness: unknown respondents could be disproportionately low-income. Different claims have different robustness.

**Do not erase the qualification or the result.** The bounds prevent a generic caveat from swallowing all the evidence, while the assumptions prevent a mathematical bound from being advertised as full causal validation.

<a id="disclosures"></a>
## 14. Article information — authors, funders and access

**Source location:** Article Information.

The authors report no conflicts. Two Minnesota medical foundations funded the work, and the statement says funders had no role in design, analysis or publication decisions. AAMC supplied the dataset; Shahriar and Miller are identified as having full data access and responsibility for integrity and analysis. These are the source's disclosures, not independently audited accounts of every institutional relationship.

Separate supplying data, financing analysis, authoring conclusions and endorsing conclusions. The AAMC disclaimer specifically separates authors' views from institutional policy. It would be misleading to turn publication of an analysis using AAMC data into evidence that AAMC adopted a particular interpretation or reform.

The article-specific CC BY statement permits preservation with attribution. It does not establish that row-level survey data are publicly downloadable or may be republished. An open-access article, an original PDF, a supplementary methods file and a reusable dataset are distinct objects.

For a later question about a threshold, discrepancy or subgroup rule, the corresponding author and data-responsible authors are the relevant provenance trail. Blackball should preserve that trail without interpreting institutional prestige as an additional reason to accept a number.

<a id="references"></a>
## 15. References — a map of separate evidence, not six replications

**Source location:** References 1–6. These are retrieval leads unless separately inspected.

Reference 1, Raffoul and colleagues, concerns workforce recruitment and training. Its role in the argument is background and institutional purpose, not a second measurement of this cohort's parental income.

Reference 2, Bailey and Dynarski's work on college entry and completion, connects the medical question to broader educational inequality. It should be read for its own populations and dates before importing its results into medical admissions.

Reference 3, Morris and colleagues, addresses demographic inequity over a longer period. A longitudinal demographic trend cannot be reconstructed from this three-year parental-income table, and the measures should not be conflated.

Reference 4, Morrison and Cort's AAMC pipeline analysis, is especially relevant to the missing pre-entry stages. Retrieve its aspirant, applicant and enrollment denominators before citing an admission or application probability.

Reference 5, Fenton and colleagues, addresses socioeconomic adjustment in admissions. Its proposed or evaluated intervention is a separate evidence object. This letter does not establish its effect merely by recommending attention to disadvantage.

Reference 6, Shahriar and colleagues' financing-plans paper, uses the same 2017–2019 entry period. Related measures from overlapping respondents can be useful, but they are not independent confirmation by a second population. Plans for paying also are not completed repayment histories or observed wealth.

**Citation-graph rule:** count underlying measurements, not the number of publications mentioning them. A claim repeated in a literature review has not acquired another independent sample.

<a id="supplement"></a>
## 16. Supplement — a remaining review obligation

The article links an eAppendix and eReferences. Access attempts did not yield readable supplemental content during this annotation pass. No claim is made to have reviewed its full methods, software, tests, weighting or additional tables.

The unresolved questions are concrete: exact pooling across years; classification of unknown income; mapping from questionnaire categories to national thresholds; weights applied to CPS-ASEC; construction of the chi-square comparisons; handling of multiple identities; and reconciliation of the small displayed count differences. These are questions to answer from the supplement or author documentation, not assertions that the study made a particular error.

The source dossier is complete for the inspected main article's sections, figure, table and disclosures. The full-study methods review remains incomplete until this separate source object is read. Acquiring the original main PDF does not close that obligation.

<a id="cross-paper"></a>
## 17. Reading medicine beside law — access before accumulation

[After the JD's annotations](../../after-the-jd/2004/section-notes.md) begin later in the professional sequence and measure different aspects of family background. Its father-occupation statistic and this parental-income distribution cannot be averaged into a single inherited-career percentage.

The useful combination is structural rather than numerical: specify entry, allocation of work, pay, debt, support and ownership as separate stages; then ask which institutions observe which people. Medicine's entrant survey does not observe lifetime accumulation. A law survey of admitted practitioners cannot recover everyone who paid for law school but never qualified. Combining two selected samples does not automatically supply the missing people.

For the user's question, compare people at the same ages as well as the same years since qualification. Record a threshold of security, age at first reaching it, setbacks below it, family transfers and the losses borne during the wait. Keep people who have not reached it in the denominator. “Not reached by the last observation” is not proof of “will never reach,” but excluding that person from an average time to success is also wrong.

The [paired analysis](../../professional-inheritance/README.md) develops this time-and-resources ledger. It is an original research specification, not an invented estimate that either source supplied the missing lifetime answer.
