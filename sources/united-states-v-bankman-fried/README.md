# United States v. Samuel Bankman-Fried (FTX)

**Court:** United States District Court for the Southern District of New York  
**District-court case:** 22-cr-673 (LAK)  
**Trial judge:** Lewis A. Kaplan  
**Appeal:** United States v. Bankman-Fried, No. 24-961-cr (2d Cir.)

This folder is a primary-source index to the criminal prosecution arising from the collapse of FTX. It favors the actual pleadings, transcripts, orders, and appellate opinion over press descriptions of them.

Bankman-Fried was convicted by a jury on November 2, 2023 on all seven counts in the S6 indictment. On March 28, 2024 he was sentenced principally to 25 years' imprisonment and ordered to forfeit $11.02 billion. Judge Kaplan denied a later Rule 33 motion for a new trial on April 28, 2026. The Second Circuit affirmed the judgment on June 12, 2026.

## Mirrored public-domain federal documents

The `pdf/` directory is populated from the source URLs below. These are federal-government works or federal judicial opinions/orders. See the copyright note below.

### 1. Original indictment - December 2022

**Local:** [`pdf/2022-12-original-indictment.pdf`](pdf/2022-12-original-indictment.pdf)  
**Source:** https://www.justice.gov/d9/press-releases/attachments/2022/12/13/u.s._v._bankman-fried_indictment_0.pdf

The original indictment charged eight counts. Its basic theory was already recognizable: customer deposits at FTX were diverted through Alameda Research; investors and lenders were allegedly given false information; the indictment also included campaign-finance allegations. This is the compact starting document before the prosecution expanded and then narrowed the charging instrument used at trial.

### 2. S5 superseding indictment - Dkt. 115 - March 28, 2023

**Local:** [`pdf/2023-03-28-s5-superseding-indictment-dkt-115.pdf`](pdf/2023-03-28-s5-superseding-indictment-dkt-115.pdf)  
**Source:** https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/bankman-fried-superseding-indictmentOCR.pdf

This is the most detailed indictment in the charging history and is worth keeping even though it was not the final trial indictment. It describes three concrete mechanisms in unusual detail: FTX customer fiat deposits flowing through Alameda-associated bank accounts; privileges in FTX's code that let Alameda carry a huge negative balance; and allegedly false balance-sheet information supplied to investors and lenders. It also alleges illegal political contributions and a bribe of at least $40 million to one or more Chinese government officials to unfreeze Alameda trading accounts holding more than $1 billion in cryptocurrency.

### 3. S6 superseding indictment - Dkt. 202 - August 14, 2023

**Local:** [`pdf/2023-08-14-s6-trial-indictment-dkt-202.pdf`](pdf/2023-08-14-s6-trial-indictment-dkt-202.pdf)  
**Source:** https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/sbf-august-2023-superseding-indictment.pdf

This is the seven-count indictment tried to the jury. It charges wire fraud on FTX customers, conspiracy to commit wire fraud on customers, wire fraud on Alameda lenders, conspiracy to commit wire fraud on lenders, conspiracy to commit securities fraud on FTX investors, conspiracy to commit commodities fraud on FTX customers, and conspiracy to commit money laundering. Compared with S5, it is a useful reminder that the actual trial was narrower than the whole history of accusations surrounding FTX.

### 4. Government opposition to new trial - Dkt. 587 - March 11, 2026

**Local:** [`pdf/2026-03-11-government-opposition-new-trial-dkt-587.pdf`](pdf/2026-03-11-government-opposition-new-trial-dkt-587.pdf)  
**Source:** https://cdn.arstechnica.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/US-v-Bankman-Fried-DOJ-Motion-to-Deny-New-Trial-3-11-26.pdf

This 49-page prosecution memorandum is valuable as a retrospective map of the trial evidence. It answers Bankman-Fried's claimed newly discovered evidence by pointing back to cooperating witnesses, contemporaneous spreadsheets and messages, code evidence, insolvency evidence, and Bankman-Fried's own trial testimony. It is an advocacy document, not a neutral history, but it gives a dense set of pointers into the trial record.

### 5. Order denying new trial - Dkt. 595 - April 28, 2026

**Local:** [`pdf/2026-04-28-order-denying-new-trial-dkt-595.pdf`](pdf/2026-04-28-order-denying-new-trial-dkt-595.pdf)  
**Source:** https://business.cch.com/srd/2026-04-28-16-23-33-363-590940_2246_1042926.pdf

Judge Kaplan denied both the Rule 33 motion and Bankman-Fried's recusal request. The court held that the proposed witnesses were not newly discovered, that Bankman-Fried had known of them and the proposed substance of their testimony before trial, and that the claim that their absence resulted from government threats was contradicted by the record. The order is a short judicial summary of why the post-trial factual theory did not reopen the verdict.

### 6. Second Circuit opinion - No. 24-961-cr - June 12, 2026

**Local:** [`pdf/2026-06-12-second-circuit-opinion-24-961-cr.pdf`](pdf/2026-06-12-second-circuit-opinion-24-961-cr.pdf)  
**Official source:** https://ww3.ca2.uscourts.gov/decisions/OPN/24-961_opn.pdf

The Second Circuit affirmed. The opinion is probably the best single compact judicial account of the case after trial: it recounts the FTX/Alameda relationship and the proof at trial, then addresses challenges to evidentiary rulings, advice-of-counsel procedure, jury instructions, discovery, and the $11.02 billion forfeiture judgment. On the loss issue, the panel held that an intention eventually to repay does not negate wire fraud based on unauthorized temporary misappropriation. It also upheld the forfeiture order against statutory and Eighth Amendment challenges.

  The jury is sworn, Judge Kaplan gives preliminary instructions, and both sides give opening statements. This is the cleanest place to see the prosecution's and defense's competing stories before the evidence came in.

- **Dkt. 368 - October 17, 2023 trial transcript (187 pages):** https://www.docketalarm.com/cases/New_York_Southern_District_Court/1--22-cr-00673/USA_v._BANKMAN-FRIED/368/

- **Dkt. 374 - October 26, 2023 trial transcript (246 pages):** https://www.docketalarm.com/cases/New_York_Southern_District_Court/1--22-cr-00673/USA_v._BANKMAN-FRIED/374/

## Copyright / mirroring rule used here

The repository uses a conservative distinction:

1. **Works prepared by U.S. federal officers or employees in the course of official duties** are not protected by copyright under 17 U.S.C. § 105. The U.S. Copyright Office specifically lists federal judicial decisions as U.S. government works.
2. **Federal court transcripts are public domain regardless of whether produced by a court employee, contractor, or transcriber.** The Judicial Conference's *Guide to Judiciary Policy*, vol. 6, ch. 5, § 560 states this explicitly.
3. **Private-party filings are not assumed to be public domain merely because PACER or another docket service makes them public.** They are linked unless a separate license or public-domain basis is established.
4. A government filing that bundles substantial third-party attachments is treated cautiously and may be linked rather than mirrored even when the government's own memorandum is public domain.

Copyright Office, 17 U.S.C. § 105: https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html#105
