# Ford executive / insider memoirs: Morsey and Iacocca

## Why these belong in Blackball

Corporate memoirs are evidence, but they are evidence with a source position.

They can preserve meetings, arguments, names, internal conflicts, product decisions, and first-person recollections unavailable elsewhere. They can also allocate credit retrospectively around the narrator.

Do not label them “propaganda” and stop reading. Do not treat self-attribution as independent proof either.

## Chase Morsey Jr. — *The Man Who Saved the V-8*

Full title:

*The Man Who Saved the V-8: The Untold Stories of Some of the Most Important Product Decisions in the History of Ford Motor Company*

WorldCat records a 2013 CreateSpace edition. Kirkus reviewed it as a memoir by one of Ford's early “Whiz Kids” and describes Morsey's account of arguing in 1949 that Ford should retain V-8 models.

The title itself is a causal claim about individual credit. That makes it especially useful for Blackball.

### Questions to test

- What was Morsey's formal role at the time of each decision?
- Who designed the engine and vehicles?
- Who gathered market evidence?
- Who had final decision authority?
- What do meeting minutes, product-planning documents, engineering records, patents, and other participants say?
- Which later product successes are attributed backward to one meeting or one executive?
- How does the memoir distinguish marketing, engineering, finance, manufacturing, labor, dealer, and executive contributions?

Sources:

- WorldCat record: https://search.worldcat.org/title/879334559
- Kirkus review: https://www.kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/chase-morsey-jr/the-man-who-saved-the-v-8/

## Lee Iacocca with William Novak — *Iacocca: An Autobiography*

Bantam published *Iacocca: An Autobiography* in 1984.

Google Books' edition metadata describes Iacocca as trained in industrial/mechanical engineering, entering Ford as an engineering trainee in 1946, moving into sales, and eventually becoming president of Ford and later Chrysler's chief executive.

That career is useful precisely because “engineering education” and “engineering occupation” diverge inside one famous automobile biography.

### Blackball use

Treat the autobiography as:

- first-person retrospective evidence about what Iacocca says happened;
- evidence about how a corporate leader narrates merit, conflict, product credit, management, and institutional power;
- a source to compare against engineering-team histories, labor histories, government records, loan records, board materials, and other participants.

Do not turn “engineering degree” into an explanation of why he became a corporate president. Reconstruct the sales, management, network, organizational, political, and historical path.

Sources:

- Google Books, *Iacocca: An Autobiography*: https://books.google.com/books?id=QahcU-eW4wkC
- WorldCat bibliographic record: https://search.worldcat.org/title/Iacocca-%3A-an-autobiography/oclc/10974869

## Comparison rule

For every executive memoir:

**claim by participant ≠ independent verification**

But:

**interested source ≠ useless source**

Preserve the claim, name the claimant, and find controls.
