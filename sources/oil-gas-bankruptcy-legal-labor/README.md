# Oil-and-gas bankruptcies: royalty owners, creditor priority, and the work of lawyers

**Initial primary-source dossier — 2026-10-14.** Research status: three bankruptcy cases and a fourth judicial opinion located and inspected at the level indicated below. This is a source map and a set of bounded findings, **not** a completed outcome audit, a survey of all fracking bankruptcies, or an allegation of misconduct by a named lawyer.

## Why this belongs in Blackball

The question is not merely whether a teenager thinks being a lawyer brings income or social standing. What does a lawyer's *successful work* accomplish for the client, and what does it mean for people on the other side? Corporate restructuring offers a way to investigate with court-filed pleadings, confirmed plans, lawyer-retention and fee applications, creditor claims and actual payment records.

One October 14, 2026 X screenshot supplied in conversation shows a post attributed to Moe Tkacik asserting that operators use Chapter 11 proceedings in Houston to wipe out small landowners. The screenshot shows only a truncated post, not the full post, identified cases, or its research records. Treat it as an **attributable allegation and investigation lead**, not as independently established proof. It was responding to a separate claim about wealth conspicuously enjoyed by Pennsylvania residents in gas-drilling areas; the screenshot is not sufficient to establish the speaker's whole argument or any individual resident's finances.

### Necessary distinctions

- **Filing for Chapter 11** is not by itself proof that a royalty holder was deprived of a valid property right or went unpaid.
- A mineral or royalty ownership interest, a lease, an unpaid prepetition debt, and a disputed deduction can receive different legal treatment.
- A confirmed plan's **distribution provision** establishes prescribed treatment, not proof of ultimate individual payment or nonpayment unless matched to claims and disbursements.
- An operator's statement that it has authority to pay royalties does not prove that all royalties were paid correctly or in full.
- Named law firms' role as debtor counsel, opposing counsel, or claimant counsel does not by itself establish a specific lawyer's fee, subjective motives, or any wrongdoing.
- Professional compensation should be measured from **court-approved fee applications**, not assumed to be a fixed share of the client's gains.

## Case 1 — Rex Energy / R.E. Gas Development (Pennsylvania)

**Case:** `In re R.E. Gas Development, LLC`, No. **18-22032 (JAD)**, U.S. Bankruptcy Court, Western District of Pennsylvania. Rex Energy and related debtors sought Chapter 11 relief **May 18, 2018**.

**Primary records inspected:**

1. [Rex Energy's SEC Form 8-K reporting the petition](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1397516/000119312518167436/d583290d8k.htm) — establishes venue, petition date, and the existence of bankruptcy proceedings.
2. [August 24, 2018 asset-purchase agreement with PennEnergy Resources](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1397516/000119312518258199/d614840dex21.htm) — SEC-hosted court-sale transaction document; specifically lists **“Contested Royalties”** as Disclosure Schedule 7.2(a)(ii). The existence of that schedule heading establishes a category of dispute; it does **not** disclose an amount or decide the dispute.
3. [Findings and order confirming the amended liquidation plan, with plan text](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1397516/000119312518304338/d622929dex22.htm) — Chapter 11 plan, **Article II.C.4**: **Class 4 general unsecured claims receive no distribution**; **Class 2 second-lien claims** are provided specified distributions; priority and other secured classes have different treatments. This is a specific, substantive legal outcome. Do **not** assume every royalty owner was a Class 4 creditor.
4. [Rex Energy's June 2018 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1397516/000156459018020532/rexx-10q_20180630.htm) — reports that the bankruptcy court authorized Rex to make royalty payments, a crucial qualification of claims that royalties were automatically erased.

**Legal work to trace:** asset sales under §§ 363/365; claim priority; treatment of unsecured creditors; debtors' counsel (Jones Day and Buchanan Ingersoll & Rooney named in the filed plan); parties who retained or lost enforceable economic claims. **Unknown:** which individual landowner claims, if any, fell in Class 4; actual historical balances and disbursements; individual professional fees.

## Case 2 — Chesapeake Energy (Houston)

**Case:** `In re Chesapeake Energy Corporation`, No. **20-33233 (DRJ)**, U.S. Bankruptcy Court, Southern District of Texas, Houston Division. Petition **June 28, 2020**.

**Primary records inspected:**

1. [January 16, 2021 confirmation order and fifth amended Chapter 11 plan](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/895126/000089512621000016/ex212021-01x16confirmation.htm) — filed court order and plan; debtor counsel include **Kirkland & Ellis** and **Jackson Walker**.
2. [Chesapeake 2020 annual SEC report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/895126/000089512621000078/chk-20201231.htm) — company reports that the bankruptcy court authorized payment of **mineral interest owner royalties** along with specified other obligations, and describes the automatic stay.
3. [Kirkland & Ellis's March 1, 2021 case description](https://www.kirkland.com/news/in-the-news/2021/03/bankruptcy-spotlight_a-conversation-with-kirkland) — **the law firm's own characterization**, not independent neutral evaluation: it describes debtor counsel prevailing in a contested confirmation against the unsecured creditors' committee and reducing funded debt by more than $7 billion, with reorganized control allocated to specified lenders.

**Legal work to trace:** litigating confirmation, replacing debt with equity, contesting claim-class recoveries, negotiating lenders' treatment and the effect of the automatic stay. **Unknown:** what happened to each Pennsylvania leaseholder/royalty owner and to any disputed prepetition underpayments. The firm's public account of *success* concerns its debtor client, not evidence that individual lessors prospered or lost.

## Case 3 — Gulfport Energy (Houston), particularly instructive contrary evidence

**Case:** `In re Gulfport Energy Corporation`, No. **20-35562 (DRJ)**, U.S. Bankruptcy Court, Southern District of Texas, Houston Division. Petition **November 13, 2020**; confirmed **April 28, 2021**.

**Primary records inspected:**

1. [Court order confirming reorganization](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/874499/000121390021023524/ea140017ex2-1_gulfportenergy.htm) — a detailed **royalty carve-out**:
   - Paragraph **64** says debtors/reorganized debtors must continue paying *undisputed* amounts owing on royalty and working interests, including reconciled suspense funds, in the ordinary course.
   - Paragraphs **65–66** preserve Claugus Family Farm and multiple **named royalty claimants'** claims to recover amounts alleged due, including prepetition unpaid royalties and contested charges; they preserve specified rights from plan releases/injunctions. A name in the court document is **not** proof a claimant ultimately recovered money.
   - Paragraphs **67–69** address other individual royalty disputes, litigation, and property interests.
2. [Amended joint Chapter 11 plan filed as SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/874499/000121390021023524/ea140017ex2-2_gulfportenergy.htm) — provides for **allowed professional fee claims to be paid in cash** from an escrow after court determination; this establishes a compensation mechanism, not an amount actually earned by a specific lawyer.
3. [Gulfport Form 8-K regarding confirmation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/874499/000121390021023524/ea140017-8k_gulfport.htm) — confirms date and case identity.

**Legal work to trace:** creditor and debtor counsel's competing terms, the means by which royalty claimants obtained express carve-outs, fee allowances versus claimant recoveries, and post-confirmation litigation/payment. The preservation clauses **directly prevent** using this confirmation as proof that bankruptcy simply wiped out these specified royalty claims. Conversely, an intact right to sue does not prove successful collection.

## Case 4 — Sanchez Energy (Houston): an actual judicial ruling on contract rejection

**Case:** Sanchez Energy Chapter 11 (filed **August 11, 2019**), and adversary proceeding **No. 20-03198** before the U.S. Bankruptcy Court, Southern District of Texas.

**Primary record:** [May 6, 2021 memorandum opinion, `Occidental Petroleum Corp. v. Sanchez Energy Corp.`](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-txsb-4_20-ap-03198/pdf/USCOURTS-txsb-4_20-ap-03198-0.pdf), court-hosted on govinfo, 31 pages.

The court held that reorganized debtor Mesquite could **reject specified executory agreements**, while Occidental retained granted **real-property interests**. This shows a particular type of lawyer-produced result: redefining obligations while leaving other property rights intact. **Occidental was a corporate counterparty, not a small Pennsylvania landowner**; do not mislabel this as an instance of harming small royalty recipients.

## The work/product/beneficiary test

For each matter or order, make a row with:

`lawyer / firm → paying client → filing or negotiated clause → relief requested → objection/opposition → relief actually ordered → enforceability → person or class benefiting → adverse party → money ultimately received or lost → fees paid to each firm`.

The public **“lawyer = respectable well-paid career”** story does not answer any of these questions. Nor is the converse **“lawyer = always crushes the weak”** supported by these four files. These cases establish that lawful corporate legal work can shift substantial economic burdens, that some creditors can receive zero under a confirmed plan, and that other claimants can preserve property or payment rights through litigation.
