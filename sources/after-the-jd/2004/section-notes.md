# After the JD — section-by-section critical annotations

[Source record and full-PDF link](README.md) · [Originating Blackball question](../../../llm/questions/professional-class-career-inheritance.md)

These are original analytical notes to read **beside** the source, not a transcription or exhaustive summary. Page references identify the passage or table being interrogated. Questions, proposed measurements, causal diagrams, and invented numerical examples below are Blackball's analysis; they are not findings attributed to the report's authors. No survey microdata have been reanalyzed.

The organizing question is not merely whether becoming a lawyer can pay. It is **who gets to enter, who pays for the attempt, who absorbs failure, and how much earlier one family can give its child security than another family can obtain it through work**.

<a id="front"></a>
## Front matter — provenance before authority

**Read alongside:** [pp. 1–10][p1].

Keep four roles separate: the people who designed and analyzed the study; the organizations that financed it; the organizations that published it; and the people represented by its sample. A publisher's institutional standing is not an additional independent observation. Five organizations repeating one table still supply one underlying measurement.

The source record must distinguish the edition's date from the date an online copy was uploaded or encountered. A directory name in a URL is not the date of the research. Likewise, digitization or a fresh web address cannot make an old cohort current.

For Blackball, acknowledgments are an accountability map. Who could answer a question about a sampling decision? Who retains the questionnaire? Who could authorize an archival copy? Who might have an institutional interest in the way educational and professional outcomes are presented? Those are different questions. Funding is neither a certificate of impartiality nor, by itself, proof of manipulation.

A useful review keeps both favorable and unfavorable results available. The target is not to force the whole document into a story of success or a story of exclusion. It is to identify the exact population for which each statement holds, then ask what happens to the people outside that population.

**Preservation test:** a bibliographic record, source link, notes, and an archived binary are four separate objects. The first three do not prove the fourth exists. The [source record](README.md#source-preservation-and-review-status) states the actual boundary.

## Section 1 — the denominator is part of the argument

**Read alongside:** [pp. 13–15][p13]; compare the [methods appendix][p89].

Begin with a sequence of populations rather than the phrase “law graduates” used loosely:

**considering study → applying → enrolling → completing → qualifying for practice → securing work → obtaining stable earnings → accumulating assets.**

At each transition, record how many began, how many passed through, how many left, how much they spent, and how long they waited. A study beginning after a selection point cannot, without additional data, estimate losses before that point.

For nested events, the multiplication rule gives:

P(stable professional work) = P(enroll) × P(complete | enroll) × P(qualify | complete) × P(stable work | qualify).

This is not an assumption of independence. Each probability is explicitly conditional on the earlier transitions. Its purpose is to prevent a high rate at the last transition from silently replacing the whole pathway's probability.

**Hypothetical counterexample:** out of 1,000 people who begin an expensive program, suppose 600 reach the population a later survey contacts, and 570 of those have the desired outcome. The later conditional rate is 95%; the rate among the original 1,000 is 57%. Neither number can replace the other. These are invented numbers, not this report's estimates.

The time origin also matters. “Three years into practice” and “age thirty-five” answer different questions. A person who qualified after a prolonged detour can resemble another entrant in experience while having lost substantially more working years.

**Blackball use:** attach the eligibility definition to every downstream statistic, including favorable employment and satisfaction findings. A promise made before tuition is paid must be evaluated from before tuition is paid, not only among those who emerge from the process.

## Section 2 — parental background, access, and the 69% finding

**Read alongside:** [pp. 19–21][p19], especially [p. 20][p20] and Table 2.2.

The [source record](README.md#citation-and-exact-locator) gives the verified statistic and its exact wording in paraphrase. The essential mathematical distinction is between:

P(professional-family background | entrant)

and

P(entrant | professional-family background).

They are not interchangeable. Bayes' rule requires the population sizes:

P(entrant | background) = P(background | entrant) × P(entrant) ÷ P(background).

An occupational-origin distribution among entrants can demonstrate who is represented there. It cannot alone give an individual's chance of entering. Comparing it with a background population also requires that the population actually be relevant: appropriate ages, cohorts, geographic scope, and the same occupational classification. Dividing two mismatched percentages does not repair the mismatch.

Do not collapse parental profession, parental income, net worth, and access to a family practice into one binary “privilege” variable. Consider two hypothetical households: a salaried professional with large liabilities and no hiring authority, and an owner of a profitable business who can finance training and provide a first job. Their occupations alone may rank them differently from their capacity to transfer opportunity.

The father's occupation is not a complete family balance sheet. A serious extension would record both parents or guardians, other supporting relatives, housing, guarantees, tuition payments, introductions, and actual ownership transfers. Unknown support must remain unknown rather than being inferred from a school name.

**Causal question:** when did a resource change the path? Help before admission, support during study, and a referral after graduation are distinct interventions. A child need not inherit the identical occupation to inherit a substantially better starting position.

**Normative boundary:** finding unequal starting positions raises the justification question. Explaining how a family secured an advantage is not an argument that institutions should award it.

## Section 3 — a job label is not the whole outcome

**Read alongside:** [pp. 25–29][p25], Tables 3.1–3.2 and Figure 3.1; inspect the eligibility qualification on p. 26.

Use a multidimensional outcome record: employment, actual work performed, hours, pay, security, autonomy, training, ownership, and suitability to the person's stated objective. “Employed” is too coarse to stand in for every one of these. Equally, work outside an intended profession should not automatically be described as failure: the person's alternatives and preferences matter. Calling every departure successful flexibility would be just as unsupported.

The office/firm distinction deserves explicit coding. A small local office can belong to a large organization. Combining local workplace size with total employer size can create an apparent comparison between independent small businesses and large firms when the organizational contrast is not actually that simple. Keep employer identity, local office, parent organization, and ownership separate.

For class analysis, do not treat occupational setting as a free choice merely because it is observed. A defensible choice account needs the applications, offers, refusals, geographic constraints, and affordability of waiting. “Works in setting A” does not imply “could have taken setting B but preferred A.”

A useful counterfactual comparison is not simply prestigious job versus less prestigious job. Compare the realized outcome against the accessible alternatives available before undertaking the expense. Someone can earn a respectable salary and still have paid heavily for little improvement over an attainable alternative. Another person can gain greatly from a modestly paid role. Neither evaluation follows from the occupational label alone.

**Retrieval target:** link initial hiring records to the qualification cohort and to earlier educational entrants. Keep people without a job in the accounting; do not drop them because a table of workplace characteristics has no category for them. Also distinguish inherited practice ownership from ordinary employee entry rather than merging both under “private practice.”

## Section 4 — hours, tasks, responsibility, and service

**Read alongside:** [pp. 33–37][p33], note 9, Tables 4.1–4.3, and Figure 4.1.

**Measurement correction:** the hours question concerns the last week, not necessarily a typical week. [p. 33, note 9][p33]. A one-week observation should not be multiplied into a lifetime workload without examining seasonality, leave, unusually busy periods, and the way the reference week was selected.

An hourly comparison needs consistent definitions. Salary divided by reported hours is an accounting ratio, not a measure of effort, social value, or moral entitlement. It can omit bonuses, unpaid preparation, availability requirements, commuting, and benefits. Conversely, counting every waking hour as work would also distort the comparison. Specify the activity boundary before calculating.

Responsibility is ambiguous without supervision and resources. Handling a matter alone could be evidence of trust and useful development. It could also mean insufficient staffing and little support. Repetitive work inside a prestigious institution could build knowledge or confine someone to a narrow role. The task description, feedback received, error exposure, and later assignments are needed to distinguish these possibilities.

Specialization should likewise be separated into concentration of time, depth of expertise, and a label the worker uses. Repeating a narrow task often does not logically establish mastery; broad assignments do not logically establish shallow competence.

For service measures, separate participation from intensity. For a nonnegative hours variable with zero assigned to nonparticipants:

E[hours] = P(participates) × E[hours | participates].

This identity holds for means. It does not hold for medians. A large conditional mean can coexist with little participation, and an all-person median can be zero despite substantial work by a minority.

**Blackball question:** which forms of socially useful work are counted, excluded, or paid under another heading? A volunteering measure alone cannot rank the total public contribution of occupational groups.

## Section 5 — earnings are a flow, wealth is a stock

**Read alongside:** [pp. 41–44][p41], especially Tables 5.1–5.3.

Do not convert a salary comparison into a wealth comparison without a household account. Over a defined period, a simplified net-worth identity is:

closing net worth − opening net worth = income received − consumption − taxes − interest + net gifts and inheritances + asset-value changes,

provided the components are defined consistently and all relevant items are included. Borrowing raises both cash and liabilities; paying down principal lowers both. Neither operation by itself creates net worth. Confusing principal repayment with an additional loss while already counting the original expenditure double-counts the cost.

**Hypothetical example:** two workers have identical salaries and annual saving, but one begins with a gift and the other with debt incurred to obtain the same qualification. Equal current earnings do not make their starting stocks equal. The time required to close the difference depends on actual saving, transfers, interest, and asset changes. A gross salary cannot answer the user's question about years to security.

For institutional comparisons, separate the distribution across job settings from pay within each setting. A difference between school groups could reflect different jobs obtained, different pay in apparently similar jobs, or both. Deciding whether setting is a mechanism or a variable to hold fixed depends on the causal question.

Do not average subgroup medians to reconstruct an overall median. As a mathematical counterexample, samples [0, 0, 100] and [1, 2, 3] have medians 0 and 2; their equally weighted median average is 1, but the combined sample has median 1.5. No salary interpretation is intended by these invented values.

A comparison at the same grade also needs care: a grade is not automatically a common measurement scale across schools, courses, or selection systems. A school-rank category should remain a measured institutional label, not be silently relabeled intelligence or competence.

**Research target:** estimate the joint distribution of earnings, debt, transfers, age, and employment spells. Ownership and accumulated assets should be outcomes in their own right, not rewards assumed to follow from a professional title.

## Section 6 — satisfaction cannot answer every question

**Read alongside:** [pp. 47–50][p47], Figure 6.1 and Tables 6.1–6.2.

Keep at least four judgments separate: liking the current work; liking the workplace; regarding the education as worth its cost; and regarding access to the occupation as fair. A respondent could consistently give different answers to all four. Satisfaction is not a substitute for the other outcomes simply because it is easy to report.

**Scale warning:** the factor scores on p. 50 are centered on zero. A negative relative score is not automatically an unhappy respondent. [p. 50][p50]. More generally, never apply the verbal labels from one scale to a transformed score on another scale. Record the original questions, response endpoints, direction, transformations, and aggregation method.

If several items are combined into a factor, ask what evidence supports treating them as a common construct. Correlation among salary, promotion, and status ratings does not establish that respondents regard them as morally equivalent. Comparisons between groups also require that the questions and scoring function comparably for those groups.

Do not dismiss favorable self-reports as necessarily mistaken. That would replace the observed answer with the reviewer's preferred answer. But do not use favorable answers among observed participants to speak for those excluded from the sample. Both mistakes erase people: one erases respondents' testimony; the other erases nonrespondents and nonentrants.

An association between satisfaction and a work setting does not show what would happen if the same people moved between settings. Selection, expectations, alternatives, and prior experiences can matter. A plausible compensating-differences story still needs evidence; it is not licensed merely by a combination of lower pay and higher reported satisfaction.

**Blackball use:** preserve the positive testimony while locating it precisely. Then ask the independent accountability question: who supplied the cost, who received the benefit, and what happened to those who did not arrive at a satisfying job?

## Section 7 — leaving, remaining, and the options actually available

**Read alongside:** [pp. 53–54][p53], Table 7.1.

Separate an intended move, an attempted move, an offer, an accepted offer, and an actual transition. None is automatically the next. A person can want to leave and lack an alternative; another can expect to remain and later be dismissed. A statement about future plans is not a measured transition rate.

A useful event record contains the origin job, destination job or nonemployment state, dates, interruption length, reason, and who initiated the separation. Promotion, closure, dismissal, family relocation, completion of a fixed appointment, and departure for better work should not become one undifferentiated “mobility” outcome.

Time at risk matters. Someone observed for four years has more opportunity to change jobs than someone observed for one year. A simple fraction who have ever moved confounds exposure time with the underlying likelihood of moving. Start and end dates are therefore essential rather than administrative decoration.

Similarly, staying is not an unambiguous success measure. It can represent acceptable work, a valuable relationship, fear of losing benefits, debt pressure, or inability to obtain another offer. Leaving is not automatically failure. The evidence needs to distinguish these mechanisms rather than moralizing one state.

For the family-resource question, propose a comparison around the financial ability to wait between jobs. A family-funded interval might support a better search; it might also have no effect in a market with no suitable openings. These are competing hypotheses. Measure liquid resources, transfers, vacancies, search effort, offers, and the costs borne during the interval.

**Blackball extension:** trace whether a move changes earnings, security, hours, autonomy, and wealth. A higher-status destination accompanied by much higher costs is not necessarily a gain on every dimension. Keep the entire transition, including unemployment, visible.

## Section 8 — gender, work allocation, and household constraints

**Read alongside:** [pp. 57–60][p57], Tables 8.1–8.3.

Entry, allocation, remuneration, ownership, and retention are different stages. A group can be well represented among entrants yet face a different distribution of assignments or later opportunities. Conversely, a difference in one aggregate outcome does not identify the mechanism producing it.

**Text/table check:** compare p. 58's pay generalization with the government equality in Table 8.2. [pp. 58–60][p58]. Preserve the table's qualifications instead of sharpening prose into a universal statement.

When comparing pay, define whether the target is the total difference, a difference within job categories, or the effect of a particular decision. Controlling for hours, setting, specialization, or family responsibilities may answer a narrower question, but those variables could themselves be consequences of unequal opportunities. Adjustment is not automatically correction; its interpretation depends on the causal structure.

A candidate pathway is:

access to assignments → experience and client relationships → later pay or ownership.

A second is:

household care obligations → available schedules and locations → jobs considered and accepted.

These are hypotheses to test, not findings established by drawing arrows. They show why a one-time salary comparison cannot locate the whole mechanism.

Networking should be made concrete. Attendance at an event is not the same as being introduced to a decision-maker, recommended for work, or given an assignment. Count invitations, sponsorship, substantive feedback, and opportunities delivered, not just whether someone socialized. This is an institutional research question, not an instruction that excluded workers should spend more unpaid time networking.

For household comparisons, align age, partnership status, number and ages of dependents, and the timing of observations. A snapshot early in a career cannot establish lifetime effects of marriage or children. Record whose unpaid work makes another person's paid-work schedule feasible.

## Section 9 — race, geography, and the danger of pooled comparisons

**Read alongside:** [pp. 63–68][p63], Tables 9.1–9.4; compare classification rules in [the appendix][p90].

A pooled comparison and a within-location comparison can point in different directions without either being arithmetically wrong. As an invented mean-pay example, suppose one group is concentrated in a high-pay city but earns less than another group within each city. Its pooled mean can still be higher. The conclusion depends on whether the question concerns the overall lived distribution or differences within comparable locations.

Do not turn this possibility into an automatic explanation of the report. Check the actual groups, location mix, job mix, sample sizes, and joint data. Furthermore, the usual weighted-average calculation works for means; it cannot simply be applied to pooled medians.

Geography can occupy different causal roles. It might affect access to employers; it might be partly chosen under financial constraints; it might itself reflect exclusion or recruitment. Holding location fixed can be useful, but it may remove part of the pathway one wants to understand. State the target before presenting a preferred adjusted number.

For a table of distributions, verify which direction sums to a whole. The fraction of a group located in a city is not the fraction of that city's lawyers belonging to the group. Reversing the denominator can transform a geographic pattern into an entirely different claim about local representation.

Classification is also an analytical decision. Retain the distinction between a respondent's identities and the mutually exclusive code used in an analysis. An imposed priority order should not be mistaken for a natural description of multiracial identity. Suppressed or missing cells must not be read as zero outcomes.

**Class connection:** compare resources within groups as well as across them. A selected professional sample cannot represent every person sharing one demographic category. The aim is to locate barriers and advantages, not assign an occupational essence to a group or substitute an exceptional entrant for everyone left outside.

## Section 10 — financing, debt, gifts, and the ability to survive risk

**Read alongside:** [pp. 71–75][p71], Tables 10.1–10.3.

**Denominator check:** Table 10.1 reports financing shares, not the percentage of people receiving each kind of help. Table 10.3 requires separate salary and debt denominators. [pp. 73–75][p73].

An average of individuals' financing percentages is not the fraction of all dollars supplied by a source. Invented example: one student spends 10 units financed entirely by family; another spends 90 units financed entirely by loans. The average individual family share is 50%, but the family share of total spending is 10%. Both describe something real in that example, but they answer different questions.

Debt also has two distinct margins: having any debt and the amount conditional on having it. Let z be the fraction with zero debt, and let F₊(d) describe debt among borrowers. For nonnegative debt amounts d:

F(d) = z + (1 − z)F₊(d).

When z is at least one half, zero is an overall median. When z is below one half, the overall lower median corresponds to borrower quantile (0.5 − z) ÷ (1 − z). Thus identical borrower medians do not establish identical debt distributions across populations with different zero-debt shares.

A ratio of separate salary and debt medians is not the median of individual debt-to-income ratios. The pairing matters. To estimate repayment burden, obtain each person's debt, interest terms, payment schedule, income, taxes, essential expenses, and support, all measured at compatible dates.

Most importantly, financing is not only an eventual liability. It determines who can enter, remain, wait, relocate, repeat an examination, or refuse an unsuitable job. Equal observed debt among workers in two settings would not by itself show that debt had no effect on access to those settings. People priced out before observation would be missing from that comparison.

**Risk-insurance question:** family help can be a payment already made or a credible promise to cover a future loss. The second may change choices without appearing as a transfer in the year surveyed. Record actual payments separately from guarantees and from merely assumed family generosity.

The relevant outcome is not simply whether a debtor earns a professional salary. It is the household's realized return after costs and the downside borne when the intended conversion fails.

## Section 11 — schooling can teach, certify, and supply employers

**Read alongside:** [pp. 79–82][p79], Table 11.1 and Figures 11.1–11.2.

Separate three possible institutional services: teaching usable skills; providing a credential that employers recognize; and delivering access to employers. A school might perform differently on each. Evidence that a graduate obtained work does not establish which service produced it. Evidence of useful teaching does not establish access to a job.

Ask where the causal account begins. If family resources help secure admission to a school whose recruiting arrangements later yield a job, the final contact may be an institutional recruiter rather than a relative. Measuring only the last hiring contact could miss the earlier family contribution. Conversely, the mere presence of a relative in a profession does not establish that the relative intervened.

A candidate mechanism is:

family support → educational access → recruiting access → offer → accepted job.

Another is:

family contact → direct introduction → offer.

These routes should be measured separately. Neither needs to explain all cases. An analysis that controls for school before asking about the total contribution of family background may remove part of the first route by construction.

When reading evaluations of courses, practical experience, or contacts, distinguish perceived usefulness from causal effects. A graduate's retrospective judgment is evidence about that judgment. To establish how an experience changes outcomes, one needs a comparison that addresses who could obtain the experience, earlier preparation, costs, and alternative uses of the same time.

Importance ratings should not be added as though they were mutually exclusive placement shares. Several experiences can matter to one respondent. Nor is the rating of an experience among people who had it the chance of obtaining a job by undertaking it.

**Accountability target:** what did the institution promise before enrollment, what access did it actually supply, and what happened to students who received the instruction but no suitable offer? A useful follow-up should not turn into another recommendation to buy experience without specifying who supplies the opportunity and bears the loss.

## Section 12 — what would answer the fast-track question?

**Read alongside:** [pp. 85–86][p85].

Keep description, explanation, prediction, and justification separate. A descriptive distribution can identify unequal origins. A causal analysis can investigate mechanisms. A forecast needs evidence about another cohort or period. A moral defense needs premises about what people and institutions owe one another. No one step supplies the others automatically.

The user's “how long, if ever?” requires an outcome with a defined clock and threshold. Examples could include sustained adequate earnings, positive net worth after educational liabilities, sufficient liquid reserves to survive unemployment, or a specified wealth level. These are distinct definitions of security; choose and report them rather than treating “wealth” as self-explanatory.

Let T be the age at first reaching the specified condition. The useful distribution is P(T ≤ age | family resources), measured for the full population relevant to the promise. It must include those who have not reached the condition. A mean time among successful cases answers a narrower and potentially misleading question.

At a finite follow-up date, distinguish “not yet observed to reach” from “will never reach.” The latter cannot be inferred merely because observation ended. Death, disability, withdrawal from the targeted career, or a different chosen goal also require explicit handling; they should not silently disappear from the denominator.

A good comparison records both elapsed years and costs absorbed during those years. Someone who reaches the same income later after debt and unstable work has not experienced the same path shifted harmlessly on a calendar. Equally, the size of that loss must be measured rather than asserted from the label alone.

**Bottom line for research:** build a joint account of entry, time, losses, transfers, work, and assets. Preserve null and contradictory results. Do not let an aggregate success story erase exclusion, or let a valid criticism of exclusion justify inventing the missing outcomes.

## Methods appendix — selection, response, weighting, and uncertainty

**Read alongside:** [pp. 89–91][p89].

The [source record](README.md) identifies the central population boundary. This appendix should be read before exporting any table to another argument, not treated as optional technical decoration.

**Draw the sampling chain.** Distinguish the population intended, the administrative frame available, the people selected, the people located, those found eligible, those responding, and the valid answers to a particular item. “Response rate” without its denominator is incomplete. A response rate among located eligible people cannot be applied unchanged to everyone originally selected.

**Keep item-level availability visible.** A valid respondent may not answer every question or receive the same questionnaire in every survey mode. A parental-background cell, a salary cell, and a debt cell need not describe precisely the same people. Putting them next to one another does not establish a joint relationship. To calculate a personal burden or transition, observations must be linked for the same individuals.

**Treat oversampling as a design feature requiring appropriate use.** More observations of a small group can improve within-group analysis. It does not authorize treating the resulting combined sample proportions as population proportions without the relevant design adjustments. Conversely, refusing to use an oversample at all can throw away information. The correct choice depends on the estimand and sample design.

**Account for clustered selection.** When people are sampled through locations or institutions, their outcomes may not supply as many independent observations as the same number of unrelated draws. An ordinary independent-observation standard error can then be inappropriate. Do not fabricate confidence intervals without the design information or microdata needed to calculate them.

**Nonresponse is not solved by a large sample.** In an invented example, surveying more people who remain contactable after successful entry does not recover the experiences of people whose failed transition made them difficult to contact. Larger numbers can narrow random error around a systematically selected population. Compare available frame characteristics, document adjustments, and test sensitivity rather than assuming either no bias or inevitable fatal bias.

**Conditioning can conceal a mechanism.** Suppose, hypothetically, financial support and another selection-relevant attribute both help someone enter a profession. Among those who enter, people with little support may be unusually strong on the other attribute. A comparison restricted to entrants could then understate or even reverse an association found before selection. This is a possible selection mechanism, not a demonstrated explanation of these data.

**No retroactive population expansion.** Weighting observed respondents cannot by itself recreate people absent from the sampling frame. A project about returns to purchasing education needs evidence on purchasers who never become eligible for the professional cohort. That is a separate data-collection obligation, not a footnote that disappears after a statistical adjustment.

**Minimum reanalysis record:** target population; cohort dates; inclusion rule; sample and item counts; survey mode; classification rules; missingness handling; design weights; clustering; precise outcome definitions; uncertainty method; and a list of people or stages the analysis cannot represent. Without those, preserve the table as a bounded historical finding rather than a universal rate.

## Institutional back matter — who should answer for the pathway?

**Read alongside:** [pp. 93–96][p93].

Read organizational descriptions as statements about organizational purpose, not as verified evaluations of performance. An institution's mission to improve a profession does not demonstrate that its programs do so. A concern with access does not demonstrate that access is adequate.

For Blackball, the useful question is what each institution can observe and control. A school can observe enrollment and instruction. A licensing body can observe examinations and admission. Employers can observe offers, assignments, and pay. Financiers can observe borrowing and repayment. No single actor's favorable metric necessarily describes the person's complete experience across all these systems.

This fragmentation can produce an accountability gap even without anyone fabricating a number. One actor reports graduation; another reports outcomes only after qualification; another reports salaries only among employees. The person who paid and failed between those stages can vanish from every institution's headline outcome while remaining responsible for the costs.

That is a hypothesis about how fragmented measurement can mislead, not a claim that every named sponsor deliberately obscured losses. To test it, compare published definitions with the promises made to prospective entrants and identify which organization, if any, tracks the whole path.

**Institutional questions:** who releases questionnaires and documentation? Who can connect educational entry to later qualification and work without excluding failures? Who reports adverse outcomes? Who benefits from expanding enrollment? Who bears a failed participant's financial loss? Who has the power to change admission, training, funding, or hiring practices?

The normative question returns here: a family's understandable wish to protect a child does not itself justify preferential treatment by institutions controlling other people's opportunities. Research should identify the concrete rule or decision at issue rather than substituting a story about parental affection for an account of institutional responsibility.

## Reusable checks for later Blackball answers

An answer drawing on this dossier should be able to pass these tests:

1. **Population:** does it keep entrants distinct from applicants, educational purchasers, and the general population?
2. **Measure:** does it distinguish occupation, income, net worth, support, satisfaction, and institutional rank?
3. **Denominator:** does each percentage, mean, median, and ratio retain the people actually included?
4. **Time:** does it retain the cohort and observation period, and distinguish age from years since entry?
5. **Causation:** does it separate observed associations from candidate mechanisms and counterfactual claims?
6. **Accountability:** does it count costs and people left outside rather than only describing successful destinations?
7. **Provenance:** can the reader reach the exact source page, and is archival status described truthfully?

These are editorial and analytical checks. Their presence is not a claim of independent replication, automated acceptance, or completed follow-up research.
