# Retraction Watch: a study of manufactured scholarly plausibility

**Status (2026-10-16):** First source-anchored reconnaissance, not a systematic census or a completed investigation of any named author's entire body of work. This branch deliberately precedes the proposed Georg Rasch / psychometric-method audit. It is a Blackball evidence corpus, not a verdict that everyone in academia is dishonest.

**Additional matched cases (2026-10-16):** [Retraction Watch ↔ Blackball: statistical modeling, education, medical machine learning, financial replication, publishing economics and critic power](blackball-crosswalk-2026-10-16.md). This dossier separates retractions from technical disputes, contested investigations and publishing stings, and records what remains untested.

## Central question

**How can a weak, invalid, invented, or commercially convenient claim be made to look like scientific knowledge, and why might it be rewarded before anyone checks whether it is true?**

The problem is not exhausted by the articles that have been formally retracted. A statistically incorrect paper may survive; an author can have a real career selling methods with weak evidence; a journal can be profitable while publishing poor work; and a retraction can reflect an honest mistake rather than deceit.

Study the *production and certification of plausibility*:

1. **Generate material:** invented data, copied writing or images, purchased authorship, incomprehensible technical prose, motivated post-hoc analysis, software output presented as proof, embellished research claims.
2. **Give it academic form:** familiar notation, discipline-specific terminology, long citations, authoritative-sounding journals, institutional affiliations, keywords, indexing, conferences, impressive biographies, textbook chapters and reviewer reports.
3. **Pass or capture a gate:** ordinary fallible review; sham reviewers; special issues; editor-mediated mandatory citations; publisher acceptance targets; journals impersonating recognized titles. These are different mechanisms and must not be lumped together.
4. **Turn acceptance into a valuable asset:** tenure, promotion, credentials, contracts, journal revenue, author charges, subscriptions, citation rankings, course adoptions, consulting, prestige, readers' professional trust.
5. **Make correction difficult:** editorial discretion, confidentiality, lack of raw data, costs of checking, threats to existing reputations, slow notices, weak incentives to investigate old publications.
6. **Shift the losses:** competitors passed over, students taught unsupported claims, practitioners taking bad advice, public research funders, patients, investors, and ordinary workers who may not have the status to challenge the claim.

These are mechanisms to **test**, not allegations automatically proved whenever one observes a publication.

## Early findings backed by documentary cases

**The same publication has at least three distinct markets.** Someone can pay to *read* (libraries/institutions); someone can pay to *publish* (author, grant, employer); someone can purchase the resulting citation, credential, sales pitch, access to promotion, or status. Wiley explicitly discloses subscriptions, read-and-publish deals, and volume-based open-access publication revenue; its 2026 securities filing describes growth through publishing more research. This identifies an incentive, not proof every publisher or article is fraudulent. [Wiley 2026 SEC 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/107140/000162828026045111/jwa-20260430.htm).

**Quality gates can be monetized or bypassed.** Retraction Watch's investigation of the Russian 123mi.ru operation describes authorship positions sold, ghostwriting and sham peer review, based on discovered advertisements and contracts; its investigators explicitly caution that indicators for *individual* manuscripts may be circumstantial. [Paper mill investigation, 2021](https://retractionwatch.com/2021/12/20/revealed-the-inner-workings-of-a-paper-mill/). A 2026 impersonation case used a near-identical prestigious publisher name to market coauthorship. [Authorship offer, 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/10/12/springer-journals-online-authorship-for-sale/).

**An editor can convert apparently procedural requirements into rank and money.** A 2026 Retraction Watch investigation examined citation and paid-registration requirements in a surgical journal network, finding the mandatory guideline references materially contributed to a journal's impact factor. Whether a specific recommended guideline is scientifically useful must be checked separately from whether the editorial demand was financially conflicted. [Surgical publishing investigation, 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/03/12/riaz-agha-international-journal-surgery-research-registry-wolters-kluwer/).

**An elegant formula cannot rescue an invalid derivation.** In a finance/accounting investigation, a journal's committee concluded that the equations in a published paper did not produce the results reported, and the actual specification was econometrically inconsistent. [Review of Financial Studies case, 2019](https://retractionwatch.com/2019/11/13/the-methodology-does-not-generate-the-results-journal-corrects-accounting-study-with-flawed-methods/). Separately, retracted organizational-psychology work depended on an incorrect level of analysis, yielding unpersuasive model fit when corrected. [Walumbwa case, 2014](https://retractionwatch.com/2014/04/30/florida-leadership-researcher-walumbwa-notches-sixth-retraction/).

**Prestige can precede inspection.** In 2026, clinical machine-learning papers using readily inspectable, questionable publicly hosted data were withdrawn after investigators found duplicate observations and absent provenance. An author had appealed to widespread prior publication as a defense, which is not a data-integrity check. [Clinical dataset investigation, 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/05/18/kaggle-dataset-clinical-models-stroke-diabetes/). Another case documents a supposed medical dataset behind *The Lancet* and *NEJM* papers that could not be independently audited; an African clinical tool built from those data was withdrawn. [Surgisphere downstream withdrawal, 2020](https://retractionwatch.com/2020/06/06/group-withdraws-covid-19-scoring-tool-based-on-surgisphere-data-following-nejm-lancet-retractions/).

**The gatekeepers have conflicts with one another, not a unified interest.** A mathematics journal's editorial board resigned after a dispute over special issues, substantially greater publication volume and author charges. The publisher's explanation was that the old subscription business was economically unsustainable. Both interests matter; the original correspondence and policies are the evidence. [Journal of Geometric Mechanics, 2023](https://retractionwatch.com/2023/09/28/after-resigning-en-masse-math-journal-editors-launch-new-publication/).

## Evidence hierarchy / refusal to launder a source into fact

- **P — primary**: independently read original retraction notice, funding or contract statement, public filing, code, dataset, correction, or attributable admission; preserve its exact limited claim.
- **R — reported**: Retraction Watch independently reports and interviews, perhaps quoting primary documents; this documents *what the reporter established*, not all underlying facts by independent replication here.
- **A — allegation/investigation**: somebody has identified a red flag; an inquiry may be open; no adjudicated misconduct inferred.
- **M — methodological assessment**: our own proof, calculation, code replication, falsifying example or benchmark. **No M-level technical adjudication has yet been executed by this reading exercise.**

The case ledger does not silently upgrade R/A to M/P. A source may be P for the fact that a publisher announced a retraction, but only R or A for the claim that the author acted intentionally. Don't use citation counts or retraction counts as direct estimates of misconduct: detection and retraction rates depend on field, scrutiny, publisher, time, and editorial policy.

Retraction Watch itself warns that the database does not systematically cover corrections or expressions of concern, that revision records can double-count underlying works, and that some dates are imputed or misreported. [Retraction Watch Database User Guide](https://retractionwatch.com/retraction-watch-database-user-guide/); [Crossref documentation](https://www.crossref.org/documentation/retrieve-metadata/retraction-watch/). The open full dataset is maintained at [crossref/retraction-watch-data](https://gitlab.com/crossref/retraction-watch-data).

## Blackball relationship to class, careers and power

Do not presume that a prominent person has earned superior intellectual judgment because a journal, university, credential board or publisher accepted their output. Equally, do not presume an economically desperate outsider is intrinsically less honest or competent. Investigate **who has the power to certify a result, deny admission, define misconduct, sell access, demand unpaid evaluation labor, or keep an inquiry private**.

Two inquiries are related but not identical: *How does an academic claim acquire money and authority?* and *Does the claim actually withstand mathematical or empirical examination?* Neither should substitute for the other. The second can disprove the first party's appeal to reputation even when no retraction occurs.

A library's payment, an editorial seal and a textbook title may explain how something circulates; none establishes correctness. When the claim is mathematical, build a small counterexample or proof. When empirical, find the actual measurements, selection, perturbations and independent tests. When it promises profitable financial decisions, require a realizable, after-cost, risk-adjusted out-of-sample result—not a polished historical backtest.
