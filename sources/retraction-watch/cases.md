# Retraction Watch case ledger — failure mechanisms, not a shame list

**Snapshot: 2026-10-16.** Selected published articles inspected through web reading and discovery. These are **not** rows from an analyzed copy of the full database; no completeness or prevalence claim. **R** = RW reporting of the cited information; **P** = a primary filing/retraction notice or institutional statement quoted or linked in that reporting, not necessarily separately audited here; **A** = allegation or investigation, not a proved violation. An article's headline is not an adjudication.

## 1. Fabricating or selling the appearance of scholarship

| Case | Mechanism / what the record supports | Status / needed independent check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Inside the Russian paper mill, 2021](https://retractionwatch.com/2021/12/20/revealed-the-inner-workings-of-a-paper-mill/) | Researchers matched website advertisements for manuscript titles, authorship slots, claimed ghostwriting and fake reviews to paper records. Describes an *organized supply chain* selling certification, not simply an individual's invented dataset. | **R/A.** Investigators explicitly called individual-paper connections indirect/circumstantial; recover contracts, advertisements, manuscript histories and editorial records before accusations against each author. |
| [A company calling itself “Springer Journals Online” sells authorship, Oct. 12, 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/10/12/springer-journals-online-authorship-for-sale/) | An emeritus professor received an unsolicited paid-coauthorship pitch from an organization using a name similar to reputable publishers; Springer Nature said it was unaffiliated. | **R/P for solicitation and denial.** Not proof that any particular advertised article was published. Examine corporate existence and manuscripts. |
| [Authorship for sale sting, 2017](https://retractionwatch.com/2017/09/13/authorship-sale-journals-willing-add-authors-papers-didnt-write/) | A sting reported some publishers accepted the addition of a noncontributing fictional author for a fee. The 16% figure concerns the test's contacted sample and classification, **not 16% of all journals**. | **R.** Inspect original sting sampling protocol and denominators. |
| [Fake peer-review detection via duplicate comments, 2022](https://retractionwatch.com/2022/02/21/how-to-find-evidence-of-paper-mills-using-peer-review-comments/) | A publisher's analyst found repeated reviewer language across different submissions. Fake referee identities can produce plausible review histories. | **R/A.** Duplicate prose is a screening signal; check account metadata and original reviewer identities. |
| [Springer Nature psycholinguistics retractions, 2025](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/06/17/springer-nature-journal-psycholinguistic-research-retractions-authorship-peer-review-manipulation/) | Publisher inquiry into manuscript authorship and reviewer manipulation following a whistleblower investigation; some authors disputed retractions. | **R/P for notices; contested attribution.** Preserve author rebuttals and exact notice reasons. |
| [International Wound Journal, 2025](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/03/31/wiley-international-wound-journal-retracts-over-200-more-papers/) | 242 retractions as of the March report following investigation of compromised review; numerous retraction notices state acceptance rested on a corrupted review process. | **R/P.** Do not infer that every underlying conclusion was experimentally false from an invalid review procedure. |
| [Springer special-issue cleanup, 2024](https://retractionwatch.com/2024/11/07/another-springer-nature-journal-has-retracted-over-300-papers-since-july/) | More than 300 retractions across guest-edited issues; publisher reported editorial and submission-process changes. | **R/P.** Check notices, issue guest editors, provenance of peer-review files. |
| [Tortured phrases in Microprocessors & Microsystems, 2021](https://retractionwatch.com/2021/07/19/tortured-phrases-lost-in-translation-sleuths-find-even-more-problems-at-journal-that-just-flagged-400-papers/) | Nonsensical paraphrase patterns, images appropriated elsewhere and special-issue review weaknesses can pass as technical writing. | **R/A** for individual text and image claims; trace matched originals. Technical vocabulary may *camouflage* nonsense. |
| [1,500 off-topic papers in a materials proceeding, 2022](https://retractionwatch.com/2022/09/28/can-you-explain-what-these-1500-papers-are-doing-in-this-journal/) | Investigator found many papers outside stated disciplinary remit, including suspicious prose and titles reportedly advertised for author slots. | **R/A** for mill interpretation; disciplinary mismatch alone is not fraud. Inspect full papers. |
| [Five computer-generated papers, 2021](https://retractionwatch.com/2021/02/17/publisher-retracting-five-papers-because-of-clear-evidence-that-they-were-computer-generated/) | Publisher withdrew papers with recognizable SciGen/Mathgen/Physgen-style generated language: syntactically scientific but semantically empty. | **R/P.** Representative falsifiable example of jargon without an underlying mathematical result. |
| [126 medical “protocols” flagged after years, 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/02/20/medicine-journal-wolters-kluwer-flags-126-papers-researchregistry-protocol/) | Repeated suspicious registration metadata, implausible prose, very short submission-to-acceptance intervals; the journal placed expressions of concern after concerns were reportedly communicated years earlier. | **R/P for expressions of concern; A for full misconduct theory.** An EOC is **not** itself a retraction. |
| [Medical student and submission-ready evidence syntheses, 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/07/21/exclusive-medical-student-in-nepal-behind-busy-research-factory/) | Documented messages offering authorship in systematic reviews/meta-analyses or abstracts to those able to present, travel, or secure publishing-fee waivers. | **R/A.** Need original authorship contributions and provenance before calling a particular study fabricated. |
| [Research paper mill in Iraqi physicist publishing venture, 2025](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/09/19/exclusive-iraq-physicist-oday-al-owaedi-fired-ministry-publishing-scam/) | Reports payment terms and promised publication in indexed journals; document names a 400-paper service arrangement. Authors reportedly faced publication fees roughly exceeding local monthly pay. | **R/P for quoted agreement and dismissal reporting; A for disputed roles.** An instructive *buyer/seller/class* chain. |
| [Journals targeted by bogus submissions, Bohannon sting, 2013](https://retractionwatch.com/2013/10/03/science-reporter-spoofs-hundreds-of-journals-with-a-fake-paper/) | Deliberately deeply flawed manuscript submitted under fictitious author/institution identities. Some ostensibly reviewed outlets accepted it. | **R.** A targeted sting is a sample of responses to one deliberately flawed manuscript, not the failure rate of science. |
| [Attempt to republish a Nature paper in 602 journals, 2019](https://retractionwatch.com/2019/12/24/a-stress-test-for-journals-what-happened-when-authors-tried-to-republish-a-nature-paper-in-more-than-600-journals/) | **Important negative control:** only four journals accepted the undisclosed duplicate and all four were in the suspected-predatory stratum, according to the investigators. | **R.** Counters lazy extrapolation from a single sting. Report the 308 replies, nonresponse and different editorial dispositions. |

## 2. Manufactured prestige and the publisher's business

| Case | Mechanism / what the record supports | Status / needed independent check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Citation manipulation and affected journal metrics, 2012](https://retractionwatch.com/2012/07/05/a-first-papers-retracted-for-citation-manipulation/) | Editorial overlap and excessive citations used to boost ranking. The publisher disputed whether the conduct met the *cartel* label while acknowledging particular papers had improper citations. | **R/P for retraction and publisher statement; disputed group attribution.** Separate citation inflation from ordinary within-field references. |
| [How to spot a citation cartel, 2017](https://retractionwatch.com/2017/01/18/spot-citation-cartel/) | A proposed network-screening algorithm identifies unusually concentrated author–journal citation relationships. | **R/METHOD.** The developers explicitly say scores alone cannot establish a cartel; human review necessary. |
| [Surgical citation / reporting-guideline network, 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/03/12/riaz-agha-international-journal-surgery-research-registry-wolters-kluwer/) | Editor-directed citations, citations to the editor's guidelines and a paid registry overlap with the same journal network's finances and reported impact-factor growth. RW cites bibliometric estimates: three guidelines contributed 3.4 points of the journal's 15.3 impact factor in 2022. | **R with reported primary correspondence and ranking data.** Guideline content may be independently useful; don't infer fraud or illegality. Verify financial conflicts, citation instructions, original Web of Science counts. |
| [Citation-network / unusual h-index, 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/05/27/h-index-citation-networks-retractions-special-issues/) | Particular guest-edited issues had many papers withdrawn for inappropriate citations and review concerns; named parties disagreed over roles and denied involvement in rings. | **R/P and disputed attribution.** Distinguish abnormal graph structure from proof of coordination. |
| [Wiley paused Hindawi special issues, $9m lost revenue, 2023](https://retractionwatch.com/2023/03/09/wiley-paused-hindawi-special-issues-amid-quality-problems-lost-9-million-in-revenue/) | Company attributed **$9 million year-over-year quarterly revenue shortfall** to a publishing pause while reviewing compromised submissions. This is a direct measurable relation between article flow and cash. | **R/P** based on company's earnings release. Loss in *revenue*, not proof of $9m profit or revenue from misconduct. |
| [Journal of Intelligent and Fuzzy Systems, 2025](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/05/07/journal-intelligent-fuzzy-systems-jifs-sage-retracted-papers-revenue/) | Retraction Watch's fee-by-year reconstruction estimated **$427,850** in publication charges across **1,610** retracted articles. | **R/derived estimate.** No proof the fees were actually collected on every paper; some historical fees estimated. **Most charges predated Sage's acquisition** of IOS Press, per Sage spokesperson. |
| [Mathematics editors resign over publisher control, 2023](https://retractionwatch.com/2023/09/28/after-resigning-en-masse-math-journal-editors-launch-new-publication/) | Journal of Geometric Mechanics editors alleged special-issue and volume targets (fivefold publication expansion) undermined their authority; publisher cited fewer than ten subscriptions and financial sustainability. | **R/P for correspondence; disputed motives.** Concrete example of quality control and revenue policy being contested by insiders. |
| [Statistics and Computing associate editors resign, 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/07/09/the-exploitation-still-remains-stats-journal-associate-editors-resign-over-3000-publishing-charge/) | Editorial protest at a roughly $3,000 author fee after switch to full OA; especially salient for un-funded independent researchers. | **R/P for policy and resignation.** Fees create exclusion even if every article accepted is good. |
| [Review of Social Economy editorial resignations, 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/07/23/editors-of-economics-journal-resign-after-association-renews-taylor-francis-contract/) | Editorial leadership objected to its society's continuing dependence on publisher royalties; association disputed claims that fully APC-funded publication was planned. | **R, disputed interpretations.** Follow financial statements/contracts before assigning motive. |
| [French corporate responsibility journal editorial resignations, 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/09/11/editors-of-french-journal-on-corporate-responsibility-resign-after-ownership-transfer/) | After ownership transfer, €1,350 publication charges and a withdrawal charge were introduced without prior editors' approval. | **R/P for described charges and resignations.** No finding of a particular fraudulent paper. |
| [A paper mill money trail extends beyond a journal](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/09/08/article-broker-a-techo-china-journal-editors-publishing-deals/) | Article-broker pitching and allegations around author fees and editorial access; some alleged institutional identity connections were disputed as hijacked accounts. | **R/A.** Verify identity and transactions instead of inferring them from a name/address. |

## 3. The mathematics or inference itself fails

| Case | Mechanism / what the record supports | Status / needed independent check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Finance/accounting: reported methodology not the methodology used, 2019](https://retractionwatch.com/2019/11/13/the-methodology-does-not-generate-the-results-journal-corrects-accounting-study-with-flawed-methods/) | Special committee said published equations did not generate reported estimates. Actual specification included an economically unjustified omitted term; the paper's inferences were not robust using the described specification. | **R/P quoted journal committee.** Ideal Blackball test: execute printed regression on disclosed data/code; independent replication and baseline. |
| [Organizational psychology: wrong level of analysis, 2014](https://retractionwatch.com/2014/04/30/florida-leadership-researcher-walumbwa-notches-sixth-retraction/) | A paper on leadership, trust and group outcomes was retracted after incorrect calculation of fit statistics from an analysis-level error. Re-estimation did not support the claimed model. | **R/P quoted notice.** Contrast *a fitted model* with evidence for proposed latent social traits. |
| [Published data eventually turned into significant findings, Wansink case, 2017](https://retractionwatch.com/2017/02/02/backlash-prompts-prominent-nutrition-researcher-reanalyze-multiple-papers/) | The researcher's own account of turning an initially null experimental result into several papers led to objections of undisclosed exploratory searching, hypothesis reformulation and sample-number inconsistency. | **R/P for self-described workflow; criticism separately reported.** Exploration is not wrong; selling selected discoveries as preplanned confirmatory evidence is. |
| [A data-fabrication admission following p-value tampering, 2017](https://retractionwatch.com/2017/03/07/hindsights-bitch-colleagues-dissect-painful-retraction/) | Retraction Watch reported a laboratory investigation and collaborator account: a researcher duplicated/deleted cases to cross a conventional significance threshold. | **R/P** for reported admission/institutional investigation. A sharply identified mechanism, not a claim that all p-hacking is deliberate data fabrication. |
| [Management scholar admits falsification, 2018](https://retractionwatch.com/2018/03/21/marketing-researcher-admits-to-falsification-resigns/) | Publisher described author's admission to deliberately changing results in papers to improve apparent support for hypotheses. | **R/P for quoted acknowledgment**, not merely a suspicious-looking figure. |
| [Disputed factor-analytic interpretation in *Neuron*, 2014](https://retractionwatch.com/2014/04/14/anonymous-blog-comment-suggests-lack-of-confidentiality-in-peer-review-and-plays-role-in-a-new-paper/) | Invited preview authors raised concerns about the inferential use of factor analysis, and described difficulties getting critiques printed alongside the accepted article. Original author replied in the RW comments with substantive technical disagreement. | **R/A/disputed scientific analysis, NOT a retraction.** Read both Haier et al and Hampshire et al and reproduce the factor rotation/within-vs-between-group arguments. |
| [WHO-related infection prevention trial retraction, 2018](https://retractionwatch.com/2018/03/26/paper-used-to-support-who-guidelines-on-preventing-infections-has-no-scientific-validity/) | Retraction notice said independently examined statistical tests did not support apparently significant reported p-values. The study had been referenced in clinical guidance. | **R/P for retraction notice.** Link to the guideline version, exact recommendation and its post-retraction status. |
| [Voinnet paper retracts after changed statistical test, 2015](https://retractionwatch.com/2015/09/08/5th-retraction-for-voinnet-follows-correction-eoc-to-plos-genetics-paper/) | Reported previous use of t-tests where more than two datasets required a different comparison and related image errors; researchers withdrew central claim amid corrected analyses. | **R/P.** A formula that computes a number is not proof it is an appropriate test. |
| [Climate-impact economics paper corrected twice, 2015](https://retractionwatch.com/2015/07/22/second-correction-for-controversial-paper-on-economic-gains-of-climate-change/) | Multiple incorrectly transcribed inputs to a high-profile figure; uncertainty and noncomparability across the component economic studies challenged fitting a single headline curve. | **R/P for corrections; interpretation disputed.** Duplicate data, assumptions and fitted curve must be audited directly. |
| [P values and ordinary human sciences, Gelman interview, 2017](https://retractionwatch.com/2017/02/09/traditional-statistics-often-counterproductive-research-human-sciences/) | Explains selection on significance, weak measurements, forking analytical paths and why passing a threshold cannot retroactively validate study design. | **R/analysis** rather than a finding about any individual article. Use as a source for a *test*, not as universal condemnation. |

## 4. Bad data and downstream harm despite the seal

| Case | Mechanism / what the record supports | Status / needed independent check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Public clinical-ML training datasets for stroke/diabetes, 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/05/18/kaggle-dataset-clinical-models-stroke-diabetes/) | Analysts found thousands of duplicate patient records and implausibly tidy data without sound provenance. Three *Scientific Reports* papers had been retracted by report time, with more cases being examined. | **R/P for named notices; A for other papers.** The same fake/low-quality training data can contaminate multiple high-accuracy classifiers. |
| [Surgisphere and clinical severity scoring, 2020](https://retractionwatch.com/2020/06/06/group-withdraws-covid-19-scoring-tool-based-on-surgisphere-data-following-nejm-lancet-retractions/) | Highly prominent medical publications retracted after critical source data could not be examined; a clinical decision aid derived from those data was consequently withdrawn. | **R/P** for notices and medical organization's withdrawal. This proves insufficient audit access at a consequential interface; does not settle every underlying medical question. |
| [Paper mills contaminate systematic reviews and meta-analyses, 2026 study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0895435626003057) | Study of already-retracted systematic reviews/meta-analyses found **191 of 1,071** had *reported* paper-mill involvement. | **Secondary empirical study, conditioned on detection and retraction.** Not a 17.8% prevalence estimate among *all* clinical evidence syntheses. |
| [Paper-mill retractions across journals, BMJ 2022](https://www.bmj.com/content/379/bmj-2022-071517) | Cross-sectional study of **1,182** retractions involving suspected/identified paper-mill work, based on the RW database through June 2022. | **Secondary study** of observed retractions. Denominator cannot estimate undetected fraud; journal mix and inspection practices matter. |
| [Research integrity patterns among paper-mill retraction journals, 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41073-025-00177-9) | Identified 142 journals retracting 2,051 mill-linked articles in 2020–22, including concentration in a minority of journals. | **Secondary descriptive study.** Association not proof of cause; publication models and subject mixes confound simple comparisons. |

## How to use the ledger

- The *mechanism* is more transferable than the name: reverse-engineer the way a source acquired credibility and the exact point where a test was omitted or defeated.
- Preserve dissent: a flagged article may be vindicated; a politically or personally inconvenient critique is not automatically correct either.
- Distinguish field-level and journal-level selection effects. An increased retraction count might mean more wrongdoing, more publication, better policing, an audit backlog, or a new correction policy.
- Never infer that “not retracted” means scientifically sound. A failed independent reproduction test may be more directly relevant than an editorial notice.
- Re-read the original paper, code, tables, data and notices before giving an individual a scientific **verdict**. The RW article supplies leads and documented reporting, not a substitute for the examination.
