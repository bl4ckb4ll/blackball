# Validity audit: how to defeat a plausible-sounding technique

**Purpose.** Assess the *content* of a scientific or technical claim before its author's rank, journal, citations, publishing contract or persuasive prose. Applies to statistics, psychometrics, medicine, machine learning, and investment books. The historical investigations in [cases.md](cases.md) motivate tests; they do not execute these tests on a new claimant.

## Split five questions that are often blended together

1. **Mathematical correctness:** Is the equation correct under explicitly stated assumptions? Does the algorithm implement it?
2. **Novelty:** Does the invented name describe substantially the same object or algorithm known in linear algebra, probability, statistics, optimization or another field? Identify the exact mathematical equivalence and genuinely different assumptions, not a rhetorical similarity.
3. **Measurement validity:** What has been physically observed, recorded, labeled or constructed? Is the inferred “factor,” “ability,” “risk,” “signal,” “brain network” or “intelligence” a measured thing or only a parameter in a chosen model?
4. **Predictive/causal validity:** Does the model transfer to genuinely unseen subjects, times, institutions and interventions? Are the selection and leakage pathways closed? Does the inference survive alternative specifications and appropriate controls?
5. **Value and incentives:** Does use improve outcomes versus an explicit alternative after costs? Who sells it, who is required to buy it, who bears the losses, and who judges its success?

A method may pass 1 and 2, fail 3 and 4, and still succeed commercially at 5 *for its seller*. Equally, an honestly described exploratory analysis may be valuable without supporting a grand causal claim.

## Adversarial test sequence

| Gate | Required evidence | Rejection signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Claim extraction | Quote precisely what is asserted in original paper/book, not a review or PR slogan; include time, population, outcome, thresholds, assumptions. | “The model is sophisticated,” without specifying what it claims. |
| 1. Define objects | List directly observed data, representations, estimated parameters, inferred objects, institutional labels, and promised real-world outcomes in different columns. | A coordinate, factor loading, citation index, class label or estimated parameter is quietly promoted into an independent entity. |
| 2. Mathematical reduction | Rewrite in conventional mathematical language; test dimensions, invariances, limits, special cases, and short counterexamples; identify prior equivalent techniques. | Renaming a familiar regression, likelihood, factorization or model-selection operation is sold as evidence of novelty. |
| 3. Implementation fidelity | Can printed equations and pseudocode, applied to the stated inputs, generate *the published tables*? Re-run from raw data and exact versions. | Different equations, hidden features, data transformations or fitted parameters were actually used. See [finance/accounting example](https://retractionwatch.com/2019/11/13/the-methodology-does-not-generate-the-results-journal-corrects-accounting-study-with-flawed-methods/). |
| 4. Data provenance | Collect source, dates, population, units, patient/instrument IDs, selection/permission procedure, missingness, duplicates, raw images and label creation. | “It's a widely used public dataset” replaces a provenance statement. See [clinical ML case](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/05/18/kaggle-dataset-clinical-models-stroke-diabetes/). |
| 5. Statistical consistency | Independently reconstruct sample sizes, totals, summary statistics, reported p-values, uncertainty, confidence intervals and fit measures; inspect unit of analysis and repeated measurements. | Impossible denominators, p-values, group sums, wrong degrees of freedom or models. See [psychological fit-statistic retraction](https://retractionwatch.com/2014/04/30/florida-leadership-researcher-walumbwa-notches-sixth-retraction/). |
| 6. Search and selection | Demand the *number of trials*: tested hypotheses, endpoints, model architectures, features, combinations, hyperparameters, periods and excluded observations. | The winner alone is reported with a nominal p-value or Sharpe ratio, as if selected independently of the search. |
| 7. Leakage | Trace the timeline from each predictor through normalization, feature selection, target construction, fold assignment, future windows and evaluation. Require group/time independence appropriate to the task. | Random row split when individuals/time windows occur in both train and test; preprocessing fitted on the full sample. |
| 8. Negative controls | Shuffle labels, replace true signals with noise, run trivial predictors; specify which tests must fail. Use adversarial known-bad examples. | The “discovery” survives shuffled labels or can be generated by a null pipeline. |
| 9. Competing explanations | Compare with a simple linear baseline, constant predictor, unadjusted association, relevant domain control, and prior methods under *identical conditions*. | New method's success disappears once fair baselines or changed assumptions are imposed. |
| 10. Held-out prediction | Lock data, code, metric, evaluation populations and minimum meaningful effect **before** the final holdout. Require truly future or independently gathered observations when temporal deployment is claimed. | Performance only exists on revisited backtests or split choices selected after looking at test results. |
| 11. Decision utility | Compare net outcomes with alternatives, account for transaction costs, clinical errors, human costs and false-positive actions. | A change in AUC, p-value or fit statistic is presented as economic/social value without decision analysis. |
| 12. Follow the money | Identify contract terms, book/APC fees, editorial conflicts, grant dependence, patents, consulting, reporting requirements and who buys the score/citation. | A source that benefits from acceptance is treated as independent validation. |
| 13. Challenge the gatekeeper | Find critical responses, errors reported before publication, retraction/correction history, admissions, independent attempts to reproduce, and their treatment by editors. | Prominent status is used instead of an answer to the mathematical or data objection. |
| 14. Re-test the verdict | Blind reviewer to author and institution; apply same rubric to a famous work and obscure comparable work. Retain falsifying and favorable controls. | Same evidence receives a different technical verdict when prestige cues are swapped. |

## Two simple failure demonstrations

**Repeated people masquerading as independent ML examples.** Suppose records for a patient are duplicated across rows, and a classifier effectively learns a patient identifier. In a random *row* split, the same patient's label can be memorized in the training sample and recalled in test, yielding nearly perfect apparent generalization even when the labels have no predictive relationship to medical features. On an unseen-*patient* split, that memorization cannot predict the new labels; under independent balanced random labels, chance accuracy is 50%. This demonstrates the **structural** failure of row independence, without asserting any actual medical paper used precisely this toy construction. Patient-level splits can still fail for site, period, treatment or measurement confounding.

**A useful decomposition does not instantiate a latent thing.** If a covariance model uses a factor-loading matrix L, multiplying L by an orthogonal matrix Q preserves L Lᵀ because (LQ)(LQ)ᵀ = LLᵀ. Additional identification constraints may choose one parameterization; they do not, just by being imposed, prove that each factor is an independently existing psychological object. Likewise, writing a likelihood with a logit link specifies a probabilistic model, not an ontological result. Measure and test the substantive predictions, not the elegance of the parameterization.

An especially relevant reported case is the 2014 dispute over interpretation of component/factor analyses of human intelligence in *Neuron*. The critics and original author disagree. Blackball must inspect the technical arguments instead of treating the prestige of either journal or critic as a decisive observation. [Retraction Watch source and contrasting statements](https://retractionwatch.com/2014/04/14/anonymous-blog-comment-suggests-lack-of-confidentiality-in-peer-review-and-plays-role-in-a-new-paper/).

## Finance and Marcos López de Prado: a case to *audit*, not to certify

The existence of published work about backtest overfitting does not prove an author ran a profitable investment business, nor does its technical correctness establish outperformance for a client. In *Advances in Financial Machine Learning* (Wiley, 2018), Chapter 7 concerns purged cross-validation; his coauthored work with David Bailey addresses selection bias and the deflated Sharpe ratio. These are **objects of analysis** rather than an exemption from analysis:

- [Book contents, including cross-validation and backtesting](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/advances-in-financial/9781119482086/p03.xhtml).
- [Bailey and López de Prado, *The Deflated Sharpe Ratio*, 2014](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2460551).
- [His publication listing](https://www.quantresearch.org/Publications.htm), which establishes what is *claimed/published*, not performance.

**Required adversarial examination:**

- Take each named technique and derive it without its marketing vocabulary. For purging, specify each label's information interval, show exactly when training samples overlap test windows, and test for leakage from preprocessing, features, clustering, portfolio formation, corporate actions and repeated model selection even after purging.
- For *probabilistic*, *deflated* and related Sharpe measures, identify the null, assumptions about return distributions and dependence, effective number of attempts, selection, missing trading strategies, and whether inputs to the adjustment are externally observed or guessed.
- Audit empirical claims with all strategy candidates, release dates, raw market data, locked decisions, transaction/financing costs, risk exposure and a genuine subsequent period.
- Evaluate financial results **relative to the S&P 500 on a stated risk-adjusted basis**, as appropriate for the strategy's opportunity set, and report losses, drawdown, turnover, capacity, taxes where relevant, exposure and selection. A good-looking absolute Sharpe, theoretical correction or retrospective backtest does not suffice.
- Follow any paid course, book, proprietary tooling, consulting or investment product separately. What did *customers actually achieve net of fees*? Require the denominator (all clients/periods, not testimonials or surviving products).
- Preserve contrary evidence about his claims and any defense, and distinguish *mathematical error*, *empirically useless method*, *deceptive marketing*, *recklessness* and *intentional fraud*. Those conclusions require different proofs. The user's suspicion is a research lead, not itself a primary finding.

**Status: NOT VERIFIED.** No independent execution of these implementations, trading returns, sales claims or published empirical experiments was completed in this Retraction Watch reading pass. Do not say “totally plausible, therefore credible” or “published, therefore sound.” Equally do not assert a personal fraud finding without the particular evidence.

## Before Rasch

Do not begin with an exposition whose names and equations silently assert psychological abilities. Begin with the actual response data and a *precise* proposed measurement operation. Specify unidimensionality, conditional independence, invariance, population sampling, alternative explanatory models, and what observation would force rejection. Distinguish an identified statistical parameter from a validated unit of measurement or psychological cause.

The way to review Rasch is the same way to review purportedly revolutionary finance/medical methods: mathematical reduction → observable predictions → failure cases → independent tests → power and incentive audit. This branch establishes the framework; it has not yet executed the Rasch audit.

## Dispositions for each named paper

Maintain a decision record with one of:

- **Unexamined:** marketing or citation trail located, original material not inspected.
- **Specified:** exact claims, inputs, equations, payment and independent checks stated.
- **Counterexample:** formal contradiction or generated falsifying example, with the *exact assumption violated*.
- **Reproduced:** independent execution verified on same data and inputs.
- **Generalized:** independent holdout/replication passes against baseline.
- **Not corroborated:** attempted tests don't confirm; open questions and evidence preserved.
- **Disputed:** competing calculations or interpretation with live primary references.
- **Invalidated:** precise demonstrated inconsistency or notice, restricted to its supported scope.

Never equate “published”, “peer reviewed”, “highly cited”, “not retracted”, “retracted” or “under investigation” with one of these scientific outcomes without the accompanying examination.
