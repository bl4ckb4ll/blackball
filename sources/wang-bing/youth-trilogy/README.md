# Wang Bing — YOUTH trilogy

## Source status

**Source type:** long-form observational documentary trilogy.

**Director:** Wang Bing.

**Films:**

1. *Youth (Spring)* / *Qing Chun (Chun)* — 2023
2. *Youth (Hard Times)* / *Qing Chun (Ku)* — 2024
3. *Youth (Homecoming)* / *Qing Chun (Gui)* — 2024

The trilogy follows young migrant garment workers in and around the textile workshops of Zhili, Zhejiang, China. Distributor material describes the project as filmed over five years. The films should be treated as primary audiovisual observations selected and edited by a filmmaker, not as representative-sample statistics for Chinese garment labor.

Discovery lead supplied to Blackball:

- YouTube playlist: https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PL3i_CDwekCijWK_R5g1Q6C9CWqDQWFJvk

The playlist is a viewing/discovery route. It is not itself the provenance record for the films.

## Why Blackball cares

The trilogy is unusually useful for Blackball because it moves a large abstract argument — China, globalization, manufacturing, trade, labor costs, migration, capitalism, development — down to the level of actual people and an observable production system.

A viewer does not first have to agree on a theory of globalization or choose an ideological camp. The camera records young people sewing garments, negotiating piece rates, changing jobs, arguing with bosses and coworkers, eating, sleeping near work, dating, fighting, going home, marrying, and returning to the production town.

That does **not** replace economics. It establishes part of the human and institutional object that economics is supposed to explain.

For Blackball's public-facing work, this is an important model:

> **show first → annotate second → argue third**

A person can encounter the workers and the labor process before being asked to accept a conclusion about them.

## The trilogy

### 1. Youth (Spring) — 2023

Cannes lists *Qing Chun (Chun) (Youth (Spring))* as a 2023 feature directed by Wang Bing, produced as a France/Luxembourg/Netherlands production, and selected **In Competition**. Cannes gives a running time of 212 minutes.

Icarus Films gives a 215-minute running time for its release. Preserve the discrepancy rather than silently normalizing it; festival and distributor versions or cataloguing conventions may differ.

The distributor describes Zhili as a center of the children's garment industry and the workers as teenagers and people in their early twenties arriving from surrounding provinces. It describes small independent workshops located in the same concrete buildings as sparse worker dormitories. Its synopsis specifically identifies negotiations over piece-work rates, decisions to stay or quit, relationships, pregnancy, shared living conditions, and the overlap of work and ordinary youth.

Cannes's 2023 editorial note calls Zhili the Chinese garment capital and describes rural youth from along the Yangtze River coming there for work.

This first film is therefore valuable for:

- the physical organization of small-workshop garment production;
- work and housing occupying the same built environment;
- migrant youth entering industrial labor;
- piece-rate bargaining rather than a purely abstract wage variable;
- the coexistence of ordinary youth culture and difficult labor conditions;
- the amount of social life that occurs inside or immediately around production.

### 2. Youth (Hard Times) — 2024

Icarus Films lists *Youth (Hard Times)* as the second installment, 226 minutes, Mandarin with English subtitles, released in 2024.

Its synopsis makes labor conflict more explicit. It describes disputes over working conditions, a workshop owner beating an employee and fleeing, workers left without a boss or pay, and workers organizing around the workshop's sewing machines. These are unusually concrete scenes for Blackball because the relevant objects are visible: owner, worker, machine, pay obligation, workshop, and collective response.

Locarno's 2024 festival records place *Qing Chun (Ku) (Youth (Hard Times))* in the **Concorso Internazionale** as a world premiere. The festival's awards record gives it a jury Special Mention and the FIPRESCI Prize. The festival material is useful not because an award makes the film true, but because following a major festival program leads directly to a film with substantial Blackball relevance.

Blackball uses include:

- wage/payment conflict as an institutional relation rather than a number alone;
- ownership of productive equipment;
- what happens when the employer disappears or refuses an obligation;
- worker solidarity and its limits;
- mobility between workshops;
- the relation between an informal-looking workplace and larger structures of capital and state power.

Records:

- Icarus Films: https://www.icarusfilms.com/df-youht
- Locarno Film Festival 2024 annual report / awards record: https://www.locarnofestival.ch/dam/jcr%3A59cf5d62-f84f-4aac-8ca4-bc9c7d3166c6/LFF-RAPPORTO-ANNUALE-2024-EN.pdf

### 3. Youth (Homecoming) — 2024

Venice lists *Qing Chun: Gui (Youth: Homecoming)* in the **Venezia 81 Competition**, directed by Wang Bing, as a France/Luxembourg/Netherlands production. Venice gives a running time of 152 minutes. Icarus gives 160 minutes; again preserve the cataloguing/version discrepancy.

The Venice synopsis starts as the Lunar New Year break approaches and the Zhili workshops empty. Workers need to be paid in order to travel home. The film then follows journeys from the Yangtze region toward workers' hometowns, including Yunnan, and follows marriages before workers return to Zhili.

Icarus describes it as the final installment and says the trilogy was shot over five years. Its account emphasizes workers trying to secure pay before travel, journeys to rural homes, weddings, and return to Zhili after the holiday.

This matters because the labor process does not end at the factory door. The film connects production to:

- seasonal migration;
- remittances and family obligations;
- marriage and household formation;
- rural places that reproduce the urban industrial workforce;
- travel time and distance;
- the boundary between workplace, home, and family life.

Records:

- Venice / La Biennale: https://www.labiennale.org/en/cinema/2024/venezia-81-competition/qing-chun-gui-youth-homecoming
- Icarus Films: https://www.icarusfilms.com/df-youhc

## What the camera makes visible

### 1. Production is a social arrangement, not just an output number

The films make it possible to ask who owns the shop, who owns the machines, who obtains orders, who sets or negotiates rates, who carries nonpayment risk, where workers live, and how easily they can leave.

These questions are more informative than talking about a national labor-cost figure without knowing the mechanism underneath it.

### 2. Piecework changes the meaning of time

Where pay depends on pieces completed, 'hours worked' is not the whole employment relation. Speed, order flow, per-piece rates, delays, machine availability, defects, bargaining, fatigue, and the worker's ability to move to another shop can all matter.

Blackball should chase documentary/statistical sources for each of these mechanisms instead of turning a film scene directly into a population estimate.

### 3. Work and home are not cleanly separated

The dormitory/workshop arrangement matters. A conventional employment dataset may record occupation, earnings, and hours while missing how thoroughly the workplace structures eating, sleeping, friendship, courtship, conflict, and leisure.

### 4. 'Chinese labor' is made of individual workers

For an American trade or employment argument, one of the trilogy's strongest uses is simply to block an abstraction error. 'China' is not the person operating a sewing machine. A claim about competition with Chinese production should survive contact with the actual workers, employers, workshops, supply arrangements, households, and bargaining relations involved.

This is not a demand for sympathy as a substitute for analysis. It is a demand that the analysis identify the human beings and institutions it is talking about.

### 5. The worker's life extends beyond the labor market

The third film is especially useful here. Employment feeds into travel, kinship, marriage, household formation, and return migration. A model that stops at wages and employment can miss much of what work is for and much of what it costs.

## What the trilogy does not establish by itself

Do not promote these films into evidence for claims they cannot carry.

They do not by themselves establish:

- the prevalence of the depicted conditions across all of Zhili;
- the prevalence of those conditions across China's garment industry;
- a representative wage, hour, injury, turnover, or exploitation rate;
- who ultimately captures the margin on garments sold abroad;
- which brands or consumers are linked to the filmed workshops unless independently documented;
- the net effects of international trade on American or Chinese workers;
- whether tariffs, unions, industrial policy, migration restrictions, socialism, capitalism, or some other macro policy would improve the observed conditions;
- what happened outside the camera's presence;
- that editorial selection is neutral merely because the style is observational.

For prevalence and causal claims, attach independent labor statistics, supply-chain records, contracts/orders where recoverable, wage studies, migration data, trade data, and worker/employer testimony.

## Evidence status

Use three distinct labels when propagating material from the trilogy:

- **observed in film** — an event, spatial arrangement, conversation, or work process actually visible/audible in the film;
- **film/distributor claim** — contextual information supplied in synopsis, press kit, interview, or distribution material;
- **externally corroborated** — independently supported by documentary, statistical, archival, or scholarly evidence.

Do not silently move a claim from the first two categories into the third.

## Related Wang Bing trail

Icarus identifies *Youth (Spring)* as a successor to Wang Bing's *Bitter Money* (2017), which also follows rural workers moving to Huzhou for textile-factory work. That makes *Bitter Money* an immediate retrieval target rather than an unrelated recommendation.

Other Wang Bing work should be surveyed separately. Do not assume thematic relevance from the director's name alone; inspect each film.
