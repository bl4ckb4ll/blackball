# *Youth (Spring)* — Cannes 2023 press-kit notes

## Source

Festival de Cannes press kit for Wang Bing's *Youth (Spring)*:

https://cdn.festival-cannes.com/media/uploads/2023/05/159614.pdf

This document contains a Cannes-era interview with Wang Bing. Statements below are therefore **filmmaker statements**, not independently verified statistics merely because they appear in an official festival press kit.

Relevant original press-kit pages: pp. 3–7.

## Production method and access

Wang says that people he knew near Suzhou introduced him to contacts in Zhili, including workshop managers. He says this chain of introductions eventually gave him unusually free access to factories and dormitory blocks. That matters methodologically: the apparent intimacy of the footage was enabled by a specific access network, not by an invisible or institutionally random camera.

Wang says filming extended from **2014 to 2019** and produced roughly **2,600 hours of rushes** before the finished project was shaped in editing. He says sorting began in 2018, filming ended in spring 2019, and serious editing was delayed by the pandemic until 2021.

Blackball use:

- record the filming window separately from release year;
- treat the films as a very large selected archive rather than a short visit;
- ask how 2,600 hours were reduced to the released trilogy and whether interviews/Q&As explain the selection process;
- recover editor interviews or production records if available;
- do not infer that repeated exposure makes the resulting sample statistically representative.

## Seasonal structure

Wang describes Zhili garment production as seasonal. In the interview he says production generally pauses or stops between late February and June and resumes in July. He says the initial shooting period ran September 2014 to June 2015 and that the end of the first film corresponds to the end of the production season, when workers return home.

This supplies a concrete mechanism for the trilogy's movement from workshop life toward return migration. It should be checked against independent sources on the Zhili children's-garment industry.

## Scale claims that need source-chasing

Wang gives several quantitative claims in the interview:

- roughly **20,000 workshops** in Zhili;
- roughly **300,000 migrant workers** arriving each year;
- about **20,000 business owners**;
- roughly **400** owners in a year allegedly absconding with money, which he characterizes as about 2%;
- Zhili workshops allegedly supplying **80–90%** of China's domestic children's-clothing market.

These are high-value leads, but the press kit does not establish the underlying datasets in the passage inspected. Blackball should not reuse them as settled facts until the source trail is recovered.

Retrieval targets:

- Huzhou / Zhili local statistical yearbooks;
- Zhejiang provincial industrial statistics;
- Chinese garment-industry association material;
- scholarly work on Zhili's children's-wear cluster;
- migration/labor studies using Zhili as a field site;
- trade or market reports with an explicit denominator and year.

## Workshop organization claims

Wang contrasts Zhili's dispersed small workshops with larger garment factories elsewhere in the Yangtze Delta. He says production in Zhili is distributed among thousands of small individual or family businesses and that the average unit has fewer than 20 workers.

He also describes families, relatives, neighbors, or groups from the same village traveling to Zhili and sometimes working together in one workshop.

This is a useful hypothesis about how labor recruitment and social trust operate:

**village / kin network → migration → workshop recruitment → dormitory/workplace social group**

That mechanism should be compared with migration ethnographies rather than accepted solely from the filmmaker interview.

## Hours, piece rates, and deferred payment

On p. 5 Wang describes the workshops as private firms with very long days. He gives a typical schedule of approximately **8 a.m. to 11 p.m.**, with one-hour lunch and dinner breaks. He says wages are computed on a **piece-rate** basis and paid out at six-month intervals, and further claims that workers may begin producing an item before learning the final per-piece rate.

These are among the most important claims in the press kit because they suggest that the worker's risk is not captured by a simple hourly-wage comparison.

Potential mechanism:

**order arrives → worker produces piecework → unit price remains uncertain → payment is deferred → worker bears rate/nonpayment risk until season settlement**

Before Blackball generalizes this mechanism, recover:

- worker contracts or evidence that written contracts are absent;
- wage-payment rules applicable in Zhejiang during the filming period;
- independent worker testimony;
- labor-law enforcement records;
- scholarly descriptions of Zhili piece-rate systems;
- whether payment practice changed between 2014–2019 and film release.

## Informal credit / production finance claim

Wang describes an interlocking deferred-payment system in which a workshop owner can obtain fabric without immediate full payment, employ workers whose wages are not settled until later, sell finished garments to buyers who themselves may defer part of payment, and then settle obligations down the chain after goods are sold.

This is potentially a major Blackball mechanism because it identifies **who finances production by waiting to be paid**.

Instead of treating finance as only bank lending, the production chain may involve trade credit and wage deferment:

**fabric supplier credit + worker wage deferment + buyer credit → workshop production before final cash settlement**

This needs independent verification. If supported, Blackball should compare the cost and risk borne by:

- fabric suppliers;
- workshop owners;
- workers;
- downstream buyers;
- logistics providers.

The question is not just 'what is the wage?' but **who is involuntarily or voluntarily extending credit to whom, for how long, and with what recourse after default?**

## Director interpretation versus observable mechanism

The interview also contains broad interpretations by Wang about private enterprise, the Chinese state, banks, trust, reputation, regional commercial culture, and Zhili as an unusual economic system.

Keep these in the **filmmaker interpretation** lane. Some are strong, sweeping claims and should not be promoted to externally corroborated facts without substantial source-chasing.

The narrower mechanisms are more immediately reusable:

- dispersed small workshops;
- migrant recruitment through family/village networks;
- long and seasonal work cycles;
- piece-rate compensation;
- delayed settlement;
- production finance through deferred obligations;
- work/dormitory proximity;
- return migration at the end of the season.

Those mechanisms can each be independently tested.

## Why this press kit matters to the documentary-research lane

The film alone shows a great deal, but the press kit reveals the research value of festival infrastructure. A festival record can supply:

- filming dates;
- production history;
- filmmaker access method;
- editing history;
- claims that can be converted into retrieval targets;
- related earlier films;
- a map of which apparent mechanisms need independent corroboration.

That is exactly why Blackball should mine festival press kits and Q&As, not merely festival film titles.
