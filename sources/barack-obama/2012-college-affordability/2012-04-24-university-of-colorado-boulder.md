## Investment wording

Early in the address, Obama said:

> college isn’t just the best investment you can make in your future

The continuation explicitly calls it the best investment in the *country's future*, too. This is the **the best** superlative, stronger than the **one of the best** form also used at UNC earlier that day. “Best investment you'll ever make” is a remembered paraphrase, not an exact transcript quotation.

## Bounded claim ledger

| ID | What Obama asserted | What the transcript actually establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CO-01 | College is the best individual and national-future investment. | He publicly made the claim; no comparison of all feasible private investments or heterogeneous student returns was provided. |
| CO-02 | Unemployment for Americans with college degrees or more was about half the national average. | A 2012 population-level **asserted statistic**; not a causal effect or personal job forecast. |
| CO-03 | Degree holders' incomes were twice those of people lacking high-school diplomas. | The selected comparison is not with otherwise comparable high-school graduates, and does not incorporate tuition, noncompletion, or foregone earnings. |
| CO-04 | A University of Colorado degree would be a student's best tool for attaining family, housing, and retirement security. | Campus-directed aspirational claim; no Colorado program-level placement study appears in this speech. |
| CO-05 | Student borrowers then left college owing about $25,000 on average, and debt postponed homes, enterprise, family, and retirement saving. | Borrowers, **not all graduates**. Underlying source/denominator not independently checked here. |
| CO-06 | He and Michelle Obama had substantial educational debt, earlier payments exceeding their home mortgage payments, and paid off their loans around 2004. | A first-person autobiographical illustration, not a representative return calculation. |
| CO-07 | Unless Congress acted, an expiring rate cut would let the relevant Stafford rates double on July 1, adding an estimated $1,000 to the average affected student's debt per year. | Conditional contemporary forecast; he described over 7 million students nationwide and almost 170,000 in Colorado as affected. Not a claim about all loans or current rates. |
| CO-08 | Expand access and aid, restrain tuition costs, change federal lending, pressure Congress on rates. | Administration and legislative positions as he presented them; implementation and outcomes need separate verification. |

## What surrounded the investment sentence

The address moves from national credential-group averages to the American promise of home, family, and intergenerational prosperity; recounts the GI Bill, his mother's grants, and his and Michelle Obama's loans; recognizes tuition inflation and debt hardship; and then argues for grant/lending changes and prevention of the July interest increase. He explicitly asks students to contact Congress via **#DontDoubleMyRate**, and criticizes Republican fiscal positions. Removing that mobilization goal would materially change the speech's interpretation.

## Open questions

1. What are the underlying 2012 statistical sources, age adjustments, enrollment/completion denominators, and definitions of earnings?
2. For a prospective student, what is the distribution of *net* lifetime gains, including debt, noncompletion, opportunity cost, and job/major matching?
3. Does the “best” claim hold against an apprenticeship, direct employment, community college, or another feasible opportunity?
4. Which of the concrete policy measures were subsequently enacted, and how did their effects differ from the broader investment proposition?

**Evidence classification:** first-party historical speech record. Strong for wording and contemporaneous advocacy; insufficient by itself for the truth of private-return superlatives.
