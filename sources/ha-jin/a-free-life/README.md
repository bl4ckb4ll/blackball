# Ha Jin — A Free Life

*A Free Life* (2007) is a literary source for several Blackball questions:
education, migration, professional expectation, ordinary work, family
obligation, artistic vocation, business ownership, health insurance, and the
amount of protected time a person can preserve for intellectual work.

The source matters because it puts those elements in the same sustained
narrative. It does **not** turn one fictional protagonist into a population
estimate.

## Nan Wu

Nan Wu is the novel's protagonist, not a survey respondent or biographical
stand-in whose whole career can be attributed to Ha Jin.

The inspected *Bostonia* profile makes the comparison useful precisely because
it also marks differences. It describes Nan as taking years of hard, menial
work, buying and running a restaurant, later selling it, taking night-watchman
work with health insurance, and continuing to write poetry. Ha Jin explicitly
contrasts his own path with Nan's: Jin stayed in school and did not own and run
a restaurant.

That gives Blackball two distinct source types:

- **novel evidence**: what *A Free Life* represents through Nan;
- **author-comment evidence**: what Ha Jin says about similarities and
  differences between his life and Nan's.

Neither source type establishes prevalence.

## Why Blackball should retrieve this novel

Questions the novel can sharpen include:

- What can substantial education and intellectual ambition mean when available
  work is service, manual, or small-business work?
- What happens to artistic work when a household must first secure income,
  housing, schooling, insurance, and business survival?
- How does education function differently as cultivation, status, and an
  occupational promise?
- How should ordinary restaurant or service work be represented without using it
  merely as shorthand for "failure"?
- What constraints come from migration, language, legal status, social networks,
  political rupture, and family responsibility?
- What changes when art is a vocation but not an adequate income source?
- Who gets protected time for writing, and through what material arrangement?

These are retrieval questions. The answers require both close reading and, when
they become empirical, independent data.

## Syllabus evidence

The University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa Department of English lists *A Free Life* as
a primary text in a seminar on Asian diasporic literature:

https://english.hawaii.edu/course/course-2159/

That proves one documented teaching use. It does not establish frequency across
universities.
