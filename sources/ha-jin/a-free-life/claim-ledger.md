## D. Blackball questions / inferences

These are questions to investigate, not facts established by the novel:

- How often do highly educated immigrants work in occupations that do not use
  their formal training?
- How much does small-business ownership increase or reduce autonomous time?
- How strongly do health-insurance arrangements constrain occupational choice?
- How often does education function mainly as cultivation rather than as a
  reliable occupational route?
- Which classes of workers can preserve time for art, study, or research?
- Which household resources make such protected time possible?

Each empirical question needs its own population, time period, denominator, and
source base.

## E. Claims the novel does not justify by itself

Do **not** cite *A Free Life* alone for:

- immigrant earnings distributions;
- graduate-school placement rates;
- restaurant-worker prevalence;
- probabilities of downward occupational mobility;
- prevalence of artistic aspiration among immigrants;
- causal effects of higher education;
- causal effects of immigration on occupational status.

The book can motivate those questions and supply a literary representation. It
cannot supply their denominators.
