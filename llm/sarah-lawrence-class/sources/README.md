# Source map and credit ledger — Sarah Lawrence, class, and the “Sarah Lawrence girl”

This is the evidence boundary for the Sarah Lawrence class/cultural-type dossier.

A recurring problem here is that **the stereotype itself is evidence**. A hostile or romanticized description can be valuable evidence that a cultural type existed while being poor evidence about the actual student population.

## B. Contemporary founding-era journalism

https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,729789,00.html

**Use:** contemporary public account of the founding. It says William Van Duzer Lawrence feared educated women were avoiding what he regarded as their “real career”—marriage—and describes the planned institution, tuition, and curriculum in the language of the period.

**Current state:** **inspected**.

**Caution:** *Time* is a journalistic intermediary. Use Lawrence’s own correspondence when exact intent matters.

## C. The 1963 “Sarah Lawrence girl” portrait

### Paul S. Cowan and L. Geoffrey Cowan, “Expansion Threatens Sarah Lawrence Ideal,” *The Harvard Crimson*, March 9, 1963

https://www.thecrimson.com/article/1963/3/9/expansion-threatens-sarah-lawrence-ideal-psarah/

**Use:** unusually direct period evidence for Sarah Lawrence as an elite cultural type. The article opens with the formulation **“a classless community for the very rich,”** describes high tuition and few scholarships, and then constructs a representative “Sarah Lawrence girl” around individuality, intellectual self-consciousness, sexual/social sophistication, and identification with serious women writers.

**Current state:** **article inspected**.

**Caution:** this is a Harvard student-newspaper profile with all the biases of period journalism. Its descriptions are strong evidence for the *stereotype* and contemporary perception; they are not population statistics.

Thank Paul S. Cowan and L. Geoffrey Cowan for leaving a detailed period record of how Sarah Lawrence’s pedagogy, wealth, and student image were being interpreted in 1963—even where the article’s language and assumptions now require critical distance.

## D. Joan Didion and Salinger

### Joan Didion, “Finally (Fashionably) Spurious,” *National Review*, November 18, 1961

Original review of J. D. Salinger’s *Franny and Zooey*.

Accessible secondary/reprint routes:

- https://www.enotes.com/topics/franny-zooey/criticism/criticism/joan-didion-review-date-1961
- https://bookmarks.reviews/a-self-help-book-for-sarah-lawrence-girls-joan-didion-on-franny-and-zooey/

**Use:** the key literary-critical bridge. Didion class-positions Salinger’s spiritual/intellectual crisis as upper-middle-class and uses “Sarah Lawrence girls” as the compressed social label.

**Current state:** **substantial reprint/secondary presentation inspected; original National Review issue should still be retrieved for archival completeness**.

Thank Didion for making the class inference explicit rather than treating Salinger’s characters’ disgust with status as proof that they stand outside status.

## E. Anne Roiphe and sexual nonconformity as public stereotype

### Anne Roiphe, “The Trouble at Sarah Lawrence,” *The New York Times Magazine*, March 20, 1977

**Use:** evidence that by the late 1970s Sarah Lawrence could be publicly represented through anxiety about visible lesbian life and changing female norms.

**Current state:** **bibliographic identity confirmed; direct full article not yet inspected in this repository**.

Independent confirmations / later context:

- 1977 psychoanalytic bibliography confirming title/date: https://guilfordjournals.com/doi/10.1521/jaap.1.1977.5.4.499
- 2023 *Nation* historical discussion of the Times’s treatment of gay people: https://www.thenation.com/article/society/new-york-times-trans-coverage-gay-rights-history/

**Caution:** do not use Roiphe’s piece as neutral evidence for the percentage of lesbian students. Its historical value here is partly as an artifact of public anxiety and stereotype production.

## G. Porochista Khakpour — class experience from inside Sarah Lawrence

### Porochista Khakpour, *Sick: A Memoir* excerpt, “A New Yorker, and a Sick Person,” Longreads, June 7, 2018

https://longreads.com/2018/06/07/a-new-yorker-and-a-sick-person-2/

**Use:** first-person account of arriving at Sarah Lawrence in 1996 as a scholarship student. Khakpour remembers the college as a rich-girl liberal-arts environment and recalls classmates explicitly talking about their fathers subsidizing scholarship students.

**Current state:** **excerpt inspected**.

Thank Khakpour for recording the class boundary from the position of somebody who crossed it rather than only from the viewpoint of affluent observers.

## H. Jamie Jordan — an explicit genealogy of the “Sarah Lawrence girl”

### Jamie Jordan, *Ghosts & Dreams*, Sarah Lawrence College MFA in Writing, submitted December 2023

Institutional repository PDF:

https://scholarlyworks.slc.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/98ba0b16-37a6-4165-bf62-31590af97c2f/content

**Use:** Jordan explicitly distinguishes two types:

1. an early **“Lawrence girl”** associated with wealth, whiteness/WASP identity, and the founder’s gendered marital ideal;
2. a later **“Sarah Lawrence girl”** associated with intellectual/artistic independence and figures such as Carly Simon, Yoko Ono, and Lesley Gore.

Jordan then makes Anne Roiphe’s 1977 article part of the type’s later history.

**Current state:** **relevant section around p. 45 inspected through the institutional repository**.

**Caution:** this is an MFA manuscript blending memoir, cultural criticism, and historical reconstruction. Its claims about the founder, Jewish quotas, and Roiphe should be followed upstream to archival/period sources—which this dossier is doing.

Thank Jordan for naming the historical split in the type instead of treating “Sarah Lawrence girl” as one unchanged object across a century.
