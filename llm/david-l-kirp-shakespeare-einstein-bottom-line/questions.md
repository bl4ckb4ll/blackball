## Questions the dossier should eventually answer precisely

### Prestige

- What does Kirp mean by prestige: reputation, rank, student selectivity, faculty reputation, or an interacting bundle?
- Which causal arrows are documented rather than assumed?
- Which evidence comes from Roger Geiger, McPherson/Schapiro, Frank/Cook, or others rather than Kirp?
- Does the prestige mechanism differ between elite research universities and lower-status tuition-dependent institutions?

### Class / selection

- To what extent does Kirp treat higher education as producing education versus allocating positional advantage?
- Does he distinguish institutions selling instruction from institutions selling access to a prestige hierarchy?
- Which parts of the admissions market actively construct the class signal later attributed to student “merit”?

### Internal markets

- Exactly how did USC and Michigan assign tuition revenue and costs to units?
- What behavior changed after the accounting rule changed?
- Which failures were accounting artifacts, which were genuine demand signals, and which were governance failures?
- What coordination mechanisms replaced or constrained RCM later?

### Labor

- In the NYU case, what is the empirical relationship between star hiring and contingent teaching rather than merely their chronological coexistence?
- Which salaries, teaching shares, or employment conditions were measured, and for what year?
- What did graduate-worker unionization change in Kirp's interpretation?

### Public subsidy and professional schools

- What was the exact decline in state support Kirp uses in the Darden argument and what is the denominator?
- Does self-financing increase educational autonomy from legislatures while simultaneously increasing dependence on corporate/student customers?
- Which schools can plausibly self-finance, and what happens to fields that cannot?

### Knowledge ownership

- Which contracts imposed secrecy, publication delay, patent ownership, or database restrictions?
- Were those restrictions exceptional, typical, or temporary?
- Did they measurably change scientific output or merely create governance risk?

### Open knowledge

- Was MIT OpenCourseWare financially sustainable because it functioned as advertising/prestige rather than a tuition product?
- What did Columbia Fathom actually sell, who bought it, and why did the business model fail?
- Separate course *materials* from course *instruction*, credentialing, tutoring, assessment, and community.

### Vocational / for-profit education

- Which things does Kirp think DeVry or certification providers do better than conventional universities?
- Which things are lost under centralized customer-service management?
- What is the difference between useful occupational responsiveness and allowing employer demand to define the whole curriculum?

## Model-use warnings

- Do not convert `Kirp reports X at institution Y around 2000` into `universities do X`.
- Do not use a review's page quotation as proof that the reviewer agrees with the quoted claim.
- Do not use current institutional conditions to “correct” a historical case without clearly separating then from now.
- Do not infer causation from a chapter's juxtaposition unless Kirp or the upstream evidence actually supports the causal step.
- Do not collapse prestige, revenue, profit, price, cost, selectivity, and class into synonyms.
- Do not attribute collaborators' chapters solely to Kirp.
- Do not erase interviewees, administrators, or upstream scholars when their testimony/data carries the factual load.
