## Primary material added to the evidence graph

### Kirp, “The Corporation of Learning: Nonprofit Higher Education Takes Lessons from Business”

Open UC Berkeley Center for Studies in Higher Education paper, May 2003:

https://cshe.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/publications/rop.kirp.5.03.pdf

This is unusually useful because Kirp states his method directly. The paper focuses on USC resource-center management and UVA privatization, says that the forthcoming book uses **more than twenty cases**, and explicitly says those cases are not representative in a statistical sense. They are used as revealing or prospective cases.

That creates two Blackball rules:

1. `cases.md` is currently a **chapter-level case-cluster index**, not an exhaustive inventory of every case in the book.
2. Do not convert Kirp’s case studies into prevalence estimates or claims that a described mechanism is typical of all universities.

The paper also makes the balancing principle explicit: Kirp is not arguing that universities can leave markets behind. His question is what institutional counterweight protects scholarly community, openness, inquiry, and public-service goods when market incentives point elsewhere.

**Evidence state:** primary Kirp source inspected; parallel to important parts of chapters 6–7 and to the book’s concluding frame, but not a substitute for those book sections.

### Kirp + Holman, “This Little Student Went to Market”

Published excerpt/version in *The American Prospect*, September 16, 2002:

https://prospect.org/2002/09/16/little-student-went-market/

This remains the strongest openly inspectable primary source for the chapter-1 enrollment-management argument. It directly exposes the category translations among recruiting, advertising, aid/pricing, curriculum/product design, prestige, and strategic use of aid.

**Evidence state:** substantial primary coauthor excerpt inspected.

### Berkeley, *The Novartis Agreement: An Appraisal*

Near-contemporaneous University of California, Berkeley review:

https://newsarchive.berkeley.edu/news/media/releases/2004/07/admin_novartis_review.pdf

The appraisal is valuable because it tests several claims that can otherwise be flattened into “corporate money caused secrecy.” It distinguishes blanket research confidentiality from nondisclosure attached to proprietary Novartis tools/data; records the first-right-to-negotiate patent arrangement; describes pre-publication review and possible patent-related delay; and explicitly cites Kirp and Berman’s 2002 *California Monthly* article.

Use this report as an institutional check on specific contract terms. Do not treat an institutional review as neutral merely because it is primary documentation; compare it with the agreement, faculty-governance material, and independent scholarship.

**Evidence state:** upstream/near-contemporaneous institutional source inspected for the chapter-11 evidence graph.

## Coverage gaps that matter most

The dossier now has broad structural coverage, but book-text coverage remains shallow. The next source work should be driven by the gaps that can change conclusions, not by easy-to-find snippets:

- inspect the complete conclusion, because it is Kirp’s own cross-case synthesis rather than another institutional example;
- map all 50 pages of notes to distinguish fieldwork, institutional records, journalism, scholarship, and Kirp’s interpretation;
- expand the 13 chapter headings into the **more-than-twenty-case** inventory Kirp says the book actually uses;
- retrieve the parallel/open publications by chapter collaborators where they exist;
- follow USC/Michigan, UVA/Darden, Berkeley/Novartis, Columbia/MIT, Open University, and DeVry claims upstream into contemporary institutional records;
- preserve skeptical reception and disagreement rather than using praise or criticism as a vote count.

## Completion rule

Do not call this book “covered” merely because every chapter has a row here. A defensible high-coverage state requires, at minimum:

- every chapter and the conclusion inspected in full;
- the notes mapped claim-by-claim for the major mechanisms;
- the more-than-twenty underlying cases enumerated rather than collapsed into 13 chapter labels;
- important numerical and causal claims checked against their upstream source where recoverable;
- collaborator credit preserved;
- serious disagreements retained explicitly.

Until then, answers should say which layer they are using: chapter text, excerpt, parallel Kirp/coauthor publication, review reconstruction, institutional record, or later corroboration/critique.
