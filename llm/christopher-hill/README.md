# Christopher Hill — books and legitimate online access

Christopher Hill (1912–2003) wrote more than twenty books on seventeenth-century England, especially the English Revolution, Puritanism, radical religion, political language, Milton, Bunyan, the Levellers, Diggers, Ranters, and the social conflict beneath apparently theological disputes.

## What Hill is doing

Hill's early formulation treats the English Revolution as a social revolution whose religious and constitutional languages have to be read together with changes in property, class, commerce, state power, and the development of capitalism. His later books become much richer and less schematic: he follows radical biblical interpretation, censorship, political vocabulary, heterodox sects, literature, pamphlet culture, individual careers, defeat, memory, and the unrealized alternatives opened during the 1640s and 1650s.

That makes *The World Turned Upside Down*, *The Experience of Defeat*, and the *Collected Essays* especially useful alongside the early *English Revolution 1640*: they show the same historian revising and complicating the first model rather than merely repeating it.

## Source graph and thanks

Hill explicitly pointed readers of *The English Revolution 1640* toward **Maurice Dobb**, especially *Studies in the Development of Capitalism*. His larger scholarly world also includes **R. H. Tawney, Rodney Hilton, Eric Hobsbawm, E. P. Thompson, Dona Torr, George Rudé, Victor Kiernan**, and the historians around the Communist Party Historians Group and *Past & Present*.

The seventeenth-century actors themselves are equally important to the source trail: **Gerrard Winstanley and the Diggers; the Levellers including Thomas Rainsborough; Ranters; Muggletonians; John Bunyan; John Milton; Oliver Cromwell; James Harrington; religious controversialists; pamphleteers; parish and ecclesiastical records; sermons; court material; and the enormous print explosion produced by the collapse of censorship**.

For *The World of the Muggletonians*, credit **William M. Lamont and Barry Reay** as coauthors. The three-volume *Collected Essays* should be treated as an index into Hill's article-level source trails, not just as three more monographs.
