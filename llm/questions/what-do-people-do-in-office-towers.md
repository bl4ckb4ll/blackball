# What do people do in Manhattan's office towers?

Status: question map and primary-source retrieval queue (2026-10-14). This is not a finding that office work is useless or harmful.

## Central question

**What exactly do people do in New York City's tall office towers, why does it pay so much, and is the work socially beneficial, harmful, or largely redistributive?**

An occupation's compensation, firm's revenue, economic contribution, physical production, and social value are different variables. Do not use one as evidence of another.

**Corporate bankruptcy law is one example, not the subject as a whole.** The specific oil-and-gas case work already lives in [issue #157](https://github.com/bl4ckb4ll/blackball/issues/157); this broader investigation is tracked in [issue #159](https://github.com/bl4ckb4ll/blackball/issues/159).

## Method: walk the city and inspect the work

Eventually follow real streets and buildings. For every building studied:

1. Fix a **building address, date, and source for tenant occupancy**. Firms move. Do not mix tenant lists from different years.
2. Identify firms, departments, and occupations operating there. A brand or a floor lease alone tells little about daily labor.
3. Recover a **particular paid assignment**: contract, court motion, transaction, placement, advertisement, consulting engagement, financing or other documented deliverable.
4. Identify who pays whom, what fee or wage is at stake, the client's objective, the work performed, and any outcome visible in primary records.
5. Identify other parties affected. Count both gains and losses; distinguish claimed benefit, proposed change, legally authorized change, executed change and realized cash.
6. Record alternatives and counterexamples; preserve work of genuinely useful coordination and successful prevention of harm alongside adverse cases.

Prefer original filings, SEC disclosures, campaign artifacts, advertisements, client contracts, agency/practitioner documents, financial statements, payment and distribution records, dated tenant directories. A job description or executive biography cannot substitute for a documented transaction.

### Case record

```text
address / building
```

## First occupations

**Bankruptcy law.** What did an attorney secure or defeat on a concrete matter? Who received money and who did not? Cross-reference [the fracking bankruptcy investigation](https://github.com/bl4ckb4ll/blackball/issues/157). Distinguish attorneys' fees from amounts collected by clients and affected landowners; legally preserved rights do not prove collection.

**Advertising, marketing and public relations.** What is sold: information about an available product, created desire, persuasive identity, reputational insulation, or market power? Examine individual campaigns, the client's payment and resulting observable behavior. Possible social values are useful product information, discovery and creative expression; possible harms include manipulated preferences, false beliefs and demand for harmful products. Test rather than infer.

- *Mad Men*: fictional depiction of agency work and clients; useful for generating questions, not historical or economic proof.
- [Hugh MacLeod's Gapingvoid interview](https://www.gapingvoid.com/path-interview-with-hugh-macleod/): self-description of moving from a New York advertising job into cartoons and marketing. First-person account; not industry prevalence evidence.
- [MacLeod, “The Spaghetti Factory” (2016)](https://medium.com/@gapingvoid/the-spaghetti-economy-d8f66d08c8ad): a practitioner's allegation that young agency employees produced campaigns for low pay and firms captured disproportionate credit/rewards. Treat as bounded testimony, then seek payroll/contracts and corroboration.

**Executive recruiters.** Find specific mandates, sourcing/research tasks, initial contact, screening, placements, fees, employer/employee outcomes and counterfactual hiring channels. They may reduce matching/search costs or merely control access to opportunities. Do not assert either without evidence. Distinguish the occupation's work from the employer's productive business.

**Banking, finance and management consulting.** Use deal agreements, filings, case records and actual client work products when obtainable. Was the assignment capital coordination, operational improvement, monopoly protection, claim enforcement, tax or regulatory arbitrage, wealth transfer or something else?

**Educational and credential markets.** Degree holders may earn money teaching or tutoring the next cohort. Ask whether the system transmits useful skill, enables paid work, or sells repeated preparation for credentials whose economic returns are uncertain. See [Shorris, poverty, and the tutoring feedback loop](../../education/shorris-poverty-tutoring-and-material-outcomes.md).

## Production elsewhere, income here

Compare the pay and prestige of workers arranging claims, advertising goods, changing ownership or recruiting executives with those who physically manufacture and repair the underlying goods. Study outsourcing and historical racialized/exploited labor through dated contracts, wages, conditions and rights rather than a crude claim that all office labor is unproductive.

## Evidence discipline

Keep **private profitability**, **measured economic output**, **social usefulness**, **distribution of gains/losses**, and **moral judgment** separate. Each can differ. An elite credential, expensive office, salary or occupational label is not an observed service.

Do not generalize from one bad court filing, advertisement or recruiter. Do not infer a private person's character from a degree or unverified anecdote. Report when the original records cannot establish who benefited or what was produced.
