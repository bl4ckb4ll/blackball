# What are students buying from elite mathematics?

**Status:** OPEN CHALLENGE — question recorded, not answered.
**Recorded:** 2026-10-25.
**Origin:** First-person question and observation; no external evidence reviewed.

## The challenge

> What are students buying when they purchase proximity to the Institute for Advanced Study (IAS) or the Harvard mathematics department?
>
> I went to a talk about SO(3) gauge theory, webs, foams, and the four-color theorem.
>
> Almost everyone will not end up doing this work. Proximity, prestige, perhaps entertainment for some — but what are we really buying?
>
> The IAS can present this work as necessary or valuable, as serving “humanity” or representing the peaks of the human race. That is also a fundraising strategy.

## Preserve the question rather than prematurely answer it

- The talk is a concrete prompt, **not** evidence that it lacked mathematical value. Speaker, title, venue, date, and the specific mathematical claims were not supplied.
- Proximity, prestige, entertainment, intellectual participation, actual training, and prospects of doing this work are **possibilities to distinguish**, not an asserted list of what everyone gets.
- Keep **students**, **seminar attendees**, **research visitors**, **faculty**, **donors**, and **institutions** distinct. IAS and Harvard mathematics have different institutional relationships to students and payment; do not pretend they sell identical products.
- Keep the scientific/mathematical merits of gauge-theoretic work distinct from institutional promotion and the use of prestige to attract students, affiliation, money, and audiences.
- Do not infer a population rate, motive, economic benefit, or outcome from the single talk.

**Open research question:** What, precisely, is the good or service offered, who pays or bears the cost, who receives the benefits, and what share of the institutional story describes what most participants actually receive?
