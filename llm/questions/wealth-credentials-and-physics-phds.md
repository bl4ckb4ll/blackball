# Do wealth, credentials, and a physics PhD establish intelligence?

Status: open question and evidence protocol; **not** a claim about the prevalence of incompetence among physicists.

Recorded question: 2026-10-13.

## The claim Blackball must be willing to investigate

A physics PhD can be wrong, conceptually confused, inarticulate, or unable to explain mathematics it routinely uses. A person who earns substantial money can be unintelligent, technically poor, or lucky. The possibility cannot be excluded by invoking the person's doctorate, employer, salary, selectivity, or the general reputation of physics graduates as "practical."

The converse does not follow: criticism of one physicist does not establish the competence distribution of physics PhDs, and a low-paid mathematician is not automatically more capable. **Neither wealth nor poverty certifies intelligence.** Treat claims about ability as questions about demonstrated understanding, not social position.

An especially important failure is answering a concrete mathematical criticism with a prestige defense: "But physicists are smart / useful / highly paid / hired by hedge funds." That changes the subject.

## Distinct questions that should not be collapsed

1. **Mathematical understanding:** Can this particular person define the objects, state the assumptions, distinguish different structures, identify the scope of a theorem, and correct an error?
2. **Practical technical performance:** What problem was actually solved, how reliably, and how was the result checked? Avoid substituting job title or graduate training for a demonstrated result.
3. **Communication:** Can a complicated assertion be reformulated as a short, exact, checkable statement? Does jargon hide a missing proposition, or is it efficient field-specific shorthand?
4. **Hiring and promotion:** Why did an employer select this applicant? Separate evidence of competence from degree filters, institutional recruiting channels, networks, salary bargaining, and labor-market conditions.
5. **Economic reward:** Who controls and captures revenue, and why? Employment income, inheritance, ownership, rents, and financial speculation have different causal accounts.
6. **General intelligence:** Do **not** infer an unmeasured psychological trait directly from one job, one mathematical error, a degree, or income.

People may be good in one dimension and poor in another.

## Mathematical example: Lie group versus Lie algebra

A Lie group is a smooth manifold with compatible group operations. Its Lie algebra is the tangent space at the identity equipped with a Lie bracket (equivalently, left-invariant vector fields). The distinction is consequential, not merely orthographic.

As a concrete check, the real Lie algebras `𝔰𝔲(2)` and `𝔰𝔬(3)` are isomorphic, but the Lie groups `SU(2)` and `SO(3)` are **not** isomorphic; `SU(2) → SO(3)` is a two-to-one covering map. Knowing the infinitesimal structure does not by itself identify global group structure.

Source for the mathematical statement: [Vincent Bouchard, *Introduction to Lie Groups and Lie Algebras*, §4.4](https://sites.ualberta.ca/~vbouchar/MAPH464/section-su2.html), particularly the `SU(2)`/`SO(3)` discussion.

When investigating a physicist's purported confusion:

- Preserve the actual words, derivation, source, date, and intended task.
- Ask what mathematical object the statement concerns and whether global topology or representations matter to the conclusion.
- Distinguish a genuine structural mistake from a common shorthand or the physicists' convention of using Hermitian generators (`i` changes the corresponding matrix-algebra convention).
- Check whether the author can clarify the statement when the distinction is made explicit.
- Preserve the mathematician's specific objection as its own evidence, rather than treating disciplinary prestige as a verdict either way.
- If the error survives these checks, report the error without a compensatory recital of the author's credentials.

This example establishes a mathematical distinction, **not** that any particular physics PhD misunderstands it.

## Competing accounts for the "practical physicist" narrative

The claim that finance and other employers hire physics PhDs *because their training proves superior practical problem-solving* is a **hypothesis**, not an inference licensed by salary or recruiting publicity.

Possible mechanisms to test separately:

- demonstrably strong modeling, coding, experimental reasoning, or mathematical skill;
- unusually successful selection from a broad population of applicants, not the performance of every PhD;
- prestige screening that lowers hiring costs or reproduces hiring networks;
- cross-disciplinary technical skills with serious blind spots in other fields;
- access to profitable institutions, capital, clients, or proprietary data;
- survival and publicity bias: the successful recruit is visible and the unsuccessful applicant or displaced worker is not.

Mechanisms can coexist. Seek original job descriptions, named hiring decisions, first-person practitioner accounts, verified work products, source code, actual results, and documented failures. *Risk* magazine's named "People" moves can establish that an individual moved to a position, not the typical fate or ability of physics graduates.

Do not use an institutional placement percentage as independent evidence that the jobs were good, the employees competent, or the degree caused the outcome.

## What to collect

For each claim, record: exact proposition; claimant; first-person/source owner; original quotation or work sample; date; discipline and technical context; directly demonstrated ability or error; independent check; possible alternative explanation; career event (if material); evidence quality; unknown denominator; counterevidence; and the limit of the conclusion.

Research queue:

1. Find attributable, **primary** statements by mathematicians identifying specific mistakes by physicists, including Lie group versus Lie algebra and "why not just say [precise proposition]?" criticisms. Do not invent a quotation or present a remembered paraphrase as one.
2. Locate the original physics paper, lecture, or reply and check whether the criticism is accurate in context. Credit the actual critic and author.
3. Assemble original physicist-to-finance recruiting accounts and named career moves. Distinguish employer intent, public relations, actual selection, job tasks, and outcomes.
4. Seek counterexamples: physicists with careful mathematics and excellent practical results; credentialed/highly paid people with documented mistakes; capable people lacking the credentials or monetary rewards.
5. Test **status-protective reasoning** with matched accounts whose content stays fixed but labels such as "physics PhD," "mathematician," "warehouse worker," and "high earner" are swapped. Evaluate whether the burden of proof changes. See [moral-polarity study](chatbot-moral-polarity.md).

## Falsifiers and scope

- Direct work and repeated, independently checked results can support a person's competence even when the critic dislikes their terminology.
- A mathematical criticism fails if the purported distinction is irrelevant to the actual claim, rests on an uncharitable reading, or disappears after clarification.
- The hiring-prestige explanation loses force where original hiring evidence and actual work show that the technical skill was materially decisive.
- A population-level claim about physics graduates requires an appropriate population, comparable measures, sampling, and denominators, not a few amusing failures.

The American cultural equation **wealth → merit → intelligence** is itself a belief to document and test, not an established national prevalence statistic. Do not reverse it into **wealth → stupidity**.

Blackball's rule: **Criticize the demonstrated mistake without cushioning it with prestige, and credit demonstrated ability without granting it on the basis of status.**
