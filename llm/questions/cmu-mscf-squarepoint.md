# Carnegie Mellon’s computational finance master’s and Squarepoint Capital

The program presents quantitative research, trading, risk and related functions as prospective careers. It also publishes full-time and internship employment reports. The program title, an alumnus’s work, and cohort offer acceptances give different amounts of evidence about the work a newcomer could get. [MSCF career materials](https://www.cmu.edu/mscf/careers)

Chengkai Men’s interview identifies a 2024 Carnegie Mellon MSCF, an undergraduate education in economics and pre-graduation academic research internships. Carnegie Mellon describes him as a quantitative researcher at Squarepoint Capital. He describes modeling interest-rate instruments. The Class of 2025 employment report also names a Squarepoint quantitative researcher destination in New York. Men’s biography and the report do not provide enough information to identify whether the listed destination represents him or a different worker. Count one documented employer route, without treating the biography and the employer row as two hires. The Squarepoint Foundation funded 2026 fellowships for five incoming MSCF students through a $100,000 gift. The gift provides resources for enrollment. In the public faculty listing, fellowship announcement and employer report examined here, no attributable record establishes that Squarepoint recruited a named MSCF instructor into that graduate’s role. The established evidence includes a graduate’s paid research work and a school–employer funding relationship.
