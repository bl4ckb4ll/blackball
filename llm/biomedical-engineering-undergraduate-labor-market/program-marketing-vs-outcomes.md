# Occupational framing versus outcome evidence

This file records how selected universities currently present undergraduate BME to prospective students and places those statements next to available labor-market or graduate-destination evidence.

The purpose is **not** to infer fraudulent intent. A university can legitimately describe career possibilities while also having graduates who pursue medicine, graduate school, consulting, research, or non-BME work. The question is whether a prospective student sees enough quantitative information to understand the probability and cost of a **bachelor’s-only direct-to-industry** path.

---

## Johns Hopkins University

### Current prospective-student framing

Johns Hopkins’ “Careers in BME” page describes biomedical engineering as a highly ranked occupation/major, emphasizes salary and growth, says its curriculum prepares students for varied careers, and says Hopkins BME students are sought by major employers.

https://www.bme.jhu.edu/academics/prospective-students/careers-in-bme/

### The same page’s outcome disclosure

Crucially, Hopkins also publishes a destination distribution on that page:

| Destination | Share |
| --- | ---: |
| Graduate or medical school | **50%** |
| Industry | **34%** |
| Consulting | **6%** |
| Government | **4%** |
| Other | **6%** |

That is substantially more informative than merely saying the occupation is growing. The page makes visible that only about one-third of the displayed graduate destinations are industry, while half are graduate or medical school.

### Price scale

Whiting School undergraduate tuition for 2026–27 is **$68,670/year**, implying a static four-year tuition figure of **$274,680 before aid** at the current rate.

Official tuition source: https://e-catalogue.jhu.edu/university-wide-policies-information/admission-aid/tuition-fees/

### What can be said

A supportable statement is:

> Johns Hopkins markets BME with explicit job/value/employer language while also disclosing that 34% of the displayed undergraduate destinations are industry and 50% are graduate or medical school; current Whiting sticker tuition is roughly $275,000 over four years if held flat.

What cannot be inferred from those facts alone is that Hopkins “knows the degree does not lead to jobs.” Its own page documents industry placement and names employers.

## University of Michigan

### Current prospective-student framing

Michigan’s undergraduate BME page says its major prepares students to work in biomedical industry or continue to medical/graduate school and lists engineering job titles held by graduates.

https://bme.umich.edu/academics/undergraduate/

Its curriculum page says opportunities are wide-ranging, describes medical-device and pharmaceutical investment in biomedical engineers, and states that biomedical engineers are in demand.

https://bme.umich.edu/academics/undergraduate/curriculum/

### Internal research on the mismatch

Michigan-affiliated BME researchers coauthored:

Cassandra Sue Ellen Jamison et al., “BME Career Exploration: Examining Students’ Connection with the Field,” *Biomedical Engineering Education* 2(1), 17–29.

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8553099/

The paper explicitly begins from the documented concern that BME undergraduates can struggle to find industry jobs and discusses statistics and mechanisms that support concern about the transition from undergraduate BME to industry.

This is the most important juxtaposition in the dossier: **occupationally affirmative program language exists alongside research by faculty at the same university studying a known BME career-entry problem.**

That still does not establish deception. It establishes that the negative evidence is not obscure or unknowable.

### Current outcome counterevidence

Michigan’s “Why Choose BME?” material reports that approximately half of its graduates pursue full-time government/industry employment, with others going to health professions or graduate education.

https://bme.umich.edu/about/bme-culture/why-bme/

That information belongs beside the negative evidence rather than being suppressed.

### Price scale

At 2026–27 College of Engineering rates, a static four-year estimate using two lower-division and two upper-division years is approximately:

- **$90,472** for a Michigan resident;
- **$284,436** for a nonresident.

Official tuition source: https://ro.umich.edu/tuition-residency/tuition-fees/2026-2027/undergraduate/full-term/college-engineering-undergraduate

## Ohio State University

### Current career framing

Ohio State’s BME undergraduate site has an explicit **Industry** career path and advises students to use engineering career services, pursue internships/co-ops/research, and consider minors or professional certifications.

https://bme.osu.edu/biomedical-engineering-undergraduate-program/career-paths

The same page includes a feed of current BME-related postings. The mixture itself is informative: visible postings include entry-level biomedical engineer roles but also endoscopic specialist, research-technologist, Ph.D.-level associate, maintenance-equipment specialist, research assistant, and imaging-project-coordinator roles.

That is why a “BME jobs” feed is not identical to a count of entry-level engineering jobs requiring a BME bachelor’s degree.

### Ohio State research on graduate outcomes

Lewis et al. (2026) analyzed Ohio State records for 3,292 historical engineering graduates.

https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44217-026-01339-7

Industry placement:

| Major | Industry |
| --- | ---: |
| BME | **33%** |
| Mechanical engineering | **68%** |
| Chemical engineering | **69%** |
| Materials science and engineering | **59%** |

Pre-graduation engineering work experience:

| Major | Engineering work before graduation |
| --- | ---: |
| BME | **34%** |
| Mechanical engineering | **69%** |
| Chemical engineering | **58%** |
| Materials science and engineering | **59%** |

The authors describe BME industry placement as noticeably low relative to the comparison majors.

### Price scale

At 2026–27 rates, adding the published base Columbus undergraduate charges to the engineering program and learning-technology fees yields a static four-year academic-charge approximation of:

- **$74,120** for an Ohio resident;
- **$195,004** for a nonresident.

Base costs: https://undergrad.osu.edu/cost-and-aid/basic-costs

Fees: https://registrar.osu.edu/student-hub/tuition-and-fees/

The outcome cohort is historical and the tuition is current; they are not the same students. The legitimate statement is that **Ohio State currently charges these engineering rates while its own 2026 research provides unusually strong evidence that its historical BME cohorts entered industry at much lower rates than traditional engineering comparison majors.**

## The national occupation does not resolve the undergraduate-major question

BLS reports a real and well-paid occupation:

- about **23,800** U.S. bioengineer/biomedical-engineer jobs in 2025;
- median pay **$109,370**;
- about **1,200 openings per year** projected over 2025–2035;
- 8% projected growth.

https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/biomedical-engineers.htm

But BLS also says a bachelor’s in BME **or a related engineering field** can qualify. Therefore occupational growth does not tell a prospective student whether BME is preferable to ME, EE, ChE or another engineering degree for entering biomedical work.

---

## The claim that the evidence supports

The evidence currently supports this statement:

> Some universities sell very expensive undergraduate BME education using explicit industry, career, demand, employer, and occupational language. Peer-reviewed research—including research produced by faculty at universities offering BME—documents a meaningful undergraduate-to-industry mismatch, employer substitution toward traditional engineering disciplines, lower industry placement in at least one large institutional comparison, and the importance of internships and geography. Prospective students should see those facts before being given rankings or admissions advice.

It does **not** support this stronger statement as a fact:

> Universities know BME will not lead to a job and deliberately designed the major or its marketing to deceive students.

Intent would require different evidence: internal communications, deliberative records, marketing research, known-outcome disclosures, administrator testimony, or other sources establishing what decision-makers knew and intended.

That is a legitimate next research question, but it is not answered by the current dossier.
