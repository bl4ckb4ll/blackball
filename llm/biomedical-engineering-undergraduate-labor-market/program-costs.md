# Published prices for selected undergraduate BME / bioengineering programs

**Price year:** 2026–27 published rates unless otherwise stated.

## What these numbers mean

These are **sticker-price estimates before grants, scholarships, tax benefits, or other financial aid**. They are not claims about what the median student actually pays.

The four-year figures below are deliberately simple **static estimates**: apply the published 2026–27 academic charges to the relevant eight semesters/four academic years. Actual future nominal prices can rise. Where a university has lower- and upper-division engineering rates, the estimate uses two years at each rate. Housing, food, books, transportation, health insurance, and personal expenses are excluded unless a row explicitly says otherwise.

A BME department usually does not charge its own separate tuition. These are the published costs of attending the university/engineering school while pursuing the BME or closely related bioengineering degree.

The private-university examples are striking because current tuition alone is around **$275,000–$295,000 over four years** at today’s rate before aid or living costs. That does not mean every BME student pays that amount, but it establishes the sticker-price scale of the decision.

## Johns Hopkins University

### Published price

Johns Hopkins lists **2026–27 Whiting School of Engineering undergraduate tuition at $68,670**.

Official tuition and fees: https://e-catalogue.jhu.edu/university-wide-policies-information/admission-aid/tuition-fees/

Static four-year tuition:

`4 × $68,670 = $274,680`

The same official schedule lists, among other first-year/on-campus cost components, food, housing, books, transportation, personal expenses, health insurance, and a matriculation fee. Using the listed figures without waiving health insurance produces a first-year sum around **$98,482**. That broader cost-of-attendance figure should not be confused with tuition alone.

### Occupational framing

Johns Hopkins’ BME career page says BME has been ranked as a top job/major and says Hopkins BME students are sought by major employers. The same page is unusually useful because it also supplies a destination breakdown: approximately **50% graduate or medical school, 34% industry, 6% consulting, 4% government, and 6% other**.

https://www.bme.jhu.edu/academics/prospective-students/careers-in-bme/

This is exactly the kind of disclosure that should accompany an occupationally framed undergraduate major: the reader can see that industry is a major destination but **not the destination of most graduates** in the displayed breakdown.

### Occupational framing and internal research

Michigan’s undergraduate BME page says the program prepares students to work in biomedical industry as well as for medical and graduate school:

https://bme.umich.edu/academics/undergraduate/

Its curriculum page uses broad opportunity/demand language:

https://bme.umich.edu/academics/undergraduate/curriculum/

Separately, Michigan BME faculty coauthored the 2021/2022 career-exploration paper that begins from the documented concern that BME undergraduates struggle to find industry jobs:

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8553099/

This juxtaposition is evidence of a disclosure question, **not evidence that Michigan administrators intend to mislead students**. The current Michigan site also reports that roughly half of its BME graduates pursue full-time government/industry work, which should be preserved as counterevidence rather than omitted.

https://bme.umich.edu/about/bme-culture/why-bme/

## Ohio State University

### Published price

Ohio State’s 2026–27 Columbus undergraduate cost page lists base tuition/general fees of approximately:

- Ohio resident: **$14,050/year**
- nonresident: **$44,271/year**

https://undergrad.osu.edu/cost-and-aid/basic-costs

The official university fee table lists, for engineering students in the relevant credit range, a **$2,000 engineering program fee per semester** and a **$240 learning-technology fee per semester**.

https://registrar.osu.edu/student-hub/tuition-and-fees/

For two regular semesters, that is about **$4,480/year** in these engineering-specific fees.

Static four-year academic-charge approximation:

- resident: `(14,050 + 4,480) × 4 = $74,120`
- nonresident: `(44,271 + 4,480) × 4 = $195,004`

### Same-institution outcome evidence

Ohio State is the strongest cost/outcome juxtaposition in this dossier because the 2026 Lewis et al. study uses Ohio State graduate records. For the historical 2012–2019 cohorts it found **33% industry placement among BME graduates**, versus **68% mechanical, 69% chemical, and 59% materials**.

https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44217-026-01339-7

The current price and the historical outcome cohort are from different years. They should not be presented as though a 2026 student paid current tuition and experienced the 2012–2019 placement rate. The meaningful fact is that the institution now charges the published engineering rates while its own researchers have produced unusually strong evidence of a historic BME direct-industry-placement disadvantage.

## What should be collected next

Sticker price is the easiest price to document but not the economically decisive one. A stronger institutional comparison should add:

- median **net price** by family-income band;
- grant-aid distribution;
- median debt at graduation;
- BME-specific four- and six-year completion rates;
- bachelor’s-only BME industry placement;
- median starting compensation by destination;
- geographic destination and local cost of living;
- comparable ME/EE/ChE outcomes at the same university.

The desired comparison is not “expensive school versus cheap school.” It is **price paid for a particular labor-market option set**.
