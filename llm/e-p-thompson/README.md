# E. P. Thompson — books, essays, and legitimate online access

E. P. Thompson (1924–1993) is one of the central historians of class formation, labour, customary rights, popular politics, and the British New Left.

## Access rule

Internet Archive/Open Library often has a **Borrow** or **Preview** copy of Thompson's books. That is useful public access, but it is **not the same as public domain or an open licence**. Blackball should link those records rather than mirror copyrighted scans unless a rights statement explicitly permits redistribution.

Useful starting points:

- Open Library author record: https://openlibrary.org/authors/OL405788A/E._P._Thompson
- Internet Archive author/title search: https://archive.org/search?query=%22E.+P.+Thompson%22
- Marxists Internet Archive author index: https://www.marxists.org/archive/ (search for E P Thompson)

## Freely readable Thompson pieces found in this pass

These are worth linking because they are actual readable texts, not merely catalog records:

- “William Morris and the Moral Issues Today” (1951): https://www.marxists.org/archive/thompson-ep/1951/william-morris.htm
- “Socialism and the Intellectuals” (1957): https://www.marxists.org/archive/thompson-ep/1957/socialism-intellectuals.htm
- “At the Point of Decay” (1960), including the contents of *Out of Apathy*: https://www.marxists.org/archive/thompson-ep/1960/decay.htm

Other indispensable essays to track in legitimate editions/archives include “Socialist Humanism,” “The New Left,” “Time, Work-Discipline and Industrial Capitalism,” “The Moral Economy of the English Crowd in the Eighteenth Century,” “History from Below,” and the open letter to Leszek Kołakowski.

## Source graph and thanks

Thompson's work is not an isolated individual product. Blackball should keep the source graph visible.

For the British Marxist historians and New Left around him, follow **Dorothy Thompson, Christopher Hill, Eric Hobsbawm, Rodney Hilton, Dona Torr, John Saville, Ralph Samuel, George Rudé, Victor Kiernan, Maurice Dobb, Stuart Hall, Raymond Williams, Alasdair MacIntyre, Peter Worsley**, and the circles around *Past & Present*, *The Reasoner*, *The New Reasoner*, and *Universities and Left Review*.

For *Albion's Fatal Tree*, explicitly credit **Douglas Hay, Peter Linebaugh, John Rule, and the other contributors**. For Thompson's work on working-class formation and popular culture, the evidentiary base includes radical newspapers, pamphlets, sermons, religious autobiography, correspondence, songs, judicial records, statutes, trade records, customary practices, crowd action, local archives, and the writings of historical actors themselves.

A select bibliography of Thompson's own work is also identified in Harvey J. Kaye and Keith McClelland (eds.), *E. P. Thompson: Critical Perspectives*, pp. 276–280.
