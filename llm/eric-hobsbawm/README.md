# Eric Hobsbawm — books, “long nineteenth century,” and open article trail

Eric Hobsbawm (1917–2012) is the historian the phrase **“the long nineteenth century”** is most closely associated with. His standard sequence is *The Age of Revolution* (1789–1848), *The Age of Capital* (1848–1875), and *The Age of Empire* (1875–1914), followed by *Age of Extremes* for the “short twentieth century.”

## Best complete bibliography

Do not try to reconstruct Hobsbawm's entire publication history from a short selected-works list. The University of Edinburgh maintains the **Eric Hobsbawm Bibliography**, with more than 3,000 entries covering books, articles, chapters, reviews, newspaper pieces, pamphlets, unpublished work, and private papers:

- https://www.hobsbawm.shca.ed.ac.uk/
- About / scope: https://hobsbawm.shca.ed.ac.uk/about
- Biography and long-nineteenth-century sequence: https://hobsbawm.shca.ed.ac.uk/biography
- Open Library author record: https://openlibrary.org/authors/OL32997A/Eric_Hobsbawm
- Internet Archive search: https://archive.org/search?query=%22Eric+Hobsbawm%22

Open Library/Internet Archive **Borrow** means lending access, not public-domain status or permission to mirror the scan.

## Freely readable Hobsbawm articles

*Past & Present* has made a set of Hobsbawm's major articles freely available on its memorial page:

https://academic.oup.com/past/pages/eric_hobsbawm

The page includes:

- “The Machine Breakers” (1952)
- “The Crisis of the 17th Century—II” (1954)
- “The General Crisis of the European Economy in the 17th Century” (1954)
- “Twentieth-Century British Politics” (1957)
- discussion of H. R. Trevor-Roper on the seventeenth-century general crisis (1960)
- “The Social Function of the Past: Some Questions” (1972)
- “Peasant Land Occupations” (1974)
- “The Revival of Narrative: Some Comments” (1980)
- “Political Shoemakers” (1980)
- “Past and Present: Origins and Early Years” (1983)
- “A Life in History” (2002)

These are much better Blackball links than unattributed scans.

## Source graph and thanks

Hobsbawm was part of the Communist Party Historians Group and the *Past & Present* network. Keep visible **Christopher Hill, E. P. Thompson, Rodney Hilton, Dona Torr, George Rudé, Victor Kiernan, Maurice Dobb, Raphael Samuel**, and the many other labour and social historians with whom these arguments developed.

Explicit coauthors/editors matter:

- **George Rudé** — coauthor of *Captain Swing*.
- **Terence Ranger** — co-editor of *The Invention of Tradition*.
- The *Past & Present* editorial network and contributors.

For Hobsbawm's own intellectual sources and interlocutors, the full bibliography and notes repeatedly lead into **Marx, Engels, Gramsci**, labour-movement records, working-class organizations, national statistics, newspapers, political pamphlets, party archives, and a vast comparative secondary literature. Use the Edinburgh bibliography rather than pretending this short file can stand in for 3,000 entries.
