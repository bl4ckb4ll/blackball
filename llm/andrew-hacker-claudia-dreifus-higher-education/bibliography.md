# *Higher Education?* — bibliography and source trail

## Boundary

The 2010 first edition has a printed bibliography on pp. **245–254**. That pagination is confirmed by library catalog records.

The printed pages themselves have **not** yet been directly inspected. The accessible Internet Archive record is access-restricted. Therefore this file is deliberately **not presented as the book's complete bibliography**.

Instead it records:

- sources named in directly inspected Hacker/Dreifus material;
- sources attributed to the book in detailed contemporary reviews;
- sources in Andrew Hacker's 2005 NYRB precursor that clearly feed later book arguments;
- unresolved source owners that should be matched against pp. 245–254 when legitimate access is available;
- institutions used as reported cases, kept separate from bibliographic works.

## Tags

- `book-visible-primary` — source named in directly inspected Hacker/Dreifus book text or excerpt.
- `book-visible-secondary` — source attributed to the book by a detailed secondary review; direct bibliography check still needed.
- `precursor-hacker-2005` — source explicitly cited by Andrew Hacker in “The Truth About the Colleges” (2005); likely relevant source trail, but not automatically a 2010 bibliography entry.
- `institutional-case` — institution discussed/observed; not a bibliographic work.
- `exact-item-unresolved` — source owner or dataset is identifiable but the exact publication/table/report has not yet been recovered.
- `upstream-needed` — bibliography metadata is not enough; retrieve and inspect the source itself before using its finding.

## Stacy Berg Dale and Alan B. Krueger — selective college payoff

**Tags:** `book-visible-secondary`, `precursor-hacker-2005`, `upstream-needed`

Andrew Hacker's 2005 article explicitly cites:

- Stacy Dale and Alan Krueger, “Estimating the Payoff of Attending a More Selective College,” Industrial Relations Section, Princeton University, 1999.

The later published article is:

- Stacy Berg Dale and Alan B. Krueger, “Estimating the Payoff to Attending a More Selective College: An Application of Selection on Observables and Unobservables,” *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 117, no. 4 (2002): 1491–1527.

This is one of the most important upstream sources because it addresses selection directly by comparing students with similar application/admission behavior who attended colleges of different selectivity.

**Work needed:** determine which version the 2010 bibliography cites and whether the book uses later follow-up work.

# Sources about the authors' method

## Hacker and Dreifus — three years of research

- Claudia Dreifus and Andrew Hacker, “Making a smart college buy,” 2011 archive: https://vindyarchives.com/news/2011/jan/23/making-a-smart-college-buy/

They explicitly say the book followed three years of research and turn that experience into questions families should ask colleges.

## Sherm Folland — methodological characterization

- Sherm Folland, “Notes from the Dismal Science: When America's Colleges Don't Teach and Students Don't Learn: Two Recent Books Reviewed,” *Oakland Journal*: https://oakland.edu/Assets/Oakland/oujournal/files-and-documents/21_dismal_science.pdf

Folland describes the book as nonexperimental/non-survey research whose evidence, beyond scholarly study, comes heavily from visits and conversations. Treat this as a secondary characterization, not the authors' own methods statement.

## Andrew Hacker — pre-book reporting model

- Andrew Hacker, “The Truth About the Colleges,” *New York Review of Books*, November 3, 2005: https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2005/11/03/the-truth-about-the-colleges/

The essay is valuable because its online footnotes expose upstream sources for arguments that later recur in the book.

# Rule for future expansion

When the printed bibliography becomes legitimately inspectable:

1. transcribe bibliographic metadata, not copyrighted chapter prose;
2. attach each bibliography entry to chapter/page references where possible;
3. distinguish citations used as evidence from background reading;
4. retrieve upstream sources individually;
5. preserve date/version information for datasets;
6. do not replace the authors' actual bibliography with this reconstruction — compare them and record discrepancies.
