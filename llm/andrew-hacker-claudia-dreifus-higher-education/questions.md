# *Higher Education?* — research queue

These are retrieval and verification questions, not claims.

## Highest priority: inspect the book directly

1. Obtain legitimate access to the 2010 first edition or 2011 paperback.
2. Read complete chapters 2 and 4–13.
3. Inspect bibliography pp. 245–254 and compare it line-by-line with `bibliography.md`.
4. Record page numbers for every institutional case and quantitative claim.
5. Determine whether the book has acknowledgments or notes that identify interviewees, research assistants, travel support, or unpublished data.
6. Record exactly which campuses the authors say they **visited**, as opposed to campuses they merely discuss using records or other people's research.

## Method and division of labor

7. What was the actual division of labor between Andrew Hacker and Claudia Dreifus?
8. Did Dreifus conduct most interviews/site reporting, or is that an inference from Hacker's statement that her journalism would keep the book focused on real people?
9. Are interview notes, recordings, notebooks, or research files archived anywhere?
10. Were interviewees generally named, anonymized, or quoted without institutional identifiers?
11. How did the authors choose campuses? Prestige? convenience? geography? interesting practices? recommendations from contacts?
12. Was there any explicit attempt at a representative or stratified sample?
13. How many campuses/classes did they visit during the three-year project?
14. Which observations were made jointly and which by one author?

## Blackball-specific output goal

For each important claim, eventually store:

**claim → chapter/page → evidence type → institution/sample → upstream owner → original source → date → competing explanation → current-status check**

The dossier is complete only when a future model can tell the difference between:

- Hacker/Dreifus personally saw it;
- someone told them;
- they copied a statistic from another source;
- they inferred a national pattern;
- they made a normative judgment;
- later evidence corroborated or contradicted it.
