# Terence Ranger — books, bibliography, and access trail

Terence O. Ranger (1929–2015) was a major historian of African nationalism, Zimbabwe, religion, liberation war, memory, landscape, and the colonial production of political traditions. He also co-edited *The Invention of Tradition* with Eric Hobsbawm.

## Best bibliography starting points

- Open Library author record (36 works): https://openlibrary.org/authors/OL1964806A/Terence_O._Ranger
- Tim Moldram, “Terence Ranger's Works: A Preliminary Bibliography,” *Journal of Southern African Studies* 23, no. 2 (1997), 353–361: https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?RID=164083243
- JSTOR record for that bibliography: https://www.jstor.org/stable/2637627
- Internet Archive search: https://archive.org/search?query=%22Terence+Ranger%22

The Moldram bibliography covers books, pamphlets, speeches, edited collections, articles, seminar papers, and conference papers from 1957 through 1996. For “all Ranger,” that article is the right backbone; the later books below extend it beyond 1996.

## The important Ranger problem: “invention of tradition” is not the whole story

Ranger's famous contribution to *The Invention of Tradition* examined how colonial states, missionaries, chiefs, and other institutions formalized and reified practices as fixed “traditions.” His later work became more self-critical about overly simple versions of that model. The Blackball source trail should therefore keep early and late Ranger together: nationalism and revolt; invention and reinterpretation of tradition; religious change; liberation war; memory; landscape; city; and his retrospective reconsideration of nationalist history.

## Source graph and thanks

Ranger's books depend heavily on archives plus interviews, oral tradition, local political memory, religious institutions, landscapes, and African intellectual and political actors. Do not reduce the evidentiary chain to “Ranger says.”

Explicit collaborators to preserve:

- **Eric Hobsbawm** — co-editor, *The Invention of Tradition*.
- **Ngwabi Bhebe** — co-editor on the two liberation-war volumes.
- **Jocelyn Alexander** and **JoAnn McGregor** — coauthors of *Violence and Memory*.
- The many Zimbabwean interviewees, families, veterans, religious communities, local historians, archivists, and political actors whose testimony made the work possible.

For Ranger's complete pre-1997 publication graph, use Tim Moldram's bibliography rather than reconstructing hundreds of entries from memory.
