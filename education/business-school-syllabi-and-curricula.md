# Business education: documented curricula, syllabi, and books

**Status — 2026-10-16:** One dated, institution-published degree curriculum and one publisher-verified book contents list have been inspected. No individual course syllabus, classroom instruction, required textbook adoption, or graduate outcome is claimed to have been verified here.

## Working rule

Record **what is actually taught and required** before evaluating it. Do not start by assuming that business degrees are fraudulent, empty, practical, rigorous, or automatically valuable. Test each claim at the level at which evidence exists.

Keep these separate:

- **Degree curriculum:** a university's published required courses and credit rules.
- **Course syllabus:** the specific material, schedule, assignments, examinations, grading, and readings for a named offering.
- **Book contents:** what a book covers, not proof that any school assigns it.
- **First-person judgment:** a reader's or student's experience, not a general estimate of educational quality.
- **Educational, occupational, and social value:** separate questions, not conclusions supplied by course names or salaries.
- **Practicality for whom, and under what conditions?** A concrete technique can be readily executable in a particular finance task without being usable by every reader. Check whether a person has assets, access to capital, eligibility for credit, a job applying the material, or a financial decision in which that technique matters; do not assume those opportunities are available. Conceptual clarity, technical applicability, access to act on the knowledge, personal economic benefit, and social value are distinct observations.

## Technical reading record 1 — Robert Zipf, *Fixed Income Mathematics*

- **Bibliographic record:** Robert Zipf, *Fixed Income Mathematics*, first edition, Academic Press / Elsevier, 2003; print ISBN **978-0-12-781721-7**.
- **Primary publisher source:** [Elsevier book page, including table of contents](https://shop.elsevier.com/books/fixed-income-mathematics/zipf/978-0-12-781721-7).
- **Source kind:** publisher bibliographic description and contents list, not verified university course adoption.
- **Subjects listed by publisher:** interest and compound interest; present value and annuities; bond prices and accrued interest; discount securities and yield quotations; total return; bond-price volatility, duration and convexity; calculus and probability applications; interest-rate term structure; uncertain cash flows; mortgage-backed securities; futures and options.
- **Reader testimony, 2026-10-16:** The project owner remembers this book as *very practical and easy to understand*.
- **Syllabus status:** **Suggested reading for this research syllabus**, **not** evidence that Ross, or any other named institution, assigned this book.

This is a positive case worth retaining for **clear mathematical exposition and concrete applications**. But “practical” must be qualified. Bond duration and convexity can be directly useful to someone pricing securities or managing an investment portfolio; they may have little immediate practical use to someone without assets to manage or work in which those calculations arise. The mathematics of mortgages similarly has a different practical meaning for people who can obtain a mortgage and people excluded from that market. Understanding an exclusion or a product can still be informative; that does not make access or a profitable action available. Whether the knowledge improves employment or earnings, or whether the supported economic activity benefits society, requires separate evidence.

**Name disambiguation:** This book's author is **Robert Zipf**. [Zipf's law](https://xlinux.nist.gov/dads/HTML/zipfslaw.html) is named for **George Kingsley Zipf**, a different person (NIST Dictionary of Algorithms and Data Structures).

## First-person institutional observation

The project owner reports that, at the school he attended, the business school **seemed more accountable** than some other departments. The school, dates, standards, and observed practices have not been identified in this record. Treat this as a firsthand research lead, not as a measured ranking of departments or a universal property of business schools.

Accountability itself needs specification: published learning objectives, marked assignments, consistent grading, external assessment, occupational competence, disclosure of costs and outcomes, or meaningful remedies when institutional representations fail are different possible measures.

## Collection protocol: record the syllabus, not a stereotype

For additional schools, degrees, cohorts, and courses, collect the following with dated original links:

1. **Institution, degree, admission year and official curriculum bulletin.** Preserve required courses, sequence, prerequisites, credits, electives, and non-business requirements.
2. **Actual course syllabi by term and instructor.** Capture topics, assigned readings, exercises, exams, grading policies, workloads, and stated learning objectives. Mark missing syllabi as **not obtained**.
3. **Demonstrated subject content.** Distinguish a title such as “Business Analytics” from the mathematics, data, software, cases, and assignments actually used.
4. **Institutional accountability evidence.** Record assessments, policy enforcement, standards, published placement data, denominators, and what the school represents to applicants. Do not infer accountability from branding or accreditation alone.
5. **Work and outcomes, separately.** Look for employer tasks, genuine technical application, access to hiring, earnings, excluded students, costs, and alternative learning routes.
6. **Counterexamples in both directions.** Document practical and demanding business classes as readily as weak ones. Avoid turning criticism of university bundles into an assumption that their business content has no value.

The [business-school/university-bundle research question](../llm/questions/business-school-university-bundle.md) addresses *why* the degree combines these subjects. This file records *which* subjects and teaching materials can actually be established from evidence.
