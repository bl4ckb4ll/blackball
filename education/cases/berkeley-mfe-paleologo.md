# Berkeley MFE claims and Paleologo's account of unused finance training

Reviewed: 2026-10-13, America/Detroit. Source post: 2025-09-19.

## The question the figures did not answer

What work does the graduate actually do, which purchased courses are used,
what relocation and costs follow, and would that person knowingly buy the
degree again? An accepted offer does not answer those questions.

Haas's [Class of 2025 table](https://mfe.haas.berkeley.edu/careers/mfe-placements)
reports 70 job seekers, 67 recipients of offers and 67 acceptances.
Those remain **institution-reported counts**. The actual outcomes of the
three outside the acceptance count and the value of the other 67 transactions
remain uninspected.

## The practitioner account

Giuseppe Paleologo, [X post, 19 September 2025](https://x.com/__paleologo/status/1969189262340792564),
recounts interviewing a senior quant who said that derivatives-pricing
mathematics learned in a **CMU computational-finance master's** was never used.
Paleologo also recounts an MSFE adviser describing a curriculum oriented toward
banks of twenty years earlier, especially stochastic calculus. He separately
recalls taking four Stanford graduate courses in that subject.

The short quoted fragment is the interviewee's: **“Which I never used.”**
Paleologo is the identified narrator and interviewer; the quant is unnamed.
The adviser and the MSFE program are unnamed. This source is original testimony
about Paleologo's conversations, with an embedded account of the quant's
experience. It is not a direct interview record authored by that quant.

The source supplies a concrete contradiction to treating specialized finance
coursework as necessarily used in the eventual work. The adviser's report is
also an industry-curriculum mismatch lead. Preserve both instead of using
placement rates to dismiss the account.

### What was checked

The complete post was read in signed-in X on 2026-10-13. Its author, date,
CMU reference, embedded attribution and adviser discussion were inspected.
The original permalink is the source identity; no LinkedIn repost is used as
an independent corroborating observation.

This post does not identify a Berkeley alumnus, independently inspect Haas's
2025 cohort, or identify the interviewee's employer or city. Do not label the
unnamed quant a New York banker or Berkeley graduate by inference. If a
separate firsthand New York account is recovered, record it as a separate
source with its own identity.

## The failed inference and its correction

| Earlier move | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Read accepted-offer counts as good career outcomes | Record the school's stated event and denominator; inspect actual work and subsequent outcomes separately. |
| Use occupational titles or employer names to infer course use | Require descriptions of tasks and which training was used. |
| Say that quantitative finance uses advanced mathematics as reassurance about this purchase | Establish the specific target role and the incremental value of this degree; the generic claim does not do that. |
| Dismiss a concrete unused-training account because aggregate placement is high | Assess the account against the claim about training use; placement and course use are different questions. |
| Treat the quoted quant's degree history as Paleologo's | Keep the CMU interviewee, Stanford narrator and unnamed adviser distinct. |

**The unsupported inference is withdrawn.** No evidence here establishes that
the desired job, course use or a good bargain follows from buying the MFE.
The account does not need to falsify Haas's offer count to defeat that inference.

## Required follow-up evidence

Record public or consented individual accounts of expected role, actual duties,
course use, pay, city and necessary relocation, prior training, debt and other
costs, time in the role, departures and the person's own assessment. Keep
unknown fields unknown. Compare the degree's contribution with prior experience
and selection; a job obtained after enrollment is not automatically a job
caused by enrollment.
