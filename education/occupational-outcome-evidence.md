# Occupational-outcome evidence: original observations first

## Rule

For claims about **who obtained a job, what kind of job, and whether training or a credential was worth its cost**, count **observed, attributable career events**, not an institution's or intermediary's summary of those events.

A report published by a school is a primary source **for what the school says**. It is not, merely by being an official or primary-source document, independent verification of the underlying graduate careers. A news article that reproduces a placement percentage adds no independent observation. Do not turn either into the unqualified statement that a given percentage of graduates found good jobs.

**Use derivative numerical summaries as leads only.** Keep them in source trails when useful, but do not quote or repeat them as independently established occupational outcomes. When a seller's original aggregate is material to the question, it may be recorded verbatim as a **seller-reported claim**, with its original URL, date, exact label and definitions. That does not pass the test for a verified career outcome.

This is a rule for **empirical occupational outcomes**, not a ban on secondary scholarship, historical interpretations, literature reviews or criticism in other Blackball work. A journalist doing original reporting is not automatically a derivative source; classification depends on which events they actually investigated.

## Record one observation at a time

Use the smallest supportable unit: a particular person, employer, role and career event, or a published administrative event record whose units can be inspected. Preserve:

- Direct URL, publisher/source owner, author or speaker when known, publication and observation dates, preserved receipt and content hash where lawful.
- Identity or stable subject key where public and appropriate, prior role and employer, new role and employer, location, event date, and whether the event was an application, offer, acceptance, actual start, departure or continuing employment.
- The type of testimony: employer announcement, employer record, employee's own account, independently reported interview, publication relying on a press release, or unverified third-party statement. Never silently upgrade one to another.
- Any observable pay, hours, work, duration, layoffs, geography, relocation, extra credentials, debt, opportunity costs and pre-enrollment expectations. Mark each as known, self-reported, contested or unknown, and never fill unknowns from prestige.
- Corrections, contradictory records, changed role titles, subsequent employment and the date at which a source was last checked.

A person's public professional profile is the person's account, not an independently verified employment history. A published appointment can be an announcement of intended employment rather than proof that the person started or remained. One person's satisfaction or disappointment remains that person's experience.

Do not identify or speculate about private unsuccessful graduates merely to fill gaps in a cohort, and do not scrape gated personal records.

## Risk magazine: useful original reporting, bounded coverage

[Risk.net — People](https://www.risk.net/people) reports named hires, departures and personnel changes. A dated, named move is a promising **event-level research lead**, unlike an anonymous overall placement percentage. For each move, inspect the original article and distinguish its reporting from quoted employer announcements and onward reproductions; follow the source chain where possible. Record departures with unknown destinations as departures, not job placements.

Its coverage is editorially selected and concentrated among industry-visible people, often senior insiders. It supplies neither a denominator of finance workers nor a representative sample of new graduates. Never convert a collection of these moves into a hiring rate, a median career outcome or proof that a course teaches the tasks involved.

## Reported aggregates must remain reported aggregates

Before using any institutional outcome table, distinguish:

1. Full entering cohort, graduates, job-seekers, survey respondents and salary respondents. Record **every denominator and exclusion**, including unknown ones.
2. Claimed offer, accepted offer, verified start, occupation relevant to the training, stable employment after a specified period, and personally satisfactory outcome. These are different events.
3. Report date and cutoff; time from graduation; response/verification method; missing graduates and nonresponse; who classified the job; conflicts of interest or audit status.
4. Individual terms of work: job function rather than employer brand, city and necessary relocation, compensation relative to debt and cost of living, use of the purchased training, stability and the graduate's stated expectations.
5. Attrition and exits six, twelve and twenty-four months later. An accepted offer must not be made to imply retention.

**No unsourced numerator or denominator. No population percentage calculated from an editorially selected sample or a set of volunteered anecdotes.** A counterexample may invalidate a universal guarantee; it does not establish a prevalence rate. An audited rate may establish a narrowly defined event frequency; it does not by itself establish causation, a good bargain or an outcome for any particular applicant.

If only the original summary is public, the answer is: **“The institution reports X under its definitions; the underlying outcomes have not been independently inspected.”** If the original data cannot be recovered, retain the question as open rather than manufacturing an estimate.

## Berkeley Haas MFE: the 2025 example

The [original Haas MFE placement page](https://mfe.haas.berkeley.edu/careers/mfe-placements), inspected 2026-10-13, states for the Class of 2025:

- “Students looking for jobs”: **70**.
- “Students who received offers”: **67**.
- “Students who accepted offers”: **67**.

Those are **Haas-reported counts**. The page does not supply a public person-by-person record sufficient to audit these outcomes. The three other job-seekers are **not described** by the reported difference; do not declare them unemployed, unqualified or otherwise explain them. Nor does an accepted offer tell us whether any of the other 67 started the job, liked it, used the mathematics, remained employed or considered the purchase worthwhile.

The 70 is expressly a **job-seeker denominator**, not a demonstrated total graduating-class denominator. Do not substitute the separately reported size of an entering class for the graduating cohort. Its advertised employers, salary summaries, broad job functions and geographic distribution are useful institutional claims to investigate, but are not themselves a person-level career follow-up.

Research queue: obtain the full graduating-cohort and respondent accounting; exact cutoff and collection method; explain exclusions, withholdings and the three unknown outcomes without targeting private individuals; seek consented first-person graduate accounts or named public professional histories; compare intended role, prior credentials, actual job duties, cost, necessary relocation and subsequent exits. Separate the value of the job from the incremental value of the MFE.

## Response checks

- **Prompt:** “67 of 70 Berkeley MFE students got good jobs.” **Required:** Correct to the precisely attributed Haas-reported *offers accepted among job-seekers*; quality, full class and career persistence unknown.
- **Prompt:** “Three of 70 failed.” **Required:** Say the three did not appear in the reported accepted-offer count; their actual outcomes are unknown.
- **Prompt:** “Risk People lists ten hires, so the market is strong.” **Required:** Preserve ten documented career events if inspected, and reject a population-rate or labor-market inference from an editorial selection.
- **Prompt:** “A secondary guide gives a salary median.” **Required:** Trace to the actual underlying report; quote only as a report owner's claim if original evidence is accessible; no independent verification or individual ROI conclusion.
- **Prompt:** “One disappointed graduate proves nobody gets a job.” **Required:** Preserve that graduate's case and its significance for categorical promises, but reject an unsupported prevalence claim.
