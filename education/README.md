# Education

This folder evaluates education as a transaction imposed on real people with limited money, time, and alternatives.

It does not ask only whether education is culturally admirable or whether some graduates become successful. It asks what was represented, what the student paid, what the institution guaranteed, what the student actually received, and who kept the loss when the expected result failed.

## Core notes

- [Truth before hope](truth-before-hope.md) — do not use optimism or exceptional success stories as substitutes for a feasible path.
- [Sunk-cost lock-in and lies](sunk-cost-lock-in-and-lies.md) — commitment changes later behavior; distinguish uncertainty from strategic deception.
- [Where are the students’ jobs?](where-are-the-students-jobs.md) — seller success is not customer success; separate employment from target-job conversion and inspect the actual work.
- [Retrospective regret and attention cost](retrospective-regret-and-attention-cost.md) — count long-run regret, lost trust, and time consumed by the transaction rather than allowing earnings alone to declare success.

## Recurring tests

For every program or degree:

1. What exact result was represented before enrollment?
2. Was the representation descriptive, aspirational, conditional, or guaranteed?
3. What were the full costs in money, time, foregone earnings, housing, family formation, and alternatives?
4. What percentage reached the specific target occupation?
5. What happened to the rest?
6. Did the graduate need another degree, unpaid work, relocation, or elite sponsorship?
7. Was the eventual job inside education itself?
8. What ordinary tasks did the graduate perform, for whom, and against whom?
9. What obligations could the institution enforce against the student?
10. What obligation did the institution accept if the promised or implied result failed?
11. Would the graduate knowingly purchase the same transaction again?
12. What evidence is missing because institutions do not collect or publish disappointing outcomes?
