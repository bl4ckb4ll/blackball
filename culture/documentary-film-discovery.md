# Documentary film as a Blackball discovery lane

## Purpose

Blackball should treat documentary film as a major source-discovery and public-explanation lane, not as decoration appended after the written research is finished.

The central use is **anti-abstraction**.

A reader may not begin with the time, specialist vocabulary, or inclination to read a large literature on labor, trade, migration, education, housing, agriculture, care work, logistics, prisons, or industrial organization. A good documentary can put the relevant human beings, physical spaces, tools, routines, conflicts, and institutions in view before Blackball asks the reader to evaluate a theory about them.

This does not invert the evidence rule. Film is not automatically truer than text because it is vivid. The intended division of labor is:

**film / clip / image → discovery and concrete observation**

**Blackball annotation → identify the mechanism and the claim**

**documents / data / independent sources → test scope, prevalence, causation, and generalization**

For public presentation:

> **show first → annotate second → argue third**

## Why this matters

There is a large difference between knowing that useful material exists and knowing the five names that expose an entire body of work.

Film festivals, retrospectives, distributors, cinematheques, directors, programmers, critics, and documentary awards perform part of that search work already. Blackball can use those trails without adopting their judgments wholesale.

The discovery graph should be explicit:

**festival → program/year → film → director → related films → recurring subject → Blackball mechanism → corroborating evidence**

A strong item should generate neighboring searches. Wang Bing's *YOUTH* trilogy, for example, points backward to *Bitter Money*, sideways to films programmed with it, outward to other documentary work on garment production and internal migration, and downward to labor/supply-chain evidence that can test what the films suggest.

## Documentary is not merely an argument delivery system

One Blackball use is to put people from politically opposed audiences in contact with the same concrete object before they begin fighting over interpretation.

A film about garment workers need not first demand agreement on capitalism, communism, globalization, tariffs, China, unions, or American industrial policy. It can first establish:

- these are the people doing the work;
- this is the room in which they work;
- these are the machines;
- this is how payment is discussed;
- this is where workers sleep or travel;
- these are some of the conflicts and relationships around the work.

Different viewers may interpret what they see differently. That is acceptable. The film has still improved the argument if it forces both interpretations to answer to a more concrete world.

The Blackball objective is therefore not to assemble films that preach the Blackball line. It is to assemble films that make consequential people and institutions harder to ignore.

## Evidence discipline

Every documentary record should distinguish at least:

1. **Observed in the film** — something visible or audible in the footage.
2. **Filmmaker statement** — interpretation or production context supplied by the filmmaker.
3. **Festival/distributor synopsis** — useful metadata and discovery context, but not independent corroboration.
4. **Participant testimony** — a person's account within or about the film.
5. **External corroboration** — independent documentary, statistical, archival, journalistic, or scholarly evidence.
6. **Blackball inference** — an interpretation or mechanism proposed by Blackball.

Do not convert an unforgettable scene into a prevalence claim.

Documentary failure modes to mark explicitly:

- selection of unusually dramatic subjects;
- editing and omitted context;
- camera effects on behavior;
- translation/subtitle choices;
- uncertain chronology;
- reconstructed or staged material;
- filmmaker narration presented as fact;
- distributor copy presented as independent evidence;
- unrepresentative workplaces or communities;
- a single person's experience generalized to a population;
- emotional force outrunning what the source establishes.

## Capture schema

For every film worth retaining, record:

- title;
- original-language title;
- director(s);
- year;
- country/countries of production;
- runtime, preserving conflicting official runtimes where editions differ;
- languages/subtitles;
- production companies;
- distributor / sales agent where relevant;
- official festival selections and year;
- stable official pages;
- legal viewing routes;
- trailer/clip routes;
- transcript/subtitle availability and rights status;
- filming dates if known;
- filming locations;
- people / occupations / institutions depicted;
- labor or production process depicted;
- important scenes or sequences with timecodes when inspected;
- explicit claims made by filmmaker/participants;
- Blackball mechanisms implicated;
- external evidence needed before generalization;
- related films/directors/festivals;
- confidence / evidence status;
- licensing and quotation limits for any public-facing reuse.

## Subject tags to mine aggressively

Start broad. Documentary discovery should not be restricted to films marketed as 'labor documentaries.' Relevant tags include:

- work and workplaces;
- factories and manufacturing;
- warehouses and logistics;
- ports, shipping, trucking, rail and delivery;
- food service;
- agriculture and food processing;
- construction;
- mines and extraction;
- domestic work;
- care work, hospitals and elder care;
- schools, colleges and training;
- credentialing and professional entry;
- migration and remittances;
- unemployment and job search;
- housing and eviction;
- prisons and policing;
- bureaucracy and public benefits;
- informal work;
- street vending;
- debt and consumer finance;
- supply chains;
- deindustrialization and plant closure;
- occupational injury and disability;
- unions, strikes and worker organization;
- management and ownership;
- family life around work;
- technology changing work;
- status, prestige and social class.

The valuable film may be categorized by migration, family, architecture, food, environment, or youth rather than labor even when work is central to what the camera records.

## First-pass mining procedure

For each festival/year:

1. Get the official program or archive.
2. Filter loosely by the subject tags above.
3. Read synopses only as discovery metadata.
4. Open promising film records and director biographies.
5. Follow each strong film to the director's earlier/later work.
6. Follow production companies and distributors when they reveal a coherent nonfiction catalogue.
7. Save legal trailers, clips, interviews, Q&As and viewing routes.
8. Create a source record for films that survive inspection.
9. Extract concrete mechanisms/scenes only after viewing or reliable transcript inspection.
10. Attach independent evidence before using a film for prevalence or causal claims.
11. Repeat with adjacent films from the same program rather than returning immediately to keyword search.

This is deliberately more like walking a library shelf than issuing isolated searches.

## Public-facing presentation

Blackball's evidence corpus can be deep without making depth an admission requirement for the reader.

A useful presentation ladder is:

**30–90 seconds** — a legally usable excerpt, trailer fragment, still, or concise scene description that establishes the human object.

**5–15 minutes** — interview, festival Q&A, short documentary, or longer excerpt giving enough context to avoid a slogan.

**feature / series** — legal viewing route for the complete film.

**discovery trail** — director, related films, festival program, neighboring work.

**evidence underneath** — data, primary documents, books, archival material, source notes.

**Blackball annotation** — what the material establishes, what it suggests, what remains contested, and what evidence would decide the larger question.

The short layer should point down to the deep layer rather than pretending to replace it.

## Recommendation and advertising integrity

Blackball may recommend things that cost money and may eventually accept advertising, sponsorship, affiliate revenue, or paid leads. The credibility rule should be stronger than 'disclose the ad.'

A practical standard:

> **Would Blackball still tell the reader this is worth their time or money if nobody paid Blackball?**

If the answer is no, do not sell the recommendation.

At minimum:

- label paid placement clearly;
- keep payment out of evidence/confidence judgments;
- do not allow a sponsor to suppress negative evidence or limitations;
- do not rank an inferior educational/cultural item above a better one merely because it pays;
- retain a noncommercial source trail where possible;
- record price, access and geographic restrictions when recommending paid viewing;
- distinguish 'useful lead' from 'verified source';
- prefer advertisers/products/events that improve rather than consume Blackball's credibility.

The relevant asset is reader trust. An advertisement that is genuinely useful to the reader can strengthen the product; an irrelevant or bad recommendation spends that asset.
