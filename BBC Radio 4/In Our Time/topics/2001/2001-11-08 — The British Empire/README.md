# The British Empire

- **Broadcast:** 8 November 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547kp`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547kp
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The programme asks how a comparatively small state acquired and governed territories across the world and why the empire dissolved so rapidly in the twentieth century. Trade, naval force, private companies, settler expansion, finance and administrative improvisation produced no single uniform imperial system.

The guests connect metropolitan interests to colonial rule and resistance. Claims about civilization and free exchange are tested against coercion, racial hierarchy, extraction and the uneven political arrangements through which local intermediaries and British officials exercised power.

## Guests

- **[Maria Misra](../../../people/Maria Misra.md):** brings the history of British rule in India and the practical compromises of imperial governance.
- **[Peter Cain](../../../people/Peter Cain.md):** examines finance, metropolitan elites and the economic interpretations of British imperial expansion.
- **[Catherine Hall](../../../people/Catherine Hall.md):** traces how colonial relations shaped race, gender, class and political culture within Britain itself.

## Analytical hooks

- colonial rule and metropolitan institutions as reciprocal history
- finance, administration and negotiated local power
- race, gender and class produced through imperial relations
