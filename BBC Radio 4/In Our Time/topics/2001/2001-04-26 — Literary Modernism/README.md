# Literary Modernism

- **Broadcast:** 26 April 2001

## Summary

The episode considers the early twentieth-century experiments grouped under literary modernism: fractured narration, unstable viewpoints, compressed allusion and deliberate breaks with inherited form. It asks whether these innovations answered war, urban life, mass media and changing ideas of mind, or became an autonomous specialist aesthetic.

The guests also confront modernism’s social position. Formal difficulty could challenge complacent conventions, but it could also define an educated minority against a mass readership. Canon formation therefore belongs to the subject alongside technique.

## Guests

- **[John Carey](../../../people/John Carey.md):** brings his critique of modernist hostility toward mass culture and ordinary readers.
- **[Laura Marcus](../../../people/Laura Marcus.md):** examines modernist narrative, memory, gender and the altered experience of time and the city.
- **[Valentine Cunningham](../../../people/Valentine Cunningham.md):** places modernist works within literary history and tests claims of an absolute break with earlier writing.

## Analytical hooks

- formal innovation versus social claims about ordinary readers
- elite culture, mass culture and exclusion
- canon formation and retrospective movement labels
