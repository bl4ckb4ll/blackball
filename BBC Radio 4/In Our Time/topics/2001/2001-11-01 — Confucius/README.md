## Summary

The episode examines Confucius through the sayings and conversations assembled in the *Analects* and through the much later tradition made in his name. Learning, ritual, cultivated conduct, family obligation and humane government are presented as mutually reinforcing practices rather than as an abstract system.

The guests distinguish the historical teacher from imperial Confucian institutions and from modern stereotypes of obedience. Textual transmission, commentary and competing Chinese traditions show how Confucius became both a critic of rulers and a source of state orthodoxy.

## Guests

- **[Frances Wood](../../../people/Frances Wood.md):** brings expertise in Chinese texts, manuscripts and the historical settings through which Confucian materials survived.
- **[T. H. Barrett](../../../people/T. H. Barrett.md):** places Confucianism among the religious and intellectual traditions of China and traces its institutional development.
- **[Tao Tao Liu](../../../people/Tao Tao Liu.md):** examines the language, literature and interpretive tradition of the *Analects*.

## Analytical hooks

- text transmission and layered interpretation
- teaching converted into state and educational institutions
- Confucianism as a changing tradition rather than a single fixed doctrine
