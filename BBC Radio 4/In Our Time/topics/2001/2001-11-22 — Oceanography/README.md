## Summary

The programme examines how the oceans became objects of systematic science despite their depth, scale and inaccessibility. Voyages, sounding lines, specimen collection, laboratories, ships and later instruments turned scattered observations into maps of circulation, seafloor and marine life.

Oceanography is shown as a coalition of physical, chemical, geological and biological work rather than one method. The discussion also follows its institutional dependence on navies, fisheries and state expeditions, making practical purpose part of the history of knowledge.

## Guests

- **[Margaret Deacon](../../../people/Margaret Deacon.md):** reconstructs the expeditions, institutions and changing questions through which ocean science developed.
- **[Tony Rice](../../../people/Tony Rice.md):** describes deep-sea life and the practical limits of sampling environments people cannot directly inhabit.
- **[Simon Schaffer](../../../people/Simon Schaffer.md):** examines instruments, credibility and the organization required to make observations at sea authoritative.

## Analytical hooks

- hidden labour, expeditions and infrastructure behind scientific facts
- sampling environments that observers cannot directly inhabit
- instruments, coordination and credibility at sea
