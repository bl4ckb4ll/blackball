# Fossils

- **Broadcast:** 22 March 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547d3`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547d3
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The episode follows fossils from curiosities and disputed “figured stones” to evidence for extinction, deep time and biological change. Their interpretation required more than finding recognizable forms: strata, comparative anatomy, dating and theories of the Earth had to be joined into a historical science.

Plant, animal and chemical evidence reveal different parts of that history. The discussion also shows how museum collections, field practices and classification made singular fragments into claims about vanished organisms and environments.

## Guests

- **[Richard Corfield](../../../people/Richard Corfield.md):** explains how geochemistry and the geological record are used to reconstruct ancient environments.
- **[Dianne Edwards](../../../people/Dianne Edwards.md):** brings fossil plants to the history of life on land and to methods for reading fragmentary remains.
- **[Richard Fortey](../../../people/Richard Fortey.md):** connects fossils, taxonomy and stratigraphy to extinction, evolution and the institutional history of palaeontology.

## Analytical hooks

- inference from fragmentary physical evidence
- classification, stratigraphy and institutional authority
- deep time, extinction and changing reconstructions of ancient environments
