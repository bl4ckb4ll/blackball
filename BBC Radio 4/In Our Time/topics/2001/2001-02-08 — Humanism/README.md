## Summary

The programme asks what “humanism” has meant across classical learning, Renaissance scholarship and modern secular ethics. The term can name a curriculum grounded in ancient texts, a confidence in human capacities, or a worldview explicitly opposed to religious authority; those meanings overlap but are not identical.

By following the word through different periods, the discussion shows that humanism was repeatedly reconstructed rather than handed down intact. It also tests the tension between claims made in the name of universal humanity and the historically narrow groups permitted to speak for it.

## Guests

- **[Tony Davies](../../../people/Tony Davies.md):** maps the changing meanings of humanism and questions narratives that treat it as one continuous doctrine.
- **[Lisa Jardine](../../../people/Lisa Jardine.md):** places Renaissance humanism in its educational, political and material settings.
- **[Simon Goldhill](../../../people/Simon Goldhill.md):** examines classical models and the later uses made of Greek and Roman texts.

## Analytical hooks

- historically unstable category presented as a continuous tradition
- classical texts as educational and political resources
- canon formation and the institutional uses of claims about the human
