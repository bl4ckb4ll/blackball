# The Philosophy of Love

- **Broadcast:** 29 March 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547dh`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547dh
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The discussion asks whether love is an emotion that simply happens, a judgment about another person, a practice sustained over time, or some combination of these. It moves between erotic desire, friendship, attachment and moral concern, testing whether one account can cover them all.

Ancient philosophy, modern accounts of sexuality and literary representation expose tensions between freedom and possession, particular attachment and universal ethics, reason and bodily desire. The episode treats love as philosophically difficult precisely because it joins evaluation to vulnerability.

## Guests

- **[Roger Scruton](../../../people/Roger Scruton.md):** develops an account of erotic desire, personhood and the moral meaning of sexual relations.
- **[Angie Hobbs](../../../people/Angie Hobbs.md):** brings ancient Greek treatments of eros, friendship and the good life to the discussion.
- **[Thomas Docherty](../../../people/Thomas Docherty.md):** examines how literary and critical traditions represent love, desire and the self.

## Analytical hooks

- desire, personhood and moral judgment
- ancient, theological and modern concepts should not be collapsed
- explaining conduct versus evaluating or excusing it
