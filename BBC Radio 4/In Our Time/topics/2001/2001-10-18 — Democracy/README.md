# Democracy

- **Broadcast:** 18 October 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547jm`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547jm
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The programme asks what qualifies a political order as democratic and why the answer has changed from direct participation in ancient city-states to representation in large modern states. Rule by the people immediately raises questions about which people, through which institutions, and with what protections for dissent and minorities.

The guests compare classical, early-modern and religious traditions rather than presenting democracy as a single Western inheritance. Elections appear as one mechanism among many; citizenship, deliberation, accountability, law and exclusion determine what popular rule actually does.

## Guests

- **[Melissa Lane](../../../people/Melissa Lane.md):** explains ancient Greek arguments about citizenship, participation and the dangers attributed to democracy.
- **[David Wootton](../../../people/David Wootton.md):** traces early-modern changes in sovereignty, representation and the legitimacy of popular rule.
- **[Tim Winter](../../../people/Tim Winter.md):** examines how Islamic political and religious traditions can engage claims of consultation, authority and democratic government.

## Analytical hooks

- ancient participation versus modern representation
- citizenship, exclusion and sovereignty
- translation of democratic claims across different political and religious traditions
