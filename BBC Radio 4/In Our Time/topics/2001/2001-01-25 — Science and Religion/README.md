# Science and Religion

- **Broadcast:** 25 January 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p005479y`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p005479y
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The discussion asks whether science and religion make rival claims about the same world, address different kinds of question, or overlap in ways that make conflict unavoidable. Evolution is a central case because it can be treated as an empirical explanation, a challenge to particular doctrines, or a premise in broader arguments about purpose and value.

The guests distinguish scientific findings from philosophical naturalism and religious commitment from literal cosmology. They also test whether a clean division between factual and moral domains clarifies the dispute or conceals the social authority exercised by both scientific and religious institutions.

## Guests

- **[Stephen Jay Gould](../../../people/Stephen Jay Gould.md):** argues from evolutionary science and considers the proposal that science and religion occupy non-overlapping domains.
- **[John Haldane](../../../people/John Haldane.md):** brings philosophical theology to questions of causation, explanation, value and divine purpose.
- **[Hilary Rose](../../../people/Hilary Rose.md):** examines science as a social institution and questions attempts to isolate knowledge from power, ethics and lived consequences.

## Analytical hooks

- different kinds of explanation: causal, metaphysical, moral and theological
- domain-separation arguments and their limits
- science and religion as institutions with social authority, not only sets of propositions
