# Third Crusade

- **Broadcast:** 29 November 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547ls`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547ls
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The episode examines the crusade launched after Saladin captured Jerusalem in 1187. Frederick Barbarossa, Philip II and Richard I mobilized different armies and political interests; the campaign recovered coastal territory but did not retake Jerusalem.

The guests reconstruct motives and outcomes from both Latin Christian and Islamic sources. Religious vows, dynastic rivalry, logistics, negotiation and later heroic legends complicate a story often reduced to a duel between Richard and Saladin.

## Guests

- **[Jonathan Riley-Smith](../../../people/Jonathan Riley-Smith.md):** explains Latin crusading institutions, vows and the religious arguments of the participants.
- **[Carole Hillenbrand](../../../people/Carole Hillenbrand.md):** uses Arabic sources to reconstruct Saladin’s world and Muslim responses to the crusade.
- **[Tariq Ali](../../../people/Tariq Ali.md):** adds a political and literary counterpoint concerning Saladin’s later reputation and the uses of crusading memory.

## Analytical hooks

- Latin and Arabic sources reconstruct different political worlds
- religious vows, institutions and material strategy
- medieval events versus later uses of crusading memory
