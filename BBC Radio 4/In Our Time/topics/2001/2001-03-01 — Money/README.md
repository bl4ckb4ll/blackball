# Money

- **Broadcast:** 1 March 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547ch`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547ch
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The discussion treats money not as a self-explanatory object but as a changing institution built from credit, accounting, law, state power and confidence. Coins, paper claims and bank balances work differently, yet each depends on enforceable relations among issuers, holders, creditors and governments.

The episode follows money’s capacity to coordinate exchange while redistributing power and risk. Financial innovation, public debt, wages and household survival appear as parts of one history rather than as a purely technical story of increasingly efficient payment.

## Guests

- **[Niall Ferguson](../../../people/Niall Ferguson.md):** traces the development of public debt, banks and financial institutions alongside war and state power.
- **[Richard J. Evans](../../../people/Richard J. Evans.md):** supplies modern European historical cases in which money, inflation and political legitimacy interact.
- **[Jane Humphries](../../../people/Jane Humphries.md):** brings labour, households and social distribution into a history often told from the viewpoint of states and financiers.

## Analytical hooks

- money as an enforceable institutional relation rather than a neutral token
- public debt, banking, war and state capacity
- households and labour versus histories told from the viewpoint of financiers and governments
