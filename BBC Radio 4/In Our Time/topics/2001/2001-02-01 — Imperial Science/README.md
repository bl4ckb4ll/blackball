# Imperial Science

- **Broadcast:** 1 February 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547b5`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547b5
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The episode examines how scientific collection, classification, mapping and agricultural “improvement” were entangled with European empire. Knowledge helped states identify resources, govern territory and reorganize production, while imperial travel and conquest supplied specimens, observations and problems to metropolitan institutions.

The discussion avoids a simple choice between science as neutral truth and science as nothing but domination. It asks which findings travel beyond their original institutions while still accounting for unequal authority, appropriated local knowledge and the material purposes for which research was funded.

## Guests

- **[Richard Drayton](../../../people/Richard Drayton.md):** shows how botany, improvement and state administration joined scientific inquiry to British imperial projects.
- **[Maria Misra](../../../people/Maria Misra.md):** connects scientific authority to the practical governance and political economy of empire, especially in India.
- **[Ziauddin Sardar](../../../people/Ziauddin Sardar.md):** provides a postcolonial critique of who defines valid knowledge and who bears the consequences of imperial science.

## Analytical hooks

- knowledge production joined to conquest, classification and administration
- who defines valid knowledge and who receives its benefits
- scientific authority versus the coercive institutions that sponsor and use it
