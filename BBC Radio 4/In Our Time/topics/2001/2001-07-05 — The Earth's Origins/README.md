# The Earth's Origins

- **Broadcast:** 5 July 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547hl`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547hl
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The discussion asks how the Earth formed and how people learned to reconstruct events billions of years before human observation. Accretion in the early solar system, differentiation into core and mantle, bombardment, cooling and the emergence of crust provide the physical account.

A second story concerns evidence and timescale. Rock structures, fossils, radiometric methods and comparative planetary science replaced short chronologies with deep time, while leaving open difficult questions about the earliest surviving record.

## Guests

- **[Simon Winchester](../../../people/Simon Winchester.md):** recounts the historical development of geology and the field practices that made deep time visible.
- **[Cherry Lewis](../../../people/Cherry Lewis.md):** explains the long effort to establish the Earth’s age and the role of quantitative dating.
- **[John Cosgrove](../../../people/John Cosgrove.md):** connects large-scale geological structures to the physical processes that shaped the planet.

## Analytical hooks

- deep time made visible through field practice
- measurement and dating methods versus inherited chronology
- instruments, maps and material evidence in geological knowledge
