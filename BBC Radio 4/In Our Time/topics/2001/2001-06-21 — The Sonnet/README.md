# The Sonnet

- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547gy

## Summary

The episode studies how a tightly constrained fourteen-line form has supported centuries of change. From Petrarchan sequences through Shakespeare and later revivals, rhyme, turn and compression create recognizable expectations that poets can satisfy, redirect or break.

Love is central to the sonnet tradition, but the discussion also follows political, religious and reflective uses. The form’s durability shows how convention can generate invention rather than merely restrict it.

## Guests

- **[Frank Kermode](../../../people/Frank Kermode.md):** places the sonnet within Shakespearean criticism and the longer history of interpretation.
- **[Phillis Levin](../../../people/Phillis Levin.md):** brings a practising poet’s account of constraint, sound, sequence and revision.
- **[Jonathan Bate](../../../people/Jonathan Bate.md):** traces the English sonnet tradition through Shakespeare, Romanticism and later literary history.

## Analytical hooks

- formal constraint as a productive condition
- textual transmission, sequence and revision
- a literary form remade across historical periods
