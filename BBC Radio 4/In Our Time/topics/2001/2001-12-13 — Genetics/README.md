# Genetics

- **Broadcast:** 13 December 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547md`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547md
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The episode traces genetics from Mendel’s breeding experiments through chromosomes, population genetics and molecular accounts of DNA. A gene can be followed through inheritance, evolution and biochemical action, but those uses do not make every trait a simple output of one hereditary unit.

The guests distinguish genetic causation from genetic determinism and connect laboratory mechanisms to variation in populations. The discussion also considers ageing, selection and the new ability to intervene in hereditary material.

## Guests

- **[Steve Jones](../../../people/Steve Jones.md):** sets Mendelian and population genetics within evolutionary history and argues against simple gene-for-trait stories.
- **[Richard Dawkins](../../../people/Richard Dawkins.md):** explains gene-centred natural selection and the sense in which genes persist through differential reproduction.
- **[Linda Partridge](../../../people/Linda Partridge.md):** brings experimental work on ageing and life history to the relation between genes, environment and selection.

## Analytical hooks

- gene-centred explanation versus genetic determinism
- heredity, environment, development and selection
- experimental findings versus extrapolation to complex social traits
