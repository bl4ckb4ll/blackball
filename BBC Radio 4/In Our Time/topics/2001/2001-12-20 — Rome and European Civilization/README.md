# Rome and European Civilization

- **Broadcast:** 20 December 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547ms`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547ms
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The programme asks how Rome became a repeatedly claimed ancestor of European civilization. Law, language, citizenship, urban form, empire and Christianity all passed through Roman institutions, but later societies selected and remade these inheritances for conflicting purposes.

The guests distinguish the lives of Romans from Rome’s later symbolic authority. Republic and autocracy, civic incorporation and conquest, cultural exchange and slavery were all Roman possibilities, so the legacy cannot be reduced to an uncomplicated civilizing gift.

## Guests

- **[Mary Beard](../../../people/Mary Beard.md):** brings Roman social, religious and political life into view behind later monumental images of Rome.
- **[Catharine Edwards](../../../people/Catharine Edwards.md):** examines Roman literary and cultural self-representation and its reception in later Europe.
- **[Greg Woolf](../../../people/Greg Woolf.md):** explains imperial integration, provincial change and the contested process often called Romanization.

## Analytical hooks

- Romanization as a contested and uneven process
- provincial agency versus one-way cultural diffusion
- later European identities built through selective claims on Rome
