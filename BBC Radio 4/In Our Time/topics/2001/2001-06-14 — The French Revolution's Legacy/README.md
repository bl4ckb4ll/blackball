# The French Revolution's Legacy

- **Broadcast:** 14 June 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547gg`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547gg
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The programme considers what survived the French Revolution beyond the governments it created and destroyed. Rights, citizenship, equality before law, national sovereignty and mass political mobilization became durable resources, while terror and counter-revolution supplied equally durable warnings.

The discussion follows these inheritances through nineteenth-century politics and literature. It separates the Revolution’s own contradictory course from later movements that claimed or rejected its authority, showing that a legacy is an active struggle over usable history.

## Guests

- **[Stefan Collini](../../../people/Stefan Collini.md):** traces the Revolution’s place in modern political language and later intellectual argument.
- **[Anne Janowitz](../../../people/Anne Janowitz.md):** examines how revolutionary hope, violence and reaction were worked through in Romantic writing.
- **[Andrew Roberts](../../../people/Andrew Roberts.md):** connects the Revolution to war, Napoleon, conservative reaction and the political order of nineteenth-century Europe.

## Analytical hooks

- event chronology versus long institutional afterlives
- emancipation, violence, war and reaction
- revolutionary language carried into Napoleonic and nineteenth-century state formation
