# Food

- **Broadcast:** 27 December 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547n1`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547n1
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The episode treats food as historical evidence rather than biological fuel alone. Ingredients, preservation, kitchens, recipes, restaurants and table manners reveal trade, technology, household labour, status and changing ideas of taste.

The guests join documentary history to practical reconstruction and global comparison. What people could eat depended on ecology and income, while what they considered proper or refined was made through institutions ranging from courts and markets to cookbooks and public restaurants.

## Guests

- **[Rebecca Spang](../../../people/Rebecca Spang.md):** examines restaurants, public dining and the political culture of food in modern France.
- **[Ivan Day](../../../people/Ivan Day.md):** uses recipes, equipment and reconstructed practice to show what historical cooking physically required.
- **[Felipe Fernández-Armesto](../../../people/Felipe Fernández-Armesto.md):** places cuisines and food exchanges in comparative global history.

## Analytical hooks

- household labour hidden behind cuisine and taste
- trade, ecology, preservation and kitchen technology
- status and refinement made through courts, markets, cookbooks and restaurants
