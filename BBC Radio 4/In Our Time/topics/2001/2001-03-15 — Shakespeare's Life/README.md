# Shakespeare's Life

- **Broadcast:** 15 March 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547ct`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547ct
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The programme asks what can responsibly be known about Shakespeare’s life from a documentary record that is real but fragmentary. It separates evidence about property, family, theatre companies, publication and litigation from later anecdotes and from attempts to read every dramatic voice as autobiography.

The guests show how biography depends on archival survival and interpretation. The scarcity of intimate evidence does not make Shakespeare unknowable, but it does place limits on claims about belief, sexuality, personality and the direct relation between an author’s life and the works.

## Guests

- **[Katherine Duncan-Jones](../../../people/Katherine Duncan-Jones.md):** brings biographical scholarship and a close reading of the documentary record.
- **[John Sutherland](../../../people/John Sutherland.md):** examines how publication, textual transmission and later literary institutions shape the life we reconstruct.
- **[Grace Ioppolo](../../../people/Grace Ioppolo.md):** uses manuscript and early-modern theatre evidence to distinguish archival fact from biographical speculation.

## Analytical hooks

- archival evidence versus biographical speculation
- theatre, publication and textual transmission as material institutions
- later canon-making shapes the life that readers think they know
