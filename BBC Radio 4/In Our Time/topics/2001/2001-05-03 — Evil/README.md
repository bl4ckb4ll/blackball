# Evil

- **Broadcast:** 3 May 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547g3`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547g3
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The discussion asks whether evil names a distinct moral reality or merely intensifies the judgment that an act is very bad. Nietzsche’s attack on Christian morality raises the historical question of how the concept developed and the ethical question of whether abandoning it clarifies responsibility or weakens our account of atrocity.

The guests compare theological, ancient, continental and analytic approaches. They distinguish explaining harmful conduct from excusing it and ask whether “evil” identifies agency, character, institutions or a metaphysical power.

## Guests

- **[Jones Irwin](../../../people/Jones Irwin.md):** uses Nietzsche and continental philosophy to examine the genealogy of evil and the possibility of affirming life beyond inherited moral categories.
- **[Stephen Mulhall](../../../people/Stephen Mulhall.md):** brings moral philosophy to questions of agency, intelligibility and whether some wrongdoing resists ordinary explanation.
- **[Margaret Atkins](../../../people/Margaret Atkins.md):** examines Christian accounts of sin, privation, virtue and responsibility.

## Analytical hooks

- historical genealogy of a moral category
- explanation versus excuse
- individual agency, character, institutions and metaphysical accounts
