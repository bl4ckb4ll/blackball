# The Glorious Revolution

- **Broadcast:** 19 April 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547fk`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547fk
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The programme revisits the removal of James II and the accession of William and Mary in 1688–89. It asks whether this was a popular revolution, an elite coup backed by foreign invasion, a religious war, or a constitutional settlement whose meaning was constructed afterward.

The discussion follows church, Parliament, taxation, succession and the different experiences of England, Scotland and Ireland. The settlement constrained monarchy in important ways but did not establish modern democracy, and its celebrated bloodlessness depended on excluding violence elsewhere in the three kingdoms.

## Guests

- **[John Spurr](../../../people/John Spurr.md):** explains the religious and political alignments that made James II’s rule and removal possible.
- **[Rosemary Sweet](../../../people/Rosemary Sweet.md):** brings social and civic history to the reception and longer consequences of the settlement.
- **[Scott Mandelbrote](../../../people/Scott Mandelbrote.md):** examines the intellectual and religious arguments through which contemporaries justified the change of regime.

## Analytical hooks

- the label 'revolution' versus contemporary claims of restoration and settlement
- religion, succession, property and parliamentary authority
- retrospective constitutional narratives versus contested events
