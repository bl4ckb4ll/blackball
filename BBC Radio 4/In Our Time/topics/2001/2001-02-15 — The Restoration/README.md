# The Restoration

- **Broadcast:** 15 February 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547bx`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547bx
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The episode considers the restoration of Charles II in 1660 after civil war, regicide and republican government. It asks whether the event restored an older constitutional order or created a new settlement shaped by the experience of revolution.

The discussion follows monarchy, Parliament, churches and the three kingdoms rather than reducing the period to court manners. Religious exclusion, memories of violence and disputes over sovereignty made the settlement unstable and helped prepare later constitutional conflict.

## Guests

- **[Mark Goldie](../../../people/Mark Goldie.md):** explains the political and religious ideas through which monarchy and allegiance were reconstructed.
- **[Richard Ollard](../../../people/Richard Ollard.md):** brings biographical and monarchical history to Charles II’s return and public image.
- **[Clare Jackson](../../../people/Clare Jackson.md):** places the Restoration in the wider British and Scottish aftermath of the civil wars.

## Analytical hooks

- legitimacy reconstructed after civil war and regime change
- religious settlement as a political institution
- English narratives tested against the wider British and Scottish history
