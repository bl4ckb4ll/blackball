# Oscar Wilde

- **Broadcast:** 6 December 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547m3`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547m3
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The programme considers Wilde’s plays, fiction, criticism and public persona together. His epigrams and aestheticism turned style into an argument about morality and social convention, while his celebrity made the trials and imprisonment of 1895 into a public destruction as well as a legal case.

The guests connect literary form to class, consumer culture, sexuality and performance. They ask how later commemoration can recognize persecution without reducing a writer’s work to biography alone.

## Guests

- **[Valentine Cunningham](../../../people/Valentine Cunningham.md):** places Wilde within late-Victorian literary culture and the later canon.
- **[Regenia Gagnier](../../../people/Regenia Gagnier.md):** connects aestheticism and identity to political economy, readership and the marketplace.
- **[Neil Sammells](../../../people/Neil Sammells.md):** examines Wilde’s rhetoric, theatricality and deliberate construction of a public style.

## Analytical hooks

- public style as deliberate cultural production
- aestheticism, readership and the literary marketplace
- later canonization reshaping a contested public life
