# Black Holes

- **Broadcast:** 12 April 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547f4`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547f4
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The episode explains how general relativity permits regions from which light cannot escape and asks how objects once treated as mathematical curiosities became central to astrophysics. Stellar collapse, event horizons, singularities and the effects of strong gravity provide the theoretical framework.

Observation supplies a second line of argument: compact binary systems, X-ray sources and the motions of matter around galactic centres. The discussion distinguishes indirect evidence for black holes from direct images that did not yet exist in 2001, and connects the objects to wider questions about galaxy formation and quantum physics.

## Analytical hooks

- indirect observation and theory-laden inference
- mathematical possibility versus astrophysical evidence
- compact objects across stellar, galactic and cosmological scales
