# The Enlightenment in Britain

- **Broadcast:** 18 January 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p005479m`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p005479m
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The programme challenges the idea that the Enlightenment was principally a French event that Britain merely observed. It examines British and Scottish developments in science, medicine, commerce, moral philosophy, religion and political argument, while asking whether a single movement can contain such different projects.

The discussion places intellectual change inside institutions and conflicts: print, clubs, universities, imperial expansion, war and state formation. It therefore treats the Enlightenment neither as pure emancipation nor as a detachable set of great ideas.

## Guests

- **[Roy Porter](../../../people/Roy Porter.md):** reconstructs the social and intellectual settings in which British science, medicine and public argument developed.
- **[Linda Colley](../../../people/Linda Colley.md):** connects Enlightenment thought to British state formation, Protestant identity, war and empire.
- **[Jeremy Black](../../../people/Jeremy Black.md):** places British developments in their international, military and political context.

## Analytical hooks

- national intellectual histories versus transnational networks
- print, universities, war, empire and state formation as conditions of ideas
- emancipatory claims alongside imperial and administrative power
