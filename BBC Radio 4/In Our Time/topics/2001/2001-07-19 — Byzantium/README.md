# Byzantium

## Summary

The episode treats Byzantium as the continuing eastern Roman Empire rather than as an exotic interval between antiquity and modern Europe. Constantinople anchored a state that combined Roman law and administration, Greek language, Orthodox Christianity, diplomacy and military adaptation for more than a millennium.

Political narrative is joined to material and textual evidence. The discussion considers iconoclasm, court culture, relations with Islam and western Christendom, and the transmission of learning, while questioning the stereotypes built into the later word “Byzantine.”

## Guests

- **[Charlotte Roueché](../../../people/Charlotte Roueché.md):** uses inscriptions, texts and the social history of late antiquity to connect Byzantium to the Roman world.
- **[John Julius Norwich](../../../people/John Julius Norwich.md):** supplies the long political narrative of emperors, wars, diplomacy and the survival of Constantinople.
- **[Liz James](../../../people/Liz James.md):** brings visual and material culture, especially religious art, to institutions often described only through politics.

## Analytical hooks

- eastern Roman continuity versus later decline narratives
- political chronology joined to inscriptions, art and material culture
- the naming of Byzantium as a retrospective historical operation
