# Napoleon and Wellington

- **Broadcast:** 25 October 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547jy`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547jy
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The discussion compares Napoleon Bonaparte and Arthur Wellesley, Duke of Wellington, as military commanders, political figures and later national symbols. Their careers intersected through the wars of Revolutionary and Napoleonic Europe but were shaped by very different states, institutions and ambitions.

Waterloo is treated as an endpoint rather than the whole comparison. Strategy, logistics, coalition warfare, imperial administration and public image help explain both men’s successes and the myths later built around them.

## Guests

- **[Andrew Roberts](../../../people/Andrew Roberts.md):** compares command, personality and political reputation, with particular attention to Wellington.
- **[Michael Broers](../../../people/Michael Broers.md):** places Napoleon within the institutions, regional conflicts and imperial administration of Napoleonic Europe.
- **[Belinda Beaton](../../../people/Belinda Beaton.md):** examines visual representation and the construction of the two commanders’ public images.

## Analytical hooks

- individual command versus military and state institutions
- biography and counterfactual reputation
- visual representation and political myth-making
