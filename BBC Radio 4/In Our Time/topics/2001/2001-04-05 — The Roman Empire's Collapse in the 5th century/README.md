# The Roman Empire's Collapse in the 5th century

- **Broadcast:** 5 April 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547ds`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547ds
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The programme examines the political fragmentation of the western Roman Empire during the fifth century. It tests familiar explanations—barbarian invasion, military failure, economic contraction, administrative overload, religious change and internal civil conflict—without assuming that one cause operated uniformly across the empire.

The guests also question the word “collapse.” Roman institutions and identities disappeared in some places, persisted in others and were recombined under successor regimes, while the eastern empire continued. The distinction between catastrophic fall and uneven transformation is therefore itself part of the historical problem.

## Guests

- **[Charlotte Roueché](../../../people/Charlotte Roueché.md):** uses late-antique evidence, including inscriptions and the eastern Mediterranean, to show institutional continuity as well as rupture.
- **[David Womersley](../../../people/David Womersley.md):** places modern explanations against Gibbon’s influential account and the later historiography of decline.
- **[Richard Alston](../../../people/Richard Alston.md):** examines the empire’s military, social and administrative structures and how they changed at regional level.

## Analytical hooks

- collapse versus transformation
- regional variation and uneven institutional continuity
- western political fragmentation versus eastern Roman survival
