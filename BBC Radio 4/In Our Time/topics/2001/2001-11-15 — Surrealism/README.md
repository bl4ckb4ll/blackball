## Summary

The discussion follows Surrealism from Dada and André Breton’s manifestos into painting, poetry, film and political experiment. Dreams, automatic writing, chance and startling combinations were meant not merely to make a new style but to loosen the authority of conscious reason and ordinary social life.

The guests test that emancipatory claim against the movement’s own exclusions and discipline. Psychoanalysis supplied a language of unconscious desire, yet artists and clinicians did not mean the same thing by it, and Surrealism’s anti-institutional gestures quickly generated institutions of their own.

## Guests

- **[Dawn Adès](../../../people/Dawn Adès.md):** provides the art-historical account of Dada, Surrealist practice, exhibitions and international development.
- **[Malcolm Bowie](../../../people/Malcolm Bowie.md):** connects Surrealist literature to French intellectual history and psychoanalytic interpretation.
- **[Darian Leader](../../../people/Darian Leader.md):** examines what the movement borrowed from psychoanalysis and where artistic ideas of the unconscious diverge from clinical ones.

## Analytical hooks

- the unconscious moving between clinical, literary and artistic institutions
- avant-garde revolt and later institutionalization
- international movement versus Paris-centred narrative
