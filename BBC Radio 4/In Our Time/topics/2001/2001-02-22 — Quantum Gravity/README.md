# Quantum Gravity

- **Broadcast:** 22 February 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00547c4`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547c4
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The programme examines the unresolved attempt to describe gravity within quantum physics. General relativity treats spacetime as dynamical geometry, while quantum theory describes other interactions through quantized fields; each works extraordinarily well in its own domain, but their assumptions collide in black holes and the earliest universe.

The guests compare approaches rather than presenting a settled synthesis. They discuss loop-based and other programmes, the possibility that spacetime is not fundamental, and the unusual evidential problem created when decisive effects occur at scales far beyond direct experiment.

## Guests

- **[John Gribbin](../../../people/John Gribbin.md):** sets out the physical problem and translates competing approaches for a general audience.
- **[Lee Smolin](../../../people/Lee Smolin.md):** presents loop quantum gravity and the possibility that spacetime has a discrete underlying structure.
- **[Janna Levin](../../../people/Janna Levin.md):** connects quantum-gravity questions to cosmology, geometry and the global structure of the universe.

## Analytical hooks

- live research programme versus settled empirical result
- mathematical consistency versus observational discrimination
- whether spacetime is fundamental, continuous or emergent
