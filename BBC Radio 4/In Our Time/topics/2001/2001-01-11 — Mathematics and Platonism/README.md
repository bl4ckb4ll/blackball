# Mathematics and Platonism

- **Broadcast:** 11 January 2001
- **BBC programme ID:** `p0054799`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p0054799
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

The episode asks whether mathematical objects are invented by people or discovered as features of a reality independent of us. It uses the Platonic tradition to examine why mathematical structures can seem timeless and necessary even though mathematical practice is historical, notational and carried out by embodied people.

The discussion also turns to the striking applicability of mathematics in physics. The guests separate several claims often run together: that mathematics has internal objectivity, that nature is mathematically describable, and that numbers or forms therefore inhabit a distinct metaphysical realm.

## Guests

- **[Ian Stewart](../../../people/Ian Stewart.md):** brings the perspective of mathematical practice, where structures are constrained and discoverable even when notation and questions are invented.
- **[Margaret Wertheim](../../../people/Margaret Wertheim.md):** supplies the cultural history of Pythagorean and Platonic claims about number, order and the cosmos.
- **[John D. Barrow](../../../people/John D. Barrow.md):** connects mathematical realism to cosmology and to the physical sciences’ reliance on mathematical description.

## Analytical hooks

- discovery versus invention in mathematical practice
- internal mathematical objectivity versus metaphysical Platonism
- the applicability of mathematics in physics does not by itself settle ontology
