## Summary

The programme examines Dickens as a novelist formed by serial publication, journalism, theatre and a rapidly changing urban readership. His plots, caricatures and memorable voices made social institutions visible while also converting suffering into popular entertainment.

The guests consider childhood, work, law, debt, the city and domestic life across the novels. They also ask how biography and public performance shaped Dickens’s authority, and why his enormous popularity has alternately raised and lowered his standing in literary criticism.

## Guests

- **[Rosemary Ashton](../../../people/Rosemary Ashton.md):** places Dickens within nineteenth-century intellectual life, reform debates and metropolitan culture.
- **[Michael Slater](../../../people/Michael Slater.md):** brings biography, journalism and serial publication to the interpretation of the novels.
- **[John Bowen](../../../people/John Bowen.md):** examines Dickens’s narrative methods, social imagination and changing critical reception.

## Analytical hooks

- serial publication and the economics of literary production
- social reform, metropolitan institutions and narrative form
- biography, journalism and later canonization
