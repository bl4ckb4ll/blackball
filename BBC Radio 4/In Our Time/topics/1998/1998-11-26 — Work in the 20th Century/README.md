# Work in the 20th Century

- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

This episode asks how industrialization, management and late-century “flexible” capitalism changed the experience of work. Richard Sennett focuses on the personal costs of unstable institutions and short-term employment. Theodore Zeldin takes a longer historical view of work, aspiration and human relationships. Melanie Phillips contributes a journalistic and social-policy perspective.

For Blackball this is a core episode because it treats work as more than employment status. A person can “have a job” while losing schedule control, continuity, craft identity, institutional memory or bargaining power. The discussion also raises a question that recurs throughout the project: whether technological and organizational change actually improves workers' lives or merely changes the form in which insecurity is imposed.
