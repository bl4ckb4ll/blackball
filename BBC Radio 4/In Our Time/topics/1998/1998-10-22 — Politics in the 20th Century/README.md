# Politics in the 20th Century

## Summary

This episode looks back at the twentieth century through the nation-state: why states gained such enormous claims on loyalty, whether those claims were weakening by the century's end, and what happens to political morality when power is exercised through institutions too large for ordinary people to see directly. Gore Vidal approaches the subject as a critic of American political culture and empire; Alan Clark approaches it as a historian and Conservative politician concerned with the nation-state and British political tradition.

The useful contrast is not simply left versus right. Both guests treat political language as something that can conceal power. The disagreement is over which political loyalties remain legitimate and whether the nation-state is a protection against larger concentrations of power or one of their principal instruments.
