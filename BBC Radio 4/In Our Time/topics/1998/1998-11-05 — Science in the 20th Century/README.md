# Science in the 20th Century

- **Broadcast:** 5 November 1998
- **BBC programme ID:** `p005457h`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p005457h
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

John Gribbin and Mary Midgley examine the extraordinary authority science acquired during the twentieth century and the limits of that authority. The programme considers specialization, the relation among scientific disciplines, the place of doubt inside science, and the temptation to turn successful scientific methods into a complete philosophy of life.

Gribbin speaks from physics, astronomy and science communication. Midgley presses on reductionism and on the way values can be smuggled into supposedly value-free descriptions of human beings. The result is useful for distinguishing science as a set of empirical practices from “scientism”: the stronger claim that scientific explanation is the only serious kind of explanation.
