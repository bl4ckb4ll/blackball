## Summary

The first *In Our Time* asks what changed about war during the twentieth century and, especially, how technical power altered moral responsibility. The discussion connects mass warfare, the ability to know about suffering at a distance, human-rights language and the Just War tradition. Rather than treating military technology as a neutral sequence of inventions, the episode asks whether new capacities to destroy and to witness destruction changed what states and individuals could plausibly claim not to know.

The episode also asks whether the century's wars produced stronger restraints on violence or merely new ways to describe old problems. Its useful tension is between historical explanation of why wars happen and moral claims about what people owe to strangers caught in them.

## Blackball hooks

- technology does not by itself equal progress; destructive capacity and information can increase together
- distance from suffering versus responsibility for it
- institutions that formulate rules of war versus people who actually experience war
