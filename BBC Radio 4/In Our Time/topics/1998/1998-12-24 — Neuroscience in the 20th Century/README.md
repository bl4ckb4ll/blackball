# Neuroscience in the 20th Century

## Summary

Susan Greenfield and Vilayanur Ramachandran review a century in which neuroscience learned vastly more about the physical organization of the brain while consciousness remained an unresolved problem. Ramachandran's work with unusual neurological cases shows how injuries and perceptual anomalies can expose the machinery normally hidden by ordinary experience. Greenfield approaches the problem through neurophysiology and pharmacology.

For Blackball, the episode is valuable because lesion studies and perceptual disorders are natural experiments: they can identify necessary mechanisms without automatically establishing a total theory of a person. That distinction matters whenever biological evidence is recruited into claims about intelligence, responsibility or human worth.
