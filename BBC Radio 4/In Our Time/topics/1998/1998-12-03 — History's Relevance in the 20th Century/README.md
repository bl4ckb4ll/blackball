# History's Relevance in the 20th Century

## Summary

Simon Schama and Antonia Fraser discuss what separates history from historical fiction and why the distinction matters even though historians necessarily select, arrange and narrate evidence. The episode asks how imagination can help reconstruct a past that survives only in fragments without allowing invention to substitute for evidence.

The programme is a useful methodological source for Blackball. A vivid story can reveal relationships that a table misses, but vividness is not verification. Conversely, insisting on “facts” does not remove interpretation: choices about which facts to collect and whose testimony counts are themselves historical decisions.
