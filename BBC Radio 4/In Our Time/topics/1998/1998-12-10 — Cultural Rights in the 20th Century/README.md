## Summary

On the fiftieth anniversary of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, Homi Bhabha and John Gray debate the tension between universal rights and cultural difference. The hard case is not whether cruelty is desirable but who gets to define a supposedly universal standard, how that standard is enforced, and whether appeals to culture can themselves be used to shield domination.

Bhabha brings postcolonial cultural theory and an emphasis on hybrid identities that do not fit clean civilizational boxes. Gray brings political philosophy, pluralism and skepticism toward universal progress narratives. The episode is useful for separating a universal minimum against coercion from claims that one culture's institutions constitute the universal endpoint of history.
