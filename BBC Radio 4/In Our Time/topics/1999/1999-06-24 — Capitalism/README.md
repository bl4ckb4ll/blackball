## Summary

Anatole Kaletsky and Edward Luttwak discuss capitalism after the collapse of the Soviet bloc, when triumphalist accounts of markets were especially prominent. The programme returns to Marx's critique while asking what social costs accompany “turbo-capitalism,” globalization and weaker restraints on markets. For Blackball the useful move is to separate aggregate growth from its distribution: wages, job security, hours, bargaining power, ownership and exposure to risk can worsen for some people even when total output rises.
