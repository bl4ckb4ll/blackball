## Summary

A. C. Grayling and John Carey discuss utopian thought from imagined ideal societies to political projects that tried to reorganize real people in the name of a perfected future. The central tension is between the value of imagining institutions better than existing ones and the authoritarian danger of treating disagreement as an obstacle to perfection. For Blackball, the useful discipline is to turn ideals into dimensions that can actually improve rather than demand a totalizing verdict that a society is simply good or bad.
