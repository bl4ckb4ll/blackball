## Summary

Richard Wollheim and Jonathan Dollimore discuss the history of the idea of the individual: whether modern individuality is a universal feature of human beings or a concept shaped by particular philosophical, economic and literary institutions. The discussion ranges from Renaissance literature to Marx, Freud and Foucault. The useful distinction is between the existence of persons and the historically variable social categories through which persons are expected to understand themselves.
