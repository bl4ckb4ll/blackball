# The Great Disruption

- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

Francis Fukuyama and Amos Oz discuss the claim that the shift from industrial to information economies produced a broad disruption of social institutions, family patterns and community life. Fukuyama presents the thesis associated with *The Great Disruption*; Oz provides a literary and political counterpoint. For Blackball, the episode is useful only if “disruption” is decomposed into measurable changes rather than treated as a single moral diagnosis: employment stability, household structure, crime, association, bargaining power and social trust can move differently.
