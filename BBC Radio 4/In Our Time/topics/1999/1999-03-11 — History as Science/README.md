## Summary

Jared Diamond and Richard J. Evans debate how far history can use general causal explanations comparable to those of the natural sciences. Diamond emphasizes geography, ecology, domestication and disease as large-scale constraints on historical development. Evans emphasizes contingency, institutions, ideas and the evidential practices of historians. The central danger is symmetrical: refusing structural explanation can make history anecdotal, while overextending a structural model can turn constraints into destiny.
