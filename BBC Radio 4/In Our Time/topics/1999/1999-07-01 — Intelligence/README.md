## Summary

Ken Richardson and Michael Ruse examine what intelligence tests measure, how intelligence came to be treated as a single quantifiable trait, and how claims about heredity became entangled with eugenics and racial hierarchy. Richardson brings educational psychology and criticism of simplistic IQ models; Ruse brings philosophy and history of evolutionary biology. For Blackball this is a core methodological episode: a test score is an observation produced by a particular instrument, not by itself a complete theory of intelligence, genetic causation, social worth or occupational potential.
