# Feminism

- **Broadcast:** 7 January 1999
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00545b0`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00545b0
- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

Helena Cronin and Germaine Greer discuss what twentieth-century feminism changed and what remained unequal at its end. The programme moves between political and economic inequality, cultural expectations, and arguments about sex difference. Cronin brings an evolutionary perspective; Greer brings a feminist literary and political critique. The important methodological issue is to keep descriptive claims about biological difference separate from normative claims about what opportunities, rights or social roles people should have.

## Guests

- [Helena Cronin](../../../people/Helena Cronin.md) — philosopher of biology and evolutionary theory.
- [Germaine Greer](../../../people/Germaine Greer.md) — writer and feminist critic.

## Blackball hooks

- biological description versus social prescription
- formal equality versus material access and power
- which differences are measured, and what conclusions are inferred from them
