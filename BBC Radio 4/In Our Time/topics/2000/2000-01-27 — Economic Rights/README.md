## Summary

Amartya Sen and Will Hutton discuss whether political freedom is meaningful without the material capabilities needed to use it. Sen's work on welfare economics, famine and capabilities challenges the idea that income or aggregate output alone measures human well-being. Hutton brings a political-economic critique of institutions that generate wealth while distributing insecurity and opportunity unevenly.

For Blackball this is a core distinction: formal permission is not the same as practical access. A person can possess a legal right while lacking food, health, education, time, bargaining power or money needed to exercise it. Economic growth therefore needs to be decomposed into actual capabilities and distribution.
