## Summary

Paul Strathern, Mary Archer and John Murrell discuss the history of the chemical element from older theories of matter through modern chemistry and the periodic table. Archer and Murrell bring chemistry; Strathern brings history and popular exposition. Mendeleev's table is especially important because classification did not merely summarize known substances: its structure supported predictions about missing elements later discovered.

For Blackball this is a clean example of a useful model earning confidence through risky successful prediction rather than prestige alone.
