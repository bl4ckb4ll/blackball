## Summary

Richard J. Evans and Eric Hobsbawm discuss what history can explain and what use knowledge of the past has for the present. Both are major historians, but they differ in scale and emphasis: Hobsbawm is strongly associated with broad structural histories of capitalism, class and political transformation, while Evans stresses archival evidence, contingency and the discipline's methods.

The episode is useful as a methodological warning against both extremes: disconnected anecdote cannot explain large historical patterns, but a grand pattern that makes every event inevitable ceases to be an adequate account of evidence and agency.
