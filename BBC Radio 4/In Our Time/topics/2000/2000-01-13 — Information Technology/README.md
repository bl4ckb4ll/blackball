## Summary

Charles Leadbeater and Ian Angell discuss the social consequences of networked computing near the peak of the first internet boom. The programme asks whether information technology disperses power and expands individual choice or instead creates new dependencies, surveillance, labor-market instability and institutional concentration. Leadbeater brings writing on the knowledge economy and public policy; Angell brings information-systems research and a more skeptical account of technological change.

The episode is valuable as a prediction audit. Claims made in 2000 about work, family, democracy and economic organization can be compared with later outcomes rather than remembered only through hindsight.
