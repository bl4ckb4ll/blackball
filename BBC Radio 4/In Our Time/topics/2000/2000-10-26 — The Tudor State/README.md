# The Tudor State

- **Broadcast:** 26 October 2000
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00546xd`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00546xd
- **Coverage:** bbc-verified

## Summary

John Guy, Christopher Haigh and Christine Carpenter discuss whether the Tudor period created a distinctly modern English state or whether later historians exaggerated the break from medieval government. The guests bring complementary expertise in Tudor politics, religion and late-medieval political society.

The methodological issue is continuity versus named rupture. A dynasty change or famous monarch can make periodization easy, but state capacity should be measured through taxation, administration, law, local enforcement, military organization and the reach of central authority rather than inferred from the label “Tudor.”
