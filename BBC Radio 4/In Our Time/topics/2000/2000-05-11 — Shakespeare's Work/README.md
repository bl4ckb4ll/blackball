## Summary

Frank Kermode, Michael Bogdanov and Germaine Greer discuss Shakespeare as text, theatre and cultural institution. Kermode brings literary criticism; Bogdanov brings the experience of directing Shakespeare for performance; Greer brings literary scholarship and criticism of inherited cultural authority.

The combination is useful because a play is not exhausted by a printed canonical text. Performance conditions, actors, audiences, editing and institutional prestige change what the work does in practice. Blackball should preserve the distinction between demonstrated literary achievement and the additional authority supplied by educational and cultural institutions.
