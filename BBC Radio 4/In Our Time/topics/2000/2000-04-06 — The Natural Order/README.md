# The Natural Order

- **Broadcast:** 6 April 2000
- **BBC programme ID:** `p00546ql`
- **BBC:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00546ql
- **Coverage:** bbc-verified

## Summary

Colin Tudge, Sandy Knapp and Henry Gee discuss how living things are classified and how evolutionary theory changed the meaning of biological categories. Knapp brings botany and taxonomy; Gee brings paleontology and cladistics; Tudge brings biology and science writing. The episode shows that classification is not mere labeling: a classification system encodes hypotheses about relationships and can change when evidence or methods change.

For Blackball this is a useful analogy only if used carefully. Human administrative or social categories can also look natural after institutions stabilize them, but biological taxonomy and social classification have different evidential foundations.
