## Summary

Jonathan Dollimore, Thomas Lynch and Marilyn Butler discuss death through literature, ritual, religion and the practical handling of the dead. Dollimore brings cultural and literary theory; Butler brings literary history; Lynch is both a poet and a funeral director, giving the discussion an unusually direct occupational perspective on death as material work rather than only symbolic subject matter.

For Blackball, Lynch's presence is particularly useful: institutions around death depend on workers who transport, prepare and bury bodies, even when public discussion concentrates on theology, grief or aesthetics.
