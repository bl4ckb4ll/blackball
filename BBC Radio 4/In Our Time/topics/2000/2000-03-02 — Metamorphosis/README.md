## Summary

A. S. Byatt and Catherine Bates discuss metamorphosis as a literary form from Ovid through later European literature and Kafka. Transformation stories make identity unstable: bodies change, people cross boundaries between human, animal and object, and social roles are exposed as less fixed than they first appear.

The episode is primarily a literary source. Its value for Blackball is interpretive rather than empirical: it can illuminate how cultures imagine status, embodiment and identity, but literary transformation should not be converted directly into claims about social prevalence or causation.
