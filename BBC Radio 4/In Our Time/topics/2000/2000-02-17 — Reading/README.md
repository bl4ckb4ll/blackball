## Summary

Kevin Sharpe and Jacqueline Pearson discuss reading as a historical practice rather than a timeless private act. Who could read, what was available to read, whether reading was silent or communal, what authorities censored, and what kinds of reading were considered dangerous all changed with class, gender, religion and print culture.

For Blackball this belongs beside literacy material. Literacy is not a binary possession detached from institutions: access to texts, time to read, schooling, censorship, price and the social uses to which literacy can be put determine much of its practical value.
