## Summary

Sarah Barber and Andrew Roberts discuss republican political thought in Britain, especially the seventeenth-century experience of civil war, regicide and the Commonwealth, and its later relation to monarchy and constitutional government. Barber brings scholarship on English republicanism; Roberts brings a broader political-historical perspective.

The episode is useful for separating a state form from actual distributions of power. Removing a monarch does not automatically create popular rule, and retaining a monarch does not by itself tell us how much political authority the institution exercises.
