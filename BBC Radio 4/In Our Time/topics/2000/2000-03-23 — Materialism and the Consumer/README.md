## Summary

Rachel Bowlby and William Gibson discuss consumer culture, shopping and the way commodities become part of identity and imagination. Bowlby brings literary and cultural history of retail and consumption; Gibson brings science fiction shaped by advertising, technology and commercial environments.

For Blackball the central correction is to keep the consumer and worker sides of the economy in the same frame. Cheap goods and abundant choice are benefits to consumers, but they do not reveal the wages, bargaining power, working time or coercion involved in producing and distributing those goods.
