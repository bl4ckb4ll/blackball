## Summary

John Houghton and George Monbiot discuss climate change at the opening of 2000, when the underlying greenhouse mechanism was well established but public argument still often treated uncertainty about magnitude, timing and regional effects as uncertainty about whether the problem existed at all. Houghton brings atmospheric physics and experience co-chairing the IPCC scientific assessment; Monbiot brings environmental journalism and political criticism.

For Blackball this is a useful early forecast record. Separate the physical claim that greenhouse gases alter radiative balance, estimates of future warming, estimates of specific harms, and policy judgments about what should be done. Each has different evidence and uncertainty.
