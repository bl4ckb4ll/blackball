## Summary

Robert Service and Vitali Vitaliev discuss Lenin's role in the Russian Revolution and in the construction of the Soviet state. Service brings archival historical scholarship and biography; Vitaliev brings a writer's experience of Soviet society. The discussion asks how much later repression was already present in Leninist institutions and how much should be attributed to subsequent conditions and leaders.

For Blackball this belongs with the history of delegated coercion. Revolutionary ideology matters, but so do the concrete institutions—party organization, police, censorship, prisons and administrative chains—that turn political decisions into everyday force.
