## Summary

Arthur I. Miller, Michael Howe and Juliet Mitchell discuss the idea of genius: whether exceptional achievement is innate, produced through development and work, attributed retrospectively by institutions, or some combination. Miller brings history of science and creativity; Howe brings psychology and research on expertise; Mitchell brings psychoanalysis.

For Blackball this belongs directly with the distinction between demonstrated ability and access to an arena in which ability can be developed and seen. A biography of a famous achiever samples only someone who reached the gate; it cannot by itself tell us how many capable people were excluded before comparable work was possible.
