- **Broadcast:** 5 October 2000

- **Coverage:** cross-checked

## Summary

Ian Kershaw, Niall Ferguson and Mary Fulbrook discuss how historians explain Hitler and the Nazi regime without reducing the history either to one uniquely evil personality or to impersonal structures that make individual decisions disappear. Kershaw's work on charismatic authority and “working towards the Führer” is especially relevant to the way lower-level officials anticipated and implemented perceived wishes. Fulbrook brings social and German history; Ferguson provides a comparative historical perspective.

For Blackball this is a core delegated-coercion topic. Mass murder required leaders, but it also required bureaucracies, police, military units, camp staff, local administrators, firms and collaborators who converted policy into material acts.
