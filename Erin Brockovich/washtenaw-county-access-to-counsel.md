# Washtenaw County access to counsel

Washtenaw County is a concrete local test of the difference between **a legal-assistance program existing** and **a person actually obtaining usable representation**.

## Officially advertised routes

### Washtenaw County Bar Association Lawyer Referral and Information Service

Michigan Legal Help describes the Washtenaw County Bar Association Lawyer Referral and Information Service as providing referrals to local attorneys after a prepaid administrative fee. The current description says the caller receives an attorney referral and an initial consultation, after which any further representation and fees are arranged directly with the lawyer.

This is a referral mechanism, not a guarantee that affordable ongoing representation will exist.

### Modest-means / reduced-fee programs

Michigan sources also describe reduced-fee or "modest means" programs for people who earn too much to qualify for ordinary legal aid but cannot afford normal market rates. Current State Bar of Michigan materials explicitly make availability conditional on the legal issue, income/assets, and the availability of participating lawyers in the area. They also state that the program is not free and that participating lawyers may require hourly fees and an up-front payment.

Those conditions are important. "There is a modest-means program" is not equivalent to "a qualifying person can obtain a lawyer for the matter they actually have."

## User-reported local failure — preserve as a lead

The user reports having attempted two routes in Washtenaw County that appeared, from their public presentation, to offer affordable access to counsel:

1. a bar lawyer-referral route that was expected to provide meaningfully discounted legal help; and
2. a reduced-fee / quasi-pro-bono route in which lawyers were supposed to accept cases for people needing assistance at less than ordinary rates.

The user's report is that **neither produced usable assistance as advertised or reasonably understood**.

Do not generalize this into "the programs never work" without reconstructing the events. This is precisely the kind of case Blackball should document carefully because program-level existence statistics can hide failure at the point of use.

## Reconstruction checklist

When the user is ready, record separately for each attempt:

- approximate date;
- exact program/service contacted;
- legal problem and practice area;
- household-income / eligibility facts relevant to the program;
- what the website, staff member, brochure, court, or other source represented;
- fee paid, if any;
- number of attorneys supplied or contacted;
- whether attorneys returned calls;
- consultation terms;
- fees/retainers quoted for actual representation;
- whether the matter was outside the program's covered practice areas;
- whether no participating attorney was available;
- whether the referral attorney declined the matter;
- whether the quoted cost was materially different from the expected "discount";
- the concrete point at which the service ceased to be useful.

## Outcome measures

For referral and modest-means programs, do not measure only:

- number of calls;
- number of referrals;
- number of participating lawyers;
- number of consultations.

Also measure:

- percentage of callers who obtain an attorney willing to handle the actual matter;
- effective hourly/flat cost after referral;
- required retainer;
- time to representation;
- abandonment rate after referral;
- matters excluded by practice-area restrictions;
- geographic gaps;
- cases where a referral technically occurred but no representation followed;
- user understanding of what the program promised before paying or applying.

The relevant Blackball distinction is:

**front door: "we help people find affordable legal assistance"**

versus

**back door: did this particular person actually get a lawyer at a price they could use?**

## Current public-source snapshot

As of the September 2026 source check:

- Michigan Legal Help describes the Washtenaw County Bar Association referral service as requiring an administrative fee and providing an initial attorney consultation, with later fees negotiated separately.
- State Bar of Michigan modest-means materials describe reduced-fee legal help but expressly condition referral on subject matter, financial eligibility, and participating-lawyer availability.
- State Bar materials also acknowledge that participating modest-means lawyers are limited and are not available in all communities.

These official descriptions should be preserved beside the eventual first-person case history rather than used to overwrite it.
