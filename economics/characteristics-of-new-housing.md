# U.S. Census Bureau — Characteristics of New Housing

## What this is

The Census Bureau's **Characteristics of New Housing** is an annual statistical product derived from the **Survey of Construction (SOC)**. It describes newly built privately owned housing in the United States rather than the entire existing housing stock.

This is useful as a long-run material-living-standards series: it records what a newly constructed dwelling physically contained, not just its price.

Official landing page:

- https://www.census.gov/construction/chars/index.html

Survey of Construction overview:

- https://www.census.gov/construction/soc/

## What it measures

The published tables include or have included characteristics such as:

- square feet of floor area;
- air-conditioning;
- bedrooms and bathrooms;
- heating fuel and heating system;
- lot size;
- parking and garages;
- exterior wall material and construction method;
- number of stories;
- sales price and price per square foot;
- financing;
- characteristics of multifamily units and buildings.

Current tables:

- https://www.census.gov/construction/chars/current.html

The current Census page separately publishes tables for air-conditioning and square feet, among many other characteristics.

## Historical depth

Older annual publications preserve long time series. The 2009 *Characteristics of New Housing* volume, for example, contains air-conditioning tables reaching back to **1973**. This makes the series useful for tracking the diffusion of household technologies and changes in the physical scale of new homes across decades.

2009 historical volume:

- https://www.census.gov/construction/chars/pdf/c25ann2009.pdf

Historical annual-table archive (2003–2017 collections):

- https://www.census.gov/construction/chars/historical/tables.html

## Microdata

The Census Bureau also publishes annual **Survey of Construction microdata**, allowing analysis below the level of the precomputed Characteristics tables.

The Characteristics landing page links the current SOC microdata releases.

## Interpretation

A crucial limitation is also what makes this source complementary to the American Housing Survey: the Survey of Construction describes **new construction**. It should not be read as a direct description of all houses occupied by Americans in a given year.

For questions such as “what fraction of the entire housing stock had air-conditioning?” or “what were the characteristics and costs of existing occupied units?”, use the American Housing Survey alongside this series.

## Why keep it in this corpus

GDP per capita is only one description of changes in living standards. Housing data provide direct observations of changes in material consumption: dwelling size, cooling, heating technology, bathrooms, garages, and other durable features. In combination with price, income, labor-time, and distributional data, these series make it possible to ask what economic growth physically bought households.

## Reuse / preservation note

These are U.S. Census Bureau statistical products and public-use data. Census policy encourages citation, reuse, replication, and verification of public statistical products. Census Bureau employee-created works generally are not subject to U.S. copyright protection, although particular files should still retain source and provenance metadata.

Citation policy:

- https://www.census.gov/about/policies/citation.html

For preservation, mirroring the relevant public tables or microdata is reasonable, but the repository should keep the original Census URL, release year, filename, and retrieval date beside every mirrored file.
