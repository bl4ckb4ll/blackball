# Public economic releases — March through August 2026

These sources are chosen to match Minnesota Implement's actual exposures before consulting broad national headlines.

## Steel and fabricated-metal input costs

### Bureau of Labor Statistics — Producer Price Index, July 2026

- Source: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ppi.t02.htm
- Series of interest: **steel mill products**.
- July 2025 → July 2026: **+22.5%**.
- June → July 2026, seasonally adjusted: **+3.9%**.
- Fabricated structural metal products: **+6.2%** year over year.

Why it matters: Koeckeritz's shop-level examples are much larger than +22.5%, but the official commodity series confirms that steel-specific inflation was severe even though aggregate final-demand PPI was only +4.7% year over year. His purchase mix, quantities, supplier terms, and less-common specifications can plausibly move much more than the broad steel-mill index.

### Federal Reserve — Beige Book, Minneapolis District, July 15, 2026

- Source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/beigebook202607-minneapolis.htm
- Ninth District input-price pressure remained elevated.
- Nearly half of surveyed firms reported higher nonlabor input prices in June.
- Manufacturing contacts continued to report elevated raw-material prices, **especially metals and polymers**.
- Construction contacts expected further increases in steel, copper, and plastic inputs.

Why it matters: this is geographically much closer to the West Concord shop than national PPI and independently reports the same metals-cost pressure.

## Freight

### Bureau of Labor Statistics — Producer Price Index, July 2026

- Source: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ppi.t02.htm
- Series: **truck transportation of freight**.
- July 2025 → July 2026: **+10.9%**.
- June → July 2026: **−1.8%**.
- Final-demand transportation and warehousing services: **+10.0%** year over year.

Why it matters: the official series says freight remained substantially more expensive than a year earlier even after easing in the latest month. It does not directly measure an individual LTL shipment of a bulky snowplow from rural Minnesota to the East Coast, so Koeckeritz's quoted $600 → $1,400–$1,800 route cost should not be expected to equal the index.

## Farm customers and capital spending

### Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis — Agricultural Credit Survey, Q1 2026

- Source: https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2026/higher-input-costs-pressure-district-farmers
- More than 75% of Ninth District agricultural lenders reported lower farm incomes in Q1 than a year earlier.
- **76%** reported lower net farm income district-wide.
- **65%** reported lower farm capital spending.
- Minnesota was weaker than the district average: **90%** of Minnesota respondents reported lower net farm income.
- The outlook remained soft: 61% of district respondents expected capital spending to decline further.

Why it matters: Minnesota Implement sells attachments to farmers and utility-tractor users. This is direct evidence that a key customer class had less capacity or willingness to make equipment purchases.

### Association of Equipment Manufacturers — U.S. tractor and combine sales

Primary series page: https://www.aem.org/market-share-statistics/us-ag-tractor-and-combine-reports

Recent releases:

- May 2026: https://www.aem.org/news/agricultural-tractor-and-combine-sales-see-declines-across-the-us-and-canada-in-may-2026
  - U.S. agricultural tractor sales: **−21.6%** year over year.
  - U.S. combine sales: **−56.1%**.
- June 2026: https://www.aem.org/news/us-and-canadian-sales-of-combines-see-slight-increase-in-june-2026
  - tractor sales remained below a year earlier while combine sales recovered.
- July 2026: https://www.aem.org/news/us-and-canadian-sales-of-agricultural-tractors-and-combines-decline-in-july-2026
  - U.S. agricultural tractor sales: **−10.9%** year over year.
  - combine sales: **−5.3%**.

AEM is an industry association, not a government agency, but its monthly member sales data are a substantially closer demand indicator than national GDP.

## Weather-sensitive snowplow demand

### National Weather Service — Rochester, Minnesota, winter 2025–26

- Source: https://www.weather.gov/arx/wintermeteorological2526
- Rochester-area meteorological-winter snowfall: **17.2 inches**.
- 1991–2020 normal: **35.3 inches**.
- Departure: **−18.1 inches**, or roughly half the normal amount.
- Average snow depth was also below the long-term average.

Why it matters: Rochester is a useful nearby southeastern-Minnesota weather station for West Concord. This independently supports the mechanism Koeckeritz described: lower snow use can postpone plow replacement. Weather is not a national macroeconomic variable, but for a business concentrated in snowplows it can dominate sales.

## Regional manufacturing demand

### Federal Reserve — Beige Book, Minneapolis District, July 15, 2026

- Source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/beigebook202607-minneapolis.htm
- District manufacturing activity increased modestly overall.
- Contacts nevertheless reported substantial volatility.
- An equipment producer described recent sales as **erratic** and said there was a sense that overall demand was softening.
- Agricultural conditions worsened slightly.

This is a useful example of heterogeneity inside a single official report: “manufacturing increased modestly” and “equipment demand is erratic/softening” can both be true.

## National manufacturing — the broad comparison

### Federal Reserve G.17 — Industrial Production and Capacity Utilization, August 18, 2026

- Source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/current/
- Manufacturing production: **+0.2% in July** and **+1.2% year over year**.
- Manufacturing excluding motor vehicles and parts: +0.4% in July.
- Manufacturing capacity utilization: **76.0%**, 2.2 percentage points below its long-run average.

Interpretation: national factory output was growing modestly. This does not tell us that every fabricated-metal shop, farm-equipment supplier, or rural manufacturer was growing.

### Census Bureau — Advance Durable Goods, August 26, 2026

- Source: https://www.census.gov/manufacturing/m3/adv/current/index.html
- July durable-goods new orders: **+1.1%**, to $339.3 billion.
- Excluding transportation: **+0.4%**.
- Transportation equipment led the increase.

Interpretation: a positive headline partly driven by transportation equipment is not a direct measure of small fabricated attachments. Use the ex-transport number as a less composition-sensitive comparator.

### Institute for Supply Management — Manufacturing PMI, July 2026

- Source: https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/reports/ism-pmi-reports/pmi/july/
- Manufacturing PMI: **55.6**, seventh consecutive month of expansion and the highest reading since May 2022.
- New Orders: 56.7.
- Production: 58.5.
- Prices: 71.1, still rising rapidly.
- ISM listed steel, cold-rolled steel, hot-rolled steel, stainless steel, steel products, freight, ocean freight, and fuel among commodities up in price.
- Steel and hot-rolled steel were also listed among commodities in short supply.

Interpretation: the same report can say “manufacturing is expanding strongly” and “steel/freight costs and shortages are severe.” This is one of the clearest demonstrations that Koeckeritz's account need not contradict an expansionary PMI.

## Small businesses nationally

### NFIB Small Business Economic Trends — July 2026

- Source: https://www.nfib.com/news/monthly_report/sbet/
- Optimism Index: **99.8**, above its 52-year average of 98.0 and the highest since August 2025.
- Uncertainty Index: **91**, well above its historical average of 68.
- Expected real sales softened by two points.
- Positive profit trends improved but remained net negative at **−16%**.
- Capital-outlay plans improved nationally.

Interpretation: average small-business optimism improved, but uncertainty and negative profit trends remained substantial. NFIB respondents span many industries; this should be treated as a distributional comparator, not a direct test of a steel fabricator.

## Census high-frequency business survey

### Business Trends and Outlook Survey (BTOS)

- Source: https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/data
- Updated biweekly and designed to track business performance, revenues, employment, demand, prices, and expectations.
- The survey supports state and sector breakdowns and is therefore a better future source for repeated comparisons than a single national headline.

Use: pull Minnesota and manufacturing cuts for the same dates as future Minnesota Implement videos. Preserve the vintage/date of each extraction rather than overwriting old observations.

## Steel/import policy

### White House — steel/aluminum/copper tariff proclamation, June 2026

- Source: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/further-adjusting-the-tariff-regimes-for-imports-of-aluminum-steel-and-copper-into-the-united-states/

Use this as a primary policy source for tariff changes affecting steel and other metals. Do not infer the shop's exact procurement-cost increase from the statutory tariff rate: domestic pricing, inventories, product exemptions, origin, freight, mill utilization, distributor margins, and product specification all intervene.
