# Minnesota Implement field report — August 2026

Prepared: 2026-08-31

## Research question

How far can the economic experience of one small manufacturing business diverge from public descriptions of “the economy,” and how much of the apparent divergence disappears when the public data are narrowed to the same geography, industry, customer base, inputs, freight mode, and weather exposure?

This directory treats the linked Minnesota Implement video as a **field report from one business**, not as a representative sample and not as something to dismiss merely because national aggregates differ from it.

Observation unit:

- Minnesota Implement, West Concord, Minnesota
- small welding/fabrication manufacturer
- custom attachments for skid steers and utility/farm tractors
- unusually heavy exposure to steel, freight, farm capital spending, and snow-removal demand

Comparison window: approximately 2026-03-01 through 2026-08-31.

## Provisional finding

There is a real **headline-versus-exposure mismatch**, but considerably less contradiction than a national headline alone suggests.

National indicators in July 2026 could reasonably be summarized as expansionary: ISM's manufacturing PMI was 55.6; Federal Reserve manufacturing output rose 0.2% in July; Census durable-goods orders rose 1.1%; and NFIB's Small Business Optimism Index rose to 99.8.

But public series closer to Minnesota Implement's actual exposures tell a much rougher story at the same time:

- BLS: steel mill products were **22.5% more expensive than a year earlier** in July; truck freight was **10.9% higher** year over year.
- Minneapolis Fed: Ninth District manufacturers still reported elevated raw-material prices, especially metals; an equipment producer called sales “erratic” and said demand appeared to be softening.
- Minneapolis Fed agricultural credit survey: 76% of district lenders reported lower farm income in Q1 and 65% reported lower farm capital spending; in Minnesota, 90% reported lower farm income.
- Association of Equipment Manufacturers: U.S. agricultural tractor sales fell 10.9% year over year in July and had fallen 21.6% in May.
- National Weather Service, Rochester: winter 2025–26 snowfall was 17.2 inches, 18.1 inches below the 1991–2020 normal — directly relevant to a firm for which snowplows are a major product.

The useful hypothesis is therefore not “the statistics are false” versus “the owner is wrong.” It is that aggregate series can combine sectors moving in opposite directions, while a small firm's outcome is dominated by a narrow bundle of inputs and customers. Minnesota Implement is exposed to expensive steel and freight, weak farm-equipment purchasing, and low local snowfall even while other manufacturing categories are growing.

## Method

For each business claim, prefer the narrowest public comparator available:

1. same firm / same owner contemporaneous interview;
2. same geography (Minnesota / Federal Reserve Ninth District);
3. same customer market (farm and compact-equipment buyers);
4. same input (steel rather than general inflation);
5. same service (truck freight rather than a broad transportation index);
6. same non-economic shock (snowfall rather than “consumer confidence”);
7. only then national manufacturing or small-business aggregates.

Do not treat a national mean as a rebuttal of a local observation. Conversely, do not generalize one shop's experience to the national economy without an independent distributional measure.
