# News and industry sources — March through August 2026

This directory complements the primary economic releases with sources that can explain mechanisms hidden by aggregate indexes. Preference is given to the same business, steel-market participants, freight-market data, and reporting that explicitly distinguishes output growth from cost pressure.

## Same business / same owner

### ABC 6 / KAAL — “Local business gives insight into the impact of economic trends”

- Published during spring 2026; interview refers to the March Owatonna Farm and Power Show.
- Source: https://www.kaaltv.com/news/local-business-gives-insight-into-the-impact-of-economic-trends/
- Subject: Galen Koeckeritz / Minnesota Implement, West Concord.
- Relevance: strongest independently reported comparator because it is the same proprietor only months before the August YouTube video.
- Concrete observations: steel purchase costs, snowplow share of sales, East Coast freight, missing/low-volume steel products, trade-show sales, and comparison with 2008/COVID.

This source should remain adjacent to the video note rather than being treated as an independent sample of another firm.

## National manufacturing reporting

### Reuters — August 3, 2026 — U.S. manufacturing at a four-year high; input prices elevated

- Source: https://www.reuters.com/business/us-manufacturing-activity-jumps-more-than-four-year-high-july-2026-08-03/
- Reports the July ISM PMI at 55.6, the highest since May 2022.
- Also reports the prices-paid gauge at 71.1, slower supplier deliveries, widespread pricing volatility, high freight costs, longer lead times, and supply constraints.
- Reuters notes that 62% of ISM respondent comments were negative even while the composite PMI was strongly expansionary.

Why it matters: this is almost the ideal “both can be true” source. A composite manufacturing index can strengthen because orders, production, employment, inventories, and slower supplier deliveries contribute to it while individual firms simultaneously report punishing input costs and supply problems.

## Steel pricing and availability

### Steel Market Update — July 14, 2026 — mills benefit while buyers wait for supply

- Source: https://www.steelmarketupdate.com/2026/07/14/smu-survey-mills-benefit-from-tariffs-as-buyers-wait-for-supply/
- SMU's flat-rolled-steel survey found tariffs holding prices up while manufacturers and traders reported higher costs.
- Buyers reported scarce spot tons and stalled imports.
- Manufacturers reported squeezed margins and fewer supply options.
- Survey respondents also cited elevated freight costs and shipping disruption.

Why it matters: Koeckeritz's statement that mills were concentrating on more profitable products and that some less-common inputs became hard to obtain is more naturally tested against buyer reports about spot availability than against aggregate industrial production.

### Reuters — July 30, 2026 — ArcelorMittal on U.S. steel-tariff costs

- Source: https://www.reuters.com/business/us-steel-tariffs-still-cost-arcelormittal-600-million-year-cfo-says-2026-07-30/
- Use as a large-producer / cross-border counterpart to the small-shop account.
- It is useful for documenting that tariff incidence is heterogeneous across mills, importers, downstream manufacturers, and exporters; do not assume the effect on a steel producer is the same as the effect on a fabricator buying steel.

## Freight

### DAT Freight & Analytics — August 11, 2026 — contract rates rise despite falling volume

- Source: https://www.dat.com/company/news-events/news-releases/dat-contract-van-and-reefer-rates-make-record-june-to-july-gains
- Dry-van and refrigerated contract linehaul rates posted their largest June-to-July increases on record.
- Rates rose while freight volumes fell across dry van, reefer, and flatbed.
- July spot and contract linehaul rates were materially above July 2025.

Why it matters: this is direct evidence that weak volume does **not** necessarily imply cheap freight. Capacity can tighten enough for shipping prices to rise while demand/loads are soft — closely matching the kind of experience a small shipper can report.

### Reuters — June 25, 2026 — FedEx Freight

- Source: https://www.reuters.com/business/fedex-freight-forecasts-up-6-revenue-growth-rest-2026-2026-06-25/
- Reports rising freight rates and higher fuel surcharges while FedEx Freight also experienced weaker shipments and higher wages.
- Again, weaker shipment volume and higher shipping cost are not mutually exclusive.

## Weather / product-market mechanism

### National Weather Service — Rochester meteorological winter 2025–26

- Source: https://www.weather.gov/arx/wintermeteorological2526
- Snowfall near Rochester International Airport was 17.2 inches, 18.1 inches below normal.

This is not “economic news,” but it belongs in the comparison directory because a snowplow-heavy manufacturer's sales can be driven by physical wear and replacement cycles. Omitting weather would falsely attribute all weak plow demand to macroeconomic conditions.

## Source-use rules

1. **Do not average away mechanism.** A strong PMI is not evidence against a steel-price spike.
2. **Do not over-generalize the anecdote.** One shop's steel invoice is not the national steel price index.
3. **Separate quantity from price.** Freight volumes can fall while freight rates rise; equipment output can rise while farm-equipment retail demand falls.
4. **Separate product mix from the macroeconomy.** A low-snow winter can hit a snowplow producer even during national expansion.
5. **Prefer downstream evidence for downstream firms.** Steel-mill profits or output are not the same thing as margins at a small fabricator buying steel.
6. **Preserve date and vintage.** These are fast-moving 2026 observations. Future updates should append rather than rewrite what was publicly knowable when the video was posted.
