## Claims requiring upstream recovery

### Farley: 5,000 openings

Needed:

- original episode URL/audio;
- exact date;
- full surrounding context;
- whether “Ford has” means Ford Motor Company employees, independently owned Ford/Lincoln dealership positions, or a mixture;
- whether 5,000 is a vacancy count, bay count, requisition count, inferred shortage, or another measure;
- geography and denominator;
- whether positions were simultaneously open or an annual flow.

### Farley: $120,000

Needed:

- exact wording and whether it is “is,” “can be,” “up to,” average, target, or top technician pay;
- pay-plan type;
- whether benefits/overtime/bonuses/training/apprentice compensation are included;
- distribution among the 5,000 openings;
- required Ford certification/experience;
- actual posted ranges for the relevant requisitions.

### “five years to learn it”

Needed:

Separate at least:

1. time from zero experience to employable entry technician;
2. time to independent competency;
3. time to Ford Senior Master or equivalent certification;
4. time to the earnings level used in the headline;
5. survivorship/attrition through that interval.

Ford's own current programs show that entry training and the high-skill/five-year statement are not the same thing.

## Quantities Blackball should compute when data permit

For a cohort entering at time 0:

- N entering training;
- N completing formal training;
- N hired into target technician work;
- N still in occupation at 1, 3, 5, and 10 years;
- annual clock hours at work;
- annual flat-rate hours paid;
- gross wage income;
- benefits;
- worker tool purchases and financing;
- tuition/fees/debt;
- unpaid training time;
- injury/leave/exit;
- certification level;
- local availability of jobs matching that certification;
- earnings distribution conditional on survival and unconditional on survival.

The advertised high-earner number should be reported only after this funnel, not before it.

## Falsification / counterevidence

The dossier should change if better evidence shows any of the following:

- $120,000 is genuinely guaranteed to essentially all of the 5,000 currently open positions regardless of flat-rate production;
- Ford's technician wage distribution is dramatically higher than the BLS dealership distribution because Ford dealers are an unusual subset;
- tool costs are predominantly employer-paid for the relevant Ford roles;
- flat rate is uncommon in Ford dealerships or the advertised jobs use guaranteed clock-hour/salary pay;
- attrition is low and most entrants who want to remain reach the advertised earnings within roughly five years;
- current local first-party postings routinely support the headline amount with attainable requirements.

Blackball should actively look for these results rather than treating contradictory evidence as a nuisance.

## Related shortage decomposition

Do not equate “vacancy exists” with “workers irrationally refuse good work.” At minimum separate:

- too few people exposed to/trained for the work;
- training cost or duration;
- employer experience/certification filters;
- location mismatch;
- wage/pay-plan dissatisfaction;
- tool/capital requirements;
- schedule or physical demands;
- retirement;
- workers transferring to adjacent occupations with better risk-adjusted compensation;
- employer inability/unwillingness to raise guaranteed compensation;
- dealership workflow/management problems;
- technology/product changes increasing training burden.

A shortage is an outcome to explain, not an explanation by itself.
